===Validate===
Ensure you know what problems you are addressing, and that the client has these problems.
===Obey Rules===
If a client asks for a specific format or media, ensure you deliver to that spec.  Otherwise you may be disqualified before they even read your proposal.
===Prove it===
Tell them what we can do for them and why it's right for them.  Focus on them, and the benefits they will receive.
===Be brief===
Clients have little time to read proposals.  Get to the point with compelling reasons.  Short sentences aid understanding and retention.  

Bullet points can condense thoughts into simple phrases.

Only include relevant copy!
===Be friendly===
Unless the client is in a very formal culture, be friendly.  Write as if you were speaking to them face to face.  We, Us, etc.

Simple words are more effective.  Contrast use vs utilize.

===Use graphics===
Don't try to paint the complete solution.  Determine the message you want to convey.  Use a picture to enhance the message.

Use titles and captions to explain the picture.  We look smarter if we can take a complex idea and display it in a simple graphic.
===Don't Oversell===
Stick to what they need.

If you limit your recommendations to their listed needs you show that you listened.

===Follow up===
Demonstrate interest with a followup call or email.  See if anything more is needed.  Find out why regardless if we win or lose.

===Language to avoid===
*Automation (unclear to clients what this is)
*Best of Breed (not always looking for best - looking for best fit).
*Deep Understanding (implies you know more about their business than they do)
*End to End (signals trying to sell them more than they need)
*Service Delivery Excellence (means different things to different clients)
*Real Time (clients are skeptical about the true real time capabilities)
*Resiliency (has an IT focus taint)

Also avoid words that make a promise that you can't fulfill
*Superlatives
**optimum
**minimum
**maximum
**best
**least
*Imply Perfection
**unique
**any, all, ever, none
**always, never
**world class
**uniquely qualified
*open ended or vague
**but not limited to
**includes
**for example, etc, e.g.
**turn key
**skills or knowledge transfer
*Legal Implications
**Best Effort - can imply you will expend all cash/resources.
**Partner/partnership/alliance
**guarantee
**not to exceed
*Imply guarantee or warranty
**complete
**insure
**ensure
**assure
**meet future needs
**increase competitiveness

==Presentation Deck==
Once done, look in Slide Viewer mode to ensure each slide title fits and flows.  Use a headline that encapsulates the slide.

Keep the number of words to a minimum.  Long lines hamper reading.

If leaving behind, include notes written in paragraphs.

Don't use clip art.  The competition does so you won't be differentiated.

Don't use animations and effects that distract from the presentation.

Focus on benefits and value.
*be provocative
*be prescriptive
*use bullets, graphics and charts to make content attractive
**be visual.  Avoid un-needed words
*use quotes to build credibility.
*be specific.  Be clear.
*consistent layout, fonts, sizes
