==Six Sigma Change Management==
Changing times means changing the way business is done.

Change agents 
*think positively
*take risks
*communicate well
*lead

You become a change agent  by focusing on the client.
===Role of a change agent===
*client champion
**become an expert in the client business
***External opportunities - general trends, business conditions, opportunities, threats to current business
***internal opportunities - change in methods, or improved process will be beneficial.
*client advocate
*client coach
===Responsibilities of a change agent===
#Find
##determine where change is needed
##You need to determine how to fill the needs gap.
#Probe
##investigate the requirements and potential impacts.
##what will bridge the needs gap
##what will the proposed changes require
##what will be the impact of the proposed change
#Rally
##Gather support.  
##Get key stakeholders to buy in.
##expect resistance - if you've done your homework their reasons for resistance won't be a surprise
##Be prepared to counter - If you've covered requirements, impacts and benefits resistance will melt.
##Point the way to excellence - create a vision and a roadmap that leads to it.  Drive a decision of the heart but allow them to use their head to justify the support.
#Lead
##Build and lead the team.  Also see [[Team Building]]
##Recruit the best people available.  This is most likely cross functional.
##Establish credibility.  
##Guide to success.

===communications planning===
Bringing change means changing people's behavior.  To do so, you need to communicate the details with:
*passion
*conviction
*clarity
*concision

To do so you want to:
#target the message
##right message to the right people
#segment the message
##communicate the need, vision, strategy and results
#deliver the message
##use appropriate media to reach the audiences
====Target the message====
Management, stakeholders and change team generally need a face to face meeting.
*brief intro
*role of management as champions
*exec summary of the proposed change and its business case.
*communicate the change to those that will be impacted
**expect resistance
**anticipate arguments
**Be prepared to counter the arguments
**Be ready to allay the fears.
*meet regularly with the change team
**clear ongoing understanding of the process
**individual responsibilities
**progress towards milestones and ultimate objective
====Segment the message====
*Need
**The business case - current state, problem, future state, goal, and the needs gap.
*Vision
**Paint an enthusiastic picture of the future.  Use facts to back it up.
**Have an elevator speech ready to build excitement
*Strategy 
**how will you arrive at the goal
**methods
**processes
**responsibilities
*Results
**Communicate regularly.
**Progress
**visual aids, charts, graphs.

====deliver the message====
Deliver to your internal targets.


Also be prepared to communicate with employees, customers, partners and the industry at large.
*posters
*newsletters
*website
*email
*formal presentation
*press releases
*advertising
*trade media
*business publications.

==Understanding Resistance to Change==
You need to be able to look beyond the objections of the naysayers to envision the future.

Change is a fearful thing.
===Types of Resistance===
The default reaction to change is fear.  The response to fear is resistance.

Resistance may manifest in different ways.
#resistance may be encountered from senior staff members or major stakeholders
##new tech solutions
##loss of territory
##loss of control over efficiency
##high stress environments coupled with added task may cause personal reaction.
#Resistance may also be encountered on the very change team assembled to make the change.
##lack of desire
##lack of acceptance
##lack of support

Team level resistance can manifest as
*non attendance
*non performance
*lack of interest
*lack of punctuality
*lack of participation
===Overcoming Resistance===
#recognize the resistance
#make maps - understand similarities and differences between key stakeholders.  
##Meet with key players and talk about your idea.  
##Group agreeable players as the basis of a coalition.
##Map areas of resistance to develop your strategy to address and defuse.
#make deals - find common ground. Build consensus.
##concerns and correlations amongst stakeholders.  What to address them.
##consensus and coalitions.  Can you overcome one persons resistance by overcoming the resistance of someone else.
#make meetings matter
##punctuality
##participation
##equality
##etiquette

Surprise is the enemy.  You need to anticipate events so that you can impact them.

===Building a Culture of Change===
*commitment to customer satisfaction
*commitment of senior management
*commitment of employees

====Customer Satisfaction====
Customer satisfaction is achieved through
*operations - all activities must be towards customer satisfaction
*processes - establish processes to measure customer satisfaction
*programs - training programs to improve customer satisfaction.

====Commitment of Senior Management====
*long term commitment - can't be changing stream every couple of months.
*mentoring - must be role models for employees
*reinforcement - positive performance should be regularly but immediately rewarded, same as poor performance.

====Commitment of Employees====
*impact - middle managers are most impacted by customer centric change
*threat - middle managers are directly affected by changes, and also bear responsibility for making the changes work.
*employees - development and training, monitoring and performance.
*suppliers - shift from buying on price to buying on quality to lower long term cost.

Employees are often fearful.
*training - As employees take on more responsibilities for their own performance, training becomes even more important.
*teams - synergy from the team is crucial.
*goals - aggressive goals encourage efficiency.
*rewards - creates motivation.
