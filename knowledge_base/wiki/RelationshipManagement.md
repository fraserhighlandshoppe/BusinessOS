==Relationship Management==
===Why Relationship Management Matters to You===
 https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-relationship-management-matters-you-keith-ferrazzi

When I was growing up in Pennsylvania, my father emphasized that I needed to get to my caddy job at the local country club a half hour early. At the time I assumed that was just his immigrant work ethic encouraging me to work twice as hard as everyone else to get ahead, but I still went in early, using the time to learn tips on how the greens were playing that day. I’d make sure the people I caddied for knew the proper pin placement or how thick the rough was to help them play their round better. They always seemed to appreciate the tips and saw me as more than just some kid carrying their bag. I always worked more rounds than the other caddies and learned, intrinsically, the value of managing those relationships. It’s a lesson I carry with me to this day.

It’s no secret people want to do business with individuals and companies with which they feel a connection. The question is how best to create those connections with customers. Technology has stepped into that gap with relationship capital companies like RelSci and BoardEx, and even LinkedIn, providing the insights to help people in business get to know their current and potential clients and customers better. I’ve written about my favorite networking apps before, and they’re a great way to stay on top of the relationships that matter most to you.

Beyond just the research, though, modern customer relationship management (CRM) technology is invaluable to the planning, scheduling and maintenance of your relationships. You can use it to keep yourself up to date on developments and news with your client companies, to personalize your outreach, support direct communications with clients and to help you hold teammates accountable for their relationships.

One of the greatest advantages of the modern apps and companies that are dedicated to relationship management, though, is that they don’t just tell you who the people are that you need to know; they help you find out who they know and, maybe most importantly, who you have in common and how. This information not only helps you discover how best to reach out to someone, but how best to serve them when you do.

Of course, tech can never fully replace human interaction. LinkedIn Connect may tell you when a major client is celebrating a milestone – it can even send the email for you – but without putting yourself into the outreach, you won’t fully succeed. You’re still responsible for “warming” your emails and pings. You have to determine who needs what kind of outreach and when.

For all this to work seamlessly, the level of attention needed to manage all your business relationships requires talented, experienced professionals. No executive can manage his/her client list alone and I would be lost without my brilliant Relationship Manager, Sarah Scott. Her ability to manage and develop my extensive network, prioritize events, meetings and calls around my travel, as well as client engagements and a sales pipeline has kept me on top of my schedule and prepped for anything that may come my way.

If I learned anything on that golf course growing up, it’s that relationships matter across all facets of our lives – but they need to be nurtured and managed with care. Whether it’s a friend, family or a client, people like to know they matter to you. Luckily, today, we have so many tools at our disposal to make that management easier. All we need to provide is the sincerity and generosity to make those relationships a success.
