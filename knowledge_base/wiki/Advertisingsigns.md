==4 Tips to Break Through The Clutter on Shared Monument Signs==
 https://www.rainmakersigns.com/blog/shopping-mall-monument-signs-that-break-through-the-retail-clutter-and-get-noticed?hsFormKey=b7dcf95998f2559665caa72f7d422351
by Deborah Scott on September 8, 2014


It's easy for a business’s sign to get lost in the clutter. We all drive by these types of signs every day.  In fact, we are bombarded with signs, all vying for our attention.  All good business practices come from viewing a situation from a prospective customer's perspective.  So how can we help prospective customers identify, recall and find our business more easily?  

The easy answer is by making your sign stand out.  An effective sign helps you establish a relationship with every person who passes by your business. Your sign can work two ways to bring customers to your door.  First, if a customer is trying to find your business, your sign will help them locate your storefront and navigate access streets or parking lots.  But what about those potential customers that are not explicitly looking for your business.  Well, your sign is forming an impression each time they pass by. The goal is that your store is top-of-mind when they have a need for your services or products. The challenge is to design a sign that will stand out and get noticed.

There’s no definitive right way to design a sign that will get noticed, but there are some good guidelines to follow. Here are five of the most important:

===Keep It Simple===

Think about this from your customer's perspective.  For the most part, they are driving when they pass your business - hopefully, paying attention to the road and not their phone or surroundings. They may have used a navigation device to get near your business, but as they approach they will start scanning signs looking for your specific location while paying attention to traffic and traffic signals around them.  When a person is multi-tasking, their brain automatically filters distractions. They will only have about 2-5 seconds to scan a busy multi-tenant sign.  A sign that has excessive text and colors could be completely ignored rather than help in branding and navigation.  Note how easy it is to spot the Target sign in the image above.  Simplicity stands out.

Keep your sign as simple as possible.  Ideally just the business name, identifying colors and font, and perhaps a logo.  If your logo is complex or surrounds your name this is an opportunity to develop a simplified version that retains your core equities and identifiers.  

 
===High Contrast===

The human eye is drawn to high contrast images.  White or light text on a black, navy blue or a dark ground typically has the most impact and is most visible from a distance.  (See upcoming post on using high contrast colors for your signs)  If your branding includes highly contrasting (light and dark) colors by all means use those.  If it has low contrast, ie. a variety of blues and greens or yellows and oranges then consider putting it on a contrasting light or dark background, respectively.  

Another component to consider in creating high visibility and contrast is the "stroke width" of your font.  A "fat" font (think fat sharpie rather than a finely sharpened pencil) will increase contrast, visibility and legibility to help your business get noticed.  A fanciful, scripted font may never get noticed on a large sign.  

You could use a different colored border around the edge of the sign to draw eyes into the core message. An experienced sign designer can help you with this the process.  Keep an open mind while being true to your brand and equity.  While no one wants to alter their brand, always keep in mind your objectives and make sure your sign achieves those objectives.

 
===Take Context Into Account===

Shopping mall monument signs with lots of businesses can quickly become cluttered.  There is no clear communication hierarchy and each business screams out to get noticed.  Before you plan your sign take the context of where your sign will be positioned into account.  We recommend taking a photo of the sign and then pasting in a few different versions of your sign to see which one will stand out the most.  This is difficult, if not impossible to assess in isolation.

In the examples above notice all the signs that are red or dark text on a white background. All that white "real estate" begins to merge together.  Maximize your impact by filling your space with a unique color that stands out form the rest.  If surrounded by signs with white backgrounds, add a colored back ground.  If surrounded by signs with dark backgrounds, consider a white or light background.  Your goal is not to blend in.  Taking the other signs into account increases your changes of standing out.

 
===Size and Position Matter===

If your signage size or position is negotiable, by all means make the most of your opportunities.  

#Remember that people read and scan from top to bottom and left to right.  All things being equal, the most desirable position will be at the top and to the left.       
#The larger your sign the more likely it is to get noticed.  Look back to the Target example in the image above.  The Target sign appears to be the largest retailer sign.  (Actually the Stop n Shop sign is the same size but by adding the red strip at the bottom, they effectively made their sign appear smaller.)  The point is, the larger signs stand out.  In some cases these are reserved for the anchor stores in the mall - but in other instances access to these larger spaces in negotiable.    If your permanant space is not negotiable ask if you can access temporary vacant spaces in the sign to expand the size and impact of your sign.  

Check out the example below.  While there was a vacancy in this small strip mall, Subway took advantage (in a good way) of the extra signage space that became available.  Once Royal Pet Pantry moved in, the sign was updated with the space shared evenly by both tenants.  While there was an expense for Subway to change the size of their sign during the vacancy and then change it back again, the extra business they were able to attract from the larger sign more than likely made up for the extra cost.  

 
===Note About Franchise Signage===

While you may have limitations on the changes you can make to signage for your business, your greatest opportunity resides in how you negotiate the signage portion of your lease.  Whether you are negotiating the lease terms yourself or working with a commercial real estate agent, make sure you attempt to negotiate size and placement of your sign in your property's monument sign.  Additionally, most franchise logos are supplied with versions that include either a light or white background or a dark background (see Subway sign example above).  Take the time to consider the neighboring sign when you select which background will stand out the most on your property's monument sign.

Running a business is competitive in every aspect and your sign is no exception. You face stiff competition to capture attention. With a little creativity and the help of a skilled designer, your sign can garner attention and attract new customers.
