==Recognizing Revenue==
===Revenue Reversal===
Time and materials contracts are relatively simple for recognizing revenue.  As time is billed, revenue is recognized.
*costs come in monthly
*revenue is billed and recognized monthly

For fixed price contracts with milestone payments, this is more complex.  
*costs come out on a monthly basis
*income only comes in once the milestone is delivered.
*accounting rules may allow revenue to be recognized before the milestone date.
**accrued: costs incurred + portion  of the milestone's gross profit
**this assumes that the right cost and right revenue all line up.
**if a milestone slips, the recognized revenue may end up being less than the costs.
**with these rules, if the milestones slip so that the milestone dates are longer than 120 calendar days, that 4th month may have all of the revenue from the previous 3 months reversed making a huge loss for that 4th loss.

To avoid this, have interim milestone payments.

Also, be aware of the impacts of payment milestone dates moving.

===Multi-Element Deals===
Multi-element deals may also prevent revenue recognition.  
 A gas company installs a tank at a customer site.  
 If the gas company promises to provide free gas for the next 10 years, 
 they can't recognize all of the revenue in the first year and thereby inflating thier books.  
 This is part of SOX!

===Financial Satisfaction Gaurantees===
Retention fees means the client withholds a part of the fees until a short time after completion and satisfaction.

clawbacks means a portion of the fees will be refunded if part of the delivery is failed.  You can't recognize this revenue until the client signs off on the satisfaction.
