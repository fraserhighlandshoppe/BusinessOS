Started out:
*Lead generation whitepapers.
**Facts
**Figures
**Reason and logic
**Start by addressing a problem
**Cause and effect
**Citing studies.

This approach is only mildly successful.  So he then took the same information, but instead of the dry facts, he started telling stories.  Real customers with a personality and a real problem, their interactions through the organization.  Telling the ripple effect all the way to the board room and the impact on bottom line.

Communicating via story is more powerful because the prospect's natural reaction is to resist the marketing effect once they recognize it as such.  This leads to a barrier preventing an open mind consideration of your product.  

*Fear of the unknown
*Fear of being misled
*fear of locking foolish in front of friends and families
*fear of the cost of time or money
*fear of losing money.

If you are too direct too early in the sales cycle, the fear kicks in.  To disarm the fear, empower your prospects.  Show them your competence through a demonstration of story.  Respect and acknowledge their intelligent.  Make your sales point indirectly, allow them to make their own conclusion.

People don't care about what you've done until they know why they should care.  

When telling the story, you get away from the braggart sales pitch by using a story that describes a situation they may have experienced, with the emotions and actions.  Only at the end do you mention who/what you are trying to sell to fix that problem.  You are trying to evoke a reaction in the audience.

To be effective in your story telling, you need to know the demographics
*Friends
*enemies
*Beliefs
*Age
*Gender
*Likes and dislikes
*Magazines they read
*TV and movies they watch

You also need to know your own intention.  Also, what type of story will you tell
*First person Origin Story
*Third person parable
*everyday anecdote
*Imaginary tale of possibilities told in the second person.

A dramatic story is one where the protagonist goes through the heroes journey - they face a challenge, and how they handle them.

Regardless of time period, culture or language, the most enduring story (folklore/children's story) a hero ventures forth from the everyday world into a magical area, where the hero then has to overcome the monster.  The hero returns with a power which he then shares with his friends and family.  Extraordinary challenge overcome through courage, obtain the object of desire and share with the world.  Obtains the object maybe with the assistance of a wizard, etc.  The wizard prepares the hero by providing guidance, advice, tools.  The hero descends into the cave to face their greatest fear.  They are reborn, slain the beast, obtain the reward, return to the world.

The hero's story allows your prospect to bond with you.  You can draw parables from the story which will allow you to drive home a sales point.

Selling stories must never end in tragedy.  They must also have a premise that ties your customer to the product.
*Owning brand X makes you more attractive to the opposite sex
*Owning X makes you sage
*Owning X leads to order and equilibrium.
*Owning X makes you an insider
*Owning X means you belong to an elite group
*Owning X leads to independence.
*Owning X leads to happy and healthy children
*Owning X leads to wealth and status.

#State the problem
#Reveal the enemy
#Discover the "sword/wizard"
#Share the result.  Go from worry to joy in the tale of transformation.

A true story is best, but made up is OK.  But it has to be an accurate reflection of life.  It has to be believable. 

We are hardwired to pay attention to our senses.  Make the prospects experience something in their imagination first.  Mountains of evidence, facts and figures are overwhelmed by vivid mental images.

'''Let me tell you the story ...'''

'''I had a strange dream last night'''

The story distracts people from the here and now.  It takes them into their imagination.

The key to unlocking an avalanche of sales is to create the illusion of ownership in the prospects mind.  Before they will trade money for real ownership, they need to have the mental image of what ownership means.  Make the prospects daydream about their future life; what will it be like once they purchase your product or service.  Creating a mental movie, these are conjured up from memory.  The personal image is infinately more powerful than what can be described.

*Imagine the first time she kisses you
*Imagine turning back the clock.  Picture yourself powering through the work day refreshed looking forward to a night on the town.

Story ideas are everywhere.  You just don't recognize them because you aren't actively looking.

Tell people stories that make people feel good, strong, proud and they will buy from you.  Make them the hero that lives out their fantasies.
