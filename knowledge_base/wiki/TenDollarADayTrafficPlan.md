6/3/1 ratio - 6$ for cold traffic, $3 for warm traffic, $1 for hot traffic.

==Cold Traffic Goals and Offers==
You need to continously introduce new customers.  Without them your business can not grow.

You are making people aware of you.  You will not take a product or offer straight to them.  That would be like going up to someone and asking them to marry you.

You introduce your brand to your target market to build trust and built authority.

#Introduction/Indoctrination
##Intro Brand
##establish credibility
#Pixelling
##for running ads
#Segmentation
##Fine tuning what offers you are sending them so they are relevant

Once you know who the audience is, you should talk about the following:
*Blog posts
*social media updates
*podcasts
*content videos
*lead magnets
*Quizes and surveys
*whitepapers

==Warm Traffic Goals and Offers==
These are people who have shown some interest in you in return (FB fans, twitter followers, youtube subscriber, website visitor, email list).

These people are in evaluation stage - they've shown interest in you and your market, but they aren't ready to purchase yet.

Here you run ads to encourage these people to convert into a Lead or a (low dollar) Buyer.

Talk about:
*lead magnets (exchange something of value for their email info/contact info)
*Quiz or survey - have to opt in to see result
*free or paid webinar.
*flash sales (low dollar offer 7-10)
*Product demo
*branding videos showing more about you/your business
*book
*free trial

==Hot Traffic Goals and Offers==
These are your buyers.

We are going to try to get them to buy again or something different.

We are trying to increase the value to the customer so that they buy again.

There are also times where you are trying to reactivate them.

 Retargetting ads and email marketing.

===Goals===
*Activation - remind the customer who hasn't purchased in a while that you still exist.
*High Dollar Sales.

====Interest Targeting====
You need to put the right message in front of the right people or it wouldn't do you any good.

You need to be very specific.  You need to have a key phrase/target/interest that isn't across a broad target.

 "But no one else would" trick

You need to be your target market, or you need to interview someone who is your target market.  Otherwise you need to do market research, getting into forums and talking to your target.

#Who are the authorities, big brands, thought leaders in your niche?
#what books, magazines, newspapers do your target market read.
#what events do they attend
#what websites do they frequent
#where do they live (more important for local business but still important)
#what tools do they use
#what is specifically UNIQUE about your target market.

===Creating Content to Cold Traffic===
You want your post/page/article to be:
*entertaining
*educational
*Inspirational
This is to presell people to become a lead.

To assist you need:
*relevance (offer in content to the content).
**the better the offer matches the content, the better it converts.
*consumption (needs to be consumable.)
====Write Backwards====
Create the content with the idea of getting them to the call to action.

The page is used to:
*create a touchpoint with your brand.
*segment people (you retarget them later using ads).
*get relevant leads ([[Lead Magnet]])
*Customer acquisition/activation  (sale offer)
*Offer awareness (high ticket items, premium/membership content, etc)
*Drive Consumption (of high ticket items)

You get some conversion from blog posts, but you also get brand touchpoints.

First step is to write the CALL TO ACTION.  What is it you want them to do, and write that first.  This way you can work backwards to get a highly relevant to the offer topic.

====Choose the Topic====
Step 2 is to choose the topic to support the CTA.

Answer the following questions to get the topic.  And then answer these questions in the article/post/...
#What does your target market need to know/understand/learn before they convert?
#What barriers do they have to converting?
#What objections do they have?
#What are they worrying/thinking about/researching before they convert on your offer?
#What is their primary pain point?

====Edit for Consumption====
Need to think about
*[[Crafting the Headline|Headling]] 
**Promise
**Clear, not cute
**compelling. not wordy (10-12 words), not hype
**use a swipe file
*First sentence (Introduction)
**Short
**punchy
**curiosity based
**create a gap/question "you've finally found it..." "Here's the biggest misconception"
*Transition (these can act as stop signs)
**Each new paragraph or sub header needs a transitory line before it.
**"Lets look at each XXX in turn" "Let's begin with"

Use formatting to break up the text
*bullets
*numbered lists
*Line break (paragraphs should be around 3 sentences each)
*block quotes

Don't force your reader to work hard to read the article.

Use peaks and valleys (put article on its side and have peaks and valleys).  This means that each wide section has narrow sections (e.x bullets and lists) wrapping it.

====Deep Tease====
This is optional, and shouldn't be used every time.

*NOTE or P.S. line
*Curiosity based tease deep in the content that is teased near the top.
*"Note method 5 requires..." "P.S. Method 8 needs..."
====Insert Two Calls to Action====
Add a text based call to action with a text based link.  People are used to clicking on text links.

Add a graphic that is the CTA with the graphic launching the link.  Usually used similar to a TV commercial - in the middle of the content.
*Include a button on your graphic CTA.  People are used to clicking on buttons.  Even if they can click on the entire graphic, if they don't see the button they may not click on it.

If you can't use a graphic, use a Johnson Box (html code created box with the links in it.
