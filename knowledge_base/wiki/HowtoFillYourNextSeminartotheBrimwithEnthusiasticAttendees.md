How to Fill Your Next Seminar to the Brim with Enthusiastic Attendees …

By Daniel Levis | April 19, 2011

Info-marketers selling live events are freaking out!

With travel costs piercing the ozone layer and malaise continuing to hang over the economy like a black cloud, getting bums in those seats is becoming harder and harder. Tell me it isn’t true …

Creating the kind of attendance that blasts past breakeven and makes your events a smashing success demands industrial strength marketing. The alternative is frustration, disappointment, and potentially catastrophic losses.

 You need to be able to bust through the marketing clutter… quickly and affordably communicate massive value… and get skeptical people with an iron grip on their wallets to select your event over every other option available to them. No trivial task in today’s brutal business environment.

How can you win?

7 crucial attendance-boosting ideas
you should implement yesterday …

Start Marketing Early — The competition for your potential audience’s attention is fierce, ruthless and relentless. Between TV, radio, print media, wire line Internet, mobile computing, email, social media and heavens knows what else, the conduit to your attendee’s awareness and consideration of your event is clogged up like Meatloaf’s arteries.

Where in the past it may have taken three or four touches on average to sell a ticket, today it takes TEN or TWELVE — OR MORE…

And that means you need to begin reaching out to your audience sooner. If you hold annual events, the very best time to sell tickets to next year’s event is at this year’s event, when people are most excited about the speakers, camaraderie, and business opportunities you’ve facilitated.

Someone needs to get up on the stage and sell discounted tickets to next year’s event. Failing that, start promoting your event at least six month’s prior. And plan to send more marketing messages than ever.

Maximize Revenue Opportunities at Your Seminar — It should go without saying that the more value you offer your attendees, the easier it will be to attract them to your event. Consider tying that value to ancillary revenue opportunities that exist at your venue.

This may mean more sponsors paying you handsomely to offer event-exclusive deals to attendees… giving your speakers an opportunity to sell during their presentations while paying you a healthy commission… or packaging up the video of your event and selling it to your guests before they go home.

Naturally, the more potential revenue you can reasonably expect per attendee, the more you can invest in marketing to get them to your event.

Select a Killer Theme for Your Seminar — The name of your event and the underlying meaning of the branding and activities that define it can have a profound impact on your success.

Put your corporate objectives on the back burner for the time being and think carefully about your attendee’s biggest fears, frustrations and desires. Don’t guess. Survey your past attendees to find out what’s keeping them awake at night. What are their biggest fears and frustrations? What are their biggest dreams for the year ahead?

Your prospective attendees are all walking around with their mental antennae tuned to the same station, WIFM — What’s In It For Me? Make it crystal clear to them with the tittle and theme of your event.

Use E-events to Pre-sell Your Seminar — One of the most effective ways to sell anything is through education. If you can give someone a taste of what they’re getting when they buy a ticket to your event, many of the barriers to purchasing come crashing down.

Arrange for preview teleseminars or webinars with your speaker roster and promote them to your list of prospective attendees. Work with your presenters to deliver a good mix of mind-blowing content while at the same time raising challenges that will be addressed at the seminar. At the end of the talk, sell tickets to your seminar.

If you have a dozen speakers, that’s twelve very classy mailing excuses. Not to mention the fact that an audio-visual sales presentation extolling the virtues of your event is much more powerful than mere print.

Create Joint Ventures — Enlist partners to help you widen your promotional reach…

Chances are each speaker you’ve contracted for your seminar will have a following of their own. Give them pre-written invitations to your E-events they can send out to their people. And pay them a generous commission for each attendee who signs up for your seminar.

Your sponsors may be even more eager to provide content for these online selling opportunities than your speakers. Get them to mail their customer and prospect lists and pay them a commission on ticket sales as well. Encourage past attendees to spread the news to their friends and associates. Pay them a commission on ticket sales too.

Use Direct Response Style Marketing — I suppose there is a time for brand advertising. This isn’t it. Each time you touch a potential attendee, tell them the whole story. Tell them in detail what they’re getting out of your seminar. What exactly will they learn? Who will they meet? What advantages will they take away with them?

Make the biggest promises you possibly can… back those promises up with proof and a rock solid guarantee… ask people explicitly to sign up for your event… and give them sound reasons for doing it NOW! The words you use to get this message across are crucial. Hire a competent copywriter to craft them.

Create Scarcity and Urgency — The most effective motivator in the world is a deadline. When you begin promoting your event, offer early bird discounts. As the event approaches, decrease the value of these discounts gradually on specified dates. And advertise those dates.

Put together bonus packages for early enrollment as well. The first 50 people get something extra. The next 50 people are also rewarded but less so. And so on. Impart cachet and privilege to early signups. Give your first tiers of registration prestigious sounding names.

And finally, Leverage Social Proof — What do past attendees say about your event? What accolades have been bestowed on your event by celebrity figures within your target industry?

Invest some time and money securing the audio-visual testimony of these personalities. And use it heavily in your marketing. Send out a list of registrants as the event fills up so prospective attendees can see if people they know will be there.

Work with your testifiers to focus on the networking opportunities your seminar represents. With skyrocketing fuel prices and general inflation rearing its ugly head, it’s critically important that you accentuate the face-to-face relationship-building opportunities that only exist at in-person events.

The Bottom Line:

People can obtain knowledge and guidance without ever leaving the comfort and convenience of their home or office. They don’t need to travel thousands of miles and spend thousands of dollars to attend your seminar to do it.

What they can’t get are the intimate human connections that grease the wheels of lucrative deal making and rapid business advancement. Sell the value of pressing the flesh above all other attributes of your event.

Make a rock solid case that yours is the premiere networking opportunity in your industry. Do this first and foremost. Market your event with intensity and intelligence. Then get ready to celebrate your blockbuster success!
