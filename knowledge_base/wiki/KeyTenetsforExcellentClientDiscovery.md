==Key Tenets for Excellent Client Discovery==
The relationship with the client is evolving.  What the client needs from the relationship has evolved from a transactional business 20 years ago to a consultancy.

As clients age and their needs get more complex they need more Financial Planning.
*How much you have
*Where you have it
*Money Managemen
*Insurance
*Trust

Now we are in a trust based relationship.  We need to build an emotional connection with the client.

===3 steps in great discovery===
#Prep before the meeting
##Draft a [[KSFII Discovery Meeting Agenda]]
##Gathering information you already know and will confirm.
##Clients are OK with their advisor doing research on them (Facebook, LinkedIn, Google).  Many expect that you have.
#Engage with the client.  Listening to them.
##Listen.  STOP TALKING
###2 ears, 1 mouth.
##If you must talk, Ask Questions
#Structure the clients lives in your mind.
##Know the client's top 3 priorities.

You can't walk in with a pre-conceived solution.  If you do, you aren't conducting a discovery.  You are conducting a manipulative questioning.
*advisors (naturally) avoid questions that take them to areas of the client's lives for which the advisor doesn't have a solution.

If we latch onto buzzwords, we stop listening and start thinking about products we might sell.

The more questions we ask, and the higher the quality of the question, the more success in the meeting.

===Things on the client's minds===
*Health and Wellness
*Carear and work
**What If Downsizing/layoffs
*Passions
*Lifestyle
*Family and relationships
**Birth of a child
**Death
**Divorce

Clients can go in and out of the various phases:
*Accumulation
*Protection
*Distribution

===Discovery===
A questionnaire is hard.  It adds a layer between the emotional connection.
*Can't get to the top 3 priorities because mediating between husband and wife and their priorities
**Husband
**Wife
**Joint

Use the Discovery Cards.  
*Make sure each person has their own deck so that they aren't compromising during the discovery.
*Frequently the husband and wives top 3 priorities don't match.


Once you know their top 3 priorities, you need to determine the outcomes.
*What additional information do the clients need to make a decision
*What solutions are available

When coming back with a solution/path, must tie into the Client's top 3 priorities.
*Sometimes there are things they need that aren't in their mind.  We need to address the priorities first.  Then get their permission to add these other items.

As clients age, their needs, expectations, wants will change.
*We need to get in front of these.  Because as their life happens, if we aren't there, they will rethink the relationship.
