==Cold Email Marketing Tips==
Contents of this page have been summarized/incorporated in [[Email Followup]]
===Critiques===
 https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/cold-email-critique-avoid-8-mistakes-sean-mcpheat
I receive tons of cold sales emails each day and 99% of them are deleted without me even opening them!

I received the following email last week and it was that bad that I thought I just had to critique it for you just in case you make some of the same mistakes with your cold emails.

Now I’m not into naming and shaming, we all make mistakes but below is the exact email I received from them with the company name omitted:

__________________________________________________________________

Subject: Special Offer

Good Afternoon,

Sorry for the unsolicited email. I’m under the impression that you’re responsible for making recruitment decisions at MTD but if you’re not could you please pass this onto the person who is responsible?

We specialise in recruiting top sales people and we’ve currently got a special offer with a flat fee of just £2,000 per position.

I have attached our company brochure for background on what makes XYZ Recruitment unique and I look forward to hearing from you.

Best Regards

John Smith

__________________________________________________________________

Where do I start with this?!
====Spammy Subject Line====

“Subject: Special Offer”

This is a guaranteed “non-open” subject line or worse still it may not even make it into the inbox because of spam filters!

The two best types of subject lines to use focus around asking questions or describing a benefit or idea.

Here are a few examples:

Question example:

“Quick Question” or “Quick Question About XYZ Company”

This will create curiosity and that’s the whole objective of a subject line

Idea example:

“Idea to help you prove the ROI of your projects”

This will pique the interest of the recipient. Of course this assumes that you’ve done your homework and that proving the ROI of projects is something that the prospect really needs help with!
====Impersonal Greeting====

“Subject: Good Afternoon”

This person was shooting fish in a barrel.

Please have the courtesy to address it to the recipient!

This tells the reader (if they get that far) that it’s just a mass email and they are hoping that some mud will stick to the wall.

Instead, you should be using customised variables like [[FIRSTNAME]] within your emails to address the email to an actual person.
====Don’t Apologise!====

“Subject: Sorry for the unsolicited email”

Oh no you’re not!

Of course they’re not – they are after my money!

Never apologise. It shows that you’re not confident and indeed that you’re having a gamble on whether its right or not to send the email
====You Haven’t Done Your Homework====

“Subject: I’m under the impression that you’re responsible for making recruitment decisions at MTD but if you’re not could you please pass this onto the person who is responsible?”

This tells me that you have not got a clue about who I am and what I do.

You’re basically taking a punt that I am the right person (remember, you don’t even know my name because you never included it) and that if I am not the decision maker that I can be bothered to forward it onto someone else.

Do your homework and make sure that I am a qualified buyer
====Too Vague – So What?====

“Subject: We specialise in recruiting top sales people and we’ve currently got a special offer with a flat fee of just £2,000 per position”

Every recruitment firm says that they are a specialist and that they can recruit top sales people for my company.

I need proof!

Tell me what results the sales people you have recruited have achieved for your clients. Don’t just tell me that you’re great!

You’re also jumping the gun and telling me about the fees without building up any value.

Surely the objective of this email is to start a dialogue with me so I get to understand your firm?
====You’re Making Me Have To Work!====

“Subject: I have attached our company brochure for background on what makes XYZ Recruitment unique”

Can I really be bothered to go out of my way to find out how great you are? Don’t expect me to research or read up about your company in my own time!

You haven’t made it easy for me have you?

What’s in it for me to even open the doc!

I am just reading the same old same old that most cold emails contain
====A Wishy Washy Call To Action====

“Subject: I look forward to hearing from you”

Yeah right!

This is a very weak call to action. You’re just leaving it up to me and I have complete control over the situation.

The objective of this email is for me to take some kind of action at the end of it.

You need to tell me EXACTLY what you’d like me to do next and this needs to be crystal clear.

So, here are some examples:

    “When would be a good time to talk about this?”
    “When would be a good time for a 10 minute call about this?”
    “Just reply to this email and we’ll set up a time to chat”

====Lack Of Planning====

It was evident from the state of this email that not a great deal of planning went into it.

There were no clear objectives or a clear outcome. It was not personalised and felt like the sender was just trying their luck by sending it.

Of course, the outcome is pointless unless you’ve got a red hot subject line that makes me curious. You haven’t got a chance of anyone reading your email if it doesn’t get opened!
Here’s a quick cold email success check list:

    Create a clear goal for the email and a focused call to action before you write your email
    Ask a question or offer an idea in the subject line
    Personalise the greeting
    Tell the reader the reason for the email
    Prove your worth with examples and case studies
    Sign off with the call to action
