==Generic==
Steps extracted from WFG details

===Preframe===
If you are doing a presentation, right at the start mention that if they find value you will ask them for a list of people who would also find value.

Share the mission statement.

 If your clients know who your IDEAL CLIENT is, they are much more able to recommend people to you.

 Don't ask them if they know who might need your services.  They don't know other people's situations.
 Instead, ask them who they know that are like them
 - new kids
 - just bought a house
 - just graduated
 - some other criteria that has nothing to do (directly) with finances.

===ask===
On websites, you can have a "Share" to social media link

On websites, you can have a "share with a friend" form to email the link

During presentations, you ask for the list.
*hand them a piece of paper with the table (name, email, phone) for them to fill in.
*hand them a pen (so they don't have to hunt).
*They need to control the pen and paper so that they have taken control and ownership for the list.
===Objection Handling===
*O  "I don't have any friends or family interested in X"
*A1 "Would you agree there was valuable info here?"
*A2 "If X didn't refer you to me, would there have been a different place for you to see this?"
*A3 "Wouldn't you like to give your friends and family the opportunity to make the decision for themselves?"
===Qualify the lead===
Get some extra info such as 
*How do they know each other
*How long have they known each other
*when to call.
===Ask for the Intro===
*IF they are comfortable, they could call the lead while you are there
*You can have a letter of introduction that they can hand the lead in person
**You could also have a pre-stamped envelope that they can address, and they sign an intro to your intro letter.

===Post Meeting Qualify the Lead===
*Social Media (FB/Insta/...)
*Google them
*linkedIn

===Ask For Referrals===
“Hey! Let me ask you, who would you say out of all the people you know, are 2 of the sharpest, most business minded people you know?”

So obviously if you ask the right questions and keep it very simple, you’re going to get the right answers and outcome you’re looking for when you’re asking for referrals.

Keep it very simple for your prospect to give you referrals, make it a fun exercise for them, and make them feel comfortable that if they do refer people to you, you’re going to take care of them in the most professional manner.

===Get The Introduction===
Once you’ve gotten their details, ask the person that shared their details with you to make the introduction so it’s not weird when you
connect with them.

I usually ask them to send a quick text to let them know who I am and that I’ll be reaching out.

Ask them to share an edification point. So for example:
 “Hey John, I just want to send you a text to let you know that my
 good friend Mary might connect with you in the next couple days.
 Mary is very connected here in Dallas and she’s looking for a few
 sharp people that have an Entrepreneur mindset. I told her to
 connect with you and see how it goes”

===Connect Within A Couple Hours===

I don’t like to pounce on my people and I don’t like to look desperate so this is where you play it cool.

Within a 2-hour window (I don’t like to wait more than 2 hours) send your referral a message to set up the connection. Either text or
phone call works best.

Here’s an example of what I like to say:
 “Hey John, Tanya Aliza here. I got your info from Bob and I think
 he connected with you today and let you know that I would be
 reaching out. Bob mentioned that you have an Entrepreneur
 mind and that we should connect. Are there a few times this
 week that would work to meet for 15-20 mins?”

If you send this in a text and you don’t hear back within a few hours, pick up the phone and call. You might have the wrong number and you’ll want to rule that out. Also, any professional would follow that up with a call if they didn’t hear back.

===Schedule It===
Schedule a time that works for the both of you. In person is best, but if you can’t make an in-person meeting work, by phone is great too.

===Ask Questions & See If They’re a Fit===
This is where you have to have some posture and remember that you should be qualifying your prospects to see if you’d like working with them. Most people in Network Marketing want to sign everyone up... Not ME and you shouldn’t want that either.

You’re building a team and customer base that you want to be excited about and they should have some qualities to be able to join the ‘Dream Team.’ Here are a couple questions that I would ask during this phase of the process:
#So John, Bob mentioned that you have a good Entrepreneurial mindset, tell me a bit about that?
#What do you currently do for a living?
#What’s the end goal?

You don’t have to ask too many, but these will help you determine how to approach the pitch and what their hot points are, from there you can go into your regular process for presenting or setting up a presentation.

I’m very relaxed and non-pushy with my business and it tends to relax the situation as a whole. I never pounce on my prospects because I want them to know that I’m analyzing to see if they would even qualify to hear more.

===Set It Up===
After a bit of conversation and listening to where they’re at, if I think they’d be cool to work with, I’ll say something like:
 “John, I think you’d love what we’re working on, but I can’t know
 for sure unless you see all the details. I can’t promise you
 anything, but if you saw a way that you could make some serious
 money without it interfering with what you’re already doing,
 would you be open to learning more?”

===Thank the Referrer===
When someone shares any referrals with you, send them a Thank You card.

If any of those referrals turn into business, send them Brownies.

I use a service called SendOutCards to do this, but you can find any service you like.... just do it.

Imagine how much more business you’ll get if you take care of the people that are confident sending you business.

*Ask for referrals in a way that shows clients how they stand to benefit from giving you referrals.
*Make it simple for your clients to provide referrals by showing them your Ideal Client Profile.
*Write down the names as your clients provide them.
**Research: Find out as much as you can from your client about the referred prospect.
*Build credibility with the referred prospect by sending a good letter of introduction from the client to the referral.
*Send seven or eight value-added mailing pieces to the referral before you make contact.
*Decide exactly what you will say before you call. Anticipate common concerns.
*Don’t sell on the phone; just try to determine if there is a reason to meet and if there is a potential for a good business relationship.
**Eliminate all small talk and chitchat and open the interview with the meaningful and emotional values conversation. (Some prospects might even cry) Meaningful human connection and conversation are what builds trust.
**Politely but firmly end the interview with people who do not cooperate in the first five minutes. It’s OK not to sell everyone.
**Never talk about yourself, your credentials or your company. Let people draw their conclusions about your competence and credibility by how you behave instead of by what you say. Have a simple brochure they can read at their leisure. Give it to them only if they ask.
**Insist prospects bring all their financial documents to their first meeting and expect them to cooperate.
**Begin asking where all the clients’ money is, reviewing their financial documents and prioritizing their goals just 10-15 minutes into the first meeting.
**Let their own values and financial reality (Financial Road Map) motivate them to action. If their own truth doesn’t move them emotionally, then they must not care more about their financial success than you do, so you should disengage.  Using fear-based scare tactics and greed-oriented performance promises to sell financial products and services is a sign of poor skills or desperation, not the basis of a healthy and fruitful relationship.
**If you get objections, it’s probably because you let someone slip through your filter who should have been shown the door in the first five minutes.
**Refuse to go to work for people without a commitment to do business with you.
**“Closing” is the natural outcome of conducting a good interview and creating an accurate financial strategy. Once is the correct number of times to ask them to take action.
