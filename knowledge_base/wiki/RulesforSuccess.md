==The Founder of FedEx Shares His Top 10 Rules for Success==
 Evan Carmichael
June 2, 2016

He's the founder, chairman, president and CEO of FedEx. In 2014, Fortune Magazine ranked him 26th among the list of "World's 50 Greatest Leaders." He has an estimated net worth of $3.6 billion.

He's Frederick W. Smith, and here are his top 10 rules for success.
===Believe.===

He was crippled by bone disease as a small boy but regained his health by age 10.
===Be self-effacing.===

He had a great interest in flying and became an amateur pilot as a teen.
===Have a vision.===

In Yale, he wrote a paper for an economics class, outlining overnight delivery service in a computer-information age.
===Be willing to take a chance.===

He received his bachelor's degree in economics in 1966.
===Be a team player.===

After graduation, he was commissioned in the U.S. Marine Corps, serving for three years.
===Innovate.===

He served two tours of duty in Vietnam, flying with pilots on over 200 combat missions.
===Think globally.===

He founded Federal Express with his $4 million inheritance and raised $91 million in venture capital.
===Be prepared.===

In the early days of FedEx, he had to go to great lengths to keep the company afloat.
===Master your craft.===

While CEO of FedEx in 2008, he earned a total compensation of $10,434,589.
===Keep getting better.===

He was inducted into the Junior Achievement U.S. Business Hall of Fame in 1998.

==Hillary Clinton's Top 10 Rules for Success==
 Evan Carmichael
May 26, 2016

She's an American politician who served as the 67th U.S. Secretary of State. The wife of the 42nd U.S. President Bill Clinton, she was First Lady of the United States. She served as a U.S. Senator from New York from 2001 to 2009.

She's Hillary Clinton, and here are her top 10 rules for success.
===Be resilient.===

She attended Wellesley College, graduating in 1969, and earned a J.D. from Yale Law School in 1973.
===Get ready.===

After serving as a congressional legal counsel, she moved to Arkansas, marrying Bill Clinton in 1975.
===Find your drive.===

She co-founded Arkansas Advocates for Children and Families in 1977.
===Be direct.===

She became the first female chair of the Legal Services Corporation in 1978.
===Don't take criticism personally.===

She served on the boards of corporations including Wal-Mart.
===Compromise===

In 1997 and 1999, she helped create programs for children's health insurance, adoption and foster care.
===Be true to yourself. ===

She was elected in 2000 as the first female senator from New York -- the only first lady ever to have sought elective office.
===Keep going===

Running for president in 2008, she won far more delegates than any previous female candidate.
===Be kind.===

Leaving office after Obama's first term, she authored her fifth book and undertook speaking engagements.
===Don't shy away from a challenge.===

She announced her second run for the Democratic nomination in the 2016 presidential election.
