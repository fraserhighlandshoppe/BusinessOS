==The Problem==
*A triggering event
**death, disability, dispute, divorce leads to the buy-out of a shareholders' interet
*Problem 1 - buyer will want a low value and seller will want a high value
*Problem 2 - few companies or shareholders have the cash available.  How to fund?
*Consequence - dispute can result in long delays and significant costs (e.g. legal and valuation)
**Business, shareholders and families suffer.
Business valuation is a critical issue when crafting the buy sell agreement.

A buy -sell agreenebt is a legally binding contract. It stipulates how the shareholders interest will be reassigned if a partner dies or otherwise leaves the business.

===Common Triggering events===
*death
*disability
*divorce
*Disagreement/Dispute
*Default (bankrupty)
*Quit/Termination/Retirement
*Breach of Shareholder Agreement
*Third Party offers
**ROFR
**TAg-along
**drag-along

==Purpose of Buy Sell Agreement==
*Provides the process/framework to deal with the triuggering events to determine the value/price for the selling shares.
*create a market for an otherwise unmarketable asset.
*minimize future disputes among shareholders.
==3 Types==
===Fixed Price agreements===
*establishes a fixed price e.g. $1/share
*most have a price reset periodically
*Cons:
**price seldom reset
**value changes
**fixed price becomes outdated quicky
**increases likelihood of disputes and litigation
===formula agreements===
States a formula to determin the price for the buyout.
*could be set based on book value or multiple defined measures of earnings.
*no one formula provides a consistent and reliable value
**changes to company, industry, economy etc over time.
*how to define/interpret terms of the formula.
*doesn't allow for changes in risk factors.
===valuation process agreements===
*estalishes appraisal process to determine value
*specifies timing of payment
**in full on closing/over time/...
*iundicates if one valuator or 2 will be retained
**who and how to select
**if conclusion within 10% use average
**if 2 and conclusions out 10% spread, bring in 3rd
*does third review and select, or do third valuation?
More complex with many factors.

Bias can leak in from buyer and seller motives.

Minimize disputes:
*have single appraiser.
**select now
***gives all parties to have input and agree on appraiser before there is a potential dispute.
**value now
***once seleted, appraisuer provides a baseline valuation.  Parties can provide comments before valuation is finalized.
**repeat annual/bi-annual
***account for changes in business, economy
*address 7 critical valuation elements
*should become part of year end process. (tax returns, valuation)
May be more expensive up front, but inexpensive when compared to costs of a dispute or using 2 valuators.
==7 Critical Valuation Elements==
===standard value===
Agreement must define value
*fair market value
**minority (non controlling) interest discount to be considered
**discount to pro rata value of a non controlling interest for lack of control and lack of marketability features.
*fair value
*liquidation value
===basis of value===
value is to be determined ona  going concern basis (not liquidation basis)

presumes company will continue to operate.

liquidation value is only if expected/likely.

Liquidation values, disposition costs (i.e. taxes)
===level of value===
#Strategic control
#Financial control
##Minority interest discount
#marketability discount
===valuation date===
*specify the valuation date.
Value is different at different times
*typically date of triggering event.
===appraiser Qualifications===
*how and when valuator selected
*one/two/three?
*ensure proper valuation qualifications.
===professional standards===
*Canadian Institue of Chartered Business Caluators 
*or International Valuation Standards Council
*or Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice
===funding mechanism===
*How will purchase be funded
*Timing on payment
*interest rate
*life insurance
**IF life insurance is funding vehicle and therefore not part of value of business
**IF life insurance is corporate asset then proceeds should be added to value of business

One way buy-sell agreements
*provides opportunity for non owner (e.g. key employee or family member) to buy in using insurance proceeds.
*valuation issues apply equally here too
*how will buuy-in price be determioned?
*how much insurance coverage needed?
*key valuation include owner dependence and transferability of business to new owners.
