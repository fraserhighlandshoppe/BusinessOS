==How to talk to your insurance clients about investments==
===Starting the Conversation===
*Where did you make your RRSP contribution last year?
*How much is your RRSP earning?
*Do you have pension funds with a former employer?
*Are you happy with them?  Would you like me to review them with our investment specialist?
===What clients say===
*I'm nervous about investments
*I don't know if I'll ever be able to retire
*I haven't seen my advisor in over a year
*Should I put money into a TFSA or an RRSP?

Many people walk into the bank.  They typically don't have an emotional attachment to their bank...

==Life License Offers==
*GIC
*Annuity
*Segregated Fund
==Psychology==
Everyone is happy as markets go up.

Everyone gets nervous as markets go down.

Nervousness does not mean they should not be invested.  But it might mean they aren't in investments they are comfortable with.

==RRSP Contributions==
It's OK to have RRSPs at more than one financial institution.  However easier to manage/advise on if at one place.

It's OK to contribute to cash, and revisit the investment choice later.

You don't have to claim the deduction in the year you contribute.

==TFSA==
*Up to 6000/year for clients over 18.
*Contribution room from past.
*Can be deposited into a daily interest or guaranteed investment fund.
*Good for short term saving (i.e. less than 5 years) 
*Good for low income earners ($47630)
TFSA is after tax money.  No deduction, but no taxes owed upon withdrawal.

==Features and Suitability==

*Compared to mutual funds, ETF, stocks and bonds; Seg funds have 
**maturity guarantee   75/100 (10-15 year.  Cheap because odds of 25% loss over 15 years is small)
**death benefit 75/100   (Good for estate planning)
*Can provide guaranteed retirement income
*Can have a named beneficiary
*can be used like a family trust in some situations.
**ie set up as annuity or put in trust for lump sum at a certain age.

Most stock brokers don't sell stocks today; they sell mutual funds and ETFs.

Mutual funds transact at end of day.

ETF trades on the exchange, minute by minute.

Seg funds can be switched a couple of times a year.
