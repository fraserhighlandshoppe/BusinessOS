==Excellent Mentors==
 https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/assigning-mentors-start-isnt-enough-heres-why-carla-harris
In 2015, it is still the case that none of us can maximize our professional success without other relationships. It is not enough to be smart and work hard, you will need other people’s relationships in order to make your success. One of the most important relationships that you can have is a mentor relationship.

A mentor is the person that you can tell “the good, the bad, and the ugly” to as it relates to your career. This is the person that you can share your fears, concerns, mistakes, triumphs and career strategies with. In order for someone to be a great mentor to you, they must know you very well and you must trust them. A mentor’s job is to give you tailored advice, tailored specifically to you and your career aspirations, and it must be advice that you can successfully execute based on your skills, experiences and unique strengths and weaknesses. Don’t choose someone to be a mentor because you perceive that they are successful and can be helpful to you. While they might be successful and can give you good career advice, it cannot be tailored specifically to you, if they don’t know you very well. As a result, they may or may not offer advice that you have the ability to execute. In this case, I would call that person an outstanding advisor, which can also be quite helpful in your career, but not a mentor, using my definition.

The profile of a good mentor is as follows:
#They must know you and your personality very well;
#They must know your work context; they DO NOT need to be within your work environment, but they must understand it, the political landscape, and the key success factors for the role that you are in or that you want;
#A mentor must be unequivocal in giving you HONEST feedback. Most organizations are non-confrontational and may not articulate developmental feedback in the way that would be most helpful. A mentor on the other hand must give you real feedback about your performance and how your environment is experiencing you. Remember, you cannot fix what you do not know is broken.
#Your mentor must be someone that you TRUST. Trust is an essential element between mentor and mentee. You must trust your mentor in order to be comfortable articulating all of the intricacies and intimate details about your career. You must be able to fully disclose everything that is going on with you, so that they can give you the best advice possible.

It is very difficult for an “assigned” mentor relationship to work. Many companies assign mentors to new employees and while it is the right gesture to pair someone who is new or upwardly mobile with someone that is more experienced in the company, it is hard for these relationships to be productive and truly beneficial to the mentee because of the lack of trust that exists between the assigned persons. Trust is something that is earned and while I might respect you and admire you, if I don’t know you, I can’t trust you yet and will most certainly not share those things that could be impediments in my career. I would argue that these “assigned” relationships will be most productive if they are treated as advisor and advisee relationships. The advisee should look to the advisor to answer basic career and company questions, but not for individually tailored answers. If this is the expectation on both sides of the relationship, then both sides will feel like the relationship is productive because the advisor can offer the answer to important questions that the advisee has to posit.

Mentorship is vital because it is necessary for young and mid-level professionals to have tangible evidence that one can be successful in that endeavor. It is also extremely important to get guidance around crucial conversations involving salary negotiations, language to ask for a promotion, and new opportunities. Even if you are extremely smart, if you have never had these types of conversations, someone has to tell you how to have those conversations and how to have communicate and deliver them successfully.

A mentor is an important relationship to have, treat it as such!
