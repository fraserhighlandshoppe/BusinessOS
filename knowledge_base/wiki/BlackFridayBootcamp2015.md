===Forecast and Plan===
Once you have the mission to tell you where you are going, you then need to forecast and plan.

The Board of the company should be involved in this planning.  Should be a minimum of 3 people.  Shouldn't be more than 5 until you are out of the adolescences stage.  Also bring in the key executives (those that will be held accountable for meeting the projections).

 It is difficult to buy into the goals if they are handed to you rather than you being involved in setting them.

If you want to grow, you can't just say "we'll make what we make".

 Allocate at least 2 days a quarter for planning.

At the end of the year, project what you are going to do the next year.

At the end of each quarter, you need to review the progress.

====Product Channel Grid====

For each company/division, create a product channel grid.

The more granular you can get the better, but it is a 30000foot exercise.

[[Image: Product Channel Grid.png]]

This helps you take survey of your products.  It is easy to forget about a product.

Step 2 - estimate the percentage of revenue each profit center generates for you.  Take your best guess (this helps getting good at projecting).  These percentages go in the boxes of the profit centers.

Step 3 - Validate your guesses against actuals (you are tracking, right?)

Step 4 - Mark Xs in the boxes where the Profit Center is sold in what channels.  Where you don't have Xs this shows you where you need to diversify.

Step 5 - Determine what percentage of each product is generated by each channel.  (example - The machine is sold 20% through facebook).  Here you want to use actuals if you have them but if not use your best guess.

 When doing these percentages, you aren't worried about getting to a tenth of a percent - this exercise 
 is to help show where to spend focus on your channels, etc.  Where you give credit can be subjective 
 (someone clicks on facebook, signs up for email list, eventually buys) but we are trying to find 
 big chunks vs small chunks.

[[Image: Product Channel Grid Example.png]]

 If you see a percentage that is between 50% and 80%, this is an area of concern.

 If you have more than 80% generated by a single product, or channel if you lose that product or channel you lose your business.

Where you have small chunks (5-10%) then there is room for growth and this is where you should focus.  Where it is 50% you are probably doing fine, and 80% means you need to focus elsewhere.

If you only have 1 profit center, here is one area you can improve - add an additional profit center or two.

Each business should have a minimum of 3 profit centers and 3 channels.  This will allow you to have no more than 50% in any one place, and you can survive a major change that kills one of them.  You don't want to be 1 email away from Facebook shutting down your ad account due to a policy violation or just a mistake on their part.  Yes, it may get fixed, but it could be weeks to months...

====Choosing your projects to meet your goals====
Here you need to list your profit centers, the revenue goal, how much sales is required, and what is needed to be done to accomplish this goal.

[[Image: Where do you want to be profit centers.png]]

This helps you define what projects you will work on, because you need to be saying NO to any projects that aren't helping you towards your revenue targets!  

If your projects aren't clearly defined, or it is outside of your mission, it is a pet project.  Pets sleep on the floor, eat your food, and crap where they shouldn't.  Businesses DO NOT have pets!

This can also help you understand if the goals are reasonable - are there any channels you can really focus on (5/10%)?  If the channels are already optimized, what can you do to grow each channel.

====Compensation====
Variable compensation (such as commission, profit sharing) leads to stress.  The only people who should have this method of pay by default is those that have direct impact on revenue.  These bonuses should only be above hitting the goal, and then only a share of the amount above the goal.  The goals need to be monthly.  Annual and quarterly goals are not very motivating.

If you have a giant win, then it is appropriate to give everyone a year end bonus.  As well, you want to do it randomly so that it doesn't become an entitlement.

====Small business owner compensation====
Pay yourself a salary (CDN tax law treats this as a distribution/withdrawal) that at least covers your living expenses, even when times are tough.

People generally take too much money when things are great, and take nothing when times are bad, and then the panic sets in.

As a company, you want 60 days of fixed expenses tucked away as your emergency account.  Getting to 6 or 12 months savings is best before removing excess as distributions or used for acquisitions or other ventures.

====Replacing yourself====
 How to know when to hand off your role as CEO/Owner so that you can transition your business 
 for larger growth (and you can focus on higher priority activities in line with your new growth)
 How to know when to hand off ownership responsibilities and day-to-day tasks as the CEO

The only way you can do it is with a team of people.  You can spend a lot of time looking for someone just like you to replace you, but they won't come work for you - they are running their own business.

By looking for a group of people like you but each are better in each of their own areas, the group will be better than you as an individual.

It is still your company, so you still need to be there driving the company, but you can replace yourself with directors who have the skills you have.

==Raising Funds==
 How to finance growth of your company (and if/when should you take on loans, partners and/or investors to achieve that growth)…
 How to know how much you need to invest to fund your company growth…
 How to raise capital if you have none (or how to get going without capital)…
 How to distribute the capital you earn once the revenue starts coming in (when to reinvest, when to pay yourself and when to pay your team)…

The only reason to go get financing is because your company is growing so quickly you can't keep up.

Wait as long as you possibly can.

You need to have a bunch of things in place:
#Systems in place
#great team.
#great documentation.

Raising money because you are in decline, you won't find an investor that you want.

Avoid seeking financing just because you have an idea.  Build a prototype, test it/Proof of Concept, sell it.

It's better to have a strategic partnership.

A merger or buyout is like a marriage.  You should date them first (partnership, etc).
