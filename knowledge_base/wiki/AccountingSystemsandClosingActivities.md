Profitable and successful businesses most likely have efficient accounting systems.

==Special Journals==
Imagine a business with a hundred transactions a day.  This is 3000 a month.  This would be unwieldy to handle at the end of the month.  Many transactions are similar and done daily.

Special Journals are used along with the General Journal.  Special Journals are used for categorized transactions.

Types are:
*Sales
*Purchases
*Cash Receipts
*Cash Disbursements

Special journals:
*minimize accounting tasks
*minimize errors in accounting practices
*maintain standardized accounting records.
[[Category: Special Journals]]
===Cash Receipts===
The cash receipts journal is used to record transactions when the business receives cash.
*cash sales
*sale of assets
*investments or loans
*payment from customers

Source documents (business papers) include
*cash sales slip
*cash register summary
*cash receipt slip
*bank statement

Cash Receipt Journal
*date
*account credited
*explanation
*PR (Post Reference) (remains blank until posted to the ledger)
*Cash Debit
*Sales Discount Debit
*Accounts Receivable Credit
*Sales Credit
*Other Accounts Credit

At the end of the month the figures are posted to the ledger.  When posting, the CR column is checked to indicate that the entry has been made.

[[Category: Cash Receipts]]
===Cash Disbursements===
The cash disbursement journal is used when cash is used to make payments.

Checks are the source documents for cash disbursements.

Disbursements Journal:
*date
*cheque number
*payee
*account debited
*PR (Post Reference) (remains blank until posted to the ledger)
*Cash Credit
*Purchase Discount Credit
*Accounts Payable Debit
*Other Accounts Debit

[[Category: Cash Disbursements]]
===Sales===
The sales journal is used to record credit sales transactions.  (sale on account or sale on credit).

Many companies allow their customers to pay a number of days after the invoice (30 days is common).

The Objectivity Principal states every transaction must be supported with evidence.  The Sales Invoice is the source document that a sales transaction occured.

A business will have an AR account for each customer that has a credit account.  These accounts are considered assets since the customer has promised to pay.  (The customer has a corresponding liability on their books).

Sales on credit transactions affect two accounts - Accounts Receivables and the Sales account.

The AR account gets a debit (increase) on the ledger.

The Sales account gets a credit (increase) on the ledger.

The Sales Journal
*date
*customer account
*invoice number
*amount of the transaction
*reference at the time of posting
====Sales Returns and Sales Discounts====
Sales returns are goods and services that are returned e.g. cancelling a service after the billing date.  A credit memo is the source document of the amount being returned to the customer.

Sales discounts are given to customers who pay within a specified time frame (used to keep customers happy and loyal).  The credit memo is also the source document.

Sales returns and Sales Discounts are not recorded in the Sales Journal since the Sales Journal only records increases in AR and Sales accounts.  Therefore the returns and discounts can be recorded in the General Journal or in the Sales Returns and Discounts Journal.

The Sales Returns and Discounts account is debited.

The AR account is Credited.

===Purchases===
The Purchases journal is used to record credit purchase transactions (pay after receiving).

All Purchase on Account transactions must have a purchase invoice as the source document.  The Invoice states the credit terms.
*n/30/2/10 would be net 30 (must be paid within 30 days) and 2/10 means 2% discount if paid within 10 days.

The journal has the following columns
*date
*account
*date of invoice
*terms
*PR
*accounts Payable credit
*Purchase debit
*Office Supplies debit
*Other Accounts debit


[[Category: Purchases]]
==Subsidiary Ledgers==
The General Ledger should only hold the information needed to prepare financial statements.  Additional information about sales and purchases can be recorded in subsidiary ledgers.

The Accounts Receivable and the Accounts Payable ledgers act as supplements to the General Ledger.

Only the totals from the subsidiary ledgers are transferred into the corresponding account on the General Ledger.

Subsidiary Ledgers also allow you to divide the labor and reduce tasks.

[[Category: Subsidary Ledgers]]
===Accounts Receivable===
Money is owed by a business or individual, and must pay the company by a given date.

The AR Ledger is posted from the General Ledger or the Sales Journal.  Generally posting to a customers account is done at the end of the day.  At the end of the month the total balances are posted to the GL.
[[Category: Accounts Receivable]]
===Accounts Payable===
Money is owed to a business or individual.

The AP Ledger involves the Purchase Journal and the General Journal.

#daily - post individual suppliers accounts
#monthly - total the suppliers account balances
#monthly - post the sum to the AP account in the GL.

[[Category: Accounts Payable]]
===Verifying the Ledgers===
The Debits and the Credits should balance.

A schedule of Accounts Receivable Ledger and a schedule of the Accounts Payable Ledger are prepared after posting.  
*the schedule lists accounts from the subsidiary ledger and their balances

If the subsidiary ledger matches the balances in the controlling accounts then they are accurate.

==Closing Activities==
The End of the Accounting Period requires that all records have been entered, some accounts closed and financial statements prepared.
[[Category: Closing Activities]]
===Worksheet===
Captures and updates all necessary data.

#post to the ledger
#complete the work sheet
#prepare financial statements
#close the account.

The worksheet has
*Account
*Unadjusted Trial Balance - taken from general ledger (assets, liabilities, capital, withdrawals, revenue, expenses).  Debits must match credits.
**debit
**credit
*Adjustments - Prepaid Expenses, Prepaid Supplies, Accrued Expenses, Depreciation, Unearned Revenue, Accrued Revenue
**debit
**credit
*Adjusted Trial Balance - checks the equality of the debits and credits.  These amounts are used for the financial statements
**debit
**credit
*Income Statement - shows revenues, expenses, adjusted accounts and shows net income or loss.
**debit
**credit
*Balance Sheet and Statement of Owners Equity - assets, liabilities and owners equity accounts
**debit
**credit

Steps:
#list all accounts that hold balances in the unadjusted trial balance column.
##assets
##liabilities
##owners equities
##revenue
##expenses
##adjusted entries
#amounts for the asset accounts are copied from the unadjusted trial balance column to the adjusted trial balance column.
#amounts for prepaid and depreciation asset accounts are calculated with their adjustments and entered into the Adjusted Trial Balance column
#the amounts for liability, capital and withdrawal accounts are copied into the Adjusted Trial Balance column
#amounts for revenue and expense accounts are copied to the Adjusted Trial Balance column
#the amounts from the expense accounts that have adjusted entries are copied into the adjusted trial balance column.
#amount from asset accounts (Adjusted column) are copied into the Balance Sheet and Owners Equity column
#liability accounts are copied into the Balance Sheet and Owners Equity column.
#amounts from capital and withdrawal accounts are copied into the Balance Sheet and Owners Equity column
#revenue accounts are copied into the Income Statement column
#expense accounts are copied into the Income Statement column
#expense accounts with adjusting entries are copied into the Income Statement column
#Income Statement columns are subtotaled.
#Net Income or Net Loss calculated.
#Balance Sheet has debits and credits subtotales
#net income/loss is calculated for balance sheet.
At the bottom of the worksheet the debits and credits should balance.
