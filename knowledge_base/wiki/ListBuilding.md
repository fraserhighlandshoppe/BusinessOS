My Three Favorite List Building Methods …

http://www.daniellevis.com/?p=1024

By Daniel Levis | December 24, 2010

Long gone are the days when you could put a sign up box on your website offering a subscription to your newsletter and expect an onslaught of new leads.

Today, you need to offer something tangible and immediate to build your list…

A cool piece of software, a special report, an audio file or a video that offers to pave the way to a solution to a burning problem your target marketing is facing. Now that’s the ticket. And it all begins with research…

What’s keeping your target market awake at night? What do they want that they’re having trouble getting? How can you help?

Package that help into an offer. And then attract your best prospects to consume it for free.

Here are my three favorite methods …

Get articles published with a link to
your free offer in your by-line …

First, an important clarification: I’m NOT talking about submitting articles to article directory sites.

People trying to sell you article submission software that “spins” one article into many in order to “fool” Google want you to believe this method will get you massive traffic and tons of hot new leads. It may get you a few. But I don’t think it’s a terribly good strategy.

 See also [[MLM Generate Leads]]

Leads from article directory sites might make up half a percent of the leads I’ve generated over the years, and I’ve got plenty of articles out there on all kinds of directories.

What has worked for me long term and continues to work is the direct, manual submission of articles.

This is where you consistently crank out very high quality articles and offer them directly to a small handful of focused, high traffic authority sites, on an exclusive basis. At the bottom of your article is your byline — a 50 to 75 word blurb about you and your free offer with a link to your squeeze page.

This way, instead of the odd searcher stumbling upon your articles on an article directory site (hardly trust inspiring), you hitchhike on the publisher/reader bond of the most highly trusted sites in your niche.

You can offer your articles on an exclusive basis and still usually retain the right to reprint and repurpose them with attribution.

This way you have full access to reams of data about the sites where your articles appear.

You can research hundreds of other articles and the reader comments that accompany them on those sites.

You can laser target your articles to be hyper-appealing. And pre-sell your proposition.

Now, you won’t get TONS of leads this way, but the ones you do get will be pure gold.

They should be worth at least ten, twenty, maybe even fifty times as much as those garnered through directory submissions or any other type of so-called automated lead generation system.

Here are the steps:

    * Find a short list of authority sites with very low Alexa rankings that deal exclusively with the subject matter you’re working with, and that accept guest contributed articles.

    * Research the hot buttons of the people frequenting these sites.

    * Set a goal to pump out articles on a regular basis. One article a week, one every two weeks, one a month, whatever you can afford. Be consistent.

    * Submit them to these high traffic authority sites on an exclusive basis. Retain reprint rights if possible.

Moving on …

Use paid media placements to
drive traffic to your free offer …

This method involves a little risk. And it’s not as easy as it was a few years ago. But paying for traffic is still a great way to create a steady flow of new leads for your business.

I’ve generated over 80,000 subscribers for this ezine with this method. So I know that it works. Here are a few tips …

You can’t be lazy about converting your leads when you’re paying for traffic. You need a well oiled sales machine or you’re going to get killed. And you need to track everything…

If you’re using a network such as Google Adwords to gain distribution for your ads, use the pixel the network gives you (Google calls it conversion code) so you can see where your ads are performing and where they’re not.

This is just a little piece of code you put on your thank you page that tracks the lead back to the source site where the click was generated.

Personally, I like to use a tracking link generated by my shopping cart in addition to the pixel. This way I can track not just the initial sale, but subsequent sales as well.

Networks are good for mass market offerings, but for niche products I’d rather deal directly with a publisher. In that case you don’t have to worry about a pixel. You can just create a separate ad tracker for each publisher and you’re good to go. You can compare your spend versus your sales and find out which sites are profitable for you and which aren’t.

If you’re selling information, you’ll probably do much better with display ads than pay per click. I routinely get two to three times more revenue with direct placement ads on Google’s content network versus their search network. And banners do even better.

The key is to create a very tight match between the promise made in your banner ad and the motivations that are causing people to visit the sites where your ads are displayed. And then maintain the tight match as you elaborate on that promise, first with your squeeze page, and then with your freebie and sales copy.

Like anything else, you learn as you go. There will be waste in the beginning. But gradually you’ll get a feel for your market and start pulling new leads at a profit.

Many marketers avoid paid advertising, fearing a monetary loss. But it’s really no different to article marketing or SEO or any other form of so called “free” advertising. There’s always a risk. Here, you’re just risking your dollars instead of your time.

Leads generated with paid media are not likely to be as valuable as those generated with article marketing, as defined above. But it will be much easier for you to scale, and go for some big numbers quickly, which brings me to my favorite method of list getting…

A way that gives you the best of all worlds:

    * The potential for rapid gains in your subscriber base…

    * Very high quality leads that buy lots of stuff…

    * Relatively little manual labor required…

    * And no upfront financial risk…

Have you guessed it yet?

Get other list owners to recommend your
free offer to their subscribers …

This is by far the most advantageous method of building a list. It’s so seductive that at times I’ve made the mistake of becoming lazy about working the others.

All three of these methods actually feed each other…

The leads you generate with article marketing and paid media become bargaining chips that will help you cement joint ventures, making it easier to get endorsed mailings.

And the publicity, exposure, and branding you enjoy when your articles get published on authority sites builds credibility and attention, allowing you to close more of these deals.

It’s a synergistic process.

You should work all of them simultaneously. But if there’s one of these methods I think you should become an absolute master at, it’s definitely this one.
