==THE “ASK PHENOMENON”==
The secret to succeeding in business today is the same as it was 100 years ago:
Find out what people want, and give it to them . . .

100 years ago that was simple. But today it is not.  Because with almost unlimited choices, people want to feel like your solution is specifically designed for them.  The trick isn't customizing the products - it is perceived customization.
*Take people and put them into different groups (buckets).  
**identify what buckets exist
***which to target, which to ignore
**This allows you to communicate differently (more targeted)
**understand at a deep emotional level - better understand and better serve
Just starting out is the perfect time to use this method - ask your market exactly what they want to buy and how to sell it to them.  This is a huge advantage because you can finalize your products and website so that it is exactly what they want/need.


There is a major shift happening right now in the way people are buying online... And companies are recognizing and acting on this shift.

When I chose the orchid care market, I figured it was a small enough, specific enough niche... But what I found is that
I didn’t have ONE bucket, I actually had FOUR. I took that business from $0 to $25,000 / month in 18 months... By creating a 
CUSTOM EXPERIENCE based on their specific situation.

#People who wanted to get their orchid to bloom again after it stopped flowering 
#People who wanted to save their sick or dying orchid\
#People who needed help potting or transplanting their orchids
#And people who just bought their first orchid, and needed to know the basics

===The Psychology of Micro Commitments===
Shrinking the size of the step or commitment you ask someone to take by starting with a simple, multiple choice question 
before you ask for someone’s email address.

Whenever you ask someone to do something or make any sort of change, the brain perceives that change as a threat, triggering the fight or flight response in an area of the brain known as the Amygdala.

This happens when you ask someone to opt-in to your email list and provide their name and email.  So they FREEZE or FLEE. (Leave your website without providing their email address.)

You can “hack” that fight or flight response.
*small non threatening steps
*kaizen
*fly under the flight or fight radar

====4 Ways To Bucket====
=====JOURNEY BUCKETING=====
You’re in a market where there is a linear success path for your customers to follow

*Your market is at different stages
*You can identify beginners, intermediates, and experts
*People just starting out have different needs than those in your market for years

=====CHALLENGE BUCKETING=====
Different sticking points people run into, that aren’t correlated with one another

*Your market faces the same few sticking points over and over
*You can predict the problems they’ll encounter
*The challenges aren’t necessarily unique to beginner

=====SITUATION BUCKETING=====
Different statuses, demographics, or types that cause specific challenges

*Your market interacts with your product in different ways
*You have varying demographics (age, location, careers, etc.)
*Your product helps many types of people

=====HYBRID BUCKETING=====
Buckets that span all 3 approaches

*Your market fits into many of these categories
*You have one or two journey buckets... and one or two situation buckets which don’t quite fit within that journey
*Some of your markets challenges are related to their situation, and some are not

====Setting the Frame====
Once you have segmented the list, they get a different introduction.  
*You show them you understand their situation.  
*You show them the success others in the same position have had using your products or services.
You are selling the identical product; but by setting the frame the customer thinks it is customized to them so they are more comfortable and appreciative.

What are the few key elements that are different?

80% of your prospects fit into 3-5 buckets.
*Too many buckets means too much work
*Too few buckets means lost sales
*The other 20% probably isn't worth pursuing.
