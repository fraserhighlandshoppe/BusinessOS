Stop thinking "testimonial" and start thinking "C_______ S_______."

"C.S." stands for "Case Study."

This is a really big deal and here's why:

Testimonials are usually about YOU as the vendor or mentor or seller...
*"Kathryn is a true expert."
*"I'd highly recommend Joe to anyone."
*"We really appreciated Nancy's friendly, professional demeanor."

Kind words are great for the ego Kevin but they do absolutely NOTHING as far as helping us sell our stuff.

Enter the "case study" style testimonial.

"Case study" testimonials are much stronger because they are about your people, not about YOU. They are about the transformation your client experiences by working with you.

Can you feel the difference?

With a "case study" testimonial, you get raving fans who will say things like, 
*"Before I worked with Kathryn, I felt stuck with the techie stuff and a little disappointed in myself. After speaking with Kathryn, I had immediate clarity on what I needed to do to use technology to drive my business."

This is a real testimonial from my client Maria, by the way. Yes, I'm tooting my own horn. Yes, I can't wait for YOU to be tooting YOUR horn, too! 🎉

Here are 5 thing "case study" testimonials do for you:
*They show prospective clients what's possible for them when they work with you.
*They allow people to put themselves in the shoes of your clients to help them envision their own success stories and imagine themselves as one of your happy, paying customers.
*You stop having to flog your stuff. It becomes easier for people to say "yes" to working with you because it's a "show don't tell" approach... people are simply sharing their stories and experiences, instead of you feeling like you're having to convince people you're worth it.
*The scales tip in your favor for anyone who may be "on the fence" about working with you because they get a "sneak peek" at the other side of the purchase.
*You have amazing marketing copy to use on your website, in webinars and speeches, on your slides, in brochures... everywhere.
Here's the catch: You have to ask the right questions.

You can't just say, "Hey, can you give me a testimonial please?"

You need to ask 3 very specific questions...
#What were things like before we worked together (or swap in "before I helped you" or "before you and I talked")?
#How did I help you?
#What are things like now? Please share any specifics you can.

I cover these questions in quite a bit of detail in Video #2, which I strongly encourage you to watch. It's only about 15 minutes long.

====Bonus 4 questions====
Very soon after you'll find yourself reaching out to that person AGAIN to ask things like:

Is it okay if I use your testimonial on my website?
Do you have a picture I could use as well?
Would it be alright if I edited this a bit?
Can I use your name?
I have a webinar coming up - can I talk about you?
The problem is that you end up creating this "chasing" vibe, where you're back and forth, back and forth with this person.

It can start to feel weird. You can start to worry you're bothering them. And (this is human nature) they start to get worse and worse at actually getting back to you.

And then you wind up with a potentially awesome testimonial you never really get to use.

THAT'S why it's so crucial you ask every single necessary question in one fell swoop.

#Ask for their first and last name.
#Get their "title" (e.g. "wife and mom," "entrepreneur coach," "naturopathic doctor" etc.)
##Having a person's "title" humanizes them that much more, which boosts the credibility and power of your testimonial. Don't skip this question!)
#Ask for a picture you can use.
#Consent for 2 things: 
##to use the testimonial in your marketing as you see fit and 
##to make minor edits to their testimonial as required, without changing the substance of their comments of course.
