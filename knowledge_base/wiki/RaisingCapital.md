==Raising Capital==
The financial market diverts funds from savings and borrowers.

Banks act as an intermediary by taking the cash deposits and lending them out to people and businesses.

Primary claims are securities sold directly to the saver.

The funds for secondary claims are provided by savers through bank accounts and investments which are then loaned out by an intermediary.

There are 3 methods of transferring funds:
*direct transfer
**sell securities directly to savers (Venture Capitalists)
*indirect transfer
**Investment dealer forms a syndicate of investors who buy all of the securities from a company.  The syndicate then sells the securities to the public at a higher price.
*indirect transfer through an intermediary
**Banks, Insurance Companies and Pension funds accumulate funds from savers and then issues its own securities back to the investors.

Intermediaries compete with each other through the rates of return (interest) offered to the savers for the use of their funds.

 Real Interest Rate = ((1 + nominal interest rate) / (1 + rate of inflation)) - 1
 Nominal Interest = Principal + (Interest Rate X Principal)

The Real Interest Rate gives you the future value of the bond in today's dollars.

===Theories===


====Unbiased Expectations Theory====
Unbiased Expectations Theory states that the yield curve on an interest rate is determined by an investors expected interest rate on a security.

Carl has $10,000.  1yr term = 6%, 2 year term = 7%.

Forward Rate = ((1 + two year rate) ^ 2/(1 + one year rate)) - 1
 F = 1.07^2/1.06 - 1 = 1.08 = 8%

To make the same money over the 2 years, you would need to get the 8% rate of return for 1 year in year 2.
[[Category: Forward Rate]][[Category: Definitions]]
====Liquidity Preferences Theory====
Liquidity Preferences Theory states the changes in term structures are caused by liquidity premiums required by investors to compensate for the added risk of fluctuating interest rates.


====Market Segmentation Theory====
Market Segmentation Theory states that the term structure is determined by the supply and demand of the securities having certain maturity dates.  The supply of the securities is determined by the institutions issuing the securities.

If Carl (above) doesn't care about the rate of return, but instead cares about the maturity date, he would be applying the market segmentation theory.

Private Investors and banks prefer short terms.  Insurance companies prefer long term maturities because their liabilities are long term.

==Debt and Equity Financing==
===Debt===
Most common debt is bank loans and bonds.  Debt holders receive continual interest payments and have a fixed claim on the firm's assets if bankruptcy occurs.

====Loans====
Loans are provided by a bank if the firm can't issue its own bonds. These loans require collateral and impose restrictions on the borrowing.
====Venture Capital====
Venture Capital is given in exchange for a portion of ownership.

*capital for high risk venture
*seed capital for new companies
*invest where risk is significant.
====Leases====
Firms lease assets rather than purchase.  Leases offer tax advantages to the lessor and provide regular payments (similar to interest) to the lessee.  They are convenient, flexible and cheap.  They are usually for less than 10 years.
====Bonds====
Bonds are listed as a percentage of their value.  For example, a bond listed as 91 is worth $910 to buy a $1000 bond.

Features include 
*indenture
**contract between issuer and holder.  Details how and when it will be paid, etc.
*coupon interest rate
**Bondholders rate of return
*call feature
**allows the holder to retire the bond before its maturity date
*equity linked debt
**allows bonds to be converted to common stock at the discretion of the issuer.  Also known as convertible debt.
*sinking fund
**Issuing firms may be required to gradually reduce the outstanding balance of bonds over their life rather than having the entire debt come due at once.  This money is put into a sinking fund.
*current yield
**ratio of annual interest to the bond market price.  = Principal * interest rate/market price

Determining the value of the bond is easy, as it only needs 3 pieces of info.
 Present Value = Sum (t=1 to n) of interest/(1 + investors required rate of return)^t + Principal Payments/(1 + investors required rate of return)^n
 where n = number of periods
[[Category: Present Value]]

Reduced Bond Valuation Formula:
 Present Value = i(PVIFA (required rate of return),n) + M(PVIF (required rate of return),n)
 PVIF = Present Value Interest Factor
 PVIFA = Present Value Annuity Interest Factor
 n = periods until maturity.
 You look up the PVIF and PVIFA in tables for the required rate and the periods until maturity
Example
 Issue $1000 dollar bond, 7% interest rate.  Investor's required rate of return is 8%, maturity is 10 years.
 PV = $70(6.710) + $1000(0.463)
 PV=932.70

Bonds trade at a discount, at a premium, or at par.  The only time you will see them at par (other than initial issue) is when the coupon rate equals the market rate, or the investors required rate of return for a bond of similar risk.
===Equity===
Most common are Common Stock and Preferred Stock.  Shareholders have a secondary claim on cash flow and assets of a firm.  Stock does not have a maturity date.

Shareholders are the true owners of a firm.  They are not guaranteed any income, but they do have some say in the financing and operations of the firm.

Shareholders have the following rights:
*dividend rights
**entitle them to a portion of the firms earnings dispersed through dividends
*asset rights
**In liquidation, shareholders have a claim to assets after government, employees and debt holders
*pre-emptive rights
**shareholders can purchase a proportional amount of new issue before the public can
*voting rights
**Allow shareholders to vote on issues affecting the firm such as Board of Directors
**majority voting - directors with most votes wins
**cumulative vote - minority shareholders have some influence.  If you have 100 shares and 5 directors to be elected, you get 500 votes.
 To win in cumulative voting, number of shares needed = (num directors desired * number shares outstanding)/(number directors positions + 1) + 1
 1M shares, 7 positions.  If 1 person wants to win, they need ((1 + 1M) / (7+1) ) + 1 = 125001.
====Common Stock====
Advantages to issuing common stock:
*if short cash, don't need to pay dividends
*can repurchase
*increases financial base of firm
Disadvantages
*cost more than debt
*dividends are not tax deductible
*flotation costs are higher
*possible change of ownership and control.  Possible conflict between management and ownership.

Market value should reflect aggregate market expectations of future cash flows.

Common stock does not have a fixed rate of return or maturity date, making it difficult to valuate.  

Dividend discount models are used to do the valuation. In them the present value is the sum of the present value of all future dividends.  If dividends and earnings are expected to grow at a constant rate, the discount model can be simplified to the constant growth dividend valuation model.
 Present Value = Dividend 1 year from now / Investors required rate of return - constant growth rate.
 Dividend 1 year from now = D0 9current] (* 1 + growth)
 If D0 = 2.00 and growth rate is 5%, required rate of return = 12%
 D1 = 2.00 * 1.05 = 2.10
 PV = 2.1/ (12 - 5) = $30.00


====Preferred Stock====
Has features of both debt (bonds) and common stock.
*dividends are fixed similar to interest payments
*no fixed maturity date, non payment of dividends doesn't force bankruptcy and dividends aren't tax deductible.
*no voting rights
*more expensive than bonds
Preferred stock is assigned a par value.  If bankruptcy, the owner is guaranteed the par value plus dividends owed.

Preferred stock is referred to by its dividend, so XYZ 2.20 preferred means it pays $2.2 a year dividend. 

Other features:
*adjustable rate
**dividend rate adjusts with market interest rate to protect against value fluctuation
*convertibility
**holder can convert to common shares
*claims on assets and income
**priority over common share holders on assets and income
*cumulative feature
**requires that back owed dividends are paid before common stock dividends
*protection provisions
**voting rights during non payment of dividends, and restricting common share dividends if the sinking fund requirement isn't met
*participation
**holder is entitled to a portion of earnings in addition to dividend
*call feature
**company can retire shares before maturity
*voting rights
**special voting rights may exist when dividends aren't paid or if the firm is in distress.

Valuation
 Present Value = sum (t=1 to infinity) of Dividend per period/(1 + required rate of return)^t
Reduced formula
 PV = dividend/required rate of return.
 Example, dividend of 1.88.  Investor wants 8.35%
 PV = 1.88/.0835 = $22.51

==Cost of Capital==
Cost of Capital is the cost of raising debt and/or equity.  
===Comparing returns and costs===
A company must look at the actual return made on its current project.  Compare these returns with the cost of equity, or the cost of capital if measured before debt.

The return on a project can then be compared to the return on potential investments.
===Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)===
Factors affecting WACC
*economic conditions
*market conditions
*operating and financial decisions
*amount a firm is financed.

To calculate:
#determine the weighted market value of each capital component (bonds, shares)
#determine the investors required rate of return
#calculate

Balance sheet shows the value and weight of each component.
 Bank Debt 33M 42.8%
 Long Term Bonds 200M 28.6%
 Common Stock 200M 28.6%
 Total 700M 100%
Divide the value of each security by the firm's value gives you the percentage.

Calculate the market value of the capital structure.
*bank debt us unchanged as market value and book value is considered equal
*bonds change.
**bonds cash flow needs to be discounted back
**use the PV=PVIF and PVIFA formula from earlier
**required rate of return can be determined from public sources - it is usually equal to the coupon rate unless interest rates change
*stock market changes
**use the market cap
**required rate of return is calculated on stock using the capital asset pricing model CAPM.
 return on equity = risk free interest rate + stock beta * (return of relevant index - risk free interest rate)
 risk free interest rate is usually based upon the treasury bonds rate.
 if beta is .85, risk free rate 6%, index return is 15%, ROE is
 ROE = 6 + .85 * (15 - 6) = 6 + 7.65 = 13.65
 return on relevant index is determined by looking back at interest rates from the capital market history.
Recalculate the percentages of each component using market value.

The investors required rate of return for common stocks is the ROE in the calculation above.  For prefered stock, it is the dividend/price

You calculate the WACC using market values and the cost of raising capital.
 WACC= [Debt/value x (1-corporate tax rate) x return on debt] + (Equity/value x return on equity)
 Example
 *bank debt = 300M  Bonds = 185.7M  TOTAL 485.7M
 *Common stock = 2.4B (200M shares at 12)
 *TOTAL 2.8857B
 *corporate tax 35%
 *expected rates of return - Bonds 9% Common Stock 13.5%
 485.7/2.8857 x (1-.35)x.09 + 2400/2885.7 x .135
 = .168 x .09 x .65 + .832 x .135 = .122 = ''12.2%''
In this example it will cost 12.2 dollars for every 100 raised.  It also represents the minimum rate of return after taxes a firm must earn on its project.

The WACC is used as a benchmark to valuate projects with similar risks and debt ratios.

==Optimum Capital Structure==
Main advantage of debt is the tax benefits.  Interest payments are tax deductible.  Another benefit is it requires managers to be more disciplined since the firm has made the commitment to pay interest and principals.

Main disadvantage is the costs
*interest
*agency cost
*possible bankruptcy costs
*consumer confidence
*lower return for shareholders
*loss of flexibility
**bond holders putting restrictive covenants.

Equity financing creates owners, who can influence the company through the board of directors.
*Residual claim, so no interest payments.
*Heavily influence management decisions
*not tax deductible
*riskier method of financing.

===Information Asymmetry theory===
Assumes company knows more about its financial health than the market does.  The type of financing a firm chooses and the price of its securities sends a signal to the market about its finances.

Firms may issues certain types of securities to give a certain signal to the market.  Sometimes these are in alignment with reality :(

===Trade Off theory===
Based upon trade off of tax savings and distress costs of debt.

If you have large assets use debt.
===Pecking Order theory===
Manager have an order for financing:
#internal revenue
#debt
#stocks
They avoid issuing stocks because of the signals sent.

===Cross section in industry===
You can also look at your competitors and see what mix they have used.

===Relationships of WACC===
minimize cost of capital and maximize firms value.  Where this occurs is the optimal capital structure.

In practice this is where the cost of capital is minimized to the point just before the cost of debt begins to increase.

Equity financing is considered less risky when used with a low proportion of debt, or when there is a low debt to equity ratio.  As the debt level rises, the cost of capital also rises.  You can use WACC to determine the percentage cost for various mixes.

==Dividend Policy==
Hurdle Rate is the minimum acceptable rate of return on an investment.

[[Category: Hurdle Rate]]

When the firm has positive earnings or when revenue exceeds expenses, the firm must decide what to do with the excess
*reinvest
*hold as cash
*return to investors

The dividend policy affects the cost of equity.  For example, if the firm retains earnings it will require less capital later to expand the business.

===Dividend Irrelevance Theory===
In 1961 Miller and Modigliani proposed that a firms value is determined only by its investments making dividends a minor detail.  They argued that in a perfect market condition (no taxes, no transaction costs) the effect of the dividend policy can be reversed by shareholder.  Thus the policy is irrelevant and will not affect the firms value.

===Residual Dividend Theory===
Dividends should only be paid if the earnings is not needed to fund expansion of the business.  Otherwise the earnings are retained.
===Clientele Effect===
Investors tend to buy stock in firms that meet their preference for firms that have either high, low nor no dividends.
===Bird in Hand theory===
Dividend Income has a higher value to the investor than capital gains because it is more certain and tangible.
===Dividends are relevant===
Argues dividends are relevant because they affect:
*share value
**dividend payment affects shareholders perception of value
*risk aversion
**risk adverse shareholders prefer dividends to capital gains.
*transaction costs
**Some holders need the regular payments.  It is expensive to frequently sell small amounts of stock for income.
*taxes
**increasing taxes tend to lower dividend payout ratios.  capital gains aren't taxed until the stock is sold but dividends are taxed as earned.
*Issuance costs
**More cost effective to retain earnings (rather than paying dividends) instead of raising external capital
*agency costs
**A firm paying dividends uses the markets and must follow market regulations.  Management is therefore not being scrutinized by the owners, which reduces agency costs.

===Dividends===
*regular cash dividend
**A dividend the company is expected to maintain in the future e.g. quarterly.
*special dividend
**An additional payment over and above regular.  Typically not repeated.
*declaration date
**day the company announces it intends to pay a dividend and the dollar amount.
*record date
**used to know who should be paid dividends - who is the owner of record on that date
*payment date
**the date the dividend will be paid.
*with dividend date
**cut off date used by exchanges to stop dividend cheques being mailed to the wrong person.  Usually 5 days prior to the payment date.
*ex dividend date
**determines if the shareholder gets the payment.  Anyone holding the stock before this date is entitled to the payment.

====Dividend Policies====
*Passive Residual policy
**retain earnings if investment opportunities will pay returns higher than required rates.  do not pay dividends if the firm will have to raies equity.
*stable dollar policy
**stable dividends are preferred.  Don't raise dividends until confident that future earnings will cover the increase.
*constant payout ratio
**payments fluctuate according to current profits.
*regular + year end payout
**volatile earnings
**regular small dividend
**year end special dividend to pass out more cash.

====Dividend Payment Methods====
=====Cash Dividend=====
Pay cash as the dividend.
[[Category: Cash Dividend]]
=====Stock Dividend=====
5% stock dividend payment would mean each shareholder receives another 5 shares for every 100 that they own.

Another equivalent form of stock dividend is a stock split.  There is no difference except for the number of shares outstanding and the accounting treatment.

A stock dividend increases shareholder wealth because the price doesn't fall relative to the increase in number of shares. 

A stock split decreases the share price by the same ratio as the number of shares change.  However, it makes it easier to sell the shares.
[[Category: Stock Dividend]]
===Returning cash to shareholders===
====Equity repurchase====
Firms use cash to repurchase outstanding stock instead of paying cash dividends.
*open market
**buy stock at the market price off of the market.
*tender offer
**indicates the number of shares to repurchase, the purchase price and a deadline.  If enough shares aren't repurchased they can withdraw the offer or extend the time period.
*privately negotiated
**A firm repurchases from a large shareholder for an agreed upon price.
**rarely used.
Equity repurchase offers the following advantages over cash dividend:
*one time return of cash
*flexibility
*tax advantages
*focuses on stockholders who need income
*increases insider control
*support stock price in the market
[[Category: Equity Repurchase]]
====forward contracts====
A firm agrees to repurchase stock at a fixed cost on a predetermined date.  Used for smaller repurchases.

This allows a firm to determine its earnings per share.  However the risk is that the future price of the stock will drop, exposing the company to the financial risk that it could have repurchased those shares cheaper.  Also there is no flexibility as the price and date can't be changed.

[[Category: Forward Contract]]

===Market Signals===
The dividend policy selected by the firm sends a signal to the market regarding the financial health of the company.

===Choosing the dividend policy===
Consider:
*stability of firms earnings
**firms with low stability will pay lower dividends because they don't want to reduce dividends in the future.
*source of capital
**Firms can't pay dividends that are so high that they can't fund their internal projects.
*degree of financial leverage
**higher leverage typically pay lower dividends since they have debt payments to pay in addition to the dividends.
*stockholder characteristics
**clientele effect
*legal constraints
**Each state has different laws.  A firm can not use its capital to pay dividends.
***It must use present and net earnings
***can't pay if insolvent
*restrictive covenants
**some covenants restrict the payment allowed, or prevent payments until earnings reach a certain level.
*tax
**debate the benefits of dividends over capital gains.  IRS Code outlaws firms from retaining earnings to shield shareholders from taxes.
*liquidity
**Firm needs enough liquid assets on hand to pay shareholders
*inflation
**How much can a firm afford to pay in dividends?  Need to ensure future higher costs are covered.
*growth prospects
**fast growth requires lots of funds.  Retain earnings to avoid the costs of external financing.
*dilutions
**Want to internally finance projects, which protects current shareholders from the dilution caused by raising equity.

====Trends====
*dividends follow earnings
**dividends and earnings are correlated over time as dividends are paid out of earnings
*dividends are sticky
**Firms are reluctant to change the payment over time
*dividends follow a smoother path
**reluctance to change the dividends mean that it could still be paid out even if there is a cash flow shortage.  OTOH they won't increase the payment immediately either in case increased earnings is fleeting.
*policy follows the life cycle of the firm.
**investment opportunities and growth.
