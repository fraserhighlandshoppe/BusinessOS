About six years ago, after a heated debate, I wrote out a check for $100,000 and slid it across the table to two clients...

It was during one of my private client mastermind groups, where 20 different entrepreneurs met for two days at a time to hash out problems and opportunities in their businesses together under my leadership.

In this particular session, two partners presented a new service they intended to add to their existing enterprise.

They felt it could only be delivered several times a year, with a maximum of fifty customers each time, drawn from their existing customers already buying lesser-priced products and services.

They had done some loosey-goosey research via surveys, focus groups, and analysis of similar or competitive business and determined they could charge as much as $2000 per person.

Most of the others in the group attacked these fellows instantly, in near unanimous and rather fervent agreement that their proposed price was way, way too low for the value of the service to the right customer and the supply-demand ratio likely to exist given the pool of thousands to draw from.

But these guys were convinced that $2000 was as far as this price could possibly be stretched.

Despite my siding with the group and making my best, reasoned argument, the meeting ended in discord.

Three months later, at the next meeting, these two partners presented a more fleshed out, ready to advertise version of their proposed service, and stated their firm intent to price at $2000.00.

We debated again and that's when I left my place at the head of the table and got my checkbook.

When I handed them the check, I said, "Enough debate. If you're locked in and happy with your $2000.00 price, I'll buy all 50 customer positions. You deliver your service as you planned. You let me market as I please to your current customer list, I'm going to sell this at $10,000 per person, and make a very nice $400,000.00 spread. Three times a year. Which means I'll take home $1.2 million. Take my check and let's shake hands on this."

They refused.

They went home and rather shakily rolled out their service with a daring, to them, price of $4500, a 225% higher price than they'd proposed--and sold out the 50 spots quickly and easily. 

Today, a few years later, they sell this for $6500.00 with an $8500.00 deluxe option, and, at my urging, also figured out how to accommodate 100 people rather than 50. And they do it three times a year.

Over five years, this price strategy pushed on them is worth more than $7 million ABOVE what they were committed to settling for.

What would it mean to you to "find" an extra $7 million in your business...

Simply by trying a pricing strategy I suggest to you.

It's an all-too-common problem for entrepeneurs:

Charging fees WAY below what you're worth!

well be one of the key factors underlying

realities of business operation costs. And
wasting energy struggling to stay afloat...
based on mistaken notions of "competitiveness"
when it comes to your pricing... is a sure
path to disaster.
