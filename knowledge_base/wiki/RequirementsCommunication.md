==Communicating and Implementing Requirements==
The BA serves as a liason between the business units and the technology team (IT, Vendors, Suppliers).  The BA ensures that everyone understands each other.  The BA represents the business units ensuring that the solution meets the business's needs.

==Communication==
Communication challenges between various teams are chronic.  Between 60 and 80% of failed projects have serious communications problems.

The BA must document and present the requirements in a format and manner that all stakeholders and project participants understand them the same way.

===Requirements Communication Plan===
You need to ensure that the stakeholders receive the requirements in the format they need.  You need to ensure everyone has the right information at appropriate times so that decisions can be made.

The requirements communications plan will look similar to the project communication plan.  The difference is that the requirements communication plan ensures that the team is doing the right time where as the project plan is for ensuring the project is done right.

The plan will need to be kept up to date as the project progresses as stakeholders will change as will their needs.

===Conflict Management===
Requirements that may be discreet at the start may turn out to conflict later on.  This is especially true since knowledge is gathered over time.

Also, different stakeholders have different expectations as to what  the solution will do for them.

It is the BAs responsibility to research and resolve conflicts.  And it must be done quickly to reduce bad feelings and affecting the quality, budget and schedule.

It is essential to document the conflicts.  It is then up to the BA to get the stakeholders to understand the conflict's real issue, agree on a resolution and then move on.

Organizing the requirements by stakeholder groups is OK when there is a small number of groups.  However, documents may need to reside in different areas of the package concurrently since different groups may have interest in the same document (e.g. QA and Developers will want Use Cases).

Another way to organize them is by document type
*business requirements
*functional requirements
*technical requirements

Try to avoid creating multiple forms of the same requirements document.  This may not always be possible due to the different needs, backgrounds, knowledge and experience of the various groups.

Size and scope of the project will dictate what components need to be included.

The package will include
*An executive summary
*A glossary
*A revision log
*A revision trigger explanation
*a table of contents
*the requirements

==Formal Requirements Review==
A review is conducted to allow stakeholders the opportunity to review and verify the accuracy, validity, completeness and realism of the requirements.  This is how you obtain sign-off.

This is also known as a walk through or inspection.  Each and every requirement must be reviewed.

The review is conducted as a meeting.  A scribe is appointed to take down the suggestions and concerns raised during the review.
