===Do segregated funds have additional costs?===
 http://www.investingforme.com/classroom/investment-type/segregated-funds/faq?q=50

Segregated funds are really two separate purchases: the first is a mutual fund and the second is an insurance wrapper. The insurance coverage will increase the investment’s annual cost, above that of a standard mutual fund, by about 1.0%. As a result, segregated funds have higher management expense ratios (MERs) than mutual funds.

===Sales Charges===
In order to compensate the advisor, the funds implement a Sales Charge.

 The clients needs must be considered first!  If there is a reasonable possibility the client
 will need access to the funds before the fees decrease to zero, a FE/NL would be a better choice.
 *The industry is moving away from DSC due to increased investor awareness of fees and low cost alternatives.
We must disclose to the client which load type we are using.
====Front End Load (FE)====
*There is no sales charge upon redemption
*The sales charge is charged up front - it comes out of the initial deposit.  
*The advisor and the client can negotiate the fee between 0 and 5%.
**The 0% FL is effectively a No Load plan.
====No Load (NL)====
====Low Load (LL)====
*10% of the units can be redeemed each year free of charge (20% for RRIFs)
*If the client redeems more than the 10%, there is a Sales Charge.
**The charge decreases over time.  After 3 years the fee is 0.
====Deferred Sales Charge (DSC)====
*10% of the units can be redeemed each year free of charge (20% for RRIFs)
*If the client redeems more than the 10%, there is a Sales Charge.
**The charge decreases over time.  After 7 years the fee is 0.
====Charge Back (CB)====
*10% of the units can be redeemed each year free of charge (20% for RRIFs)
*Broker is charged a fee if the redemption is greater than the 10%.
*CB period ranges from 24 months to 60.
