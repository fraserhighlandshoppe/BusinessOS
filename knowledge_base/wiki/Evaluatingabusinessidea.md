==Evaluating a business idea==
 Matt Leitz email

It all started with my angel mother and I was watching Shark Tank and talking about her starting a new business.

Here are the 4 questions I gave her for evaluating a business idea...
===Does it fit into your schedule?===
The reality is that none of the business in Shark Tank can be done in your spare time.  Starting something from scratch takes a tremendous amount of sacrifice.  My mom isn't willing to risk it all or miss out on her retirement.
===Does it allow you to have easy wins?===
If you've failed in the past, I don't have to tell you how important it is to get some sales on the board quickly.  While the AI industry is super hot, I know some people don't want to learn something new.  If you want a spare-time business, a low-priced product can be a good bet.
===Does it require a lot of capital investment?===
This is a big barrier for a lot of people.  The reality is that all businesses have costs, but to me the worst expense is holding product inventory.  I learned this from my failed board game company.  Figure out a way to avoid letting your valuable capital sit there and gather dust.
===Does it give you the chance to scale big?===

If it doesn't give you the chance to hit it big, it's not worth it in my book.  Low-price might not make you rich overnight, but they can make a lot of money.  Make sure your products are in high demand.  Plenty of people have become millionaires a dollar at a time.


So why do I share this?

If you have a business idea, you should answer those 4 questions to make sure you're heading in the right direction.
