==Social Media==
Social business platforms allow new ways to partner or collaborate.

Engaged employees drive the client experience which then drives the business results.

Ways to showcase your expertise 
*updated profiles like LinkedIn, 
*post regular status updates, 
*maintain a robust social network
*congratulate/compliment peers in public forums

You want to engage responsibly with your colleagues and the market at large.
*think before you share
*which are your professional communities
*which are your personal communities
*which are both
*what reputation do you want to build?

Be careful when sharing your expertise and building networks
*Be authentic and genuine
**Identify yourself
**Keep your audience and relationship with them in mind
**think before mixing your connections and status updates.
**review your friends/connection lists periodically.  Especially before sharing content and posting updates.
*Be knowledgable
**You may be knowledgable in multiple areas (e.g. fixing cars and fixing computers) but not all of your friends are interested in both.
**consider the type of info to share with your networks to keep your audience engaged.
*Express your opinion but be respectful
**provide a perspective on engaging topics.
**encourage collaboration
**draw the line at disrespect
**choose your words carefully.
**avoid inappropriate comments.

Be Aware of what you share
*Is this info appropriate for this audience
**keep private data private
*Do I have the right to share it?
**respect copyright
**respect confidentiality
*Can this info be used to harm me, the company or others?
**Don't cite or reference clients, partners, suppliers unless authorized
**Don't include detailed product, strategy or business travel plans.
*Am I comfortable with the info remaining online and being redistributed?
**Once on the internet, it is there forever!

Only selected people are authorized to speak in public on behalf of any given company.  This includes posting information and participating in social business on behalf of the company.  As an individual, you must ensure you are not giving the appearance of speaking on behalf of the company.

===Location based services===
Consider that many apps now include goegraphic locations in the data stream.  For example, if you take a photo it is commonly stamped with GPS coordinates.  Did you mean to share where you are, the location of that building (e.g. child's school) etc?

Don't post updates while at a client or potential client location.  Your competitor could use that info to gleam you are working on a new deal.

===Collaboration===
When you send an attachment in email, only the recipients can benefit.  If the attachment isn't private/personal or confidential, can you post this to a (access controlled) file share/community/wiki so that the greater community can benefit?

==Protecting information==
Proprietary information including client info must be handled according to policies and law.

All information created in the course of your work belongs to the company.  It may only be used to conduct business for the company, or for purposes authorized by management of the company.

====Label Confidential====
Confidential info should be clearly identified ''Companyname confidential'' on each page of the document and at the start of each electronic file.
====Restrict distribution====
Only pass the file to those that need it, and are authorised to have it.
====Encryption====
When encryption is available and practical, use it.  This is especially important when sending the file electronically.

When sending the encrypted file to someone, do not include instructions within the same email on how to decrypt the file, as that effectively voids the encryption.

Ensure that any data on a small easy to loose (or an easily portable device like a laptop) is encrypted.
====Snail Mail====
When sending through internal mail, ensure the envelop is labelled confidential.

When sending through public mail (i.e. post, or courier) then the package must be double enveloped.  The internal will be labeled confidential and the external will not.
====Unattended info====
Do not leave confidential info unattended. 
*Shred a document when you are done with it
*lock in a filing cabinet when it is still required.
*do not leave it sitting out on a desk.

===Backups===
Almost all backup programs by default store the backups unencrypted.  Try to ensure all backups are encrypted.

===Personal Info===
People want to have some control over whether info about them is collected, how it is used and shared.

Personal Information (PI) is any info that can be used to identify, contact or locate a person.
*name
*address
*telephone number
*email address

Employee information is any PI that pertains to a current or former employee, their family members or an applicant.

Business Personal Info is any PI relating to third parties (e.g. partners, clients, prospects, suppliers, etc).
====Notice====
When collecting PI, you must state what is being collected, how it will be used, and with whom it will be shared.  Privacy laws prohibit the use of the information for purposes other than what it was collected for.
====Collection====
Only collect what is relevant and necessary.
====Use and disclosure====
You must only use the data for what it was collected.
====Security====
You must protect against loss, theft, destruction, unauthorized access and disclosure.
====Individual Access====
Upon request you must advise a person of what PI you hold on them.
====Retention and disposal====
You must keep PI for only as long as required for the purposes it was collected.

You must dispose of the data in a manner that doesn't allow it to be reconstituted.
====Transfer across borders====
Transferring data between countries has extra regulations to comply with.

Providing access to a system that has PI on it to someone abroad is considered a transfer, even if the person doesn't access the data.

===Sensitive Private Information===
SPI is PI that could be used to significantly harm an individual in a financial, employment or social way.  It includes
*country identification numbers (e.g. SIN)
*credit card numbers
*health related info
*info about race
*info about politics

You must not collect SPI unless absolutely necessary to achieve a valid business purpose.

SPI must be stored encrypted.

==Secure Computing==
Don't divulge personal information including account information and passwords

Don't click on links taking you to malicious or compromised websites

Don't click attachments that can infect your device with mal-ware.

===Phishing and Spear-phishing===
Phishing is the attempt to get large numbers of people to divulge information.

Spear Phishing is the targeted attempt.

Common attack vectors are attachments and obsfucated links in emails or on websites.

Legitimate companies will never ask you to confirm details via an email.  

Banks and other institutes will never send you an attachment you didn't ask for.

Emails from a bank will never have a clickable link.  Instead they will direct you to log in in the normal fashion, etc.

===Secure practices===
*keep your browser up to date.
**block lists
**no-script
**ad blocking
*Be wary of clicking files and links in emails.
**hover over the link to see where it really points to.
*Don't install software from a pop-up after clicking a link or attachment in an email.

===Passwords===
Don't use the same password at multiple sites.  This is especially important where they either collect your email as contact info, or worse use the email address to log in with.

*Use a combination of lower case letters, upper case letters, numbers and special characters in your password.
*review and change passwords periodically
*use password vaults if storing them on devices.
*don't use dictionary words
*don't use easily guessed PI (e.g. friends name, family members name, pets name, dates of birth etc)
*don't provide answers to password reset question that can be researched online (maiden name, school, first car, first pet)
