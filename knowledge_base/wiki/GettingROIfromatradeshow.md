==etiquette==
People who sit behind the table and don't get up to create the potential
clients don't do as well.  The table acts as a barrier.
*eye contact
*shake hands
*introductions
*ask their needs and wants
*tell them how you can solve their pains.
*collect information from them
**form.  Permission to contact
***Electric contact form so reading their writing isn't a problem
*iprice wheel
*FB/Instagram photo frame - post and tag
*draws
*Invite them to a consumer event

Hand written notes get a second glance once they are home and sorting
through what they picked up.

==Followup==
Followup is Key!.  This is another marketing track.
The fortune is in the followup.

We have exclusive in person access to the clients.  A good show
generates 5-10 qualified leads per hour.
*Customer needs to see us 7-8 times before they notice us.
*If we don't followup, it is as if we don't exist.  We lose these warm
pre-qualified leads.

===Followup stats===
48% never followup
25% only make 2nd conact
12% only make 3
only 10% more than 3
===Sales Stats===
2% of sales are on first contact
3% on 2nd
5% on 3rd
10% on 4th
80% on 5-12th contact.


===Plan===
Plan your work and work the plan
*Contact your hot leads ASAP
**text or email or send past newsletter, or...
*Contact them before the end of the day if possible
*have first text, tweet, FB post, email, postcard etc written in advance
*2-3 days later is 2nd followup
*1 week later is 3rd followup
*bi-monthly or monthly thereafter
**until they die or buy (dan kennedy)

===Set Priorites===
*after the show you should have an idea of who are your best leads
**segment leads into priorities
**connect in order of priority
*followup in timely manner
**email or phone call.
**want to contact while you are still fresh in their mind

===Tools===
*Email - vide clip
*post card
*Invitiation to event
*Newsletter
*Use contact management programs
**mailchimp/constant contact
Your first contact email should be written and uploaded before going to
the show.

#Be unique
#Be professional
#You want to put effort into the followup otherwise it is wasted.
#Consider mailing a small gift wiht a personal letter, requesting an in
persom meeting to further discuss their wants, needs, pain points.
*You want the letter/email to be personalized and creative.
**schedule proposal.  Call to action.

===email subject===
*something catchy
*what promo did you offer?
*what vendor did you co-op with?

Its a real conversation.  If you wouldn't say it don't write it.
*Reply as quickly as you can
*Mirror their tone and energy
*use a contact us form
**shorter is better
**Ask them if it is OK to text?
*mirror how much they write.
**write to fit on 1 screen on phone.
*Don't answer questions they didn't ask
*end emails with a question
*don't send attachments they didn't ask for
*don't stop the conversation if they haven't
**most competitiors give up.  its not over until they say it is
*followup - did you get my reply?  Could be your email went to spam
**are you to busy
**did you already book
**being chased by zombies
**please reply 1/2/3
*don't get mad, get creative
*careful- it will be used against you and shared online
*billboard test
*if it says sent from phone, don't send long email
*don't force a phone call on 1st email
**millenials hate talking on phone.  find them intrusive
**they want to be prepared
*Direct mail
**post card is powerful if it is attention grabbing
***something that reminds them of your booth
**paper copy of newsletter
**Call to action!

==Mailer==
*Hand written envelope get opened and read
**Contain a brocuhre and a reply card
**Use attention grabbing headline, compelling benefit, call to action
*don't just sell yourself.  Provide free info that makes them to get more.
*Encourage them to opt in to email list
*followup with email and mailers.
