===Big Hairy Audacious Goal===
What is your big goal?  What will you accomplish within the next 10 years?
 Choose 10 years because then no one can debate with you if it is achievable or not.
With your 10 year goal,
#What do you need to do 5 years out to achieve the 10?
#What do you need to do 3 years out to achieve the 10?
#What do you need to do 1 years out to achieve the 10?
#What do you need to do in the next quarter to achieve the 10?

===Reaching the Goal===
To reach the goal, we as leaders of the business need to stay on message.  You need to repeat the message every chance you get.  This helps align the entire team to the goal.

You need to put the goals in writing.  You also need to track (and publish) the progress.
 The 1 page strategic plan helps here.

Employees need finish lines.  Imagine going to the olympics where there is no finish line.

Celebrate the finish.  You can either celebrate success or commisserate failure.  Either way, celebrate.

Share the win.  Not only the finish, but also the gains.  Bonuses are always appreciated, and help motivate for the next goal.

===Management realities===
[[Category: Management]]
Your employees are scared of you even if you aren't scary.  You have the power to hire or fire.

Because you always look busy and they are scared, they won't stop you to ask if something that has been done for a long time is still relevant and needed.

You need to specifically tell your employees the following in a policy statement:
 If it is wasting time, energy, money or space, dispose of it.  This includes processes.

The whiteboard is the most important management tool.  
*It allows easy changes.  
*It allows creativity and immediate discussions.

Every Business must make decisions about 4 important things:
#People
#Strategy
#Execution
#Cash

You need to reduce your management of the business to be 20% or less of your time.  The rest of your time should be spent on market facing activities such as Marketing.


===Marketing===
[[Category: Marketing]]
You should be spending 10x the amount of time and effort on pricing as upon reducing costs.  Pricing has an immediate impact on revenue.

Pricing is the 1 P of the 4 P's (Price, Product, Place, Promotion) that makes you immediate money.  Ogilvie (one of the largest marketing firms) has re-branded these into the 4 E's

Set aside at least 1 hour a week to focus on marketing.

====In The Age of The Customer====
In this age of the customer, relationship marketing is the key.

====Key Influencers====
For any given product, you want to identify the top 50 key influencers on your target market.  These will be journalists, bloggers, and others that will publish reviews on your company and product.

You want to reach out to each of these top 50.  
#Network with them.  
#Once you have established a relationship, then you can pitch your product.  But when doing so, ask them if it was them what would they do to reach your goal.  
#Then shut up, and take notes.
#Followup by email or letter, and show that you were listening.  
##List the items you've tried and succeeded with, 
##list the items you have tried and didn't have success, 
##list the items you've thought about but discarded, and 
##list the items they gave you that is still on your action list.

====Ogilvie's 4 Es of Marketing====
=====Experience=====
All touch points, up to and including the packaging (Apple's boxes) and invoicing should be a happy/positive experience for the customer.  It shouldn't be difficult for them to unpack the product.  The invoice should be easy to read and understand.

 Work is theater, and every business is a stage.
=====Exchange (Pricing)=====
Give customers choice.  A prime example is the airlines.  They've decoupled everything they can get away with and bill it separately.  Checked baggage fees, meals for sale, extra leg room.
=====Everyplace=====
You need to be in front of your target market EVERYWHERE.  It takes more than 1 exposure to a product to gain a sale.  The more the exposure, the more comfortable the customer will be with the product, and the more willing to purchase.

You need to include social media.  Get them talking about your brand and products.

You also need multiple distribution channels.  Multiple Brick and Mortar stores, online, etc.
 He who has the most distribution channels wins!
=====Evangelism=====
Get enough customers excited about your product, and they will market and sell it for you.

What you have done can turn your customers into fans promoting for you.

Have a 2 question survey on every sale:
#On a scale of 1 to 10, how enthusiastically would you tell your friends and family about your experience?
#If you didn't rate the experience as a 9 or 10, can we call you to discuss?
 Follow up with EVERY customer that didn't rate you a 9 or 10.
===Proposals===
People will spend more time and effort to avoid a loss/pain than they will to gain something.

On a proposal, your first 2 pages should be about the pains or losses that the potential customer will experience if they don't do business with you.  Only on the third page do you start to talk about yourself.

Never email a proposal.  Only hand deliver it.  There is no way to cover every last detail in a negotiation before you put together the proposal or contract.  By sitting with them going over the proposal line by line, this gives you a chance to catch their reaction when something worries/irritates them and you can address it immediately.  This way minor annoyances don't turn into major issues.
 If you can't hand deliver it, book a phone meeting, and only email them the proposal 
 moments before the call.  That way they don't have the chance to read it without you 
 "present" to address the issues.

===Leadership===
[[Category: Leadership]]
====Drama====
Drama is what happens when things don't go right.

Drama costs you both time and money.

A checklist helps you reduce drama

=====Checklists=====
A checklist helps ensure that all steps or items are remembered.

A checklist helps create a repeatable process.

====Leadership Development====
 Coaching + Learning + Technology => Growth

No one has ever achieved Peak Performance without a coach.

Those that don't read barely have an advantage over those that can't read.

Leaders are readers.

How much time are you spending on reading (learning) versus something like angry birds?
 You can check your priorities by measuring how much time you spend on everything.

You can't pull anything out of your mind that you didn't first put there.

=====Peer Coaches=====
A peer coach is not a mentor.  They are a peer, to whom you will be accountable for working towards your goal.
 Talk (email) to them daily about your goals you are working on.
 Meet face to face weekly to provide and receive support.
 Discuss your top 5 rituals that you need to start (or stop) during the next week.
====Routines====
Routings will set you free.  They are the checklists of action.

To make improvements, you have to change your daily actions.

 Doing Less, Living More!

===Infrastructure===
[[Category: Infrastructure]][[Category: Scalable Infrastructure]]

Scaling your infrastructure increases the complexity.  Amazon has a rule that no team can be larger than what 2 pizzas would feed.  Google teams are 3 people.

2 people have 1 connection, with a complexity score of 2.
3 people have 3 connections, with a complexity score of 6.
4 people have 6 connections, with a complexity score of 24.
