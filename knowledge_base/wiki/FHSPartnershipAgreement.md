==Fraser Highland Shoppe Partnership Agreement==
THIS PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT is made this __________ day of  _February_, 2015, by and between the following individuals: 

	Kevin Fraser,  Kimberly Henry

The partnership shall commence operations as of this date, and shall continue until terminated by agreement of all partners.
===Trade Name===
The partnership shall be conducted under the name of Fraser Highland Shoppe.

===Place of Business===
The partnership shall maintain offices in Airdrie Alberta.

===Nature of Business===
The partners listed above hereby agree that they shall be considered partners in business for the following purpose:  
*The operations of a Scottish shop selling:
**giftware, 
**Highland Dance supplies, 
**parts and instruments for Pipers and Drummers.
The partnership shall be a general partnership where all partners are jointly and severally liable for the debts and obligations of the partnership.   


===Day-To-Day Operation===
#The partners shall provide their full-time services and best efforts on behalf of the partnership.  
#Each partner shall have equal rights to manage and control the partnership and its business.  
#Should there be differences between the partners concerning ordinary business matters, a decision shall be made by unanimous vote. 
#The partners may elect one of the partners to conduct the day-to-day business of the partnership. 
#No partner shall be able to bind the partnership by act or contract to any liability exceeding $250.00 without the prior written consent of each partner.

===Meetings===
The partners shall hold a management meeting at a minimum of quarterly, but may hold them more often as required.

A quorom shall be 66% of shares.

===Division of Labor===
*Kevin shall be the product expert for Piping and Drumming.
*Kimberly shall be the product expert for Highland Dance and Country Dance.
*Decisions on the expenditure of capital for the acquisition of stock shall be shared.
*Decisions to spend money on advertising shall be shared.
*Decisions affecting the pricing of inventory shall be shared.

===Capital Contribution===
The capital of the partnership shall be contributed by the partners in cash, property or services, as follows:
*A separate capital account (record) shall be maintained for each partner.
*Neither partner shall withdraw any part of their capital account.
*Upon the demand of either partner, the capital accounts of the partners shall be maintained at all times in the proportions in which the partners share in the profits and losses of the partnership.
*Services and Property shall be assigned a CASH VALUE for purposes of percentage of ownership balances.

After year 2 of the partnership, if additional capital is required all partners shall contribute according to their share of ownership.  If a partner is unwilling or unable to contribute, the other partners may make up the difference in proportion to their individual share of ownership.  Everyone's share of ownership shall be adjusted accordingly.

====Withdrawl of Capital====
No partner shall withdraw from their capital account without the approval of the other partners.

====Profit and Losses====
Profits and Losses shall be shared amongst the partners according to their share of ownership.  At the end of the year the accountant shall close the year's books and the profit or loss shall be applied appropriately to the Capital Accounts of the partners.  See [[Accounting for Partnerships]]

====Salaries====
The partners shall not be paid a salary.  Any services they provide shall be assigned a CASH VALUE to be accounted for within their Capital Contribution.

===Financials===
Signing Priveleges

Financial statements shall be issued quarterly.  
End of Month processes shall also be performed.
An Annual report shall be issued as soon as practical after year end.
The fiscal year shall be the same as the calendar year.
	Partnership Funds
	The funds shall be held in such investments and banking accounts as shall be designated by the partners.  Funds shall be held in the name of the partnership and shall not be comingled.

====Books of Accounts====
All partners shall have open access to the Books of Account.

====Banking====
All income shall be deposited in the bank accounts as designated by the CFO.

All expenses shall be payed out of the bank accounts as designated by the CFO.

===Compensation of Partners===
Methods:
*equal profit-sharing among all partners 
*a lock-step system – all partners at a particular seniority level are recognized and paid equally 
*an “eat-what-you-kill” system based solely on individual billings and collections 
*a formula scheme based on billings and seniority 
*merit-based on billable and non-billable performance 
 Also look at originating lawyer credits. What if you introduce a banking client to the firm, generating $1 million dollars in fees? How will you be compensated for work you bring in, but don’t do? 

 In larger firms, you can expect partner compensation to be set according to a balanced score card of practice skills/competencies, billings/collections and non-billable performance such as client marketing, hosting lawyer-update seminars and contributing to firm or team management. The result may be an allocation of a certain number of partnership points or units – more points mean a greater compensation figure.

 Ferguson prefers a combined compensation scheme. “The best approach is where part of the compensation figure is known and ascertainable in advance, then the gravy or a bonus deals with the super-performers.”


===Termination/Dissolution of the partnership===
Should the partnership be terminated by unanimous vote, the assets and cash of the partnership shall be used to pay all creditors, with the remaining amounts to be distributed to the partners according to their proportionate share. 
====triggers for dissolution: ====
*Aims of original venture met 
*Aims of original venture not met 
*Either or both parties develop new goals 
*Either or both parties no longer agree with joint venture aims 
*Time agreed for joint venture has expired 
*Legal or financial issues 
*Evolving market conditions mean that joint venture is no longer appropriate or relevant 

====Withdrawal/Death of Partner====
In the event a partner withdraws or retires from the partnership for any reason, including death, the remaining partners may continue to operate the partnership using the same name. A withdrawing partner shall be obligated to give sixty (60) days’ prior written notice of his/her intention to withdraw or retire and shall be obligated to sell his/her interest in the partnership. No partner shall transfer interest in the partnership to any other party without the written consent of the remaining partner(s).  The remaining partner(s) shall pay the withdrawing or retiring partner, or to the legal representative of the deceased or disabled partner, the value of his interest in the partnership, or 
*the sum of his capital account, 
*any unpaid loans due him, 
*his proportionate share of accrued net profits remaining undistributed in his capital account, and 
*his interest in any prior agreed appreciation in the value of the partnership property over its book value.  No value for good will shall be included in determining the value of the partner’s interest.

===Admitting a New Partner===
A new partner may be accepted with the agreement of a majority of shareholders.

Any new partner agrees to be bound by all covenants, terms and conditions of this agreement.

===Sale of Business===
In the event another business wishes to purchase the business, all partners must agree to the sale.  If some partners wish to sell and the others don't, a shotgun clause shall be triggered where the one side offers to buy out the other side for a declared amount, and the other side has 1 week to accept, or to purchase the other side's shares for the same valuation.

===Duty of Loyalty/Non-Compete Agreement===
#No partner will engage in any business, venture or transaction that directly or indirectly competes with the business of the partnership without prior approval (anonymous consent) of the other partners.
#Any and all business/ventures/transactions that may appear to be a conflict of interest must be disclosed to the other partners.
#A partner who retires or withdraws from the partnership shall not directly or indirectly engage in a business which is or which would be competitive with the existing or then anticipated business of the partnership for a period of  2 years, in those locations where the partnership is currently doing or planning to do business.

===Forbidden Acts===
#No partner may do any act that makes it impossible to carry out the ordinary business of the partnership.
#No Partner shall make a binding agreement upon the partnership that is outside of the ordinary business of the partnership.\

===Amendments===

This agreement may not be amended in whole or in part without the unanimous consent of all partners.

===Disputes===
This Partnership Agreement shall be governed by the laws of Alberta.   Any disputes arising between the partners as a result of this Agreement shall be settled by arbitration in accordance with the laws of Alberta and judgment upon the award rendered may be entered in any court having jurisdiction thereof

1. Decision-making. Talk about and decide how you will make decisions, especially in those cases when it’s an important topic and there is no consensus. In one of my partnerships, we had decided that major decisions had to be unanimous. When we couldn’t get there, we didn’t move forward.
2. Salaries/Distributions. According to Eiss-Proctor, these are technically 2 different sections, but they are getting at the same thing. She suggests starting with basic questions like: When will the partners be able to take money out of the business? If one of the partners wants to build a national brand, this may require keeping the money in the company longer (and thus fewer distributions) than if the other partner wants to make it a mom and pop shop and live on the small salary. Will partners ever get re-paid for the investments they put in, if so, when? You and your partners should be in agreement about the ways the money should be allocated among the owners. 
3. Death/Disability. It seems so unlikely, but bad things happen sometimes, and it’s best to be prepared. Insurance, trusts, and wills all come into play on this topic, so you’ll have to think through who in your life you trust to make decisions on your behalf; who would inherit your shares of the company; and would you want your beneficiaries to have a say in what happens to the company (or, conversely, are you prepared to share power with your partner’s spouse/family member/friend.) 
4. Dissolution. “No one ever wants to talk about this one,” says Eiss-Proctor. “But it is extremely important to discuss at the beginning of the business relationship. Figure out now, while everyone is getting along, what will happen if one of the partners doesn’t want to be involved anymore. Think ahead to a time when you and your partner(s) may not be in agreement about the business. That is not the time to start arguing about the exit strategies. The time to figure out exit strategies is at the beginning when everyone is working to make the business take shape.”
