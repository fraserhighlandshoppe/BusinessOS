==Budget Preperation==
A budget is a plan like any other but it uses monetary terms instead of descriptive terms.


===Purpose of Budgeting===
A budget determines the money going out and in during the year.

Improve priortizing your objectives and reaching the financial goals.

Major Processes:
*Planning
**strategies for meeting goals.
*Allocation of Resources
**Do we need more staff.  Will they directly increase revenue?  Will they increase capacity to increase future revenues?
*Management control
**prepare forecast
**adjust for variations
**monitor actual results
===Establishing Budget Purpose===
*Define objectives
**Align to corporate goals
*Complete a statement of financial environment
**what may cause a change in the business model (e.g. regulatory)
**trends
**industry studies e.g. financial health of competitor and potential impact on your company
*define working assumptions
**expectations that sales will [remain flat/increase/decrease]
**perceptions on workload, staff availability
**limitations you will face
*establish measurable operating objectives
**Need to measure success or lack thereof
**levels, figures or other concrete numbers.
==Budgeting Process==
A methodical process allows for improved decision making and clear goals for revenues and expenses.
===Approaches===
2 approaches dominate:
#zero based budget
##no costs are automatically justified and must be evaluated for inclusion
#historical trend budget
##relies on past budget patterns.  i.e. taking recent amount and adding a growth factor
====Zero Based====
Good for allocating resources

Time consuming as you evaluate each and every item.  Need and cost effectiveness need to be examined.

Demands first on analysis and decision making rather than size of budget.  Also includes alternatives which allows you to right size the budget to acheive goals.

Better for a difficult financial environment.
====Historical trends====
Can lead to overblown budgets as outdated info is automatically built in.

When on short time, this may be the better starting point.

Historical Trend doesn't show productivity.
===Develop a departmental budget===
#Itemize expected expenses
##salaries
##equipment
##supplies
#Total expected revenue is specified.  If revenue isn't generated, request a share of the pooled revenue.
##goods
##services
Do these contribute to company goals?

Need to back up with details.

===components for Forecasting revenues and expenses===
Revenue is total moneys earned

Income is money left after paying expenses.

Expense is the outlay of money in 2 forms
*Fixed cost
**rent
*Variable cost
**supplies
**utilities

Fixed costs can be spread over 12 months whereas variable will ebb and flow.

Revenue from line departments go into the revenue pool.

From other departments it comes out of the pool.

===Forecasting revenues and expenses===
Forecasting is never perfect.

Juggling projections of revenue and expenses month by month.

==Monitoring Adherence to Budgets==
Delays in accounts receivable can tie up your cash flow.

Having a banner sale can also cause problems.

You want to 
*encourage accountable
*make adjustments if necessary
*update future plans.

A budget is useless if not monitored.
===Analyzing variances===
Variance - numeric difference between real totals and projections.

Monitor revenue and expenses frequently.

Use detailed reports to identify the source of variance.

Checking line by line you may be able to identify places where repacking could be used to increase sales.

The better you know the data the better you can plan for distortions.

If you have a large variance, you may want to reduce the reporting intervals so that you can better follow where things are straying from plan.

Quick action may retrieve a bad situation or prevent one.
===Addressing Revenue Variances===
A 10% divergence is usually used as the trigger for extra scrutiny.

3 Basic techniques exist:
#Wait and see
##small and no pattern
#Target actions to specific instances
##look for higher revenues or lower sales
#Across the board action
##revise the budget due to a serious situation e.g. steep downturn in the economy.
