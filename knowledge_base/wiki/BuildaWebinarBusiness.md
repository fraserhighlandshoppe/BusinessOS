==killer presentation==
*If you teach something of high value, people will come to know and trust you.
*Over Deliver on your content
*Don't try to keep the "secret sauce" a secret.
**You turn people off if you keep secrets
**People trust you more if you gave away the secret
*Many people are visual learners
**Create eye catching slides
**Picture the person speaking (Put a picture of the speaker on the side for the entire presentation)
**Use wide screen slides (16:9)
***Many buying wide screens
***Better use of screen real estate
***Easier to view more info
***Allows you to leave an orientation board on the screen
*Avoid too many live demos
**outages
**Slow loading pages
**don't demo live software
***Use screen grabs (snagit)
***Software crashes on occasion
***You will know what is going to appear next.
**Use a countdown screen on the webinar before the presentation starts.  Removes confusion.
**Include instructions on un-muting the speakers, dialin numbers with conference number etc.
*Create the presentation via slides
**Powerpoint
**Google docs
*Content is more important than pretty look.  Good content and good slides is a winning formula.

==how to run Auto Webinars==
As your business grows, you will want to scale it.  An auto-webinar takes care of this.
*Pre-recorded.
*If done right, feels like a live event.
*The actual event is more important than making people think it is live.
*Do custom auto webinars with/for your JV partners
**Record main part of the presentation that is the same every time
**Partner records specific intro and exit segment
**Have your JV promote it as a webinar and then put it in their auto responder series
*Provide a replay
*Have an area allowing them to ask questions.  If you've told them in advance that you will followup afterwards, this doesn't shatter the illusion of live.
*Have an area to click for more info or purchasing.
