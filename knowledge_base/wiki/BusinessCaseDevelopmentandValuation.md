==Business Case==
A business case is input to the initiative's value propositiion.  It communicates the value and risks of the initiative.

A business case typically requires models, executive presentations, summaries of the value, cost, roadmap, risks and alternatives.

You would use the business case to
*define success
*make investment decisions
*promote an initiative.

==Business Case Valuation==
A business case is a structured approach to evaluating an investment.
*does it meet minimum financial requirements.
*prioritizing the investment

Many clients will not consider an investment without a business case.

Business cases should answer the enABLing questions:
*Alignment - how does the solution align with goals
*Business Logic - what is the business logic of how a solution improves performance
*Lasting benefits - What are the lasting cash flow, valuation and return benefits.

A business case is not just the numbers.  It includes qualitative and quantitative criteria.
===Qualitative criteria===
*Strategic fit
*business process logic
*critical success factors
*intangibles
*Alternative including doing nothing
===Quantitative criteria===
*Risk assessment
*Return and valuation analysis
*payback
*break even analysis

==Why build the business case==
*Helps establish a better understanding of challenges and goals.
*Helps the client better understand your value.
*Helps the client understand the total cost of ownership.
*Helps differentiate you from your competitors.
Some times it doesn't make sense to build a business case:
*regulatory requirements means the project will go ahead anyway
*management have approved the project already
*minimizing labour unrest where the business case would identify layoffs
Sometimes a cost benefit anaysis is enough
*final decision maker doesn't need a detailed presentation
*supportive materials are already available
===Establish the definition of success===
Provide detailed identification, quantification and timing of expected financial and other business benefits.
*measure strategic benefits
*measure financial benefits
*justify costs and assumptions
*provide the roadmap to allow identified benefits to be claimed
===Make Decisions===
Justify investments
*make the go/no go decision
*identify and secure resources
*evaluate multiple scenarios and alternative options
===Promote the Initiative through the organizations===
*communicate the benefits and convince skeptics
*Be the source for the final presentation when gaining approval to proceed.

==Cash Flow==
The after tax 
*cost of an investment 
*and the benefit from the investment.
===Incremental Cash Flow===
Business Cases only include incremental cash flow.
*Incremental cash flow are those that only result from implementing the business case.
Including non incremental cash flow distorts the business case's forecasted return and value creation.

Remember when looking at Capital cash flow to use INCREMENTAL - the change year to year.

Also, fixed assets of the solution needs to be depreciated over time so this affects your benefits.

One common error is double counting - everyone is including the same benefits in their business cases e.g. removing cost of labour.

Phantom cash flows - can't save costs you weren't going to incur, same can't book revenues you would not have gained.

===Time Value of Money===
Foundation of many financial decisions.
 Basis is that the value of a dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow
 *Receiving and then Investing the dollar today gives you a higher return than just receiving that dollar tomorrow.
 *Inflation decreases the purchasing power of that dollar.

Future value answers the question
 What is the value in the future if you know the value today and you have an expected average annual growth rate (appreciation)
 *My house is worth 100,000 and average appreciation is 3%.  What is my house worth in 5 years?

====Future Value====
FUTURE VALUE = Present Value * (1 + return)^Periods

Present Value = how much something is  worth today if I know its future value and expected average growth rate.

 Mortgage example
 *100,000 mortgage for 30 years at 6%
 **599.55 month payment
 **215,838 paid in 30 years (599.55 * 360)
 *The value of 360 payments of 599.55 in today's dollars is $100,000

====Present Value====
Present Value = Future Value / (1 + cost of capital)
 Example
 *$100 in 1 year with 10% cost of capital
 **100/(1 + .1)
 **90.91
 The present value of 100 future (1 year @ 10%) dollars is 90.91

====Net Present Value====
Most commonly used criteria

NPV = Present value of solutions benefit - present value of solutions investment (cost)

NPV = SUM[0..n] Net Cash Flow(n) / (1 + discount rate)^n

Discount Rate = the rate of return that could be earned on an investment in the financial markets with similar risk.

 Example
 *Invest 1,000,000
 *saves 512,000 per year
 **Present value of 100 in 1 year at 10% is .9091 (as seen in Present Value above)
 **At Y1 512 * .9091 = present value of 466
 **Y2 512 * (.9091)^2 = 423
 **Y3 512 * (.9091)^3) = 385
 *Present value of solution = 466 + 423 + 385 = 1274
 *Present value cost of solution = 1000
 *Net Present Value = 1274 - 1000 = $274,000
 Value is created!

====Total Cost of Ownership====
Total cost of ownership includes
*upfront investment costs
*ongoing investment
*support and maintenance contracts
*Change management and training
*Ongoing management and support

Want/need to calculate the present value of the TCO as well

*Advantage
**accounts for time value of money
**based on how companies are valued
**provides a common basis for comparisons

*Disadvantage
**Challenges in comparing solutions with different scales of investment
**e.g. Solutions A, B and C have NPV of 10M
**Solution A investment is 1M, B is 10M, c is 100M.
**All solutions are acceptable by NPV but A provides the highest benefit relative to investment.

====Return on Investment====
ROI = total solution benefits / total investment in solution.

From NPV example above, 512M * 3 /1000M = 154%

*Advantage
**Easy to calculate
**Easy to understand
**provides an initial high level screen 
***less than 100% means doesn't pay for itself
***extremely high returns (e.g. 500%) indicate benefits overstated or costs understated.

*Disadvantage
**Ignores time value of money
***solution A if 1M and .5M received for 3 years
***solution B if 1M and 1.5M received in year 3
***both solutions have an ROI of 150% but solution A has benefits immediately
**Cannot compare investments with different lives
**Not directly comparable to the cost of capitol
**Not directly comparable to the hurdle rate

====Payback Period====
How long does it take to recover the solutions initial investment?

A solutions acceptable payback period is shortened when
*operating cash flow is constrained
*financing is constrained
*greater uncertainty about future operating environment.

*Advantages
**Easy to understand
**Easy to calculate
**places greater value on earlier cash flows

*Disadvantage
**required pay back period is arbitrary
**doesn't explicitly account for the time value of money
**does not account for return

====Internal Rate of Return====
IRR provides a more refined estimate of the return and is consistent with the time value of money and directly compares to TCO and hurdle rate.

IRR is calculated using an iterative process
 Present value of solution benefits = present value of investment in solution.

Example from earlier (1M cost, 512000pa return)
 *512*(1-x)^1 + 512*(1-x)^2 + 512*(1-x)^3 = 1M
 *x = 25%
IRR = 25%

When IRR is greater than the cost of capital, value is created.

Compare the IRR to the hurdle rate to help rank projects.

*Advantages
**Directly comparable to Cost of Capital
**Directly comparable Hurdle Rate
**Accounts for Time Value of Money

*Disadvantages
**Return and Amount of value creation may not be highly correlated
**Assumes all cash flow is reinvested at IRR
