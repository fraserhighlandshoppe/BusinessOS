==Immediate Yes==
Immediate Yes = Action + Solution + Problem + Information + Trust & Credibility + Rapport.

*You need to have a REASON for them to take immediate action.
**Pain or Pleasure
*Need to have a solution to a Problem
*To get the Problem you need Information
*Prospect needs to believe you are trustworthy and credible.
*To earn trust you must build rapport!

This works whether in person, on the phone, in a sales video, etc

Miss one of the components and it becomes an Immediate Accident, or an "I'll think about it".

Goal is to cycle through these steps without looking like you are trying.  If you hit a block at a step, it is a problem with the previous step.

*No urgency - they won't take ''action''.  "I didn't have time, I had the kids,..."
*No ''Solution'' - I'm not interested, not for me.  People only buy solutions.
*If you solve more ''problems'' than the solution costs, they are IN.  (e.g. Time, money costs...)  But if you don't solve their problem there is no way to sell the solution!
*Without ''Information'', you get Apathy!  You won't be talking about their problems.
*If you aren't ''trusted'' nor credible, they will be skeptical.
**you can frame yourself as trustable with stories.
*Need to quickly build massive ''rapport''.  Otherwise there will be red flags.
**The person who is most confident and certain will lead the other.

To inspire trust, put others first. 

That single, central action empowers all legendary leaders.

It is so grounded in common sense that — like other self-evident truths — it is often overlooked.

It's easy to lead people when you put their needs first, but it's almost impossible when you're only serving yourself.

If you adopt another person's goal as part of your goal, why shouldn't they follow your lead? If you don't, why should they?

This philosophy, though, goes against the grain of popular business and social culture, in which creating trust is often reduced to various forms of manipulation, and is typically referred to as “winning” trust, as if that sacred goal were a game.

Also, people assume — without thinking it through — that if they put others first, they'll get walked all over. They won't. Counterintuitive? Yes. But you'll see it's true when you try it.

If you invoke the philosophy of putting others first, that simple, sole act will serve as the foundation of your ascension to the role of a trusted leader.

==How To Do "Facebook Recruiting" Using Attraction Marketing==
 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EpkfRLl3z3s
Most people use a PM saying "Hey XXX, I just discovered a wonderful new product, here is my affiliate link http...."

===Don't===
Post a pic of a product, use a hashtag for the company.

It may be OK to promote the company brand once a week, but more than that will feel spammy.

Post a pic with before/after with company hashtags.

Nobody cares.  At most they google to research the product, find other people selling, join them instead of you.
===Do===
use ''your'' brand hashtag.

use a video, especially on FB - entertainment age.

Keep it mysterious.

OR post a pic, but don't have the company logo there, nor the product name.  Use an alias "green water" versus boosterjuice etc.
*creates curiosity
*drives engagement

On these posts when you include a link have it go to your fanpage.
