How do you determine where you will spend your money.  Can you account for all of your spending?

In business, cash is defined as an asset.  It includes
*currency
*coins
*bank accounts
*chequing accounts

Cash is a very liquid asset because it is easily moved.  It is vunerable to
*fraud
*theft
*human error
*waste
*loss
==Internal Control==
There are many ways to control the flow of cash.  Using internal control helps avoid theft and fraud.

Small companies are often vulnerable to theft due to weak internal controls.
*proof of sale
**pre-number all sales slips and periodically check for missing slips
===Principals===
*establish responsibilities
*divide responsibilities
**having more than one employee responsible reduces the risk of fraud and error
**e.g. 2 receivers, and 2 purchasers.  Harder to slip something through when someone else is also looking at the transactions regularly.
*perform reviews
**independent auditors can verify/validate effectiveness of control system
*maintain records
*insure assets
*bond employees
*separate record keeping tasks
**those having access to an asset are not allowed to maintain the accounting records for those assets.
***salesperson doesn't do inventory control on the items they sell
*use technological controls
**cash registers
**time clocks
**scanners

===Voucher System===
Controls cash disbursement.  Uses business papers (source documents) to prove that transactions occurred.

A voucher is a file or folder for storing business papers.

*purchase requisition
**department manager creates the requisition and sends it to the purchasers.
*purchase order
**purchasers take the PR and create the PO.  They send one copy of the PR on to accounting and put one into a voucher. 
**purchaser then sends PO to vendor
*invoice
**lists customer name
**items sold
**sale prices
**terms of sale
**invoice is sent to purchasing store with the goods.
*receiving report
**when the department manager gets the goods that were ordered, a receiving report is created 
**checks for missing items
**checks for damaged items, etc.
**receiving report is then sent to accounting
*invoice approval
**Accounting department ensures that all required documents are in the voucher.
***purchase requisition
***purchase order
***invoice
***receiving report
**once all are there the invoice approval is generated and the invoice paid.
**invoice is paid
**invoice approval placed in voucher
**payment recorded

===Petty Cash===
You generally don't write checks for small amounts.  Instead they set up a petty cash fund used to pay for small purchases.

To ensure accuracy of records, only one person should handle the records
*establish and record petty cash
*prepares petty cash receipts
*summarizes the petty cash payments
*replenishes the petty cash fund.

To establish the petty cash fund
#record in the General Journal a debit to the petty cash account
#record in the General Journal a credit to the cash account
##Petty Cash is an asset, and as such will appear as a Current Asset on the balance sheet.

When petty cash is spent, the person doing the spending fills out a petty cash receipt
*what the payment is for
*date
*expense account to charge
*amount
*who approved the payment
*who received the payment

The petty cash receipt should go into the cash box.  At all times the sum of the receipts plus the cash in the box should equal the fund.

When the fund gets low, prepare a report (summary) of payments made.
#sort receipts by expense type/account
#total the receipts for each type
#prepare the summary

You also need to replenish the petty cash fund at the end of the accounting period so that you can balance the books.
*replenish the cash
*debit the appropriate expense accounts
You may find errors where not enough or too much cash was paid out
*if the fund is short, record it as an expense
**a debit to a Cash Over and Short account is made
*if the fund is over, record it when you replenish the fund
**a credit is made to the Cash Over and Short account.
If you need to increase or decrease the petty cash fund, entries are made into the same locations as when the fund was established.

===Bank Reconciliation===
bank reconciliations are statements that bring the bank records into agreement with the depositor's records.
*beginning balance
*checks and other deductions from the account
*deposits and any other additions to the account
*ending balance
The bank includes cancelled checks with your statement.  These are checks you issued that the receiver has cashed.

You reconcile your accounts with the bank
#locate errors made by the bank or your company
#disclose items entered in the books but not in the bank records
#disclose items that are in the bank records but not yet in your books

Seldom will your records match up exactly with the bank statement on the day you go to do the reconciliation.  But this is because it is a snapshot in time, and you will probably have had activity since.
*outstanding checks
*unrecorded deposits
*service charges
*interest charges
*NSF checks
*bank errors
Steps to reconcile:
#identify and compare discrepancies
#determine how to handle the discrepancies
#prepare the bank reconciliation statement
##this statement will tell how you are reconciling your records and the bank records.
#prepare a journal entry

===Gross Earnings===
Total amount of an employees pay before any deduction.

You need to determine the pay period for an employee as you hire them.
*weekly
**calculate gross earnings once a week
*monthly
**calculate gross earnings once a month, usually at the end of the month
*biweekly
**calculate gross earnings once every two weeks
*semi-monthly
**calculate gross earnings twice a month, usually on the 15th and 30th

Payment Plans
*hourly
**number of hours * hourly rate.
*salary
**Yearly salary divided by the pay period
***weekly = 52
***biweekly = 26
***semi-monthly = 24
***monthly = 12
*piece rate
**the employee is paid by the number of units produced * rate per unit
**most people with piece rate also have a base salary
**sum the base + piece rate portion
*commission
**based on a percentage of sales
*combo salary and commission
**a base salary plus a commission

Workers paid by salary, commission or piece rate are not usually paid overtime.  Sometimes they are granted time off in lieu.

Workers paid by hour are usually paid overtime for any hours over 40.  Most employers pay 1.5 times as the overtime rate.

Gross earnings = regular earnings + overtime + commission

==Recording Payroll==
When reporting payroll expenses, you have a set of documents that are required.

Recording the payroll in the general journal has the following benefits
*audits by IRS/CRA have supporting documentation
*owners/managers can review the company financial positions
*accurate accounting records.
===Preparing the Payroll Register===
Supplementary record used to record all employee gross earnings, deductions and net earnings.

You need to record
*employee name
*hours worked
*pay rate
*regular and overtime pay
*gross earnings
*deductions
*net earnings
*register total

The payroll register includes separate columns for deductions and expenses related to payroll costs.  It includes all the data necessary for recording the payroll in the General Journal.

===Recording the payroll in the General Journal===
The payroll register is then recorded in the General Journal.

These entries go into special accounts
*gross earnings 
**debit a Salary Expense Account
*FICA (EI) taxes
**credit amount into a liability account.
*income taxes
**credit taxes into the Employees Income Taxes Payable account
*hospital premiums
**Employees Hospital Insurance Payable
*net earnings
**Credit to the Salaries Payable account

Then record by
#prepare journal entry to record the payroll register info
#prepare the journal entry to pay employees
#prepare the journal entry to pay taxes levied on the employer
#prepare the journal entry to record payment of taxes and other deductions to the appropriate agencies.

Preparing to pay the employees you credited the Accrued Payroll Payable account.

Paying the employees means you debit the Accrued Payroll Payable account and credit the Cash account for the same amount.

Similar actions are taken to pay the taxes and other deductions to the right places.
