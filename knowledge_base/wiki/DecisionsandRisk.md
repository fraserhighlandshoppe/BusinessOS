Part of a manager's role is to make a series of decisions.  If there were no problems to be solved, there wouldn't be a need for managers.

==Decision Making Process==
You need to be able to back up your "it feels right" decisions with data and process.

===A Decision===
Decisions usually resolve problems or implement courses of action.  Most decisions involve choosing amongst alternatives.  Sometimes there are only 2 choices, sometimes many.

Decisions almost always involve risks.  You need to ensure you are looking at worst case scenarios
*what could go wrong
*what is the worst that can happen
*What is the cost?  Is the investment justified?
*what kind of effort needed and can we handle that?
*do we need more people?  Can we find them?
*what happens if the situation changes?
*does the decision support our short and long term objectives?
*what are the worst case scenarios, and the back up plans to deal with them

==Be Decisive==
The ability to be decisive is an essential leadership trait.  It means being able to make the right decision at the right time.

To be decisive you must
*be confident
*take time to consider your choices
*understand the implications
*approach decisions positively
*make the decision in a timely manner

Don't make your decision too quickly unless the solution is clear, or you have to have the decision immediately.

Also consider if the decision can be reversed at a later time, as this allows adjustments and corrections for mistakes.

===Decision Types===
*Experimental
**Decision isn't final until the results are satisfactory
**effective when the right move is unclear but the general direction is known
*Trial and Error
**Attempt different alternatives until the right one is found
**allows adaptation and adjustments to plans before a final commitment.
*Made in Stages
**decisions are made incrementally
**allows monitoring of risks as outcome evidence is gathered in stages.
*Cautious
**allows for contingencies
**limits the risk
**limits the gains
*Conditional
**an either/or decision with options
**establishes quick reaction if circumstances change
*Delayed
**put on hold until the time is right
**prevents decisions from being made at the wrong time.

===Price Quality Matrix===
Used to relate price to quality so that they can position themselves in the market.

 Price of product - L M H 
 vs Quality - L M H
 Grid gives you Cheap (Low and Low), Average (Medium and Medium) and Premium (High and High)

Most companies avoid cheap products.  If your end goal is Premium, you might start at Average to establish a market.  Premium is a revered and sought after classification.  Higher quality with a higher price can result in the best profit margin.

===Relative Market Share===
RMS is the combined sales as a percentage of the companies three largest rivals.  If you found that you have a large enough share you might consider reducing your premium price to sell to more people.

==Develop Creative thinking==
Many people reject creative thinking because change is a risk.  To defeat the negative thinking:
*reject "yes, but" thinking
*challenge assumptions
*create many possible solutions
*overcome rigid thinking.

Highly creative people probably get impatient with their coworkers that typically reject anything new.  If you are cautious by nature it is hard to think adventurously.

Being positive helps encourage out of the box thinking.

When people become defensive about their positions, tell them no more "Yes, but", that it is a rule.

Also ask "what are your underlying assumptions".  Sometimes people don't know.

To come up with the best idea you need to come up with lots of ideas.

Don't let group members choose favorite ideas too early.

==Safeguarding decisions==
You want to protect against things going wrong.  You will want to minimize the impacts of things not going to plan.

*minimize the risks using a risk assessment matrix
*use best case/worst case and middle ground scenarios
*assess the consequences of decisions for your staff.
===Minimize Risk===
Most decisions contain uncertainty.
*think through the consequences
*be ready to compromise
*consider negative effects
===Risk Assessment Matrix===
Matrix lists likelihood with the severity.

 Risk - L, M, H
 Likelihood - Impossible, unlikely, likely, very likely

If you have anything in a High/Very Likely box you are in trouble.

Using the matrix will help you evaluate the risk and minimize it.

===Using Scenarios===
Best Case and Worst case scenarios help you evaluate if the risk is worth the reward.

Best Case Scenario is the most desired combinations fo outcomes you can reasonably expect.  Worst case is the least desired combinations.  ''Reasonable'' is very important here because neither optimism nor pessimism should be taken to extremes.  

In the simplest form it shows whether the best case results justify the worst case risk.

===Affecting your staff===
Before making decisions that affect your staff, you need to consider a number of things to maximize their support and minimize their sabotage.

*You need to know their strength and their weaknesses
**their skills may need change
*be open and honest
*inform your staff immediately once the decision has been made.  Waiting doesn't benefit anyone as it reduces the amount of time people have to deal with the decision.
*communication is key

==Time to Decide==
Making decisions as a team can be beneficial:
*generates fresh and unusual ideas
*increases learning and personal growth
*challenges individuals and increases autonomy
*gain exposure to the big picture

Once the decision has been made, to gain support you want to
*explain the reasons for the decision and the facts behind it
**what issues made the decision necessary
**who, what, when, where, why and how
*show upper management support the decision
*explain what's likely to happen because of the decision
