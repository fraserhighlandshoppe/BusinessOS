==Advertising==
If you don't have the right words on the page, you won't sell the items.

Right message, right market, right media.

You have to go out and find the business, they won't just show up and knock on your door.
*quickly
*efficiently

When your potential clients are looking, there is a lot of clutter.

Only 20% of the potential clients are looking for the products you sell.  You need to convince the other 80% to come do business.

What is marketing and how do you make it work for your business?  So where do you start?
*You can put up a website, but don't expect to be magically found.
*Even if the clients find you, they don't show up with credit card in hand.
*The average person is exposed to 3000 advertising messages a day.

==Brand style marketing and response style marketing==
===Brand===
Big signs, color brochures, beautiful websites don't get you the initial business.  This is like winking in the dark - you may know what you are doing and why, but no one else does.

Websites, signs, brochures are about building your image, your brand.  Branding is a huge up front expense.

You may get to the point where you don't have to do marketting (repeat business, word of mouth) but this takes a lot of work and a long time.

In the mean time, you need to attract the right types of buyers to purchase your product so that you are profitable.
===Response===
====Generating Leads vs generating sales====
There is a certain demographic that would be their ideal paying customer.  Gender, age, kids/not...
*Put your ad in front of your target.
*Sell the target something they are already interested in.
*Giving something away for free generates the lead.  
**People don't generally buy from just 1 ad.
**But those that sign up for your freebies (i.e. DVD and 2 reports) are going to be interested in your products.
*Grand opening - advertise prizes (draws), not sales.
**Once they are in the store, they can see the products
**Filling out the contest entry form generates your lead database.
**If someone goes out of their way to show up and fill out your forms, they are pre-qualifying themselves.
*Now, take your database and market to them, rather than the shotgun approach of marketing to everyone.
====Copywriting Rules====
=====Create a really strong headline=====
Read Cosmo/Readers Digest/National Enquirer front covers.
*17 ways to ....
See what else has been proven to work.
=====Addressing the reader and not yourself=====
Most marketing materials is all about the company rather than about the customer.  The customers don't really care.  What is in it for the customers, not you the company.  2nd concern to them is "so what".
*Count the number of times you say You or YOURS compared to I/We/Me.  You should have 4-5 times the You's than Me's.
=====Say as much as you need to say=====
Everyone seem to think the best ads have pretty pictures, white space, and little text.  The thought is that people won't read it all.  
*Provide as much as possible.
*Let the customer choose what they want to read.
*A good sales piece sounds like a one on one conversation.
*Making it look like an article rather than an ad raises the readership.  People purchase magazines to read articles and have become used to skipping over ads.
*Attach emotions to the ad.  I.E. advertising furniture, talk about the memories that are made/experienced around the kitchen table.
====Converting the leads into sales====
People are reaching out with social networking (Twitter, Facebook).  Talk to the leads with stories.  People love a good story.  Here is where you talk about why you started the company, and what you do differently.

To find really good advertisements, look back 50-80 years.  You will see that there were few to no pictures, but lots of text.  Signature stories are used to talk about the business, getting the customers getting enthralled, wanting to hear more of the story.  They then come into the store.  Brand recognition is created through the stories.

====Measuring and Testing====
You can start with a simple campaign, measure the response, and then know if it worked or not.  This allows you to expand or change the marketing.

*Run the add.  Check the results.
*Modify it slightly, and re-run.  Check the new results.  If they worked better, keep the new, else go back to the old.

=====Measuring=====
*Have 2 different ads.  Have one ad go to one website name, and the 2nd to another.  You can then track which websites have the most responses and therefore which ad.  
*Have different ads refer to different phone/fax numbers.
*If having people fill in a form, have each form have unique tracking codes within.
*Have different ads send to different emails.
*Unique offer codes
*Different departments for snail mail.
*When customers come into the store in person, ask how they heard about the sale/offer/ad.

==Campaigns==
Targeted, you need a couple hundred to have significant results.

Blanket marketing, you will need 500+.
