==Advisors Book of Business==
Do you really own your clients?

[[Category: FindBob]]
[[Category: Business]]
[[Category: KSFII]]
[[Category: FHS]]

===Dealer===
Investment Dealers see clients as client of firm.
*advisors are salaried.  Given list of clients.
*advisor agrees to not solicit if they leave.
*Where no agreement in place dealer claims departing advisor has fiduciary duty not to solicit or contact clients.

Other end - advisors are independant agents.
*seek out and service own clients.
*dealer is vehicle through which business is placed.
*all parties expect clients to go with advisor.

Middle Ground
*advisors have some independence
*develop own client base
*firms not expressly acknowledge that clients belong to advisor.  Might allow retiring advisor to "sell" book to another advisor within the firm.
*firm may call "sale" something else - introductory fee/transition fee.
*a departing advisor leaving for another dealer may be permitted to purchase his client base.

===Advisor Side===
*Book of business is a valuable asset.
*goodwill is based upon advisor personal relationship

===Courts view===
cases:
*dealer sues advisor for soliciting clients contrary to an agreement
*dealer sues departing advisor for improperly using confidential client info to solicit clients/take steps to move clients while still at former firm.
*advisor is dismissed by dealer and sues for damage and/or loss of book of business.
====Characteristics of decisions last 20 years====
*court advises talking about ownership of Book of Business.
*likely to say dealer has a property interest in client list
*Primary consideration is client choice.  Clients are entitled to knwo when advisor leaves current dealer./
*Parties have obligation to treat each other fairly.
*Courts award damages for lost opportunity to retain clients.  Not for the loss of an asset.
*courts will enforce written agreements setting out rights and obligations.
*both advisor and dealer have right to compete for clients.
**enforceable contracts (non solicit) have impact
*advisor may not own their book of business.
*improper dismissal can result in damages.
Protect your book with a written agreement.
*can negotiate at time of departure but this is going to be difficult.
*Best to negotiate at start of relationship with firm/dealer
*even if ownership can't be agreed upon, you can deal with how clients are notified of impending departure.
