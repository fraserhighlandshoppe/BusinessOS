Financial independence isn’t life, it’s just a tool you can use to help you live a life that’s most meaningful to you.

It’s a means to an end, not the end itself. 

Can you boil down what you want your life to be in just one sentence?

 From https://www.madfientist.com/hierarchy-of-financial-needs/: 
 I want to learn and improve my skills so that I can create things that have a positive impact 
 on my life and as many other people’s lives as possible.

==Financial Independence doesn't assure Happiness==
 Inspired by https://www.madfientist.com/happiness-through-subtraction/
All of your problems are not magically solved just because you've hit your FI number.  Money doesn't buy happiness, but a lack of money can certainly lead to unhappiness.

You need to ensure that the pursuit of your FI number does not lead to unhappiness.  If you are avoiding expenditures at all costs, are you missing out on things that would make you happy?  Isolation from friends and family will make you unhappy.  Having multiple houses and cars and boats on the other hand does not increase your happiness (see discussion below on Maslow heirarchy and the financial equivalent).

Having X dollars in the bank will not make you any happier than having X-1.  X is not a magic number.

===We are terrible at predicting what makes us happy===
Bruno Frey describes in his book, Happiness: A Revolution in Economics:

 There are many processes that may explain why higher income does not have more of an effect on happiness. 
 The most important of these are that individuals adapt to their new standard of living and that they compare 
 themselves to other individuals. It is not the absolute level of income that matters most, but rather one’s 
 position relative to the past and other individuals.

From the book Antifragile:

 happiness is best dealt with as a negative concept…the ‘pursuit of happiness’ is not equivalent to the 
 ‘avoidance of unhappiness.’ Each of us certainly knows not only what makes us unhappy…but what to do about it.

Instead of pursuing things we think will make us happy, we should focus on eliminating the things that make us unhappy.
*Don't like the grind of 9-5 in an office?  Save enough to be able to quit at any time.

===Heirarchy of Needs===
 Inspired by https://www.madfientist.com/hierarchy-of-financial-needs/

Having the Heirarchy of Needs is useful in discussing financials with people, because it frames the stage of life they are in, and helps raise what is reasonable in terms of that position.  For example, pay advances and payday loans may seem silly to someone at the Independence level, but may be the only solution at the Survival level!
====Maslow====
*Self Actualization
**Acheive potential
*Esteem
**Recognition from self and others
*Belonging
**Friends, Family
*Safety
**Shelter, Clothing
*Physiology
**Food, Water

====Financial====
*Utilization
**Spend or Give
*Independance
**Pay all expenses with Investment Income
*Accumulation
**Saving for future expense
*Sustainability
**Cover all current expenses with earned income
*Survival
**Use Income + Debt to pay essential expenses

When you first leave your parent's home and start to Adult, you must pay for things that you need to survive. Using Debt may be the only option to ensure you have food on the table.  However, this is not sustainable.  Eventually you need to make enough money to pay your way.

You can live happily at the Sustainability level.  For a while.  But you are not safe there.  One unexpected expense could send you back to Survival.  You need to reach the Accumulation level. Here, you make more than you spend so you can build an emergency fund.

It’s unlikely you will be physically able to work your entire life.  You must reach the Independence level.  Most people call this retirement. 

Lastly you might reach the Utilization phase. This is when you’ll never run out of money, so your focus is aligning your spending and giving (to charities, heirs, etc.) with your life’s purpose.
