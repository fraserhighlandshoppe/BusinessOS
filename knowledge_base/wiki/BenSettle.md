Here’s a little fun for the Marvel fans:

 

Way back in the early 90’s, there was a comicbook writer named Chris Claremont who basically is the guy responsible for why there was any success with the “X-Men” movies over the past 26 years. 

 

They used his storylines, his plots, his characters, and even his dialogue. 

 

In fact, they had a lot of trouble cracking the script of the first movie in 2000 until they talked to him.

 

He made Uncanny X-Men the #1 selling comicbook in the world for years.

 

He made so much in royalties he has his own plane, if that tells you something.

 

And towards the end of his 17-year run, in issue 253, he began a storyline he’d intended to run through issue 300 - literally a 3+ year, carefully paced, long game ambitious storyline that he did for the fans, and not for metrics, sales, or bean counter approval.

 

It was going to turn into a storyline he wanted to call:

 

“The Mutant Wars”

 

But alas, it was not to be.

 

Because In his own words:

 

“The editor of the series and I had been at odds for a long time… about what direction the series was going to take. . .the editor made the decision that I was no longer going to plot the series.”

 

Instead, it was turned over to the editor who was grooming up some hotshot artists who were quickly becoming fan favorites, but who had never built anything. 

 

They hadn’t spent 17-years building the characters.

 

Or the intrigue.

 

Or all the World-Building, or anything else. 

 

They just drew cool artwork that a lot of fans got excited over and demanded more of, with sales figures going through the roof. Seventeen years and they pushed Chris out over... metrics.

 

And that ambitious storyline?


Abruptly ended well before it should have.

 

I was collecting them at the time, and remember how stupidly it ended.

 

Whole new direction in tone, theme, artwork, and stories-by-committee vs the vision of the guy who spent 17-years building it all up, serving the fans, building the fanbase, bringing in the sales.

 

And it only got worse from there.

 

Chris Claremont quit and the stockholders took over.

 

And they demanded constant new gimmicks and shoving popular characters in titles to boost sales, and yearly “events” that forced crossovers, plus cash grab devices like rebooting titles, putting issues in sealed plastic bags with trading cards inside, hologram covers, etc, all for sales.

 

And none of which was intended to serve the fans (the market) but "mine" it for as much money as possible.

 

Contrast that to what Chris was trying to do:

 

Serve the fanbase.

 

i.e., serve the market.

 

I had no inkling of any idea I’d end up in business at the time (I was in high school). But I probably learned more about how to build a long term storyline, pacing, and World-Building from the issues he wrote that he is referring to than anywhere else.

 

As for the future of the title?

 

The artwork the new guys were doing was awesome, I will give it that.

 

But all it did was create a bubble of speculators buying a bunch of #1 issues of their titles... then those same artists ALL quit in unison a year later to form their own company (Image Comics), after which it all crashed eventually.

 

The result?

 

It led to not only bankruptcy within 10 years, but suicides and deaths happened, as explained in Sean Howe's “Marvel Comics: The Untold Story”. In fact, after Claremont left the stories became a shell of what they were, parasiting off 17 years of his stories, killing off all the intrigue & nuance, rushing time lines, and force fitting social activism in.

 

It made an impact on my business later on.

 

And it is probably why I reflexively reject nearly anything that is trendy.

 

Especially when I see the same short-term thinking going on that is tactic-based, or shiny new designer platform-based, and suddenly popular with & promoted by warring goo-roos in mastermind rooms vs FIRST and foremost focusing on Service & Experience.

 

Get those two things right and the metrics tend to take care of themselves.

 

Get them wrong or ignore them, and you can watch what happens with all the constant goo-roo grifting & chasing algorithms & metrics, and selling out the principles many of them originally built their lists & businesses on.

 

This is why I wrote a couple months ago:

 

“I wouldn't give a bucket of piss for most IM goo-roos' futures.”

 

It may not be obvious yet, but you will see this in time.

 

I know where a few of these “bodies” are being buried already.

 

And I suspect it'd probably shock some people reading this email how many of their favorite goo-roos are teetering on the verge of bankruptcy due entirely to their reliance on carefully manufactured social proof + an irrational enslavement to the Tyranny of metrics, buying into their own PR, and forgetting all about the customers who made them successful in the first place.

 

It’s the difference between what I call:

 

“psychological marketing” (decision-by-spreadsheet and A/B split tests) and “Sociological Marketing” (decision by Service, Experience, and building a “world” customers love to enter and don’t want to leave).

 

I ain’t saying to ignore metrics or tests.

 

What I’m saying is don’t let them enslave you over Serving the customer.
