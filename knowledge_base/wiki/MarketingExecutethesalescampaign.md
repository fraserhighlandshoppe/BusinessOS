==Presenting at the CxO Level==
Test the proposal with the client in an informal meeting (fireside chat).  Test their reaction.  Are we on the right track.  Do we need to change anything, does the business case make sense, do we need more data.

Evaluate with the stakeholder that our view of the client situation is correct.  Are we addressing the key issues?  Are there already initiatives in the outlined area?

What are the KPI used for measuring the issue?

Is there a budget already available for the initiative

Consider the thin line between confidence and arrogance.

The storyline must be carefully prepared.
*clear understanding of the Pain and Gain
*willingness to listen
*ability and knowledge to discern and address the business and personal agenda
*respect for the fact that it is always the right time and venue from the CxO perspective
*mindset to communicate convincing and compelling value.
*Don't push your brand without relevance to the CxO
*Don't have a detailed discussion of our products/services
*don't have to casual a conversation. You need to address the specific needs, problems and pain points.

Regardless of how long the meeting is, spend significant time prepping.  
*What is the worst question the person could ask you?
*Run through your pitch multiple times and ways
*anticipate their questions
*what is really important for that person
**Need to understand their background not only professional but also personal
**past jobs
**who they regularly deal with (consultants, advisors, etc)
*understand the key agenda points
*understand the CxOs perception about you/your company
*prepare a clear storyline
*define realistic next steps.  ensure it is easy for the CxO to easily action.
*stick to the storyline, but be comfortable with differing opinions.
*be able to tell the story in 10-15 minutes without slides (small talk eats up time)
