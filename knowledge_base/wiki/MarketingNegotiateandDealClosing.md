==Negotiations and Deal Closing==
Book - Getting to Yes

#separate the people from the problem
#negotiators are people first
#every negotiator has 2 interest - substance, and relationship
##the relationship tends to become entangled with the problem
##position bargaining tends to put the relationship and substance in conflict
#separate the relationship from the substance and deal directly with the people problem
##perception - put yourself in their shoes
##don't deduce their intentions from your fear
##don't blame them for your problem
##discuss each others perception
##Give them a stake in the outcome by making sure they participate in the process
##look for opportunities to act inconsistently with their perception
#face saving - make your proposal consistent with their values
##emotion - recognize and understand emotions (theirs and yours).
##make emotions explicit and acknowledge them.
##allow the other side to let off steam
##don't react to emotional outbursts
#communicate (active listening)
##acknowledge what is being said
##put into your own words
##speak to be understood
##speak about yourself, not about them
##speak for a purpose
#prevention works best when
##building a working relationship
##facing the problem, not the people
#focus on interests, not positions
##reconcile interests not positions
##interests define the problem
#behind opposition positions lie shared and compatible interests as well as conflicting ones
#how do you identify interest?
##ask why.
##ask why not.
##realize that each side has multiple interests.
##identify shared interests and focus on mutual options for gain.
##the strongest interest are human needs (Maslow)
#talk about interests
##make your interests come alive
##put the problem before your answer
##look forward not back
##be concrete but flexible
##be hard on the problem but soft on the people.
#arguing over position produces unwise agreements and is inefficient
##as more attention is paid to positions less is devoted to meeting the underlying concerns
##arguing over position endangers the ongoing relationship
##when there are multiple parties positional bargaining is even worse
#being nice and giving in is no answer
#price negotiations must be seen as an equilibrium.  Change in price must be matched by change in product/service/conditions.
#invent options for mutual gain
#don't assume there is a fixed pie and only one answer
#don't think solving their problem is their problem.  Help them
#separate inventing from deciding.  Brainstorm
#broaden your options
#look through the eyes of different experts
#invent agreement of different strengths
#identify shared interest
#ask for their preferences
#make the decision easy

A good behaviour in negotiation is described as a creative open minded behaviour.  The negotiator should seek to invent new options that might satisfy both parties needs.  It is also wise to take the other side's needs in account.  Insist on using objective criteria.
