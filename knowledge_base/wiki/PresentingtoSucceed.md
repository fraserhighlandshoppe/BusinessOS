==Presenting to Succeed==
Successful presenters are made not born.

learn the techniques and then hone them through practice.

4 key parts of the presentation
#skill of presenter
#audience
#venue
#message

You also need to 
*meet the needs of the audience
*meet the needs of the venue
*prepare the presentation
*master your nerves

==Presenting Basics==
Putting the basics into practice can help assure success.

Things to consider when presenting (audience viewpoint):
*Confusion
**Are you talking for the audience's benefit or for your own entertainment.  What are you trying to infer?
*Frustration
**Audience has better things to do than listen to things they already know

Basics are:
*audience
*venue
*message
*techniques
**help audience retain knowledge/presentation
===Types of Presentation===
*informative
**sequence the info in a logical order.  Ask questions to check audience understanding
*persuasive
**sell the benefits of the proposal
**support the benefits with facts and arguments
**show appreciation audience point of view
*motivate
**acknowledge the audience negative feelings and then communicate vision of future in positive upbeat way.
*promote discussion
**present a set of options
**listen to audience view
**answers questions
*entertain
**tell amusing anecdotes based upon experience or based on what you know of audience
**avoid telling jokes.

Using the right style helps you meet the needs of the audience.
===What makes a successful presenter===
*behave confidently
**don't be arrogant
**eye contact
**speak clearly in normal voice
**move and gesture naturally
*interact with the audience
**direct questions at audience
**let them ask questions
**ask feedback
*demonstrate the physical skills of a presenter
**using slides etc

You also need to appear happy to be there and pleased to do the presentation.
===audience concentration===
3 external factors that affect concentration
#size of venue and audience
##a large audience will have the back of the room losing focus early
#how equipped the venue is
##introducing variety through AV good
##too hot people get sleepy
##too cold people fidget
##hard seats are distracting
##soft seats are sleep inducing
#the time of day
##later in the day is harder to concentrate
##immediately before lunch is distracting due to hunger
##after lunch people are lethargic

In favourable conditions people can concentrate for 15 minutes.  You may be able to hold them for 30 minutes but 45 minutes is all but impossible.
==Preparing your presentation==
How you prepare is more important than how long you prepare.

Good preparation is not perfecting the speech nor covering the whole topic
#keep audience in mind
#cover realistic amount of content

Steps to preparing:
#identify the objective of the presentation.
##ensure you are thinking of the audience rather than the subject
#select the content
##brainstorming
###jump from idea to idea without constraint on groupings
##linear list
###first main point and then all of its sub-points.
###then next main point and sub-points
#organize the content

===Preparing the content===
Once you have the objectives set, make notes on what you want to present.  Ensure that by covering the material these will work towards the objectives.
*choose content quickly
*create an outline
**main points and then sub points
**single words and phrases not sentences


===Organzing your presentation notes===
Put content in logical order
*chronological
Main points grouped, 
*follow logical order
*chronological
support main points by sub points.
*these are secondary content.
*include only limited content at start

#Start at general level.  
#Then examples.  
#Then audience specific data/example.
##case studies.  But don't need a lot of similar examples.

Organize your notes in the right way helps.  The wrong way can be disasterous.

Don't try to memorize your presentation as you are likely to forget something.  Instead create notes:
*full speech (seldom)(tempting if you have high anxiety but a bad idea)
**can sound flat
**when legal liability is an issue and exact wording required, read a speech
*main headings with notes (most frequent)
**important or complicated
**more useful when not as familiar with content
*main headings only (occasional)
**frequent presentation
**less complicated content
**very familiar with content

==Control yourself and your environment==
Anxiety is normal.  A little bit may help your thought process but a lot will hinder you.


===Reasons for Anxiety===
*lack of experience
*Lots riding on talk
*the centre of attention.  On your own at the front.
Irrational concerns
*They won't like me
*I'll run out of materiel
*I'll forget

The audience wants you to succeed.  You'll have too much to say, and with notes you can't forget anything.
===Controlling Anxiety===
Once you identify your fears you can conquer them.

Presentation anxiety is most common on the morning of the presentation, and the first few minutes before the presentation.

Presentation anxiety manifests in 
*increased heart rate and 
*rapid/shallow breathing
*unable to control thoughts

Routine tasks takes your mind off the work.  Don't do stressful work beforehand.

Exercise

Relax!

Control your breathing
*breath deep and slow

Think about relaxing places.  don't think about anything that will generate anxiety


===Rehearse the presentation===
Once you have prepared, you need to rehearse so that you can deliver the presentation the way you want to.

#The rehearsal should be as close to real as possible.  Simulate the real presentation conditions
##rehearse using slides and equipement
##try to rehearse in a similarly sized space
#Rehearse several times
##helps get the timing right
#Rehearse immediately after finishing the preparation
#rehearse more demanding parts more often (partial rehearsal)
#rehearse movements and gestures
#rehearse in front of an audience
##get colleague feedback
===The presentation environment===
Success depends on more than preperation.  You also need to control the environment.
#arrangement of seating
##Horeshoe allows small audience to see each other
##classroom style focuses on presenter
#position of equipment
##offset equipment makes it easier to see presenter
##for a demo the gear needs to be centered so everyone can  see.
#shape and size of room


Show up early so that you can confirm the environment.
