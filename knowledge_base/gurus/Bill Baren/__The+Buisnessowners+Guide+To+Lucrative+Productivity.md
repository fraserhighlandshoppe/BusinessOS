Productivity
Principle #1
Clean The Lint Trap
So You Can Focus
On Key Priorities

When our mind is holding too many things to do at once, we get
overwhelmed and it’s hard to figure out what’s important and what’s not. It
can even prevent us from getting ANYTHING done.
When you go to dry your clothes, there’s a lint trap in the dryer you have
to clean out. When the lint trap is completely full, your clothes don’t get dry
- and I’ve heard this can even cause a fire!
We have something like a lint trap in our own minds.
A full lint trap prevents you from being able to really decide what’s
important. And that leaves you unable to accomplish any task!
So before you attempt any other time management or productivity
strategies, it becomes critical to first clean the lint trap in your head so you
can actually see your priorities clearly.

PRODUCTIVITY STRATEGY:
Mind Dump
I recommend doing a “Mind Dump” with a coach or in a journal - the idea is to
take all of the to-dos and ideas you have in your mind, and capture them in
writing.
Keeping all the things you need to do in your head can be overwhelming but
when you get them onto paper you can actually work with them. For example,
you can put them in priority order and schedule them in your calendar.
Then you can ask yourself - what are the 3-5 biggest priorities in your
business? Which of these to-dos are going to help you accomplish your
priorities?
Write down everything you can think of that you need to do, get it all out, and
then when it’s downloaded outside your head it relieves the overwhelm or lack
of clarity in your mind.

Productivity
Principle #2
Break Your
Procrastination
Habit

As business owners, one of our biggest enemies is procrastination. We
might have 1 or 2 things that are a high priority for our business but we
never get to them until it’s too late because we procrastinate.
Procrastination occurs when we fear failure, uncertainty, or rejection.
Here are 2 of my favorite techniques for breaking out of the cycle of
procrastination and into action.

PRODUCTIVITY STRATEGY:
The Best Case/Worst Case Scenario
Think about a task you’re procrastinating on.
Then ask yourself, “what’s the best thing that can happen?” Spend 5
minutes in that space. Then spend 5 minutes thinking about “what’s the
worst thing that can happen?”
If you can become fully okay with both the best-case and worse-case
scenario of a given task, then you will realize that the fear has subsided
and you’re less afraid of what could happen. You’ll be ready to deal with
any of the emotions and situations that come up when taking on the task in
question.
Try this technique with a daunting task you’re procrastinating on, and you’ll
be surprised at how it takes away some of the procrastination energy!

Productivity
Principle #4
Take Control Of
Your Email Inbox

I’ve read that business people waste an average of one month a year on
email. Wow! By using simple email practices every day, you can save a
ton of time.
If you reduce your email time by just 30 minutes a day you will free up
more than 3 weeks in a year!

PRODUCTIVITY STRATEGY:
Don’t “Check” your Email.
“Handle” your Email.
This one is my #1 favorite time saver - it’s reducing time spent on email.
Often we “check” email several times an hour - but we don’t actually reply
or do anything with the email. This is time wasted! It distracts us and
takes us away from productive work.
Instead, schedule a block of time (30-45 minutes) on your calendar when
you can efficiently “handle” your email.
Once you open an e-mail, either answer it, file it or delete it. If you look at
an email multiple times, that’s wasting time.

PRODUCTIVITY STRATEGY:
How to quickly process (“handle”)
your email inbox
Define a number: Set a maximum number of emails to keep in your inbox.
For example, I like to have “Inbox Zero” (no emails in my inbox).
Messages that take two minutes or less to attend to: do them at once.
Messages that require a greater time commitment: Schedule a specific
time to attend to them: Drag and drop to attach email to your calendar or to-do
list for a specific time. Or assign them to someone else.
Delete aggressively: Sort your inbox in reverse order, from oldest to newest
and review for old messages to quickly delete. You can also sort by sender,
reply to all messages from that sender in one email, then delete them.
Track how much time you’re spending on email and experiment with
cutting that down to half if possible by using these strategies.

Principle #5
The Marketer vs.
Practitioner

Running a business requires spending time in two distinctly different ways:
meeting with clients (“Practitioner” time) and working on marketing the
business to generate revenue (“Marketing” time)!
These 2 types of activity require really different kinds of energy and focus.
When you know the secret to balancing these two very different demands
on your time, you hold the key to getting it all done with more ease and
flow.

PRODUCTIVITY STRATEGY:
Batch Your Time For Types Of Work
So what I recommend you do is arrange your calendar into blocks of
“Marketer time” and “Practitioner time.”
The Marketer schedule is often broken down into 3-4 hour chunks rather
than the one hour slots of the Practitioner schedule.

For example, you can create a “practitioner day” (or client day) in your
schedule, and that entire day is filled with one-hour client sessions or slots
when you work with clients.
Then on your marketing days, you’re spending time working on marketing
your business. To do this effectively, you need to be able to immerse
yourself without interruption so you can produce something really great to
help you get clients and grow your business.
Of course, when you’re in business for yourself you need BOTH Marketer
and Practitioner time. The solution? Make each day of the week a different
type of schedule.
For example:
Monday: “Practitioner” schedule (client sessions)
Tuesday: “Marketer” schedule (working on highest priority revenue
generating activities)
Wednesday: “Practitioner” schedule (client sessions)
Thursday: “Marketer” schedule (working on highest priority revenue
generating activities)
Friday: “Other Business Priorities”
This schedule lets you meet all your business needs and be immerse in
the kind of energy and focus required for the given day. This gives your
week an enjoyable, focused rhythm!
