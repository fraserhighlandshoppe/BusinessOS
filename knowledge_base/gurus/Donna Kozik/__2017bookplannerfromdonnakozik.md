Book Planner: Your Author-to-Be Guide

How to Use This Book Planner

S

mart want-to-be authors know that planning is the key to getting a book done. In fact,
if you ask anyone who has written and published a book, they will tell you that it didn’t
happen by doing a little bit here and a little bit there. It took concentrated effort after they
made it their goal (otherwise known as “a dream with a deadline”) to get a book done.
But putting the focused effort forth to write a book doesn’t come naturally to most people.
You have to not only have the vision of why you want to write a book, but you also have
to know what you need to do (and when) to understand how to put together the content,
design the interior and cover and choose your publishing method. Or, better yet, who you
can hire to help you with the technical aspects so you can focus on the more important
items.
Whew! No wonder it’s so easy to put this off. The “I’ll do it when I have time” excuse
seems like a good one—there’s too much to learn and too few hours in the day to get your
arms around it. Unfortunately, those kind of rationalizations won’t get a book done, and
there really are some good reasons to call yourself “author.”
So let’s get planning and get your book done sooner rather than later! Simply print out
and begin with this planner and you’ll be on your way.

Create Your Vision
There are many reasons why NOW is the time to get your book done:
1. Authority. Having a book is the universal symbol credibility of respect.
2. Physical proof. A book is the ultimate “show me” piece of your expertise.
3. Sign more clients. A book serves as a springboard for webinars, speaking
presentations and one-on-one conversations so you can get in front of your
prospects and convert them into paying customers.
4. Get paid more. Authors can demand more money for their consulting,
products and services.
5. Signature system. Use your book to create a process or system that puts
you on the map in your field.
6. Speaking. Your book serves as the ultimate endorsement for associations
and conferences organizers who are looking for credible speakers.
7. Put it in words. Boost your confidence in describing the “who, what, where,
why and how” you help people.

Your Next 3 Months:
Instructions
Here are several pages to get you focused and started. Add pages as needed, including
notes for your book, promotion ideas, and even follow-up books you may want to write.

Here are my best “stay on track” tips:
• Print these pages (there aren’t many) and put them in a binder, which we
will now call your “Book Binder.” 
• Put your list of designed tasks on the next page and then transfer them to
the calendar pages.
• Work the way you know best. If you’re a morning person, designate
morning as your writing time. Up with the night owls? Give yourself an
hour or so before bed to work on your book project.
• Add “Power of 5” tasks. Make a list of things you can do in 5 minutes
and keep it near-by. Have 5 minutes before a call or leaving for an
appointment? Pick an item from the list and complete it.
• See yourself at your desk (or wherever your primary writing spot is)
actually doing the work. Start with 10-second visualizations and build up
to 30-seconds or more.
• Keep the main thing the main thing. Stay focused on this project for a
minimum of 90 days before switching to another project.
