==Is now the time to add, or remove, risk to your investment strategy?==

Real estate investing is considered by many to be a risky game by some investors who don’t think strategically. They believe that luck plays a major role where with one roll of the dice they can win big, or lose it all. This is both true and utter nonsense. True, because if you are NOT a strategic investor you are just rolling the dice and hoping for the best. However, if you ARE a strategic investor, you look at the world from a different perspective: you see elements that others don’t; you see opportunities where others can’t; you see dangers lurking where others see blue skies. And with each new perspective, you make adjustments to your system to position yourself for each changing element.

You can, simply by being strategic, dramatically reduce your chance of landing on the financial snake while increasing your chance of climbing a property ladder and winning the financial game of life.

Although this is fact, and the steps to reducing risk are clear and simple recently, I have witnessed an increasing trend amongst investors to take the chance on a ‘roll of the dice’ despite the obvious increased risk it brings.

===Why the trend?===

It could be that strategic real estate investing isn’t exciting enough, it could be that strategic investing creates long term sustainable wealth rather than big hits of endorphin-creating cash, it could be that strategic investing takes thought and analysis, or it could be that people are trying to make up for losses they have created in other investments. Whatever the reason, there are a number of people out there calling themselves investors but who are really just gamblers.

===There is a time to add risk to your strategy===

Gambling with added risk can work in your favour, that's true. As a matter of fact, there are certain points in the real estate cycle that it is recommended to add a little more risk to your investment strategy. Here it is: when all the underlying economic and market fundamentals are lining up in advance of a big upward move, strategic investors like myself step up our acquisitions by loosening the property filter to allow few more in.

This can work very well in hot markets that are very clearly moving to the next upward phase of demand. In fact, we use this strategy to make a lot of money in a fairly short period of time. But these opportunities, when all the signs line up clearly indicating a positive market direction, only come once in a very long while.

The Long Term Sustainable Wealth strategy, the system that sophisticated investors follow, is a dynamic strategy that allows us to adapt to market signals, take advantage of market direction and protect our downside before markets turn. However, the keystone in this system is an investor who pays attention to the facts and figures. That is why REIN was developed and why our Members have been so successful over the last 20 years. We have a clear and unwavering focus on watching the fundamentals like a hawk for our Members, shifting strategies to match market cycle positions and protecting downside while increasing upside potential.

==The world is filled with game changers, the good and bad==
 https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/article/20140821133628-5220577-game-changer-1-persistent-and-painful-unemployment-coupled-with-giant-job-growth?trk=mp-reader-card

Whether we like it or not, in every investment vehicle, positive and negative game changers exist. Some give you an advantage while others take money and opportunity away from you. Some are predictable while others are not. The fact is change is inevitable. The Strategic Investor takes steps to minimize the down side while taking advantage of any upside the changes provide.

World economies today are sending out mixed signals, more so now than in any other time in history. These mixed signals are making it increasingly more difficult for investors to make prudent, long-term real estate decisions as it seems that the rules of the game are in constant flux. First, let’s look at a few of the more relevant game changers that should be on a strategic investor’s radar, then we’ll discuss how to play them strategically:

===Persistent and Painful Unemployment Coupled With Giant Job Growth===

The foundation of ALL sustainable real estate markets is job growth. Unfortunately, persistent and painful unemployment is being experienced in Europe, the US and certain parts of Canada. The negative effect will continue to be felt in those real estate markets.

Even if a market seems to be performing well, despite higher than average unemployment, it will inevitably correct to match the underlying economic fundamental foundation. Without jobs, a market is built on sand and one economic tremor will send it crashing to the ground.

As far as game changers go this is definitely the one with the strongest impact. You can have interest rates increasing, a double dip recession in the US and a Black Swan event (like a Civil War in the Middle East or massive protests in Asia) all occurring at once, but if you are investing in a region where jobs continue to grow or stay stable your market will be negatively affected the least and will recover first. That means, by focusing on regions with persistent job growth you are investing both defensively and offensively.

A strategic investor will be watching regional job growth numbers like a hawk. The key word in that sentence is regional. As Canada’s economy continues to be a tale of three regions, each with its own economic story, investors must pay close attention to the industries that are driving the job market in their target areas.

For instance, a real positive game changer will be occurring in the Halifax region over the coming decade with the implementation of the major ship building contract and all of the long term jobs it will be creating. Negative game changers could occur in export manufacturing regions that rely upon a weak Canadian dollar coupled with a recovery in the US and Europe for product demand. All three of these factors will remain out of reach for a few years to come. Alberta and Saskatchewan’s amazing job creation machine is back in full swing, with unemployment already dropping to ultra lows despite a population that is growing in leaps and bounds. Although the market is commodity based (and therefore subject to larger swings) the jobs that are being created now are in projects that have multi-year timelines. This means, even as commodity prices and demand shift, many of these jobs will stay in place. The additional bonus to these types of commodity based jobs: the majority of the workers are coming from outside the province and therefore are more pre-determined to stay renters (rather than property buyers) which will drive vacancy rates down and rents upward: in a province that does not currently have rental increase controls.

The disparity in job creation numbers in the US and Canada means that strategic investors have to ensure they’re doing that extra level of homework. They can no longer just look at real estate numbers, cheap property no longer means it is a good investment. Strategic investors know that price is not the determinant of a good or bad investment: the true determinant is long-term sustainable job growth. That is why they will gladly pay more for a townhouse in a market with job growth than similar one located in a city full of foreclosures and unemployment.

Right now the European turmoil is keeping a cap on growth and confidence in many regions around the world. As Canada’s second largest trading partner, the European double dip recession is negatively impacting our GDP growth in key regions of the country. This, in turn, slows the growth of our job markets in those regions. Not just banks but full countries still needing bailout funds is acerbated by the Euro countries inability to create consensus between austerity and stimulus will keep this story on the front pages for years to come.

This will bring a 2nd unclear the future of Europe’s economy becomes, the more media coverage there will be. No jobs = no sustainable real estate market.

On the other side of the coin, there are a few positives that come out of the European turmoil. The high unemployment in Europe is helping to fill jobs here in Canada, helping our economy grow, especially with the updates to the immigration policy of attracting skilled workers to fill our job (and rental) vacancies.

The turmoil is also making Canada look even more like a safe haven for investors from around the world. Never before have I seen so many very large international investors and investment firms turn their focus on our real estate and financial markets. This is manifesting with our government bonds being bought (keeping our interest rates low), our dollar being bought (especially by emerging market governments), our resource companies being bought (supporting job growth, yet stripping some of the profits out of Canada) and our residential and commercial real estate being bought up by major wealth funds and investors (keeping upward pressure on demand in key cities).

The European story will not go away soon, and the path to the solution will not be a pretty or smooth one. This will continue to bring fear and a lot of volatility into the financial markets. Strategic investors

Understand this fact and are making sure that they are not investing based on short term gains, nor are they putting their money in markets that are being propped up only by foreign inflows of cash. Foreign capital is fluid and moves more quickly than domestic funds – which means the money can leave almost as fast as it came in.

Like the Toronto and Vancouver markets, this is what the US real estate market is feeling right now. This is fine in cities that have low unemployment and are creating jobs (e.g. Houston) underlying issue to the forefront: deteriorating Consumer Confidence. The more however in many areas where purchases are being made, without the economic foundation you can see how those markets will become overheated.

And again, just like downtown Toronto and Vancouver markets, that capital will slow and stop if confidence begins to slip.

That is why it is now NOT the time to put money in markets that are overheated – Strategic Investment Capital must follow job and population growth not price. Then, when the economic waters begin to calm, the risks begin to dissipate THAT will be the time to jump into some of the ‘cheaper’ markets.

==Price Correction in Major Markets==

When major Canadian markets move, they create headlines. They are so big, that they also drive the nation’s 'average prices'. So, in the consumer’s minds, “As Toronto and Vancouver go, so goes the rest of the country.” In fact these two cities alone make up 54.1% of the National Bank Housing Price Index. That is why even the most seasoned investor can get caught in the emotions of these large markets.

Recent increases in average prices, such as those occurring in Toronto, fuelled a lot of confidence in real estate across the country even though the Toronto average price had nothing to do with the average price in Vancouver, Calgary or Halifax. It was all perception of the strength of real estate, and this perception led to more people buying property. The real game changer, though, will be when Toronto and Vancouver have average price drops in the same month. These two marketplaces make up such a large part of the Canadian average that it will push the country average down along with it, even as other cities continue to enjoy great real estate markets.

When these large markets start to slow or even slip negatively, the response will be strong and dramatic across the country. It will lead to headlines, like The End of the Real Estate Market is Here, which will turn perception of real estate strongly negative, even in markets that are enjoying record job growth and demand.

Over the next decade, the Strategic REIN Investor will need to be very focused on specific regions. They can't ignore the big picture of the two large headline-grabbing markets, but they will definitely keep them in perspective. Keeping out of the fray will be especially important as the commentary and headlines really ramp up on the ‘National Market Correction’ side.

==Interest Rates==
 https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/article/20140918190606-5220577-part-6-the-wildcards-of-canadian-real-estate-markets?trk=object-title

One of the real side effects of the economic turmoil in the world is the lack of incentive to raise our Bank of Canada rate. It is true that the Canadian economy has been performing very well when compared to many other countries in the developed world. In the past, this fact alone would have led to a strong interest rate increase by the Bank of Canada. However, today’s economic positioning is much more complex.

Our economy is directly affected by outside influences and as the world’s economies struggle to get a new foundation built it has an impact on the demand for what Canada produces (especially the 4 Fs: Food, Fuel, Fertilizer and Forest Products).

Here’s an important perspective to keep: even with our major customers in turmoil (US, Europe and now Asia) our economy has been growing strongly. Although we are not immune and there will still be economic bumps along the road, imagine the positive impact on Canada when even one of these markets get back on track.

If we raise interest rates much, it will bring in even more international investment dollars which will lead to our dollar soaring past the US dollar which will negatively affect the ability for our export focused provinces to create jobs. This, in turn, would increase the economic gap between the east and west of the country.

===Low Interest Rates Detrimental To Real Estate Market Realities===

These persistent record low interest rates are having a detrimental effect on the real estate market, literally changing the underlying foundation. By detrimental I mean pushing values in key cities higher than they should be based on the underlying economic fundamentals. It also decreases the number of long term renters in the market due to the affordability of monthly mortgage payments. The game changer will be when the low rate trend reverses and begins to move upward.

The more turmoil there is in the world, the less of a chance Canadian interest rates will move in any direction with any velocity over the coming 18 months. Despite that, we are suggesting that strategic investors continue to stress test their portfolios and any new purchase by analyzing them with an interest rate of a minimum of 1% higher than today’s rates.

Interest rate perception is more powerful than a slow interest rate change. Consumers have a very short memory when it comes to interest rates; it was only a couple of years ago that bank rate was 3% higher than it is today. That is why, once the rates begin upward, it sparks a frenzy of activity both in the real estate market as well as the mortgage re-finance market. This initial frenzy dies down after a few months of increased activity.

Although overall mortgage costs could increase for those not locked in, interest rate increases from today’s record lows would have a positive impact on long-term real estate investors as renters would feel less inclined to move towards buying a home which would lead to longer tenancies and less turn-over costs.

===Strategic Factors===
Strategic means having the game changers work for you, not against you. You can’t stop them, but you can be nimble enough to use them to your advantage. There are Five Essential Elements that all Strategic Real Estate Investors will need in the coming decade. Let’s take a closer look at each level of the strategic investor pyramid:

====Mindset====
Mindset: Hoping and guessing is now thrown out the window. A strategic mindset will set the successful investor apart from the unsuccessful one. A strategic and aware mindset is the foundation for understanding the difference between a real game changing event versus a storm in a tea cup. This ability to weigh each event and to extrapolate how it will really affect them will allow the strategic investors to stay well ahead of both positive and negative trends and set them apart from the rest of the pack.

====Real Estate Cycle====
Real Estate Cycle: Even as game changers affect the market, strategic investors know that an underlying real estate cycle is also occurring. If they don’t, investment decisions could be based on market influencers rather than market drivers, which could be a dangerous oversight. Those who invest and analyze based on influencers and game changers often end up surrounded by chaos, emotions and a tough financial state. Those who invest based on the drivers are the investors who always come out ahead over the long haul.

====Goals====
Goals: Strategic investors are fully committed to their ultimate outcome, not the monthly gyrations of a market. They set forth a set of realistic financial goals, taking into account the phases of the real estate cycle, their target market’s underlying economics and how much time and capital they realistically have to invest. They don’t allow market dips or booms to shake them off of their ultimate outcome, they just adjust.

====Strategy====
Strategy: The strategy is the master plan from which a strategic investor works. The strategies they choose provide them the tools to deal with game changing events as well as allow them to pounce on opportunities these events present. Strategic investors use only specific strategies to create and sustain wealth throughout the inevitable cycles of the real estate market. They analyze key drivers and adjust their tactics to account for market influencers and game changers.

====Tactics====
Tactics: The strategic investor does not confuse tactics and strategy. Tactics are the steps they take within their master plan to take advantage of market shifts and market conditions. Tactics are adjusted to match where the market is in the real estate cycle. Strategic investors don’t adjust their goals or overall strategy when markets change, they change their tactics to ensure they are playing both offence and defence within their portfolio.

A final game changer that I didn’t mention above, but should be kept in the top of mind for all investors, is Self-Fulfilling Prophecy. No matter how well armed you are with information, or how strongly you have mastered tactics and strategies to adjust to the other external game changers, the one game changer you can control is yourself. If you believe you can’t find deals, you won’t; If you believe that you’ll make a major mistake, you will. You must control what you can control and respond to the changes in the market you can’t control.

Often times when the goal is weak, or monetary based, potential negative game changers will be seen everywhere you look.
