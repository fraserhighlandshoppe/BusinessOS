==10 Mistakes that Kill your deals==
 Jeff Sarault
When salespeople spend too much time on the offer, the product, or value add, they can blow a deal. This is often due to a salesperson:
*Lacking understanding of the buyer's needs
*Hesitation in pushing the buyer beyond their comfort level.
Other times, salespeople can lose a deal when they focus on themselves rather than the customer.

This can happen when salespeople get too caught up in their own interests and objectives, or when they try to act like a know-it-all.

If you want to avoid making the same mistakes that kill your deals, make sure you know how to get out of your own way.

Inside this guide - discover the 10 Mistakes That Kill Your Deals - that I learned from Grant Cardone!

===Not selling the Solution===
*People and companies buy things only in an attempt to solve a problem.
*There is NO exception to this rule.
*Focus on ensuring the customer that you can solve the problem.
*You might ask, “If I could solve two or three recurring issues in your business, what would they be?”
*Then explain how your product and the company can solve the most important and maybe the other two or three problems.
*People close when they are hopefully confident their problems can be solved.

===Missing Buying Signals===
*Remind yourself to be present during your presentation.
*The customer’s vital buying signs are more important than your presentation. Don’t neglect buying signals during your presentation.
*Have a great presentation prepared but pay attention to what is important – the signs from everyone in the room who is a decision maker or an influencer.
*Ask a lot of questions and get involvement, and always look for opportunities to close early

===Refuse to Ask Hard Questions===
*Don’t miss opportunities to build trust: Ask hard questions.
*Real closers have real communication with their clients.
*Make sure you get your questions answered.
*If you don’t get the answers, you won’t close the deals.
*Some hard questions you should ask include:
**How do you feel about our pricing?”
**How do you feel about our terms?"
**Why would you do business with me when you have done it with our competitor for so long?

===Believing Price Will Solve Problems===
*Your buyer might be obsessed with price. But no one buys a price, ever!
*Despite all this energy your customer invests in trying to sell you on the importance of price —it’s ridiculous.
*Every buyer will pay a higher price.
*Customers will always spend that money on something else.
*To close the price-obsessed buyer, it takes commitment and knowledge of advanced closes

===Presenting Without the Intention to Close===
*Make it clear to the prospect that your intention is to have the product or service being used by the client this week.
**“Thanks for your time today, my goal is to have my product to your company by the end of this week.”
*If the customer shows unamused, a great response is “I understand. I just wanted you to know my intention.”
*You have to present with confidence, not arrogance, and set the stage early showing that you know your product can solve their problems.
*Open with your intention to close the deal. Be transparent and obvious about what it is you hope to accomplish.

===Not Asking For the Close Early Enough===
*Take your presentation and break it into smaller pieces. At the end of each stage ask, “Have you seen enough information to make a decision?”
*If buyer says, “No, I haven’t,” continue with the presentation.
*If the buyer says, “Yes, I have seen enough, can you do anything else on the numbers?”, continue with the close.
*Close often, close early and when they say they have seen enough, shut it down.

===Price Mystery===
*Most of us were taught to build value, then show the price. This results in the buyer becoming obsessed with the price.
*By revealing the price early, the customer is able to evaluate and think with the price, as you explain what your product or service will do.
*“Before I demonstrate our product ability to solve your problems, I want to show you our pricing so you can think with it.”
*Saving price for later is a rookie closer’s mistake.
*“That is too high” is usual. You can simply agree with the customer. “Of course it’s too much, you haven’t even seen what it will do. Allow me to show you why it’s priced this way, what it will do for you, and why it is the best value in the marketplace.

===Ignoring Influencers===
*Put attention on the decision maker, but don’t ignore the influencers.
*Take the time to ask each one, “What problem are you most interested in solving?”
*Find out why they are important to the decision and what is most important to them.
*Throughout the presentation, ask influencers for input, objections, and concerns.
*Don’t ignore anyone, they will kill your deal.
*Get the names and positions of each of the influencers. Before your presentation, ask:
**“Who else, other than yourself, will influence your decision?
**“Who else would you like involved?”

===Using a Free Trial to Close a Deal===
*Free trials are good to drive traffic but never for closing the deal.
*Free trials result in you investing more time.
*Free trials almost never work, and they create cash flow problems for your company—more outflow than inflow.
*If you can’t close the customer, find out and go get another customer.
*If you can, then take it all the way and close by getting what you deserve in exchange.
*Free will break you, your company, and most often results in a poor standard of customer.

===No Urgency===
*Too many salespeople and sales organizations never insist on closing a deal.
*If you truly believe in your company, product, and service, you must learn how to insist on closing the transaction now.
*The Now or Later Close is great for putting your deal back into play and getting it done now rather than a few days from now.
*Urgency could very well be the difference between a deal or not.
*Practice urgency and become unreasonable.
*Your sales team should train and drill on how to use pressure –it’s an art.
