WHEN NOT TO DO A RENT-TO-OWN
DEAL
There are 3 situations
when it makes more
sense NOT to put together a rent to own deal. In these situations it
is better for you to avoid the deal altogether.
These are when the property is in a poor market, when the property is a poor choice, and
finally with poor rent to own candidates.
A Poor Market is when the local market is flat
or declining.
A Poor Property Choice includes homes with
weird layouts, handyman specials, mobile
homes and rural properties.
For this article, I'm gong to focus on Poor R2O
Candidates. Most R2O Candidates are not the
type to have an A+ credit score. If they were,
they wouldn't need our services, as they could
go to the bank and simply get a mortgage to
buy their own home. We have to be choosy
when selecting deserving people who can afford a home, and who really want a home, but
cannot qualify for a mortgage right now with
traditional lenders.
Here are some of the categories of people you
should avoid working with at all costs.
"Unrealistic Expectations" - These are folks
who think they should be driving a Porsche but
can only afford a Sunfire. An example of this
would be someone who earns $40,000 a year
and who wants to buy a $750,000 home. The
numbers don't work, and they never will work.
If after explaining what they can truly afford
with their income, and they are still stuck on
getting a house that is out of their league, it's
time to cut them loose.
"Money Menaces" - These are people who
have been very foolish with their finances and
credit in the past and show no inclination to
change. This could be someone who has gone
through multiple bankruptcies, and those who
have really bad credit without any sign of im-

When Do You Make the Offer?
I am currently working with a buying client who is
looking to increase his portfolio of 3 condos to include some small multi-units like a duplex, tri-plex
or four-plex. This move to small multi-unit properties is well within his context and he has been
looking at many properties; however, he has been
unable to find just the right property until just recently. I watch the MLS regularly for new listings
and one day an eleven unit apartment building
came up for sale. I knew the property and even
though the asking price was on the high end I
thought this would be perfect for my client.
I sent the listing to my client on a Thursday and
his initial comment was it was too big and he
didn`t have enough cash for the down payment. I
never push a client to buy something unless I feel
it is a good fit and this property was definitely a
great fit for him. He said he would think about it.
Friday evening he let me know that he was now
interested but he needs to figure out how to come
up with the cash.
Monday evening comes and he says he is ready
to make an offer so I draft up the paperwork and
submit an offer with a 24-hour irrevocable which
means the Seller has 24 hours to accept the offer
and or respond, failing which the offer becomes
null and void. Then I get a call that all real estate
agents and buyers hate - the listing agent calling
to say there is another offer. Not only was there
another offer but there were actually 4 offers in
total and despite us improving our offer with a
higher offer price and larger deposit we still lost
out on the deal.
I thought it would be a good exercise to share with
you so you can decide when to make an offer and
how you can increase the odds of having your offer accepted. This month I will talk about getting
the offer accepted and next month I will discuss
the negotiating process and how to get the price
you want.
The biggest mistake I see most investors make
(new and experienced) is wanting to view the
property and do the financial research about the
property before they even have it under contract.
There are many investors out there looking for

properties similar to the
one mentioned above and
if the property is decent
and priced well then it will
sell very quickly
(competition for good deals
is very high). I knew this property would sell
quickly and this is why I encouraged my client to
act quickly. Now in his mind making an offer
within 4 or 5 days without even seeing the property was quick. My client now understands that
he should have made the offer immediately to
get it tied up at whatever price ASAP and avoid
the chance of multiple offers. Making an offer
sight unseen is a very big step for many investors to accept; however, if written properly you
can simply have a clause inserted stating that
the offer is subject to a satisfactory viewing by
the buyer within 3 days of acceptance of the offer. If, upon viewing, the buyer doesn`t like the
property then the deal is dead and there is no
risk to the buyer. The upside is that the buyer is
able to prevent other potential buyers from getting the property before him.
Now one of the biggest responses I hear from a
client is, “Okay I understand about getting it under contract asap without seeing it, but what if I
don`t want to pay his asking price?”
Let`s assume there is a property listed for
$699,000 and you believe it is really only worth
$599,000, and the maximum amount you will
pay is $600,000. That is 14% below the asking
price and the chances of making an offer for
$600,000 that will be accepted are slim to none.

Avoid These Goal Setting Mistakes
You have probably been setting goals
for years. But if you are like most people, you are not being as successful
as you would like at achieving the
goals you set. Why is this? Here are
five of the biggest goal-setting mistakes I see my clients make.
Mistake #5: Thinking you need to know where you
are going. Most success gurus, speakers and authors
insist that it is crucial for you to be crystal clear about
your goals. Yet I’ve found that at least 70% of people
don’t know exactly what they want. Unfortunately they
believe the gurus are right so they beat themselves up
for not being clear and waste time trying to get clear
about where they are going. Both activities are damaging to their success.
Solution: You may not know exactly where you are going but you definitely have a sense of direction. Simply
connect with your sense of what you want and do something, anything that will move you in that general direction!
Mistake #4: Setting long-term goals based on what
seems reasonable rather than what inspires you. Do
you set your goals by looking at where you are today
and deciding what seems feasible for you? This is an
extremely limiting approach.
Solution: Ten years is a long time. You can create almost anything in that time if you decide to. You could
earn a degree, change careers or be well-established on
a different continent if you wanted. So expand your
dreams! When you commit fully to a goal, not matter
how unreasonable it may seem today, it is almost certainly achievable.
Mistake #3: Focusing on your goal instead of the
actions required to achieve it. Many advocates of
‘goal manifestation’ emphasize the importance of visualizing and getting into the right ‘vibrational frequency’ in
order to achieve your goals. In my experience focusing
on your goals excessively is self-indulgent and leads to
failure and disappointment.

Solution: Once you know what your goal is, create a

Mistake #2: Trying to have it all. We are constantly
being told, ‘you can have it all’ however in my experience that is not true. The reason to set goals in the
first place is because committing to a specific result
creates tension between where you are now and what
you want to achieve. It is the tension that pulls you
forward, providing motivation to take action. Achieving
any significant goal requires an intense and sustained
focus. If you set too many goals, the tension and your
focus get spread between multiple priorities which, in
many cases, are in conflict with each other.
Solution: Identify the one specific goal that is most
important to you and put all of your attention and effort
onto it.
Mistake #1: Lack of desire. I can virtually guarantee
that the number one reason you are not achieving
your goals is that you are not deeply committed to
them. Sure it would be nice to achieve them but you
are complacent and not willing to do what it takes; you
aren’t willing to get uncomfortable. In ‘Think and Grow

at any task you must have a burning desire! Achieving
your goals will require you to get way out of your comfort zone; it requires far more action and discomfort
than you have taken so far.

within a few short years. Remember the great Goethe
quote: “At the moment one definitely commits oneself,

Solution: When you figure out what is REALLY important to you and resolve to do whatever it takes to
have it, it will be yours.

Out With The Old & In With The New
GenX and Millennials are entering the rental
market and this demographic is having a profound impact on the rental industry. Owners and
landlords have shifted and are still shifting on
how they used to do business because the old
way isn’t working.
Most of the rental apartments in Canada were
built in the 1970’s to early 1980’s because there
was a nationwide incentive in place for builders.
When the government eliminated the incentive,
builders stopped building apartments. That’s
why a lot of rental apartments are old and getting older.
Fifteen years ago you could find all sorts of 8
Cap to 10 Cap buildings for sale. These are non
-existent today. It’s a 4 Cap market going as low
as 2 Cap or lower in high demand city centers.
This means that what used to take 10 years to
recoup your investment is now double or triple
that if you’re a cash flow investor. In order to recoup the investment faster, investors are choosing to flip or condo convert rental apartments.
This is why the rental inventory keeps getting
lower. Some cities have put a moratorium on
condo conversions in order to keep rental apartments in their city. Otherwise, renters are forced
to seek out accommodations from private home
owners or investors.
In our experience with the new tenant demographic, they see old and they don’t rent. They
grew up with hardwood floors and granite countertops. The youth of today are not willing to sacrifice as their parents did. This wasn’t the case
four years ago. We experimented with the market and upgraded one unit with waterproof vinyl
plank flooring throughout, stainless steel appliances (fridge, stove, dishwasher), new countertops: not one renter would pay the extra $50 per
month in rent for the fancy unit. It was the last
one to get rented. Today’s renter is willing to pay
the premium for the upgraded unit. It’s an entirely different rental landscape today.
This trend means that the older rental buildings
will be less attractive to the new renter who desire modern and contemporary amenities suit-

able for their lifestyle
choices. This means
choosing from a different tenant pool, those
who are willing to
make sacrifices and those who have affordability
issues. Eventually the entire building demographic
will shift. There will be increased challenges with
higher operating costs as the building continues to
age. As new inventory is brought to market, this will
attract the tenants with the income to afford these
units.
A simple drive-by through the neighbourhood is
sufficient evidence of new construction. This gives
you a head start on making improvements today.
By the time the new inventory comes to market,
you’ve had the time to upgrade your older suites.
The one significant benefit of older apartments is
that the footprint is larger. Newer apartments are
smaller to keep the construction costs down.
Investors and builders can now build an apartment
specifically geared for renters. The number one
amenity sought after by GenX and Millennials is
free wifi next to in-suite laundry. The older generation didn’t mind so much to do their laundry in a
shared facility but today’s renter doesn’t want other
people touching their clothes. It’s not possible to
add a washer and dryer into an older building. The
washing machine needs to be insulated from vibration during the spin cycle and plumbing needs to be
added to drain the water. Think of the consequences if a washer broke down and leaked an entire load. The dryer needs to be properly vented. A
warm, damp environment is a recipe for mold and
mildew.

Where are rates headed and should I take a fixed

keep rates low. Again this month they have kept

of what might be a few things really. The first item
is the cost of funds banks use for mortgages. Investors who place large amounts of money with
banks would like to see a bit of a premium on their
return quoting things like “risk”. For example. “I
need to make a bit higher yield on my money” just
in case something happens. Recently there was a
small spike in this area, but it has since come
back down. The other factor you will hear is that if
my money has to be tied up for 5 years where I
can’t touch it, then I expect to be paid a premium
on that money. We see some of that creeping into
the market. Lastly Mortgage Bonds have become
slightly more expensive these days due to the
OSFI recent announcement that banks need to
have more cash on hand per dollar that is lent out
via mortgages. Less leverage means less profitability, and that means slightly higher rates to
make up for that “cost”.

Giving My Bad Habits a Temporary “Time Out”
For the entire month of January I gave myself a
‘time out’ from drinking any alcohol what-soever.
It wasn’t as difficult as I thought it might be, in fact,
it was quite easy. There were only 2 times I had
the slightest urge to go to the pub and have a beer
and some appies (and I think it was more for the
appies than the beer).
But let me start at the beginning and why I chose
to take a month off from the “demon rum” (and why
it is only a month off and not permanent).
I spent 3 weeks in Mexico over Christmas, and I
was definitely in ‘holiday’ mode. I drank regularly,
and I drank a lot more than I usually do… not so
much that I was ‘drunk’ per se, but I probably averaged 4-5 beers a day (compared to
that amount per week normally).

a month off of pop and energy
drinks. Awhile ago I started
drinking diet pop, usually one a
day (I know, I know - the aspartame will kill me. My kids remind

It was fun, but by the end of the trip,
I felt like I needed to detox. Coincidentally I heard on the radio that it is
becoming quite common for people
to take January off of drinking. So I
thought “why not?) and I decided to
give it a try.
And the not-so-whimsicle part is that
alcoholism runs in my family.
There may be a hereditary component and my father was a severe
alcoholic when he was younger. It
was only because he sobered up that I was even
born! Fortunately he was off the bottle for over 40
years before he passed away.
So I always have the niggling concern in the back
of my mind that I may follow in his footsteps
(although it would probably be pretty evident by
now if that were the case).

I will see how it goes, and if it’s
not a problem taking a month off,
I may (or may not) continue
drinking pop again in March.
Other ’habits’ (aka crutches) I
have on my “time out” list include: playing ’free cell’ and watching Netflix.
I like the idea of testing my mettle against my unproductive habits and showing myself that I’m in
control.
(And if I’m not - seeing it as a wake up call to get
some help. Is there rehab for diet pop addiction?)

Thankfully I didn’t feel the slightest physical ‘urge’
for booze, and I celebrated my 31 day booze diet
by… not having anything to drink. I just haven’t
felt like having one yet.

How about you? Is there anything you think you
should take a break from for a month or so? Go for
it! Give that unproductive habit a ‘time out’ - and
then you will know you are the one in charge of it,
and not the other way around.
