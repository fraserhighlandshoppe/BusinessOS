Million dollar business building ideas...
from a pizza joint? by Troy White
I am always on the lookout for unusual marketing ideas people are using to promote their
business. Many times I see these wacky marketing ideas offline, and they can easily be applied
online.
To me, the opportunity offline is great if done right.
There is very little competition (decent competition) in the physical mail space… now compare
that with your prospects email inbox! To you, this means the difference between 230 emails to
compete with… or 1 or 2 other envelopes in the mail box.
Yes, it costs more… but done like this example below… not much more (4 cents a household
with some companies). The key is in targeting the right markets, neighbourhoods,
demographics, etc.
Here’s how the pizza place did it…
Who'd have thought that a pizza take-out joint would have such savvy marketers?
I received a letter in the mail (not addressed to me personally, they had blanketed
neighborhoods near them - hint). On the front was a letter with a small bag of flour attached to
it with a staple.
The letter began... "Dear Neighbor, Why have I attached a little tiny bag of flour to this letter?
Here is why:" and the letter then goes on to talk about their 'secret flour' the chemical
structure of their flour, the high protein content, where they get their flour, and why they pay
more for their flour than the competition does.
Not hard to figure out where the idea came from! The old dollar bill mailer rings a bell, no?
They do an excellent job building their pizza dough and ingredients up, and play down the
inferior ingredients the competition uses, which explains why they have "limp, often soggy,
pizza crust".
They talk about how theirs is hand made daily, in-house, while others farm it out to
outside companies who mass produce a cheaper, lower quality dough that shows up ready to
bake.
Then they have a 100% guarantee: "if it's not the best you've ever eaten, we'll refund your
purchase price. In fact, when you stop by in the next two weeks, I'd like you to have our
signature Caesar salad with your dinner (my treat)"
Then they have a coupon at the bottom with the free Caesar salad and Focaccia bread.
This is brilliant. And why can’t YOU use the exact same idea?
You can... excuses will not be tolerated here.
You may not have a pizza business... but your business IS NO DIFFERENT.

5 Ways Entrepreneurs Can Fight the Competition

Competition is inevitable as an entrepreneur. Even if you face no opposition at the start,
competition will pop out of the woodwork, ready to challenge your market dominance. If
your idea is good, people will imitate and iterate it.
Competition is healthy for both the consumers and your product, as it forces you to develop and improve. At the
same time, you don't want to give them a chance to take the top spot from you. Here are a few ways you can fight
the competition and keep them at bay.
1. Switch Demographics
One of the first things you'll figure out as an entrepreneur is your target audience. This is whom you've designed
your product for. They'll determine how you iterate your offering and how your marketing campaign will go.
Unfortunately, the competition may want the same demographic. What's worse, they may actually have a product
that suits the demographic better.
Instead of slugging it out for that demographic, you can choose to switch demographics. Consider the advantages
and disadvantages of doing so. It will come at a cost, as this is essentially a pivot, but don't let the costs dissuade
you. It's a good source of new business, and you'll likely still keep a number of customers from your old target
market.
2. Improve Your Current Offering
When faced with intense competition, this is probably your go-to move as an entrepreneur. You've established
yourself in your target market and you don't want to give it up without a fight. One of the best ways to beat the
competition is to simply be objectively be better than them.
This is best used when the competition is intense but does not share your level of development or resources. You
can essentially bully them out of the race by offering something better. What counts as "better" depends on your
product and audience. Can you use better materials or offer faster service without changing your price? Doing
things like this isn't easy, but it's worth consideration.
3. Create a Loyalty Program
Customer loyalty is important to your success as an entrepreneur, and it's important to the company's survival when
the competition's knocking at the door. Their success, especially if you have market dominance, relies largely on
stealing customers away from you. Despite your dominance, it's not that difficult to do, especially if you haven't
worked on developing customer loyalty.
Loyalty and rewards programs are great for enticing
people to stay with your company. You can award
discounts for long-term customers. Make sure your
customers know of any programs you're offering.

4. Offer Alternate Pricing Structures
One of the best ways entrepreneurs can secure an advantage over the competition is to offer different pricing
structures. Cynics may think this means lowering your prices, but that's not necessarily the case, though it is an
option. Alternate pricing includes offering payment installment plans for big product or large orders, or new
minimum subscription plans for new consumers. This makes you more flexible as well as widens your market
reach.
5. Try a New Marketing Campaign
One of the biggest problems entrepreneurs face when the competition starts popping up is that the competition is
new. The mere fact that they're new can tempt customers into giving them the time of day, which may be all the
competition needs to encroach on your territory. When faced with that threat, you can opt to fight fire-with-fire and
try an experimental marketing campaign.
What part of it is experimental is actually unimportant. What matters is that it's new and different. It refreshes your
image and lets you stand out from the competition.
Competition is inevitable and if you're not ready to resist disruption and secure your advantage, you won't make it
as an entrepreneur. Fortunately, there are plenty of things you can do to make sure you don't lose ground. Take a
look at your pricing structures and give customers more options. Create a new marketing campaign to negate the
competition's newness advantages.
If you're out of ideas just think of it this way - to keep the competition at bay, all you need to do is to be a better
entrepreneur and business than the competition. Keep in mind that this will happen more than once and it will
happen constantly. There's always going to be someone looking to take your spot.
