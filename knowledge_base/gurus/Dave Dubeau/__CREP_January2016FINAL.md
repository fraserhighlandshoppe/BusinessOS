Mixed Use vs Commercial
I have many students ask me what is the difference between commercial residential and commercial non-residential properties because they
believe the word commercial makes all commercial properties the same with respect to financing.
When we look at purchasing and financing an
apartment building it is considered to be commercial by the financial lenders because it contains
more than 4 units therefore it is classified as
“commercial residential”. When you are looking at
a warehouse, storefront, office space, etc., and
there is no residential component then it is simply
referred to as “commercial”. Then there is something called mixed-use commercial where there is
residential and non-residential sharing a property,
such as a main-floor storefront with apartments on
the second floor.
We have talked about the benefits of financing
with commercial residential (apartment buildings)
in previous articles and I just want to remind you
that a commercial residential property may be financed up to 75% Loan-to-Value conventionally
over 25 years or up to 85% Loan to Value if you
use CMHC insured financing for up to 35 years.
When financing a commercial property or a mixeduse property, CMHC is no longer an option (with
the possible exception of a property with a small
amount of commercial mixed in with a large
amount of residential) and this means the loan to
value percentage will be lower than the 75-85%
you can get if it is just residential and the interest
rates will likely be much higher.
Here are some of the pros and cons with commercial non-residential laid out below:
Pros:


Commercial leases are much more favourable
for the Landlord. The Commercial Landlord
has many more rights than a Landlord for residential tenants.



Many, if not all, of the related expenses for the
property are usually paid by the commercial

tenant.


Renovations/updates
and maintenance are
often the responsibility of the tenant.



Rents per square foot are often much higher
than residential rents.



Typically more hands off for the Landlord.



When the economy is strong and vacancies
are low it can be very profitable.

Cons:


Having commercial and residential mixeduse will eliminate the option for CMHC financing.



Financing rates are higher.



Amortization of the mortgage or loan will be
shorter (15-25 years) therefore making your
monthly mortgage payments higher.



Often the property is unique which may
make it more difficult to rent.



During down economies a business may
close down leaving the Landlord stuck with
vacancies which may be hard to fill.



May require the Landlord to invest large
amounts of money to fix up a unit to make it
appealing for new tenants.
