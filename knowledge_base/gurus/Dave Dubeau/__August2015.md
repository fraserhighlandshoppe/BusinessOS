CONT’D - - WHAT DOES THIS HAVE TO DO WITH YOU?
3 Cs. The same 3 Cs I now abide by. The same 3 Cs that changed the direction my life was headed in .The
same 3 Cs that can help YOU change what you need to change. ~ ~ Clarify, Clean, Commit ~ ~
#1 - Clarify.
I had been stuck in a rut in many areas of my life and had NO CLUE where it was going. Didn't care wasn't watching. I was going about my day to day life without regard for the rest of my life. As soon as I
clarified my MAJOR PURPOSE in my life and business... it all started coming together. The day you get this
clarity right - TEARS should come to your eyes. Then you know you are on the right track! So I had clarity
on what I wanted and why I wanted it in my business, personal, and health.
#2 - Clean.
In order to make things now get on the RIGHT TRACK... I had to purge the old mental junk to make room
for the new mission. I purged. Crappy thoughts. Clutter. Destructive parts of my life were moved aside so I
could get to the productive parts I wanted in my life. Voila... cleaning + clarity = potential for massive
change. BUT... one thing was missing.
#3 - Commitment.
I have been in various types of martial arts for about 15 years now. In that time I have seen countless
number of people come in with great intentions to train... but 2 weeks later they disappear like a fart in the
wind. Same in business. In copywriting. In marketing la-la land. Big dreams squashed by teeny tiny
ambitions.
You have to be PIG-HEADED and willing to do WHATEVER it takes to make it happen. Serious
commitment to see your clarity and clean slate through to the grande architecture of design you want. I
created daily routines. AND STUCK TO THEM!
It worked. Fast forward 19 months and I have the health I wanted, the relationships I wanted, the business I
wanted. So if you are stuck or fed up with parts of your life...
~ ~ Clarify, Clean, Commit ~ ~ Try it. Crappy marketing results? ~ ~ Clarify, Clean, Commit ~ Unhealthy
lifestyle or relationships? ~ ~ Clarify,Clean,Commit ~ ~ You name the problem you face... ~ ~ Clarify,
Clean, Commit ~ ~
It's a simple formula that always works...
...as long as you keep the commit part locked in to your daily schedule... the results look after themselves!
Try it - you'll love the results!

Entrepreneurship: 5 Things to Do Before Making
Someone Your Partner
One of the things you'll have to strongly consider as an entrepreneur is finding a partner. A
partner can offer a lot for your start-up, from a different skillset to a dissenting voice that
may lead to better decisions. You may not always enjoy having to run things through
another person, but the potential benefits are too good to ignore. The problem is actually
building a successful partnership. There are a couple of things you need to do before committing to a business
partner.
1. Do Research on Potential Partners
Your partner's going to have a lot of influence and control over your start-up, so who you pick must be thoroughly
vetted and researched. People do research on where they want to have dinner and that's just a meal. You should do
the same for potential partners.
Take a look at their work history. Is there anything that concerns you? Do remember that people are fallible and
will make mistakes. Focus on the big picture. Study the context in which they've succeeded or failed. Was it
because they were trying something that seemed reasonable and profitable at the time or did they fail to do the
research? Was their failure due to a lack of skill or because the tide turned at an inopportune time?
Examine their skillset. Ideally, you'll have as little overlap as possible with your partner. They're here to provide
diversity through their skills and connections, not do your job.
2. Set Ground Rules
As an entrepreneur, one of your jobs is to make sure that everyone in the company knows what their roles are and
what they're expected to accomplish. That goes for your partner as well. You must both be clear on what your
responsibilities are. If you can't reach an agreement or find it difficult, it's time to look for a new partner. It's hard to
justify a partnership when you don't sync well with them.
3. Figure Out if You Have the Same Vision
Your partner will have their own reasons and goals, and that's okay. What isn't okay is if they have a completely
different idea of how the start-up should be run and where to take it. You must agree on what kind of company
culture to cultivate, what kind of actions are acceptable and which ones aren't.
Talk to your potential partner and figure out if you agree on company culture and philosophy. Once you have that
information, observe how they treat people and how they live their lives. Do they have strong ties to the
community? Do they have long-term relationships? Do they leave their work at the office or are they willing to
leap back into work should the company need them? Compare what you observe to what they told you and see if it
fits with what you want.

4. Start Them Off on a Simple Task
You don't want to leap into a partnership when you don't know how your partner does things. So go for a trial run and
give them something relatively simple to do. Examine how they do things. Do they go above and beyond what you
had them do? Are you happy with the quality of their work? Did they get it in on time and if they didn't, did they
make excuses?
5. Be Polite if It's Not a Good Fit
There may come a point when you realize it's just not a good fit. That's okay, there will be other candidates. The
problem is how the split will happen, as it can happen before you officially become partners or late into the
relationship. Do everything you can to make sure that it goes smoothly. You may be annoyed or even frustrated with
the development, but that's no reason to start burning bridges. Who knows? In the future you may require their help,
although not as a partner.
If you're going to get a partner, you must make sure that they're worth their weight in gold. You're an entrepreneur
with a start-up - there's a lot at stake. Just remember that a partnership takes time to develop. There will be growth
pains. If you do the research and don't settle for just anyone, you'll end up with a teammate who'll cover your back
and help you raise your start-up to the next level.
