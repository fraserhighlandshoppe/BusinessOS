Dealing With Competitors ….

Competition is a natural consequence of the free-market system and as a seller
on eBay you may initially consider it to be an unfortunate detriment to your business.
However, there are some positive aspects to having competitors, and a number of
ways that their negative impact on your bottom line can be minimized.
Since sales on eBay are conducted in an open manner, with the records of all
recently completed listings available for perusal, competitors who are selling the same sorts of items as
you are also conducting “marketing research” that you can take advantage of. By monitoring their
listings you can look for what sorts of prices you can expect, what sort of listing practices help sales,
and what sort of listing practices hurt sales. Although you shouldn't copy their photos and descriptions
verbatim (this would violate their copyrights) you can certainly copy their strategy without
repercussion.
If you discover a competitor that's doing poorly in some way, this represents an opportunity to
out-compete him by correcting whatever error he's making in your own listings. Just as competition
may sometimes represent a challenge to your own sales, so too can your own business represent a
challenge to your competitors. Your customers will reward you for providing a better service.
There have even been some eBay users who have successfully turned a profit by searching for
listings with problems that are likely to reduce their final sale price (misspelled keywords in the title,
for example, will prevent many interested buyers from finding the listing), buying the items, and then
immediately selling them again with a better-crafted listing. Remember to factor in shipping and other
costs if you're considering trying this yourself.
There are ways to out compete other sellers and here are some ideas.
Offer a lower starting price to entice buyers to cast bids on your listing first. In an auction-based
sale system you may even wind up selling for the same price your competitor would have, with your
low starting price merely serving to get the buyer involved in your listing rather than your competitor's.
Draw your competitor's buyers away with superior marketing. This can be as simple as using
better keywords in your title or can involve more complex and sophisticated mechanisms such as a
well-crafted “About Me” page, an eBay Storefront, or attractive listing templates. More details about
marketing are provided in the marketing chapter of this manual.
Offer better performance than your competitors. This could include accepting a wider range of
payment options, a wider range of shipping options,

Pricing is one of the key variables you can manipulate to influence your sales.
Starting price – a low starting price entices bidders and generates more interest in your listing, but runs the
risk of selling for less than you hoped for if the price doesn't get bid upward.
Reserve price – protects against having to sell at too low a price while still allowing for the low starting price to

buyers are annoyed by reserve prices and avoid listings with one.
If your reserve price isn't met but the highest bid is in hindsight still acceptable to you, you can use a second
chance offer to allow the highest bidder to buy the item anyway.
Buy It Now – an additional pricing strategy, it coexists with auction-style listing. The Buy It Now price is the

without a reserve price, once the first bid is placed.

the listing with a Buy It Now icon and the auction functions disabled. Fixed price listings are similar to how
conventional online retail sales are performed, and are most useful when you have multiple copies of the item
being listed available for sale.

5 Horrible Things You Must Accept About Being An Entrepreneur

There's a lot of potential in running your own start-up, and that potential is probably
what inspired you to become an entrepreneur. With hard work and careful decision
making, you'll eventually achieve financial independence -- in theory, anyway. The
truth is, there are a lot of horrible things about being an entrepreneur that you must
accept sooner rather than later.
Acceptance shortens the time you spend stunned and increases the time you spend solving problems. Here
are five downsides to entrepreneurship you should learn as soon as possible.
1. There is no Limit to Loss
Financial risk is a given as an entrepreneur. While creating a successful start-up with little capital isn't
unheard of, the situation will often require you to open lines of credit and procure business loans to help
the company grow. Sometimes, it's the only way to hit the market before the competition catches on.
You could easily lose everything if the competition bullies you out of the market or if your product and its
iterations are poorly received. Depending on your context, losing could come down to having to move
back in with your parents or being unable to provide for your family. If you can't deal with that risk, you
may not be cut out to be an entrepreneur.
2. You'll Lose Touch with Family and Friends
Being an entrepreneur is demanding, not just on your mind and body, but also on your relationships. Your
personal time will be consumed with reports and marketing campaigns and product development, leaving
little time for your friends and family. Eventually, you'll lose touch with some of those people.
It's up to you to stay connected to folks you care about. You can talk to them when you begin the start-up
and let them know what to expect from you. You'll probably miss out on a few important events, but you
can always reconnect once the business is stable.

Most start-ups fail in their first year. If investors seem skittish, it's likely because only 1 in 10
investments ever pays off. This isn't a unique situation; this is the history of entrepreneurship.
People succeed despite the odds, and if you're aware of how tenuous your position is, you tend to
be more careful. This may be what you need to succeed. Thinking that money's going to fall into
your lap makes you complacent, and you make even more mistakes.
Horrible things should not prevent you from building a start-up. Knowing what bad things to
expect on your journey as an entrepreneur braces you for the challenges you'll face. You can
prepare yourself mentally, physically, and emotionally. When the inevitable happens, whereas other
entrepreneurs will collapse, you'll be ready.
