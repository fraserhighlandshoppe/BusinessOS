14 Characteristics of Real Estate
Joint Venture Experts
Real Estate Joint Venture Experts are a special breed
of people – they step up to the challenge and take
action that sometimes flies in the face of convention.
They embrace some carefully-assessed risk because
they know that the rewards for their investors and
themselves can be sweet.
As a real estate investor, a Real Estate Investment
coach, and as someone who works with many other
real estate investors, I see the following chararacteristics for successful Joint Venture experts:
1. Inspired
The real estate investing joint venture expert is inspired by a larger vision. It might be a desire to quit
their job and retire early, to take care of family, or to
leave a legacy, or any other multitude of inspirational
visions. This inspiration motivates and drives them to
wake up every day and give it their very best.
2. Optimistic but also realistic
The real estate investing expert strikes a balance between optimistic (ambitiously building toward something better) and realistic (sensibly keeping their feet
on the ground). Too much of either one is dangerous,
but ambition mixed with sensibility is the best approach.
3. Action-oriented
The real estate investing joint venture expert is actionoriented, eschewing “analysis paralysis” in favour of
pushing forward. This person knows that not every
action will be 100% correct 100% of the time but they
take action anyway because they understand that
good action is better than no action at all.
4. Proactive about due diligence
The real estate investing expert knows that an investment is a business. They apply thorough due diligence principles before buying their business.
5. Team-builders
The real estate investing joint venture expert is willing
to get their hands dirty if necessary but also understands the value of a team and works toward building
one. By comparison, non-experts try to do it all themselves because they don’t see that a small investment
into a team reaps considerable dividends.
6. Risk tolerant
The real estate investing expert understands that

there are risks inherent to
every type of investment
(real estate or otherwise)
and they welcome acceptable risks because, on
balance, the rewards are greater.
7. Big picture thinkers
We are long-term thinkers, avoiding suspicious “get
rich quick” actions in favour of building long-term
value for tenants, the team, and partners. They
know that, in the short term, there might be discomfort and stretching but, in the long term, there is considerable gain.
8. Driven
We pushes higher, farther, and deeper in our quest
for success. We never settle or rest on their laurels.
While some investors might be happy to own one or
two doors, the entrepreneur stretches daily to get
more.
9. Problem solvers
The real estate investing expert knows that problems and challenges arise. But rather than being
stymied by them, they embrace challenges and
thrive on creating solutions.
10. Networkers
The real estate investing joint venture expert entreprt knows that the secret to success is not WHAT
you know but WHO you know. While others collect
accolades and even dollars, the entrepreneur collects personal contacts (and deep interpersonal relationships with each one).

Capital vs Maintenance Expense
Many people don’t understand the difference between a capital expense and a maintenance expense so I thought I would share with you some
insight into the difference between the two, as
well as the pros and cons of each. I do need to
stress that I am not an accountant and I am not
providing accounting advice so you will need to
verify this information I am about to share with
your own accountant to see how it best serves
you.

When you replace a set of taps in an apartment at
a cost of $200 then this expense would be classified as a maintenance expense. Other examples
of maintenance expenses could be the changing
of light fixtures, replacing a counter top, replacing
a new tub surround, replacing carpet in a bedroom/living room, repairing a broken window, appliance repairs, etc. Now at the year end your accountant will add up all of these maintenance expenses and use 100% of this number to reduce
the taxable income of the property for that year.
When you own a rental property you should budget for these types of maintenance expenses on an
annual basis. I was taught to budget anywhere
from $500 to $700 per unit per year for these
types of maintenance expense, depending upon
the age and condition of the property. The last
thing you want to do is buy a property and not
take these types of expenses into consideration in
your budget because this will directly impact your
profitability.
A capital expense is usually (if not always) much
more expensive than a maintenance expense.
Some easy examples of a capital expense would
be installing new roof shingles on the property,
installing a new furnace, resurfacing the parking
lot, etc… These types of improvements will have
a much longer life expectancy than a maintenance item; therefore, since the benefit is much
longer lasting the government will only allow you
to expense this cost over the expected life of the
improvement. For example a resurfaced parking
lot can be expensed at 8% per year, a new fence
at 10% per year other items may be 20% per year
and this is why you need to seek the advice of an
accountant. Many people will take the easy route
and just add these capital expenses to the overall
value of the property and expense the property at
the allowable rate of 4% per year (only 2% in first

year).
To determine the initial
capital expense for a rental
property purchased for
$600,000 your accountant
will want to know what the land value is compared to the building value. They want to know
this because you are not allowed to depreciate
the land but you are allowed to depreciate your
building by up to 4% per year in order to help
reduce your taxable income. Assume that with
the $600,000 property the land is worth
$100,000. You now have a $500,000 building
you can depreciate by up to 4% annually (2% in
year one) which translates into a $10,000 tax
deduction in the first year and a $19,600 tax
deduction in year two should you decide to use
it. If you just installed a brand new roof two
years after owning the property you have two
options:
Option #1: You just installed a brand new
roof for $20,000 which you can depreciate at
20% per year ($4,000 in year 1, $3,200, year 2
etc.. until you reach zero) and this means adding $4,000 to your expense column in year one.

how to do something before you set it as a goal. Creative minds and big thinkers ignore the realities and limitations of the present. Instead they choose a vision for

What is the best way to hold it?
There is no right or wrong, good or bad answer
on how to hold your real estate investment. It
comes down to what your intention and purpose
is. There are only two entities that are permitted
to own real estate: a person or a company. If a
person or people are not going on title, then a
corporate entity is the only other choice. Even a
Trust cannot directly own real estate.
This is why a Joint Venture Partnership is one of
the simplest and cost effective ways to hold a
real estate investment that involves multiple
owner-investors. A joint venture agreement outlines the roles and responsibilities of all the parties including the pro-rated ownership of the investment and what to do in the event of a dispute, death or incapacitation of investors or the
managing partner. When all parties are on title,
this means that everyone takes on 100% of the
liability. To be clear, this is not a pro-rated portion of your percentage of the liability. Each individual investor is taking on all of the liability. The
bank will ensure this by having everyone sign a
personal guarantee. This is the bank’s way of
mitigating their risk. Any and all cash flow that is
generated by the investment is deemed income
and is taxed at your personal marginal tax rate.
In order to provide liability protection to investors, the General Partner – Limited Partner (GLLP) structures were quite fashionable in the
1990’s and early 2000’s. The real estate investment would be held by an entity referred to as a
bare estate trustee. This means that the corporation is holding land and is not deriving any income from it. A bare estate means that the
structure is a flow through. Therefore, it does not
pay taxes because there is no income to be
taxed. The investors buy units in the Limited
Partnership and are referred to as unit-holders.
The General Partner of a Limited Partnership
assumes 100% of the liability. The liability of the
Limited Partners is limited to their pro-rated portion of the investment only if they remain silent
partners. The General Partner takes on all the
risk because the GP handles the day-to-day operations of the business. If a Limited Partner
were to become involved in daily operations and
making business decisions, they lose their limited liability status.

The cash flow generated from a GP-LP
structure must be distributed to the unit
holders. The LP is not
permitted to retain the income or it will be taxed at
the highest marginal tax rate. That’s because the
income derived from an LP is deemed passive income, not active income. The government has different rules for passive income versus active income. As you can see from the link below on the
2015 Corporate Income Tax Rates, the Federal tax
rate of 11% for a CCPC active small business is
11% versus the investment income rate of 34.7%.
http://www.taxtips.ca/smallbusiness/
corporatetax/corporate-tax-rates-2015.htm
In order to be considered an active business, a real
estate holding would need to employ five full-time
employees and one part-time employee to enjoy
the corporate tax rate. This active business status
would need to be corroborated by T4 slips. An active business is referred to as CCPC for short or
Canadian Controlled Private Corporation. Your
company will not qualify if it is controlled directly or
indirectly by non-residents or by a public corporation. The reason for this is because a CCPC is
taxed at the lowest federal tax rate of 11%. Going
forward, these rates will incrementally decrease to
9% by 2019. Corporations receive favorable taxation because small businesses stimulate the economy. Lower taxation encourages people to start
businesses.

Being A Specialist Is Just Smart!
One Type Of Property/Deal In A
Certain Designated Location.
I have worked with many people
over the past 16 years and the
ones that stood out and really excelled were the ones that had a specialty in their certain investing. I really think it boils down to the principle
of “Focus”.
Who would you rather have change out your furnace….
The handyman fellow you found on Kijiji or the guy who
has been in the furnace business and only has been
doing that line of work for 20 years? I am not saying
that both people can not do the job. But what I am saying is that there are huge quality and speed benefits
that come along with someone who only does this one
line of work. Think about it, having all of the specific
specialized tools to do the job, knowing all of the various products and their manufacturers by name. As well
knowing the pros and cons for each choice. I suppose
the same could be said about my finance specialty.
Most Mortgage Broker’s will answer “Yes I deal with
investors for sure!” The reality might be that two of
their last ten deals have been investor mortgages. My
answer is simply nine out of my last ten deals were
investor clients. The knowledge, experience and contacts would just naturally go along with this much focus. (A small plug I know :P)
I really think it just makes sense. Now let's look at the
benefits this will have for a person looking to grow their
investment portfolio. If a person was clear enough
about their investment strategy that they could answer
these questions. “What type of property will you buy?”,
“What location will you buy in?”, “What price point
would you consider?”, “What type of deal will this be?”
Eg. flip/rehab deal versus long term hold. When I am
working with folks I even get further clarity by asking…
“How many of these deals do you need in order to hit
your financial freedom goal over the next ten years?” I
can tell you that every person that I have worked with
over the years that has become financially independent
through real estate had these questions and answers
very clear from the get go. This is one lesson I personally had to learn the expensive way! Over the past
twenty years I have owned real estate in at least ten
different markets. Yikes!
Lets look at some of the benefits that come along with
being specialized. Let's say two people are looking at
the same property that just hit the market and appears
to be a good deal. While person number one is doing
their trained due diligence, the second , focusedspecialty person already has the property under contract and locked up!
It takes time to figure out what other houses in the area
are renting for and what they are selling for with what
Page 8

features etc. As well if you already know what the
long term growth expectation is for that area along
with any new enhancements (like that new highway
for example) then you would be miles ahead of the
next person that didn’t have know this information.
Another benefit to being a specialist is having your
success team already set up and ready to take on
that next piece of real estate you are bringing online.
Just imagine if a new realtor, property manager and
repair person had to be found and trained the way
you like to do business… and then you had to do it all
over again next month because you found a great
deal 3 hours away from your last one! In addition let's
say you only really spoke to that certain team a couple times a year because of the limited number of
deals you did in their specific area. Ask me how I
know this isn't the best way!
I recall getting a call from one of my tenants saying
the hot water tank burst and there was water leaking.
I did what any responsible owner would do and pulled

The contractor could sense my desperation and need
to get this issue handled fast. I ended up paying twice
the price I should have just because the guy knew I
was an out-of-town landlord in need. Unfortunately
these types of things happen.
What about buying huge bulk supplies like carpet and
paint and linoleum, for huge discounts and savings
because all of your properties have the same interior
finishings? Think of the efficiency and savings if all of
your renovation team knows exactly what you expect
without you having to even be there. Sweet!
With Focus, chasing that amazing cashflow “shiny”
deal that doesn't fit your system never happens any
more. It is like having a compass for your business
plan. Keeping you on track towards your goals and
not wasting time and money.
This just makes investment sense.

are much more stable.

Common Pitfalls For FSBO Sellers
Hot seller’s markets are apt to leave onlookers with
the mistaken impression that anyone can sell a
piece of real estate – especially when inventory is
low and buyers are in abundance. The truth however, is that sellers who go the FSBO (For Sale By
Owner) route often fail to earn top dollar for their
property. Moreover, a good percentage of FSBO
sellers end up retaining the services of a professional Realtor when the effort starts to overwhelm
them. In the end, the attempt to save a few dollars
proves unsuccessful and precious time is lost. In a
worst case scenario
that we have all
seen occur, a
homeowner selling
on their own tries
their hand in a
seller’s market only
to fail and choose to
start over again with
a Realtor only to
find that the market
has shifted in favor
of buyers.
What most FSBO
sellers fail to understand is that selling
property is a complicated process. It involves everything from staging
and marketing of the property to complex rules and
regulations governing the sale of real property. A
professional Realtor is truly a jack-of-all trades
whose job is to bring experience, knowledge and
objectivity to the table and partner with sellers to
bring about a sale that will profit both the property
owner and the Realtor. Successful Realtors who
earn top dollar for their clients have a strong working knowledge of everything from mortgage loan
financing to home staging and roof maintenance.
In order to compete with other sellers in a digital
marketplace, a FSBO seller must be knowledgeable and adept in the use of digital marketing, internet listings across a plethora of platforms and social media tie-ins to listings. A seller who is not
equipped to list, market and sell a property in the
digital age will have serious problems attracting
buyers. Above all, anyone who is selling a property
must be AVAILABLE – to return phone calls, text
messages and emails and to schedule showings at
times that are not always convenient. FSBO sellers
need to figure out how to do this while also juggling
the demands of their own career and family obligations.

Selling a home is a
stressful and emotional
undertaking. The property needs to be kept in
pristine condition at all
times. The faster the
home sells, the sooner
everyone can relax a little. FSBO sellers often hit
a wall and need the help of a professional when
a buyer does appear and negotiations begin. It
is almost impossible to represent yourself and
leave emotions out
of the equation. The
process can become
very unpleasant and
FSBO sellers often
settle on a price or
arguing point too
quickly just to make
the pain of the negotiation go away. A
professional Realtor
will not have the
same sentimental
attachment to the
property and will be
better equipped to
take on a tough negotiation and fight
for your best interests.
Before choosing to sell property on your own,
take a quick inventory of your lifestyle and
schedule. Ask yourself honestly if you have the
skills, knowledge, spare time and emotional detachment needed to sell your home? Can you
leave your workplace or family at a moment’s
notice to arrange a showing? Selling real estate
is a rewarding, dynamic business, but it is also
hard work. Work with a professional to ensure
you do not end up selling your home for far less
than it is worth.

The problem with a GP-LP structure is that you are forced
to pay out distributions even if an investor does not need
or want the income. This is why holding an investment in a
Corporation is attractive. Investors buy shares in the Corporation and are referred to as shareholders. Investors are
placed into different classes of shares. When a corporation
declares a dividend to pay out income to an investor, it
must specify which class of shares. If all the investors are
lumped into one class of share, they all must take the dividend at the time of declaration. If the investors are separated into different classes of shares, they can choose
when to take the dividend. This is a great structure for mitigating tax if an investor wants to withhold income distribution to another year.
To be clear, I am not an accountant. I have internal and
external accountants that I work with who do the detail
work. It is important for all real estate investors to understand CRA rules for passive income versus active income
because the difference in taxation is substantial.
The gist of this article is to encourage you to be aware of
the different real estate investment holdings, how your
money is taxed, how to shelter it and what structure is
most suitable for your type of investing. Everybody pays
taxes but only some people learn how to mitigate their taxes payable. There are pros and cons for each structure.
Real estate investing is a life-long learning opportunity. If
Chartered Professional Accountants must attend a minimum requirement of ongoing education in order to maintain their designation, so too must real estate investors
continue educating themselves.

an accountant or a lawyer or to a mentor is not going to

their opinion but the ultimate decision is yours based on
your intention for the real estate investment.
