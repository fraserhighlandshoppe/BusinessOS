To reinforce their hives, bees use a resin from poplar and evergreen trees called propolis. It’s basically

chances of a return visit.

Bees use the sun as a compass. But when it’s cloudy, there’s a backup—they navigate by polarized light,
using special photoreceptors to find the sun's place in the sky. The Vikings may have used a similar system:
On sunny days, they navigated with sundials, but on cloudy days, sunstones—chunks of calcite that act like
a Polaroid filter—helped them stay on course.

separate locations. What’s the shortest distance you can travel while visiting all six? Mathematicians call
this “traveling salesman problem,” and it can even stump some computers. But for bumblebees, it’s a snap.
Researchers at Royal Holloway University in London found that bumblebees fly the shortest route possible
between flowers. So far, they’re the only animals known to solve the problem.

In 36 BC, Marcus Terentius Varro argued that honeycombs were the most practical structures around.
Centuries later, Greek mathematician Pappus solidified the “honeycomb conjecture” by making the same
claim. Almost 2000 years later, Thomas Hales wrote a 19-page mathematical proof showing that, of all the
possible structures, honeycombs use the least amount of wax. And not only are honeycombs the most
efficient structures in nature—the walls meet at a precise 120-degree angle, a perfect hexagon.

up a few algorithms. Their findings improved computer models police use to find felons.

5 Management Mistakes That Are Hurting Your Profits
Managing your employees can be a tricky process. It can be difficult to find employees whom
you trust with your vision and your business. On the other hand, if your business isn't currently
performing on par with your expectations, it's possible that your management style is at fault.
Let's take a look at some things you as a manager might be doing that are jeopardizing the success of your company.
1.

You're too paranoid.

Sure, you have to make sure that your employees are doing what you need them to, but you shouldn't go overboard. If
you are investing more time and money into monitoring your employees than you are into actually productive work, you
need to stop. Don't hover around your employees' desks constantly to keep a watch on them. You need to trust that the
people you've hired are capable of performing their jobs with minimal supervision. As long as you're clear about what
you need them to do and have a regular feedback channel, let them breathe a little.
2.

You think of yourself as a friend to your employees.

You need to have cordial relations with your team. A stressful work environment hampers productivity. At the same
time, your employees need to understand your authority and trust you to make tough decisions. If you try too hard to be
a part of the gang, you'll lose their respect.
3.

You try too hard to make your office a 'cool' place.

If you're constantly thinking about how you can add new recreation zones, lounge areas and perks for your employees,
you're wasting your time. If you want to make your work environment more informal, open up additional channels of
communication. Making sure your employees are easily and freely able to communicate with you and their colleagues
will help your business more than an extra Ping-Pong table.
4.

You withhold feedback for the annual appraisal meeting.

If your employees are doing well or poorly, they need to know about it now, not six months down the line. Consistent
feedback will keep your employees motivated and give them the information they need to improve their skillsets.
Additionally, when you offer feedback to your employees, don't make it a comparison. Instead, focus on their own skills
and how they can get better at doing what the company needs them to do.
5.

You avoid conflict.

Running a business means that you're going to have to make decisions that will make some people unhappy. There is
simply no getting around this aspect of management. Whether you're taking action based upon certain market-based or
individual performance factors, you'll have situations when people are upset. Instead of avoiding these situations,
embrace them as an opportunity to interact with your employees and use their viewpoints to improve your business.
Creating the optimal work environment is one of the best things you can do for your company. If that means changing
the way you manage people, so be it.
Use the guidelines above to improve the way you lead your employees so that your business can benefit as a result.
