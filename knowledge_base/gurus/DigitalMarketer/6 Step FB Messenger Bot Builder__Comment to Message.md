Comment-to-Message
The comment-to-message growth tool is a really useful one. It lets you get creative with cool new ways to engage people with Messenger.
In a nutshell, it works by automatically sending a Facebook message to anyone who leaves a comment on one of your Facebook posts (paid or organic). From there, the person will be added to your subscriber list (if they engage with you) and can be tagged and added to any sequence you choose.
I’ve written an entire blost post on comment-to-messenger, so if you want more detail about this strategy you can find it there. But for the purposes of this EP, we don’t need to rehash all the details. Instead, I’ll just give you a few tips on how to make the most of this growth tool, then I’ll show you how to set it up using ManyChat.
Here are your 5 steps to using comment-to-message effectively:
1) Ask a Polarizing Question
The best way to generate genuine engagement on a post is to ask a question that is relevant to your audience—something that will spark some curiosity and debate.

2) Set the Proper Expectation
The comment-to-messenger functionality is still new for a lot of people, which means many users don’t always know how it works or what to expect.
That’s why you should explain everything and make sure it’s always 100% clear what is going to happen when people take the action you want them to take.
To go back to Derek’s example, notice how he lets you know that he’ll be sending you a Facebook message if you comment:
Remember: when people get confused, they give up. So explain everything. It’s better to provide clarification—even if the person doesn’t need it—than it is to confuse them.
3) Track First-Level Comments Only
By default, ManyChat will automatically send a message to EVERYONE who comments on your post—even if they’re just responding to another comment. But to give a better user experience, I recommend turning this feature ON:
If you turn this feature on, then only first-level comments will be sent a message. Anyone responding to a first-level message will not.

4) Use the “Engagement” Objective if You’re Creating an Ad
If you’re going to create a comment-to-Messenger post and promote it as an ad, I do NOT recommend optimizing for traffic or conversions. Instead, you’ll get the best results by optimizing for engagement:
This way, Facebook will optimize your ad to maximize engagement (like comments).

5) Avoid “Engagement Bait”
When writing the copy for your comment-to-messenger post, be sure to avoid what Facebook calls “engagement bait.” Engagement bait is simply any post that urges people into interacting with that post for a superficial reason.
Here are a few examples (you’ve probably seen a few posts like these in your own feed):
Comment “YES” if you love this video game!
“Like” this post if you’re an Aries to find true love!
Share with 10 friends for a chance to win a new convertible!
As you can imagine, these posts feel kinda spammy and tend to generate low-quality engagement rather than real, authentic conversation.
If you’re unsure whether your post would be considered engagement bait or not, just remember tip #1: ask a polarizing question. Do that and you will probably be sparking real, genuine discussion among your audience.
