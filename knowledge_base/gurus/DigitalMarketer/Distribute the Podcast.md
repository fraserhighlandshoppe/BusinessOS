Distribute the Podcast
We briefly talked about our podcast distribution schedule in the first module – now we’re going to go into more detail and show you how we generated over 10,000 clicks in six months.
Before we distribute the podcast throughout our channels, we take the URL to the episode’s show notes page on our site and use Bitly to shorten the link. We will include this Bitly link in our social media posts. It’s good practice to shorten a link because a long link is not visually pleasing to look at and can take up valuable space (think Twitter).
Besides being able to shorten a link, Bitly has great tracking features you can use including number of clicks, the time of day people are clicking, and what platform the clicks are coming from.
Remember, you want to distribute your podcast throughout the week, not all on one day, to avoid peaks and valleys in your downloads.
First let’s talk about…
Email Distribution
We spoke about our email distribution in the previous module. As a quick reminder, we publish an episode on Tuesdays and email our list about the episode on Thursdays.
When writing the email to announce the podcast, generally, the email’s subject line is the name of the episode.
Again, this is where the show notes come in handy. The majority of the email consists of the show notes, which make up the email’s intro and body. So the email is basically written and really only requires a closing.
Here’s an example below…
The email directs people to our show notes page on our website. Here, a person can listen to the latest episode and browse the podcast archive.
Next, let’s talk about…
Social Distribution
We will also use elements from the show notes to promote the episode on social platforms, including Twitter and Facebook.
On our social channels, we obviously promote the latest episode, but we also use the platform to call out mile stones, such as getting 300,000 downloads or publishing our 50th episode.
To maintain even downloads, we also continue to post previous episodes. This helps new people discover the podcast.
For a new episode, we will tweet about it several times in one day, broadening our exposure on that platform. In Facebook, we will boost an episode post for about a week. These strategies ensure the Twitter and Facebook posts drive traffic. You can see examples below…
On Fridays we send out website push notifications (powered by PushCrew) to promote the latest episode…
Website push notifications are clickable messages a website can send to their subscribers, directly on their desktop or mobile device. DigitalMarketer site visitors will be asked if they wish to subscribe to the notifications; those that select “Allow” will receive notifications from us.
When a push notification for the podcast episode is sent, subscribers that click on the notification will be sent that episode’s show notes page where they can listen to the latest episode from the site.
To learn more about PushCrew notifications, read this blog post: Website Push Notifications: A New Way to Follow Up with Website Visitors (Without Getting Their Contact Information).
We also use a tool called MeetEdgar (commonly referred to as Edgar) for social automation. This allows us to create a “library” of posts that can be scheduled and posted on a future date.
You can also use Edgar to create a rotating publication schedule.
Drawing from the library, Edgar publishes the posts to our social platforms on a rotation schedule, without us having to do anything extra at all. We use this tool for previous episodes, and it saves us a lot of time, while also helping to keep our download levels even.
Edgar also has a statistics section you can use to see how your content is performing, as well as how often it’s been published in that month. Based on this you can see what social media updates are the most popular, and what posts people aren’t responding to, so you can adjust accordingly, such as changing the post’s copy.
As you collect data, the days you distribute your podcast may be subject to change.
However, be sure the day you publish a new episode stays the same, so your subscribers know when to expect the latest installment of your podcast.
Important: Keep the size of your social media audience in mind when creating your distribution schedule.
If you have a small following, and you publish as often as we do on social media, you’re likely to oversaturate and annoy your followers. If you have a very large following, you may need to publish more often than we do on social media.
Pay attention to your metrics, and what your audience is saying about the rate at which you post and adjust accordingly.
