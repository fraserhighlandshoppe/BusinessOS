Offers That Work on LinkedIn
Create content that’s helpful, informative and entertaining. Make sure you have appropriate offers for each phase of your marketing funnel based on the temperature of your traffic. To learn more about determining the temperature of your traffic visit this great post by Molly Pittman.
LinkedIn analyzed thousands of ad campaigns and here are the best offer to use in each phase of your marketing funnel.
Top of Funnel
Top of funnel offers are designed to raise awareness of your product or service. At this stage of the funnel, the prospect is in the early stages of product research and they may know very little about your product or service. The following content works well to let people know how your product or service can solve their problem. This content can also help them get to know your company value and reputation.
Infographics
Blog posts
Industry trends
Influencer content
"Behind the scenes"
Exclusive content
Middle of Funnel
Middle of funnel offers are designed for people who are aware of your product or service and are entering the evaluation stage. They know what options are out there and they are comparison shopping to find the product or service which best meets their needs at the best price.
Best practice guides
Case studies
Product reviews
Analyst reports
Webinars
Blog posts
Bottom of Funnel
The bottom of the funnel offers are designed for decision makers who will authorize the purchase of your product or service. These people are usually executives interested in receiving the best ROI for their investment, productivity increases, or saving money through more efficient processes. These executives like to read case studies, research briefs and white papers to help them make decisions.
During this phase the primary users are ready to see a demo of your product and start a trial so they can “look under the hood" if you offer a technical product.
Whitepapers
Research briefs
Product demos
Trials
Product reviews and comparisons
Analyst reports
Newsletters
Recommendations
Case studies
Product news
Tutorials
Here’s an example of a sales funnel I used when I worked for an enterprise content marketing software company. Our product was similar to WordPress but it was designed to scale for very large websites for companies like General Mills, Comcast, Toshiba, and Costco. The decision-making process had three distinct phases and many departments were involved in the decision which could take up to 18-24 months to complete.
Let's take a look at how we designed this funnel...
Top of Funnel
This is the phase where a project manager would begin the research process to determine the best solution for their website. Typical Google searches might be “what is a content management system?” or “what is the best software for an enterprise website”. Typical offers were an ebook or an infographic related to “what is a content management system”. These offers educated the reader on the basics and benefits of content management systems.
Middle of Funnel
At this point the prospect decided they were going to implement a content management system and they are researching their options. There are many different types of content management systems so ebooks and videos related to “choosing the best content management systems” worked well. Also third party comparison guides also worked well during this stage of the funnel.
Bottom of Funnel
The prospect has now decided on our content management system and they have to sell it to their executives. This is where we created offers for executives that showed the ongoing cost savings for implementing a content management system, their ROI, and increased revenue they would see after implementing a new website. Third party analyst reports also helped convince the executives they were making a sound investment.
If you are selling B2B services, you would use the same approach. Tailor your offers to your target market and their decision making process.
