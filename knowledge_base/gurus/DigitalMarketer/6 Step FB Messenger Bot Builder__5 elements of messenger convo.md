The 5 Elements of a Great Messenger Conversation
Here are 5 tips to help you write better copy for your Messenger campaigns:
1) The Conversation Must Help the Consumer Make PROGRESS in Their Life
It has to be helpful and provide real value.
Luckily, you’ve already thought though how you’re going to do this—in Step 3 when you came up with your before and after states.
So USE THOSE in your ad copy!
Before/after states are really powerful. They explain the benefit that the user will get from this chatbot experience in an easy-to-understand story.
“Right now, you are [before state]. Click this button to get [after state].”
Follow the basic before/after structure and you’ll automatically write clear, benefit-rich copy.
2) The Conversation Must Have an “End Goal” for You as the Marketer
You’ve already thought about this, too—in Step 1 where you defined your goal.
Remember that goal when you write your copy and don’t get too far off-topic, or else you might compromise the results of your campaign.
But at the same time, remember that your Messenger campaign is a process, and your users have to go through it one step at a time. And when you’re copywriting you want to keep in mind WHERE the user is in the process, and always urge people to take the very next step.
If you’re creating a campaign with the goal of eventually selling a product, you can’t just come out and say: “Buy this product!”
People hear that all the time. They’ll ignore that.
Instead, think about what’s going through your user’s head at every step through your Messenger marketing funnel… and make the goal of each step in your campaign to get the person to take just 1 more step.
For example:
When you’re writing ad copy, the main goal is NOT to sell the end product. No. The main goal of the ad is to get people to click on the ad. (AKA take the next step.)
When writing your opt-in message, once again—your main goal is not to sell the end product. The main goal of the opt-in message is to get the person to opt in. (AKA take the next step.)
Write your copy with these mini-goals in mind, and you’ll find that you’re able to increase your conversion rate at every step in the funnel. As a result you’ll end up with more traffic, more leads, and ultimately more sales.
3) The Conversation Must Have Personality, Voice, & Feel as Though the Consumer is Talking to a Real Human
The user will feel a lot more engaged if they feel like they are talking to another person, rather than a bot. This is true even if the user actually realizes it IS a bot.
It’s just more interesting to us. More engaging.
So choose a personality that matches your brand. If your brand voice/image is kind of snarky, throw in a few sarcastic comments. If it’s kind and motherly, then write in a sincere-sounding voice.

4) The Conversation Must be in Messenger “Style,” Which Includes Short-Form Text, Emojis, etc.
Think about the way you use instant messages when you’re communicating with your friends and family.
You don’t text long paragraphs to people, do you? No!
You send short messages, and wait for a response. Messenger is like that. And if you write your messages to match that style of communication, you’ll get more engagement.
Keep it short and to the point, and ask a lot of questions to keep your user engaged.

5) The Consumer Needs to Have Options & Feel as Though They’re Creating Their Own Experience
Think “choose your own adventure,” rather than “reading a novel.”
Give the user lots of chances to provide input and direct the conversation. Even though they aren’t actually guiding the conversation—you are—by asking lots of questions, you can give them the feeling that they’re the ones in control.
