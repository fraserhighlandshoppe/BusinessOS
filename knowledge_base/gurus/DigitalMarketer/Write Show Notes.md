Write Show Notes
So you’ve got the podcast recorded and edited. It’s ready to rock!
But before you hit the publish button, you should write the show notes.
The show notes are an outline of the episode and provide some context to the episode, as well as the benefits of SEO, all while helping to pique a person’s interest to listen to the show.
Another benefit of the show notes is it drives traffic to your site, allowing you to segment and retarget your audience.
We typically write the show notes the day before the episode goes live, which gives us time to edit them and make the episode’s image. We post the finished podcast and show notes on our site the same day the episode goes live in iTunes.
Here’s how we approach writing our show notes…
Include These Four Sections in Your Show Notes
1. Title
One of the challenges you’ll face when naming the episode is you only have so much character space. Because the way the title appears in iTunes, you need to get to the point, or you’ll get cut off. So aim to keep your title as short as possible.
If your title must be long, aim to have the topic of that episode towards the front of the title before it gets cut off.
Take a look at the example above. The episode titles that have too many characters still get to the point quickly, so the listener knows what that episode is about.
Also, use your title to call out to that episode’s avatar – to the ones that will find value from that episode – and pique his or her interest by being as specific as possible.
If your first episode is titled, “Episode 1: Welcome,” you will get very few downloads because no one will know what that show is even about or why valuable time should be given to your show.
You must be sure to give the listener context – a reason to listen to your episode.
Writing podcast titles is a little like writing email subject lines: You have a very finite amount of space to grab someone’s attention.
Finally, when writing your podcast’s title, be direct, not cute.
You want to make sure people won’t be confused by your title. To generate downloads, people need to know what they’ll get before they click.
First be direct with your title, then, if you can make it work, you can add punchy or cute or funny to the title. With content, any content for that matter, being cute is a bonus, but not a necessary one.
It’s far more important to tell your audience what they’ll get from listening to the episode, the why of the episode, than it is to be cute.
2. Brief Description
In the description, you’ll go into more detail and provide context about the episode. Again, you need to be as short as possible in your description, or you’re going to get cut off. This is what our description looks like for Episode 1…
As you can see, we get cut off. We’re less concerned about the description getting cut off, because the description will help with your iTunes SEO and rankings.
It’s fine to have more copy with the description, but again, realize you have a small amount of space to quickly get to the point and convince someone to listen to that episode.
One of the easiest ways to grab a person’s attention is to open with a question as we do in Episode 3…
A question is an easy call out to your audience. Even though the above description gets cut off, a question is an effective way to grab a person’s attention.
When writing your description, skip the obvious to save space and get more “above the fold.” Initially, we were guilty of stating the obvious in our descriptions…
…Well, of course it’s the Perpetual Traffic Podcast. That’s valuable wasted space that pushes the episode’s hook farther down.
Avoid the obvious, and call out to your audience with relevant copy like, “Looking to sell tickets to an event?”
You only have a person’s attention for a short amount of time. Quickly call out to your audience in your description and pique their interest while their attention is on you.
Lastly, if you have a guest on your podcast, be sure to build the guest’s credibility with a line or two that describes his or her achievements and expertise…
Remember, the influence a guest has can also serve as a draw to that episode, so quickly tell people who will be joining you and what will be discussed.
3. Curiosity + Benefit Bullet Points
After the description sets the show up, use bullet points to give additional reasons why someone should listen to your episode.
We introduce our bullets with the heading, “In This Episode You’ll Learn,” followed by three to five bullet points that further state the value of the episode.
Instead of plainly stating what the episode is about, we try a different approach.
The formula we use for writing bullets is Curiosity + Benefit. We split the sentence in two: The first portion of the sentence aims to grab someone’s attention, followed by what a person can gain by listening to the episode.
The Curiosity + Benefit structure may not work for every bullet point, but we strive to include as many as we can, without sounding forced.

Creating curiosity causes people…
To click. They want to know what “it” is.
To go into the episode searching for what sparked their interest, so they can close the loop.
While the benefit explains why listeners should care by answering the, “What’s in it for me?” question.
4. Resources
Finally, any resources you mention throughout the episode, be it software, websites, or previous podcast episodes, have a section in your show notes that links out to these resources.
This makes what you’ve talked about in the episode easily accessible, and improves user experience.
Providing a resource section also allows you to cross-link between episodes. This is key because it helps maintain consistent downloads, as we discussed in the first module.
