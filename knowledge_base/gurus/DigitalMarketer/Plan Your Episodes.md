Plan Your Episodes
Before you hit the record button for your new episode, you should have a plan.
Just like your podcast needs a consistent style, each episode needs a theme or a concept. Knowing the central idea for the episode will help keep the episode organized, help you as the host stay on track, and also make it easier for your listener to follow.
When planning your episodes, a mind map may come in handy. This visual thinking tool can help you structure your show, recall previous ideas, and also help to generate new ones.
When we were starting Perpetual Traffic, we mind mapped the first six months, and it was super important to our episode organization and helped us get started. Below is a snippet of that mind map. You can view the full mind map here.
You don’t have to map six months out like we did, but it’s vital to know where you’re going, or you risk creating disorganized episodes, which won’t help you get and keep subscribers. This is especially important if your episodes build on one another.
Now, your mind map isn’t set in stone. We certainly didn’t adhere to our six-month episode plan. As you continue your podcast, you’ll see what people respond to most and create new shows based on that information. Or perhaps a timely topic, such as a case study, may present itself that you want to do a show on.
Moral of the story: Have a plan and a concept for each episode before you record it, so you know where you’re going, but also be flexible.
Let’s talk about recording the show…
We don’t rehearse an episode before we record it – we want the show to be as natural and conversation-like as possible.
The other hosts and I live in separate cities, so before we record an episode, we meet via Skype, discuss the points each of us want to make, and where we want to take that particular episode. This takes about twenty minutes, and then we know we’re all on the same page.
During our episode discussion, we create a Google Doc to map out the structure of the show.
Then we hit record, and use the Google Doc as a reference. We stay in Skype and use ScreenFlow or Ecamm Recorder to capture our podcast recording.
Another great thing about recording over Skype is even though the other hosts and I aren’t in the same room together, or even the same state for that matter, Skype helps us forget that. We can see each other’s social cues and respond to them, which we may have missed if we were simply recording the podcast over audio alone.
Also, we don’t have a set amount of time we wish to talk, nor do we impose a time limit on ourselves. Sometimes we record for well over an hour. Sometimes it’s around forty minutes.
After recording an episode, we may find an episode will work better split into two parts or as a series of episodes. If that’s the case, we re-record the intro and the conclusion and add some transitions to tie it all together.
Don’t worry about your recording being flawless right out of the gate. In the audio edit process, you can clean up anything that may be repetitive, mistakes you made, or moments of dead air.
