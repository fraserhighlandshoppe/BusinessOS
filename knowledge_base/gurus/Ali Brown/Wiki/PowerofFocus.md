The new year is a great time for a fresh start.  More than a new year's resolution.  You need to evaluate what you need to do in the next 60-90 days to build your business.

On an energetic level, 2011 is going to be different - business will be hiring.

Small businesses have the ability to make changes faster.

==Why is your business floundering==
Everyone's business looks great on the surface.

What's going wrong here?  Am I focusing on the wrong things?

==Information Overload==
*Podcasts
*Blogs
*Forums
*Clubs
*Membership events
*Telecasts
*Facebook
*Twitter

Back in the day, you'd go looking for resources and find one or two books.  Nowadays, there is too much information available that you don't have the time to digest it all.

==Focus==
*When to online marketing versus traditional
*How do you know when to hire more talent
*When do you focus on new revenue streams rather than existing ones (core offerings)
*How to tell when you are focusing on the wrong growth area
*The milestone you need to reach before offering higher ticket items.
*How to market yourself
*How to price yourself

You need to get good at marketing before you will get people paying you the big money.

Working with coaches and mentors produces better results than trying to work by yourself.  Even better is the coaches and mentors that require you to commit to performing.

You need to have a structure.  It is hard to create one from scratch.  It is much easier to take someone else's structure and implement it.

The journey is never a straight line.

While you are dreaming your vision, you also need to occasionally ground in reality.

==6 Main reasons businesses flounder==
*3% of women business owners make it to 1M.
*After 5 years more than 1/2 of business owners give up (re-write of the stat that 50% fail)

===Lack of clear step by step information available===
*Lots of information, but not clear and concise
**Blogs
**Teleseminars
*Many paths to follow
A confused mind won't focus so you can't move forward!
===Getting the wrong information===
*Wall Street Journal article this summer expanded upon this
**Adult education classes
**Small business associations
*Most of the start your own business books are small minded
**What's the big picture.  The books are very basic and simple and only focus on the very first startup steps.
**Where are the points of leverage to generate money for you 
===Lack of connections and resources===
*Need to network.  Relationship building.
**Strategic networking is a must.
**Need to find the mentors/Centers of Influence (COI)
*You are the average of the 5 people you are around the most.
*To be successful, you need to seek out exciting new friends who are successful in business.
*You also need to network for clients and customers
===Lack of funding===
*Most people starting a business have this problem.  
*Many business owners avoid debt at all cost
*It is impossible to grow a business without taking on some risk, which includes debt.
*Debt is a tool.  You just need to get an ROI on the debt.
*You need to separate personal finances from business finance.
**This applies not only to physically separating into different bank accounts, but also includes mentally
**this also applies to pricing.  You have to not attach your personal worth to what you are selling your products or services for.
===Not using your time properly===
*Entreprenuership is turning time into money.
*You need to work on your business rather than in your business.
**You need to shift your mindset from that of an employee to an owner.
**You are paid for the value you generate for someone else.  
***As an employee, it is effort based (i.e. show up 9-5)
***As an owner you are paid after you generate massive value for the customer.
*Bosses can only afford to keep employees who are creating value.
*You need to show some entreprenurialship as an employee.
*You need to be ruthless with your time.
**If we are distracted we aren't accomplishing much as each item isn't getting much time, effort, attention.
*If you can get 5 hours of focused non stop work time in a day, 160/hr gets you 200k a year.
*Ask yourself "Is what I am doing right now worth 200/hr?".  You need to ask yourself this EVERY day.
**Automate anything that can be that needs to be done
**delegate anything that can't be automated, but is lower value but still needed
***opportunity cost - if you hire someone to cook/clean/pay bills does this free up time you can use to expand your business?
**delete anything that is really low value or not needed
***entering business card details from people you will never followup with

===The wrong strategies at the wrong time===
*Success is looking up (the vision/goal) but also looking down at the steps being taken to get there.
*Taking the wrong steps leads to the business failing to take root.
You need to separate your business finances from your personal finances.  Not only physically, but also mentally.  You need to be able to recognize business decisions rather than personal decisions.  You need to detach when you start getting emotional about the decision.

==Business Model==
Most people's ego would have them at expansion even before knowing where they are.  No one wants to consider themselves as beginners.
You need to separate yourself sense of worth from your business and its state.
===Foundation===
Am I supporting myself and my business from my business income alone?

If your business isn't self sustaining, you are at foundation.

*Exploring what the right business is
*Have clients but want more
*Need help with marketing
*Need systems
*On the right track but need to move forward faster
*Want to hire someone but don't feel I am making enough money yet
*Need to work on, not in the business

#Start thinking like a 6 figure business owner.  When making decisions, think "what would that 6 figure owner do?"
#Start make decisions from where you want to be, not where you are.

Foundation Denial is when someone is focused on trying to get multiple streams of income, when they don't yet have the first one since they are not getting a product in front of clients.

*Identify the right business model for you
**What is your expertise
**How will you work with clients
**What are your goals
**What do you want to accomplish
**Are these aligned with a business model that will generate income
*Discover your most profitable niche.
**What you offer
**Who you offer it to.
**How to find your ideal client.
**How to ensure they want what you are offering.
**Great ideas are not a guarantee of a great business.
*Nailing down your perfect marking message
**What is your hook (this can be used on website, social media, brochures, talks, etc)
***What problem are you the solution to.
***Why people should pay attention to do
***how you differentiate from your competition
**What is your unique selling proposition
**What is your response when someone asks you what you do.
**What is the conversation after they show some interest in what you do.
*Refining your packages and pricing
**Start with one thing and do it really well
**Don't try to start with 8 levels of membership, multiple products, etc
*Learn to talk about your business.  
**Engaging conversations that sell but don't feel awkward or like a sales pitch.
**Perfect your elevator speech
*Referral based marketing
**85% of a service based business's traffic comes from word of mouth.
*Strategic networking and speaking
**Get in front of people
**Talk about your business
**Establish long term relationships with targeted people.
*Set up your online sales funnel
**Why they give you their name and email
**Where do they go next
**What product are they then introduced to
*Set up your online (web) business
**Take payment online
*Lead generation and followup
**Convert web traffic into people that know and trust you.
**Then convert your fans into clients
