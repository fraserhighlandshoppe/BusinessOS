Session 5: Virtual Sales &
Marketing Secrets For
Business Success
• Virtual Marketing Tip #1: Every "visitor" to your website or "statistic" in your business
dashboard is a real human with real human fears, desires, needs and wants. One danger of
working in a virtual environment is that we tend to forget that each visitor is a real human being
with secrets and wounds and hopes. That visitor isn't just a number. Each one is trying to
accomplish something in their life... trying to "get somewhere" in their life. When you remember
this, it leads you to want to understand each of person who visits your site better. You remember
to communicate to them as an individual, as a person. And when you talk to them as people,
they respond like people.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #2: Marketing & Sales is about getting customers, not about getting your
website online or getting your name out. Running a virtual business is not the same as having a
job. Unlike having a job, the name of the game in the virtual business world isn't about showing
up for work and getting a pay check - it's about doing what it takes to get customers. When you
do marketing and sales right, you increase the number of customers you get.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #3: You must discover what causes your Prospective Customer to take
action - and the best way is live and in-person. There are certain triggers that cause people to
respond in certain ways, including to make the choice to by from you. You usually will find that
survival, sex, and social status are most of the decision making and behavior of the person. Most
of us in business need to remember this - there's more going on than meets the eye, and it almost
all goes back to basic drives and motives. Using these deep drives, realize there is one appeal
that will work better to sell your product or service than another appeal. Your job is to find it.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #4: Use your sales conversion process with live chat - and eventually
email - and integrate what you learn with each. Put a live chat box on your website, and make a
note of what works to get people to go over the edge and buy from you. Next, move it to email.
Put everything you've learned into templates you can send via email, and have these templates
answer all their objections, all their fears, and explains all the benefits of your product. One of
the great things about working virtual is you can continually collect vital distinctions that occur
during your sales conversations, knowing later you can incorporate and automate them all.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #5: Once you've discovered and refined the sales process, only then
should you create an "automated" sales process. This only makes sense - once you've figured
out how to do it yourself "manually", only then can you create a machine to duplicate yourself.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #6: Ultimately your objective is to script out and automate the
"relationship building" process with your customers, from the first "hello" all the way to the point
where they trust you enough to buy your product. Imagine going to the door of one of your
customers. Once you've scripted out how a real interaction would go, automate it. You'll know
that the first time you "meet" they'll need to know who you are, some things about you to build
trust and credibility, that your product can solve their problem and get them the results their
looking for, and that they have specific objections you'll want to address as well. As you're
doing this, always talk to your prospect one-to-one, realizing that each is an individual human
with individual human needs. They aren't a big mass of "human." Write in a casual, upbeat,
friendly and trusting tone.

• Virtual Marketing Tip #7: Give your customers instant access to SOMETHING as soon as they
purchase and engage them - to increase "stick." Stick means they keep your product and don't
ask for a refund. Many people buy something and are thinking they'll return it if they don't like it.
Often the difference between a person returning or not is the simple question of whether or not
you can get them to actually use your product. If you're selling adventure travel online, when
someone purchases their travel package, email then and say "I made a video for you I'd like
you to watch that gives you tips to help you get the most out of your upcoming adventure..."
Make it fun, make it engaging. Get them doing stuff. It can make a huge difference in your stick
rate, and your re-purchase rate. If they never "get into" your product in the first place, how will
they ever know it's good and that they should come back and buy even more stuff from you?
