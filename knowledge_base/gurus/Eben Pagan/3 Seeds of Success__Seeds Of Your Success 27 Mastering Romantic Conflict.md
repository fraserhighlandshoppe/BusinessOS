Day 27:
Mastering Romantic Conflict
Here you'll get a crash course on super eﬀective techniques for
handling conﬂict in your intimate relationships.
You'll learn the trick to turning conﬂict around and using it to
strengthen your relationship, how to see your partner as your ally
even when you two are upset, the powerful role positive touch
can play in resolving conﬂict, and much more...
Conﬂict is what brings relationships down... but it's also natural and an expected
part of being in love. The idea is to learn how to catch conﬂict upstream, go
through conﬂict with minimum harm and blame, and get back to your baseline as
quickly as possible.
To love is to ﬁght. Your best bet isn't to suppress or avoid conﬂict, but to learn the
rules of the game and master the ability to move through and recover from
arguments.
Going from Opponent to Ally:
1. Most people see the other as their opponent in a duel of wits.
2. What if you and your partner were on the same team, both ﬁghting

something in common: a misunderstanding?
3. The ﬁght then becomes a team eﬀort to protect your sacred connection.
4. This is the most eﬀective way to ﬁght.
5. Become an emotional detective on the hunt for the misunderstanding. What

is their understanding? How is it diﬀerent from your intention?
All ﬁghts boil down to you don't respect, appreciate, care, or understand me. Which
all boil down to "you don't love me."
Loss of love is related to the fear of death. Parents lacking love meant death to a
young child dependent on parents for survival, so we code loss of laugh with the
same horror as death itself.
Don't be afraid of ﬁghting. It can become healthy for self understanding, and it
cultivates emotional muscles. When it gets solved constructive, it serves to
strengthen the relationship, because shared challenges deepen intimacy.

Maintain physical conﬂict during the ﬁght, even though it's the last thing you want
to do. Pulling away physical touch is a show of spite and vengeance, of trying to
hurt the other person as a response to being hurt. Be bigger than that.
Touching during conﬂict is important because it communicates to both people
that we're in this together. It's hard to stay angry when you're touching the other
person. It reminds people that love is still present during the tension.
Hold hands, put a hand on your partner's chest, etc. It doesn't matter who initiates.
The receiver should resist the ego-driven desire to pull away. It takes a lot of
courage to reach out to someone during a ﬁght, and we want to reward not reject
that valiant eﬀort.
The feeling of not being loved is the deepest source of all conﬂict.
Custom Soothing Technique:
1. When you and your partner are in a loving state, ask them what would you

most like to hear/feel from me the next time you're triggered? Explore this take notes. Could be words, could be touches, etc. Practice this several times
before you get into a ﬁght, so when ﬁghts happen you already have this
ritual in your pocket.
2. Be willing to actually pull out your notes and read them if you need to. It's

that important.
Whoever remembers the technique goes ﬁrst, and that partner gets "props" for
being brave and taking the lead.
Our ability to hold paradox is key to relationship success. One example of this is in
assuming 100% responsibility for all that transpires in the conﬂict.
1. This keeps you from seeing yourself as a victim, which keeps you trapped in

a powerless state. You might not believe you're responsible, but taking on
responsibility can be valuable. Even if it's the tiniest amount, look for ways
you could have behaved better.
2. We can never directly change the psyche of someone else - the best we can

do is change the way we relate to them. A ﬁght is a partner dance, where
each partner does their own familiar dance. If you want a diﬀerent dance,
you need diﬀerent moves. One way to do this is to look at any ways you
contributed to it.
3. Be proactive. If joy and fun are absent, you bring it, instead of blaming them

for not bringing it.
4. Lead and your partner will begin playing the same selﬂess game.

Look for the positive intention in your pattern. From their point of view, it's obvious
they are not trying to cause pain or be insensitive.
Develop your own Meta Emotional Muscle. If you're mad, how do you feel about
being mad? It's one thing to be mad, but it's another to then be ashamed of being
mad. That's our meta judgment. Just acknowledge it, and the original emotion.
1. Emotions must go through their full life cycle. Most psychological issues are

from emotions not completing their full cycle... and getting "stuck."
Appreciate your partner's shadow. Start with the parts of their body that they're
ashamed of. It is these parts that secretly crave appreciation. Acknowledge and
appreciate the body parts they don't like. Annie calls this Fetishizing Their Flaw.
Appreciate that body part so genuinely that it allows them to appreciate it
themselves.
Appreciate their ugliest emotional shadows too. In the process of generating love
for your partner’s ugliest bits, you automatically start to work thought the parts of
yourself that feel the most shameful.
1. We tend to love our partner in spots, rather than as a whole. A person is not

a thing - it's a complex process and all parts aﬀect the others. The parts we
hate might feed the very parts we love. To love authentically is to love the
totality of your partner. You can't love chocolate cake if you reject ﬂour,
butter, or sugar. You either love it, or you don't.
2. Notice it, name it, and welcome it with aﬀection.
3. "I see your saucy little bitch coming out. She's welcome her any time. What

can I do to help her feel safe? Does she know how much I love her? I do... I
adore my feisty little bitch."
4. "I sense some anger and jealousy surfacing. Did you know your anger and

jealousy is welcome here? You're my man, and you're just trying to protect
me, that must mean you love me, and I love you, especially when you're
angry."
Holding Space
1. When you realize they’re upset, make physical contact.
2. Make it ok for the other to express their emotions whatever they are,

without you taking it personally, so they can complete their emotional cycle.
Stand in your warrior pose and listen with love. Don't interrupt. What they
are saying isn't necessarily their truth; it's just their emotions talking. BUT IT
IS THEIR TRUTH.
This way, what would have lasted for a long time... dissipates much faster.
