Key Points
1. An exceptional media buyer focuses on making the first dollar, stops planning and starts
doing, masters a single distribution channel at a time, focuses on what’s working, and
measures everything.
2. Media buying is “paid” online advertising.
3. The media-buying landscape has multiple channels:

• Ad Networks are publisher and advertiser aggregators that have auction-based and fixed
pricing

• Co-Registration is primarily CPL-based (cost per lead), targeted on a user interest site and
requires back-end email support

• Portals deliver large amounts of traﬃc, but with high minimum amounts you have to
buy. They are normally CPM (cost per 1000 impressions) based

• PPV is “advertising supporting services” (Adware), which is primarily pop-up and popunder ads highly targeted to specific user profiles

• Search is Google dominated, but there is good opportunity with Bing and Yahoo! as well.
This is another auction-based CPC (cost per click) channel where the advertiser that pays
the most gets the ad spot

• Email is a large channel with lots of opportunity. You can put your own html and creative
in any format you want, and there are multiple levels of pricing

• Social Media allows for heavy demographic targeting, and you can identify a very
specific profile of prospect. Social Media like Facebook and Twitter normally have
auction-based pricing: whichever advertiser pays the most, gets the ad spot

• Mobile is a quickly expanding market, however ad creative is limited by the wide variety
of mobile devices. AdMob (Google) is the dominant mobile advertising platform

• Aﬃliate Networks bring publishers and advertisers together. You can get a large amount
of traﬃc here, but you have to be careful with traﬃc quality, especially because most
networks are CPA (cost/commission per action, i.e. lead or sale)

4. The media buying process includes these steps:

• Figure out who you are (are you an aﬃliate for someone else’s product, or do you sell
your own?)

• Find your competitors (the most successful leave clues)
• Research, study, and emulate (copy without plagiarizing)
• Pick a distribution channel
• Run tests (yes, you have to spend money)
• Get data (and make sure you record it properly)
• Refine and re-test (and do it again)
