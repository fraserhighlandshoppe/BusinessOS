Day 15:
Practicing The Skill Of
Optimism
In this session you'll learn how to avoid "learned helplessness",
how to leverage Optimism to boost your success, the critical
diﬀerence between Optimism and Arrogance, and much more...
Imagine what it would be like to be an Olympic gold medalist. Imagine all the times
you'd feel tired, you'd lose, you'd get injured while practicing... to get to the
winner's circle. All the times in your career that it seemed hopeless. What if you
gave up, just one of those many times? The reality is that if you want to win the
Gold Medal, you can't give up at ANY of those times.
The Paradox Of Believing In Yourself:
1. Whether you think you can or can't, you're probably right.
2. We must ﬁrst do things in our minds before we can do them in reality.

Learned Helplessness – Puppies:
1. A puppy is in a cage with an electrically charged ﬂoor and four high walls it

can't jump over.
2. The experimenters start shocking the ﬂoor. The puppy runs around the cage

trying to escape, but it can't.
3. Eventually it realizes it can't escape, so it just lays down and take the shocks.
4. Then they get another cage, only this one has one low wall that a puppy

could jump over. They put a new puppy in the cage, it gets shocked, and
jumps right out. But when they put the old puppy in the new cage, it's
already "learned" that it can't escape. Instead of jumping out, it lays down
and takes the shocks.
Learned Helplessness – Rats:
1. They ﬁlled two ﬁsh tanks with murky water you can't see through. In one

tank there's a submerged island the rats can't see but will eventually touch
with their hind legs. The other tank has no submerged island.
2. One group of rats swims in one tank, another in the other.

3. Then they take both groups of rats, and put them in the tank that has no

island. The rats that had the island in the previous tank swam for twice as
long before giving up than the rats from the tank with no island.
4. These rats had twice as much drive, energy, and optimism... to keep looking

for the island... than the other rats.
5. If you believe you will succeed, that there is an answer, you have more

energy to persist, and that creates more success.
Depressed people tend to be more realistic than others.
Why does Optimism work? Because to be successful, you have to keep going, keep
trying new things, working through failure, until you ﬁnd a formula that works.
High performers and world-class athletes have the ability to manufacture their own
optimism.
1. They do this by making pictures of themselves performing perfectly,

winning, etc.
Just like we can learn Pessimism... we can learn Optimism too. It's a skill. We build
this skill by practicing creating stories about how things went right in the past, and
how they can go right in the future.
When thinking about the past, think about how it's a miracle that you're alive, your
cells are working, you got born during a time of great innovation and prosperity,
how you are one of 500 billion sperm got through, how you could have died in the
past, etc.
Becoming a high performance person is about seeing what you're doing
successfully now, and believing you can do even more in the future.
There's a sweet spot between pessimism and blind arrogance.
I'm a short term pessimist, long term optimist. This is an advantage I have in life. I
know I may have to write 10 ads before I ﬁnd one works, but I know that eventually
I'll ﬁnd it. Same goes for recipes for my green drinks, etc.
