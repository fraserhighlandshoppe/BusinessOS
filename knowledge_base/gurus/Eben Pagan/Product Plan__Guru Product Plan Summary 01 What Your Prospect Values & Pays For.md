# Guru Product Plan Summary 01 What Your Prospect Values & Pays For.pdf

```markdown
Session 1: What Your
Prospect Values & Pays
For
1. In this program, you’ll learn about the art, science and psychology of turning
information into high value products that people will pay a lot of money for.
2. Info product businesses allow you to come up with ideas, test them, modify
and evolve them very quickly. This is an advantage over other types of
businesses.
3. Formula: “Customers don’t want products… they want results.” When you sell
a RESULT, and you know how to BOOST VALUE and you can charge a lot
more for your products.
4. Key Question to ask yourself: “What is VALUE?”
5. How would your customer value the solution to the challenge they are dealing
with?
6. What is the highest value thing that you can create for them, or teach them?
When you can find that one thing that would be of highest value to them, and
put all your expertise towards creating that... that’s when you’ve got a product
that will sell itself.
7. We create products that are tailored for our prospects’ highest needs.
8. Question: what is the highest value thing for a customer? Answer: the result
that they want. The expectation of the utility they will get - from that result or
outcome - is the strongest persuasion you can use in your marketing.
9. What kind of result do they want? Mental? Emotional? Or Physical? The
highest value results are physical, measurable, and tangible in the real world.
You know something is tangible from the outside if a jury would look at it and
agree that “it happened.”
10. The less work and time it takes to get the result you promise, the higher the
marketing appeal.
11. The highest form of value is the RESULT YOUR PROSPECT WANTS.

12. There are “two types of motivation”: 1) TOWARD what you want, and 2)
AWAY from what you don’t want.
13. The two types of motivation fall into the Physical, Mental and Emotional realm.
14. Humans tend to be twice as motivated by fear (avoiding something you don’t
want to happen) as by desire (getting something you want to have happen).
Problems are twice as motivating as desires.
15. “Consequential Thinking” is cause and effect thinking,. This happens when we
can see the result of how things will play out in the long-term. When humans
have a highly emotional need, they digress into “get my need met now”
survival, animal thinking. And they lose their ability to see things logically with
consequential thinking.
16. Create products that deliver on the ultimate result your customer wants.
17. Urgency: acute issues cause people to take action.
18. Value = Pain + Urgency + the specific result the prospect wants.
```
