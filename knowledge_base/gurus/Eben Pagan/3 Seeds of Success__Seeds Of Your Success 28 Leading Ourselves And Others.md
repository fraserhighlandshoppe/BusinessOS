Day 28:
Leading Ourselves & Others
In this session you'll learn why people are afraid of stepping into
leadership roles, the relationship between leadership and
conﬂict, how "jumping in" as a leader is one of the fastest ways to
grow yourself (and how to do it successfully), and much more...
Imagine being in the dark with a group of people. Then one person lights a torch.
Everyone else is eager to follow the one shining the light.
Most people aren't visionaries - they don't invest time thinking about the future
and creating paths for others to navigate the future successfully.
Visionaries have spent so much time thinking about the future, that they are
already living in the future. They come back and communicate what's happening in
the future to the rest of us.
The leader of a group is often the one who supported other leaders the best.
Most leaders have less "power" than we think they do. Not much really changes as
we switch from leader to leader, because systems are complex and resilient, and
when a new leader comes in, it's not really them making the decisions... it's more
that they're managing the process.
The idea of leadership, having responsibility for a group, triggers fear for many
people. It's easy to criticize leaders - it's much harder to step up and take on the
leader's role.
When you're a leader, you'll be managing conﬂict for as long as you're a leader. It's
important to master conﬂict. Over time you'll get the hang of it.
Even if you're afraid to become a leader, take the leap and go for it. Who you'll need
to develop into is well worth it. Realize you'll make mistakes in public in front of
others, and that's ok. It's part of the leadership process.
Part of your job as a leader is to create an environment where it's ok for others to
share any thoughts, feelings, fears, upsets, etc.
A gorilla will die for his troop. The pilot of the plane that crashed in the Hudson was
the last one oﬀ. A leader is willing to sacriﬁce for his group.

You have to learn to courageously face your fears and situations that would have
rattled you before, so those around you can get emotional stability from you. This
helps develop your own strength.
Leaders push others to become more than they thought possible. They show
people their own inner strength that they didn't know was inside of them.
