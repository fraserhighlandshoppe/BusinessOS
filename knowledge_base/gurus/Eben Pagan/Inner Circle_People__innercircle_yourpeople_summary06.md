YOUR PEOPLE: Session 6
Attracting A-Players
In this session, you’ll learn what our “Virtual Bench” is, how we use it, and how you can unleash it’s
power to fill your company with A-Players who do a stellar job growing your business (and making
everything easier for you).

• An overriding business philosophy I’ve found very useful is something like the old adage “An
ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.” Most of what I teach is designed to keep you from
screwing something up in the first place. Hiring the wrong person is one of those BIG mistakes
that can literally be a death sentence in your business.

• What we do to attract A-Players is something we were doing before we met Brad Smart and his
Topgrading process. It’s called “The Virtual Bench.”

• If you need to hire someone for your business, and you wait until you actually need the person,
then you’re going to pay the “wanting it” tax. That’s the “tax” we all have to pay in the world
whenever we really want (or need) something.

• So, instead of having to deal with all the risk that comes from scrambling at the last minute,
feeling the pressure, having to find someone, choosing someone based on pressure, and needing
to keep them around because you don’t have any choices, we’ve found it’s better to create
relationships with the kind of people you want to work for your company long before you need
them.

• At any given time, I personally am at various stages of discussion with about 10 people that I’m
speaking with about coming to work with us in some capacity. I’ll say something like “You’re a
super star, I can tell, and I can’t see a way for us to work together right now, but I’d love to work
with you at some point… could be years down the road… so stay in touch with me and give me
an update every 90 days just to let me know what you’re up to.”

• I’m always building relationships with people I could see myself working with down the road. The
reason is there’s a time when a need may come up with a particular kind of person –
administrative, technology, creative design, etc. And I want to be ready.

• The objection I get from people when I suggest doing this is that they don’t want to lead people
on, meet with them, and introduce them to people on their team… when they’re not ready to
hire them right NOW. I think that prevents 50-80% of the value people could get by building their
Virtual Bench in the way I’m talking about here.

• In my experience, I’ll take a super star – not a C player – and I tell them “I’d like to know you better,
and I don’t have any projects now but may have some exciting opportunity soon. I’d like you to
meet some people on our team. If it works out down the road, great. If not, that’s great too. And if
I don’t have anything to oﬀer you, I’ve got a bunch of great business friends who may have
something for you in the future as well.”

• Counter-intuitively, the opposite of the fear of “leading someone on” is usually what happens. If
you’re dealing with a star, they don’t want to come to 2 interviews with you, get an oﬀer, and then
have a “job” with you. They want to come kick YOUR tires for a while, too. They see an opportunity
to get to know you over several months as a dream to them.

• Most stars I meet are not totally thrilled in their current role, and at some level they wish they
would have gotten to know the company they’re currently with for a longer period of time. So if
they’re a super star, I’m usually speaking their language.

• We’ve actually institutionalized our Virtual Bench process. We get all our great networkers on our
team on a call together, we add anyone they’ve met to our Virtual Bench list, and move up to the
top of the list anyone who we’re getting close to hiring. We’ll put in a percentage of probability
that a particular person will come to work with us. If we just met someone and we don’t have any
opportunities for them, it may be 10%. Once we do an interview, it may jump up to 60%. By
grading someone by percentage, you can sort your list in Excel and you can see very quickly who’s
at the top of your list.

• Ask yourself, “If our opportunities really grow, what kind of people will we need to hire?” Then
start talking to every person you like, trust, and admire… and ask them if they know anyone like
this. What’s amazing is if you ask them “Do you know of any stars?” they’ll usually answer with no.
But if you get more specific and say, “Our business is really taking oﬀ, and I can see that in the next
3- 6 months we’re going to have a need for a great graphic designer, a great html person, and a
great project manager. Do you know anyone who’s worked with a technology company who’s a
great graphic designer?” you get a much better answer. If you listen as you’re talking to people,
your friends who are stars will talk about their friends who are also stars and who should be on
your virtual bench.
