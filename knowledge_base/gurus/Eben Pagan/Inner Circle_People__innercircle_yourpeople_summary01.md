YOUR PEOPLE: Session 1
The "People" Part Of Your Business
In this session, you'll learn the hiring mistake most small business owners make that costs them
thousands of dollars (if not their entire business), how much money a single mis-hire sucks from your
bottom line, and why hiring "super-stars" makes many of your business problems magically vanish.

• In our first three Inner Circle sessions we talked about you, your market, and your marketing. Here,
we're going to talk about your PEOPLE.

• One of the realizations that will begin dawning upon you as your business grows and starts to
scale on your way to $10 million a year and beyond, is that essentially everything that’s going to
get done is going to get done by other people. You can't do all the stuﬀ that's going to need to
get done. But you’ll also start realizing that people can be tremendous assets, if they're the right
people, they have the right mindset, they're doing the right things, they're working toward the
right visions, etc.

• But, people are also one of the most confounding elements of life. Warren Bennis wrote a book
called “Managing People Is Like Herding Cats”. When most managers or entrepreneurs get into
business, they're typically pretty idealistic, and I've noticed a fascinating paradox.

• We go into business for ourselves because we want to be free. We don't want to have to work for
someone else. We don't want a "boss" who controls our life and our destiny. So we start our own
company. It gets oﬀ the ground, we start getting a little bit of success, we start making some
money, and then we realize "I could hire other people to do this stuﬀ."

• So, we run some ads and do some networking, we find someone who we think is pretty good,
and we hire them. And then we start treating them the way we didn't want to be treated when we
were working for someone else. We start treating them like they should be a machine that does
whatever we want without question. That they should just do what they're told because we're
signing their paycheck. And they should stop trying to think for themselves, because I know
better. And then we run into all kinds of challenges.

• Brad Smart in “Topgrading” say 75% of people hired are mis-hires, and that the average cost of a
mis-hire is around 23 times their yearly salary.

• The big challenge if you hire the wrong person when your business is still small is that you can't
aﬀord 23 times their salary. If you hire the wrong person at this stage, you can have your entire
business crash.

• On the other side of the coin, with a little more understanding of people and the dynamics of
hiring and teamwork, we can get great results. Not only will the right people do a great job
helping run and grow your business, but they'll also attract other great people, because superstars tend to travel with other super-stars. One really profound lesson I've learned is that every
super-star tends to know at least one more, and usually it's more than just one. In our company,
most if not all of our managers and leaders came as a result of someone we know who is a super

• We also have a process where we hire more entry-level or support-type folks who do things like
customer support, administration, and that kind of stuﬀ. And we have a process for sorting and
sifting those people as well. If they really perform at that level, they can have just as much
opportunity as anyone else.

• If you surround yourself with the right people, most of the problems that used to become
problems go away. Your people take care of them. And then you're left to face higher order
problems... challenges that have a much higher payoﬀ if you can solve them.

• What makes a great person for your team are usually not the qualities that most people think of
as being what makes a great potential team member. There's a lot of counter- intuitiveness that
goes on here.

• One area that's sticky with a lot of people, especially intelligent and caring people, is we don't like
to grade and objectively evaluate human beings. But try to open your mind.

• In this Inner Circle, we are going to talk about systems to try to objectively grade human beings;
valuing what they're worth in the work context. This may seem de-humanizing in some way but,
one of the things I've learned about people, especially stars, is they want feedback. They want to
grow. They want to improve themselves. And if you can come up with a system that grades them
but includes information they can use to improve themselves, it becomes a win/win.
