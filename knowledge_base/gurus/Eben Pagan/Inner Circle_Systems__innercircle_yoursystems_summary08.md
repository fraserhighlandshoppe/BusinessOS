YOUR SYSTEMS: Session 8
Turning What You Do Into A Cultural
"Value" In Your Business
In this session, you'll learn how to create "values" in your business' culture, how to move from a "I" to
a "We" orientation in your business, the single most important thing ultra-successful people do that
fuels their success (and that you can start immediately).

• There's a big shift that happens when you go from doing something in your company... to that
thing becoming a "value" of your company that everyone naturally gravitates towards.

• One example is the company that shifted from general advertising to aﬃliate marketing and grew
by 10 times as a result. From now on, you could start saying "We are Optimizers. It's our #1 value.
As an example, we have the shift from advertising to aﬃliate marketing that optimized our
business by 10 times. We created the Question Killer Software that optimized our customer
service issues by decreasing incoming emails by 80%... etc." You're saying it over and over and
giving examples.

• A human can't understand something unless it has a metaphor through which to view it. To shift
from just optimizing, to everyone have that be the value, you have to say that your #1 value is
optimization over and over. You come up with a little 30 seconds rap, and you keep saying it over
and over. Every time a situation comes up, you keep hammering it back to that. Because what
most people do, if they're inside their world, working on answering customer service emails, and
you ask them "How do we reduce these customer service emails by 80%?", whoever is in it will get
scared, become resistant, and tell you it can't be done.

• You have to come back and say something that allows them to have a bigger framework to
operate within. If you say, "One of our values here is optimization. If we multiplied our marketing
investment by 10 times by just focusing on 1 channel, we can reduce our incoming customer
service emails by 80%, because that's only half the optimization we did last time with the shift
from advertising to aﬃliate marketing." If you have a few examples, and you believe in that reality
more than them, it can be powerful.

• I've spent some time talking to Dave Logan, the author of “Tribal Leadership”, about what he's
learned about culture change. He's created a model that's roughly a developmental model of
culture inside of businesses. It's a 5 level model. And it turns out there's this jump or gap between
levels 3 and 4 that's an important transformation. It's the "big shift" that's the challenge for most
cultures. When a company's culture is at Level 3, it's very individual focused and competitive.
People say things like "We're f'd. Our culture is f'd up. People are working too long. They have too
much to do." Everyone is egocentrically focused on their own progress and plight. The transition
to the Level 4 Culture is when people stop referring to themselves as "I" and start referring to
themselves and their culture as "We." It's a quantum leap, because you can't get there from here.
When you're in an "I" mindset, you don't have the reference structures or experiences to
understand how collaboration and real teamwork works, and how it can generate an exponential
quantum leap in results.

• The technique Dave recommends for making this jump happen is what he calls “Triading”.
• In Level 3 cultures there are Diads, which are one to one conversations. You get a lot of gossip,
complaining, and politics. But when there's Triading, when there tends to be 3 people all talking
together and getting involved, the whole dynamic changes. Now there's this 3rd gravitating
body.. it destabilizes the old system... and now you have to work as a team. People who are good
at Triading are good at introducing people to one another and getting them to connect and
collaborate. As you’re building your business, get triads to figure things out, and you facilitate
them. You're creating the space or context within which they are actually doing the work.

• In “The Tipping Point”, Gladwell writes about the power of "weak ties." He shows how important it
is to just have lots of weak ties to lots of people.

• Most people who aren't successful in business think those who have a lot of business success are
part of some old boys network of people they've known for 20 years and they're getting all these
favors from. But reality is a bit diﬀerent. Their success is mostly coming from referrals from weak
ties - people they've only met a few times. Successful people have a lot of these sorts of weak
connections. They are making these connections ALL the time. In fact, the main program in their
mind when they meet anyone is "Who can I introduce you to that will add lots of value to both of
your lives?"

• When you come this type of "connector" person, so many things will happen. If you meet
someone you really like and you'd like to continue a relationship with them but you simply don't
have a lot of time, introduce them to someone in your network in a meaningful and valuable way.
When you do this, they'll have joined your social circle - they'll be "in" - and a bond between you
and them will occur... even though someone else is doing the "work." The more you do this, the
more you have the interesting experience of moving from "I" to "We," and the more good things
will come back to you indirectly as well.
