YOUR MARKETING: Session 3
What I Learned From Jack Trout
And Al Reese
In this session, you'll learn why it's becoming more important than ever to hit your customer's bullseye with your marketing message, how to make every product you make a new "category" in the
mind of your customer, and why most business owners make the mistake of going "wide" instead of
going "narrow" (and what to do instead).

• The reason I like Jack and Al's stuﬀ so much is because they address a level of marketing strategy
that almost no one else talks about it. Al Reese and his daughter wrote a book a few years ago,
“On The Origin Of Brands”. They said that brands evolve just like species do. Divergence is the rule.

• In “Positioning”, one of the principles was that we are the first over-communicated society. In the
past we could deal with how much communication is happening. Fifty years ago there were only
3 channels. If there was a rocket launch, everyone was watching it. Now you have to decide do
you want to watch the rocket, or the Olympics, or the war, or the murder across the street. And
where do you want to watch it: on your TV, or your computer, or your phone, etc.?

• The reason why it's important that we "get" the idea that over-communication is a problem is that
it makes it even more important for you to focus your message like a laser beam: understand
exactly who your prospect is and their hot button need, and align the message so it really hits the
bullseye. So these needs are rising together - over communication and the important of being
"on point" with your message.

• In their book, “The 22 Laws Of Immutable Marketing”, one of the core concepts was "It's better to
be first than it is to be better." Positioning is something you do in the mind, not in the product. It's
better to be first than it is to be better implies that when you're starting a new product, creating a
new category in the mind of the perceiver is more important than anything things else you can
do. And tell people you created it and sell the new category - why it's so important. If there's not
news in what you're doing, then no one can hear it. You can also leverage this when you go to
partners to promote your stuﬀ - let them know you're the first or #1 product in the category... that
you, in fact, CREATED the category.

• Whenever you create a new product, ask yourself, "What new category am I creating in the mind?
How can I present it as news? And, most importantly, is it based on a problem or need the
customer has?"

• Al Reese and his daughter wrote another book called “Focus”. That's where I got the idea of
narrowing your focus. Most people widen their focus. This is a huge mistake.

• When you combine all these ideas - it's better to be first than it is to be better, narrow your focus,
etc. - a picture of strategy starts to appear in your mind. Figure out your customers' needs, what
language they use, look where things are headed, make every new product you create a new
category, etc. If you really create a new category out of every product - not just out of your
company, but out of every product - it makes it MUCH harder for your competitors to make you
obsolete.
