Session 8: Hiring Your First
Virtual Assistant For Your
Business
• Assistant Approach #1: Know what personality traits you're looking for when you start - and
start with an "Organizer." For simplicity's sake, let's assume there are two kinds of people in the
world. "Creatives" who generate ideas, like to change things, and do things at last minute.
"Organizers" make lists and plan, create daily schedules, and keep things on track. The first step
to finding an Organizer is to know what you're looking for one - the kind of person who wakes
up in the morning and creates a to-do list and a plan for the day. If you're a Creative type, an
Organizer will really help you stay on track. If you're an Organizer, a Creative will help take
busy work off your plate.

• Assistant Approach #2: Write your ad in a friendly, candid, personal tone - and explain exactly
what you want and don't want. (You'll probably use Craigslist or eLance to find your assistant.)
Remember there's an individual human on the other end who is going to read the ad, so make it
personal and warm. "Wanted: Super Organized Person To Help Me Organize My Business" or
"Wanted: Organizational Genius To Work In A Fun Environment. I'm looked for a highly
organized, detailed oriented, logical person to come to work for me in a real fun role to help me
organize my business." Instead of listing out technical qualifications, talk to them like a human,
"It's important you have a few years working with Microsoft office because..." or "If you don't
have a lot of experience with Microsoft Office then this role probably isn't for you because you'll
be using it a lot and there won't be time to learn." I often write ads that are multiple pages long
so I can tell the reader everything I'm looking for and not looking for.

• Assistant Approach #3: Ask candidates to write you a personal message answering several
questions and explaining why they're a good fit. If you really want to get sophisticated read
Brad Smart's Top Grading and use some of his questions. Questions can include "Please write
me a personal message and tell me what is your skill level using Microsoft Office...." etc. When
you ask them 5 questions or so, a big percentage will disqualify themselves by sending their
resume instead of following these simple directions.

• Assistant Approach #4: List all of the different qualifications you'd like in your ad - and explain
each of them in as much detail as possible. Really challenge yourself to come up with a list of all
the things you're looking for before you write the ad. One of the things you'll learn is that the
Superstars out there are as interested in questioning you and learning about your business as
you are about them. While C-Players are just looking for a job, Superstars are picky - they're
looking for a stair step in their career. They'll appreciate your long list of requirements.

• Assistant Approach #5: Enter into an email dialog with the best candidates, and note how they
respond, how long they take to get back to you, etc. Go deeper into their responses and ask
them more about their work history. When you ask about their work history, say "When I talk to
your boss and I ask them what your biggest strengths and weaknesses were, what are they
going to tell me?" This is like "truth serum" and will motivate them to give you truthful answers.
Powerful stuff. Superstars will respond quickly, be professional, and give answers that make
sense to you. You'll disqualify another 50% of respondents at this step.

• Assistant Approach #6: Delegate repetitive, frequent tasks first - like customer service, refunds,
tracking, reporting - by creating an "SOP" (Standard Operating Procedure). If you're going to
have them do customer service, open up a word document and tell them "Here's the different
kind of customers we have... here are the typical requests... here are the steps for you for each
request..."

• Assistant Approach #7: Remember that your objective is to free up as much time as possible
for... you guessed it... products and marketing. As your business grows, you shouldn't be
handling a bunch of stuff besides products and marketing. If you have a lot of busy work, you're
not doing a good job of getting stuff off your plate... or you may even be ADDICTED to your
busy work. This a big problem to look at honestly.

• Assistant Approach #8: Hire a "local" assistant to do your personal shopping, errands, etc. giving you more time for products and marketing. One of the best investments I make in my life
is having a personal assistant to take all the busy work off my plate - shopping, errands, dry
cleaning, etc.. As you grow your business, this becomes more and more important. The more
time you have for products and marketing, the more successful you will become. You can start
off slow - hire an assistant to take just 2-4 hours a week of busy work off your plate. It can make
a big difference in your productivity and your business.

• Assistant Approach #9: Keep delegating tasks to your assistant - you should ideally be either
doing money-making activities or relaxing. Avoid the "grey zone" where you're not being super
productive but you're not completely relaxing either. People making 6 and 7 figure outcomes
aren't wasting much time - they're focusing on the highest leverage tasks in their business, and
when they're not, they're recharging their batteries in a deep way.
