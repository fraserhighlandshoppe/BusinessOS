YOUR MARKETING: Session 8
The Healthy Brain Project
In this session, I go really deep with a father and son team who have a revolutionary brain
supplement. This is real, live marketing consulting, in-the-moment. Check it out...

• The problem with the term "Healthy Brain Supplements" is that it's ambiguous. It could mean
supplement for a healthy brain, or healthy supplements for the brain. In your marketing, always
try to stay away from ambiguity.

• Instead of Healthy Brain Supplements, we boil down the message to its core and meet the
customer where they're at. What are the words the customer would use to describe their
problem? "More forgetful" - good, but passive tense. "Forgetting more often." - better, it's active
and captures the customer's experience of noticing an INCREASE in their forgetfulness.
"Forgetting names more often?" - even better, because it zooms in more specifically to what the
customer is noticing.

• What does the customer think is causing the forgetfulness? Alzheimer's.
• What is the customer fearful that Alzheimer's might mean for them? Being moved to a
nursing home.

• So a series of powerful questions becomes: Forgetting names more often? Fear it might be
Alzheimer's? Want to avoid the nursing home? This is a bullet-proof line of reasoning, because it's
entirely from the customer's perspective. It's totally separate from what your own expertise to
cure the thing.

• One idea we came up with is Namenesia. You could say something like, "I call it Namenesia, and if
you find yourself forgetting names more often, and you're starting to have a little fear that this
could be Namenesia, then you need to go to my site and download my free 17-page report,
because it could keep you out of the nursing home..."

• Get the core equation as tight as possible, then only add words that make the message more
powerful and more memorable. The hot button here is forgetting, that's the one word message.
"Forgetting Names" is two words, and I think it's worth adding "names" in this situation. And then
we add "more often" because it describes the change that the customer notices.

• So, "Forgetting Names More Often," I think its worth it to get all of them in there, because without
all of them I can't know that this is exactly for me. It's the shortest way I can say it and have it
resonate with all of your customers.

• So how do we get "Forgetting Names More Often" in the name of the book? We want to name the
book the thing that's the problem in their own words. Is there a way to say forgetting Names
more often that sounds like a book title, that sounds like the answer to it is in the book?

• And how do you include the idea that forgetting names more often is worse than you think... it
means something ELSE and MORE IMPORTANT is wrong with you? And, of course, you need to
find a way to say it that doesn't sound weird. Forgetting names more often is already a big chunk adding compound meanings on top if it will confuse the customer.

• “Namenesia: Why Forgetting Names More Often Is Worse Than You Think”. Or “How To Stop
Forgetting Names And Stay Out Of The Nursing Home”. What I'm pointing to here is you may not
need to go that far. "Namenesia: Are you forgetting people's names more often lately? It’s more
dangerous than you think..." That's a potential way of wording it.

• So the end game - what the book is really about - is a vehicle that sets up the purchase of the
supplements.

• Supplements that stop Namenesia and keep you out of the nursing home. As soon as they open
up the book, there's a self-scoring test with questions like, "Have you been forgetting people's
names more often lately? Do you perceive yourself as more forgetful? Are you forgetting
numbers?" etc.

• At the bottom of the test it says, "If you answered yes to more than 3 of these questions, then you
probably have minimal cognitive impairment. I call it Namenesia. If your score is more than 3, turn
to chapter 1 right now because it could save your memory and keep you out of the nursing
home." That's the bridge between the cover and chapter 1.

• Now, chapter 1 has to say in no uncertain terms:
"If this or this is happening, you probably have this. Doctors don't know what it is, and you're
probably afraid of going to the nursing home. In fact, here's the story of 39-year old attractive and
successful Mr. X. He started forgetting names, his doctor didn't know what it was... 3 surgeries and
4 years later he was admitted to a nursing home. But there's a better way. Let me introduce
myself... my name is Dr. X. I've spend the last 25 years studying this phenomenon. I lot has
happened in the last few years. We used to be in the dark about this - we didn't really know what
was going on. In fact you may heard about using x therapy and patients still winding up with x, y,
and z. Lately I've been doing these new experiments, we've discovered this and that. With a few
simple changes to your diet you can stop this in it's tracks and keep yourself out of the nursing
home." Then tell a success story. "This person came to us, 56 years old, they had this problem, I put
them on our proprietary supplement and diet plan, and it immediately arrested the development
of this process and now their fine. I've done this with a bunch of people." The first one we tell the
whole story, the second we just say we put them on a diet and supplement regimen, so that the
reader knows they need to read the book to get the solution. You can even say, "... and that's what
this book is about. I'm going to give you the exact steps you need to take to make progress just
like this person did..."
