YOUR SYSTEMS: Session 3
Feedback To Transform Your Business
In this session, you'll learn why many business owners unconsciously encourage employee behavior
they don't like, the dangerous of punishing "bad" behavior, and how to use technology to increase
the quality of feedback in your company (and in the process, take your business to the next level).

• Feedback has the power to shape behavior. When we think of "positive" behavior and "negative"
behavior, we sometimes think of this in judgmental, moralistic terms. But Systems Thinking has an
interesting and useful take on this.

• In Systems Thinking, positive behavior is anything that increases growth, and negative behavior is
anything that represses growth. You can either encourage a behavior or discourage a behavior.
We can view thinking as a behavior too. You can't put something out of your mind, but you can
put something else in. So, if you want something out of your mind, you can drive it out by putting
something more powerful in.

• If you want someone to change, do you give them carrots or sticks? It depends on the individual
and circumstances. Not all carrots and not all sticks work. You need to find what the mixture is
that'll work for the individual in question.

• There's an interesting book called “Don't Shoot the Dog”. It talks about behavior change using the
case of a neighbor's dog barking as an example. One option is to shoot the dog - it'll never bark
again, but you're going to have new problems to deal with (one is a lawsuit).

• Another option is to yell at it, but if the dog is barking because it wants attention and you yell at
it, you're giving it attention, so it's going to just bark more. In this case, the dog is training YOU.
This is an example of negative reinforcement - you're punishing the undesired behavior.

• Positive reinforcement would be petting the dog when it's not barking. That's reinforcing the
behavior you do want, instead of the behavior you don't want. When someone does what you
want, you reward them. When they do what you don't want, you ignore them. There's one school
of psychotherapy that when the client says anything positive, the therapist pays attention to
them. When they say something negative, they therapist ignores them. This sort of feedback
helps to shape the thinking of the client into more positive modes.

• How can we increase the quality of the feedback, and the speed of the feedback, to improve your
business? I was talking to a good friend who has a business, and he's hiring people quickly and
doing direct marketing online. He was hiring smart, inexperienced young people to work in the
marketing area of his business, and he didn't have the time to go through the process to teach
these people all the principles of direct marketing. They just needed something to implement
quickly. So he had his technologist implement feedback systems so when the new people made
changes to online marketing creative, they could watch in real time as their results changed. This
way they could see with their own eyes the results of what was going on. This trained them very
quickly. They got higher quality and faster feedback by using technology.

• My experience is that when business owners start a little dashboard with their metrics on it, and
they start watching their key numbers every day, it takes very little time for them to get a diﬀerent
feeling about their business. A feeling of stability, of understanding how things eﬀect one
another. By watching it every day they get the feedback faster, and by making it visual they make
the feedback higher quality. This has a big impact on their ability to drive the business forward.

• Emotions can be a way to get feedback as well. In EST trainings, they used to do a process where
they would have someone experience a feeling of emotion, and they would close their eyes,
notice where they felt the emotion, what the physical details of it, any color/shape/texture the
noticed, etc. The person would stay focused on the feeling of the emotion, and the body would
process the emotion, and usually very quickly it would process "out" the emotion because they
were staying with it in the moment.

• What most people do when they feel an emotion is they stay focused on the things triggering the
emotion, so they're not actually feeling the emotion. This prevents them from allowing
themselves to go through a complete emotion cycle. But, by tuning into the emotion itself, being
present to it in real time, it can allow us to process it out. In many cases in the EST trainings, the
feeling was over in that moment and they couldn't feel it again, because for the first time they
had felt it all the way through.
