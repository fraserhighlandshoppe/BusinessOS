YOUR MARKETING: Session 5
Your Marketing Equation
In this session, you'll learn as I work with several people from the audience on their marketing
"equation," turning their vague ideas into riveting marketing messages that compel
the masses to buy.

• A book I highly recommend you check out is “The 5 Love Languages”. The idea here is that
essentially there are 5 language through which we express and receive love. These languages are
Gifts, Acts of Service, Quality Time, Words of Aﬃrmation, and Physical Touch.

• If I'm a Words of Aﬃrmation guy, I need to hear words of aﬃrmation like "I love you" and "You're
wonderful." If you're a Gifts person, you'll complain that words are cheap, and I'll complain that
you're materialistic. If you didn't know that there were 5 love languages, you would never tune
into the fact that this is what's happening at a deeper level. How does this relate to marketing?
Well, you can use it to really dive deep and figure out the exact "language" your customer needs
to hear to feel a connection with you.

• For example, the next time a customer writes in and compliments you on caring so much about
them, ask them what it is specifically that communicated to them that you care? Was it the words
in a newsletter? Or the fact that your customer service rep responded so quickly? Or...

• A person in pain is oriented to this moment in reality and they want out. And when they're in this
moment of pain or desire, they're very idealistic. They believe if they get the magic pill then their
whole future will be happy and amazing. If you enter into that window with them and you say
something like, "I can teach you 4 magic words..." there's a part of them that believes it will solve
all their problems for the rest of their life. When you're overwhelmed by a problem, don't
underestimate the power of idealism when it grips you. Use this knowledge to help people invest
in your product or service that will help them.

• One of the best things you can do is to boil your marketing message down to a short, simple, 2-5
word equation. Then, figure out how to have a manifestation of the equation using words or
pictures. Think about how to "show" this equation to your prospects. Once you have the equation,
you're more than half way there. It's then all about split testing and measuring results.

• Whenever I break anything into chunks, I'm looking at an ideal number of 3-7 pieces. For example,
if the equation we're trying to refine is "Internet = No Rejection," I'll want to break up the vague
term "Internet" into 3-7 things my prospects thinks make up the Internet. It could be email,
Google, having a website, porn, and YouTube. Which one of these 5 would be the more powerful
one to put equation? Which one sounds the easiest and infers the least amount of work?

• If we take the equation "Forgetting Grandchild's Name = Nursing Home," we again will want to
break down the vague term "nursing home" into 3-7 real-life components. When we break it
down, don't break it down into formal "categories." Break it down into the chunks normal humans
use to think about it. When chunking, you can go too far with zooming out or with zooming in.
We need to zoom at just the right level.
