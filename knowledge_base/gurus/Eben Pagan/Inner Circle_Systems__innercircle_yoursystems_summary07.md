YOUR SYSTEMS: Session 7
The Culture Of Your Business
In this session, you'll learn how we consciously create culture inside of our own business, why we
have no internal job titles, and what the benefit is of having a culture that emphasizes genuine
respect for each human being - no matter where they are in the "hierarchy" of the company.

• It's important to remember that the culture of any business is a system, so all the things we said
about systems apply to business cultures as well. For example, when you push against it, it pushes
back. You never do just one thing. Short term and long term eﬀects are often disconnected and
opposite. Etc.

• I want to give you a couple examples from our business about how we consciously create
culture...

• A lot of people look at our business and think we just market dating advice for men. They don't
see the bigger picture. They imagine we have a bunch of dorky young guys who are just
interested in picking up women working for the company. But when they meet the people who
work for our company, they get a completely diﬀerent picture. They're usually very impressed. The
people who work for us are mature, sensitive, family- oriented people. The reality is that our
company isn't about what it seems it's about. It's really about personal transformation.

• It's about helping people get to the next levels in their lives and transforming themselves... and in
the process also becoming more interesting, attractive people to be around. And sure we begin
with teaching guys how to start conversations, etc., but that's not really what it's about. If you
read the testimonials that people give us, most of them aren't "Dude, I learned your pick up line
and I'm getting laid like crazy." Instead, the stories that come in are "You know I bought your
book, and I went through it, and I'm getting more dates, but this other thing is happening and it's
really changed by life." And when we read these stories in front of the team, it communicates
powerfully what we're about. I don't have to say "We're not about helping people get dates and
meet women." I simply read a few of these stories and testimonials in front of the group. They
communicate that our value is transformation of people's lives, not picking up chicks are
whatever. And this helps us attract and keep high quality people working for us.

• Another important element of our culture has to do with hierarchy. One of my personal values
when I started the business was that I didn't like the whole corporate, top-down, authoritarian
control thing. And I didn't think the people who would want to work for us would like it either.
Some business do just fine with this corporate structure right from the beginning. But I saw that
inside of organizations, you have the formal hierarchy... but then there's this other thing going
on... how things REALLY get done. The people who are the true "drivers" of business often don't
like formal hierarchy all that much. So I tried to avoid that, but still have a successful business.

• In this vain, we don't use titles internally in our business. Now, there's a downside to this. With no
organization chart, when you come to work for us it takes a little longer to figure out how the
whole system works and who does what. The advantage is they have to learn who does what

• No titles also creates this concept of "we're all equal as humans." We have mutual respect for each
other. Whenever humans get together there's always a status hierarchy, but if it happens
organically, naturally, people are a lot more accepting of it. I used to tell people, "If anyone treats
you disrespectfully or less than because they make more money than you, you tell me." I would
tell everyone this because I really wanted it in our culture. We could be in a room and someone
could make $200,000 a year and another person $7/hour, but we're all paying attention and we're
all listening, because that individual is a valuable human being - we selected them - and whatever
they're talking about has potential value for the business.

• Another cultural element that's important in our business is relationships. I think of this as
positive politics. Negative politics includes backstabbing, gossip, etc. But there's positive politics
too, which is having personal relationships with all the people you work with, having a bond with
them so you can get things done very quickly. One thing I do very often on our calls is I make a lot
of jokes with the team, because I know humor and inside jokes and everyone laughing together
creates the feeling of bond, unity, and relationship. That's just one way we build relationships and
a feeling of camaraderie inside our business.
