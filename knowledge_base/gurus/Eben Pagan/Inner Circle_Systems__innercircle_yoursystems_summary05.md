YOUR SYSTEMS: Session 5
Incentives, Brainwashing, & Robots
In this session, you'll learn how armies use subtle incentives to "brain-wash" POW's, how to align the
incentives on your website to ramp up your profits quickly, and how to turn metrics themselves into
incentives that motivate your team to higher and higher levels of output.

• It seems that if the incentive is small but still present, it not only still motivates someone to do
something, but when they do it they won't consciously associate the motivation with the
incentive... they'll associate the motivation with themselves.

• For example, in Vietnam, the Vietnamese used some powerful incentive-like techniques. In one
case, the American POW would be oﬀered a few cigarettes or a little food if they entered and won
an essay writing contest on why the American system of government is not perfect. Then the
winner's essay would be read on the loud speakers in the camp. Whoever entered the contest
couldn't say to themselves they wrote the essay because of a threat, because there was no threat.
And they couldn't say they did it for some big reward, because the reward was just a few
cigarettes or whatever. So the American POW's who wrote these essays began believing they
must have done it because this is how they truly felt inside - that they had some anti-U.S. feelings.
In other words, their self-images began to change. They began to see themselves as people who
didn't agree with the American machine.

• In regard to aligning incentives, one example we can look at is the choice of words on an order
button on your website. People show me their websites all the time. I'll ask them what do you
want people to do when they're on your site. They may say they want people to opt in to their list.
But when I look on the site, I can't even figure out how to opt in to their list. I literally don't see
how to do it. How can you expect a visitor to opt in when I can't even figure out how to do it? Or
in other cases, people will have a great "cookie" (an incentive) for opting in, but the incentive isn't
obvious. An example of aligning the incentive is to take whatever the cookie or incentive is and
feature it... make it the dominant visual element on the page. That way the incentive is aligned
with what you want the visitor to do.

• An interesting question to ask is, "How do you turn metrics into incentives?" One example is what
we did on our Customer Service Team. What we did wasn't too much, it was just enough. We used
a piece of software to visually show everyone on the team how many tickets per hour each of the
other team members we're doing. By having everyone see everyone's performance - by making it
"transparent" - it increased our eﬃciency by 50%. Now the person who's not performing that well
knows how they are performing relative to the top performers and is motivated to learn how to
perform better, and the top performers realize how great of a job they're doing and keep doing
it... perhaps even sharing their "secrets of success" with the team members who are having a
harder time.

• One of the big paradoxes of humanity is that we like to think of each person as a special, unique
perfect individual... but most of us act like robots, all the time. The way to wake up and become
more free and take more control of our lives and eﬀect reality more is to see and realize how
much of a robot we are. That perception that we are these automatic machines, and noticing that
we do most things unconsciously, see if you can get into that mode for several days. Just observe
it as it's happening... here I am staying up late when I said I wouldn't. When you see this you can
begin making changes to the system itself.
