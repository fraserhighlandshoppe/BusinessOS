Session 6:
Communicating In Romantic
Relationships
In session 6 you’ll learn how to communicate in your romantic relationship. You’ll learn how
aggression and sex are tightly intertwined, and how masculine and feminine energy can create and
maintain attraction between you and your partner.
• When it comes to dating, mating, love and intimacy, the logical brain goes out the window.
We’ve been mating a lot longer than we’ve been thinking logically.
• Conrad Lorenz, in his book “On Aggression”, discovered that whenever there is aggression it’s
almost always happening between members of the same species. When a lion kills a gazelle, for
example, that is predator behavior, not aggressive behavior.
• Also, when two animals of the same species are fighting it almost never results in death, only
submission. Most fighting and aggression has to do with mating.
• If you want to maintain attraction between you and your partner, you must understand how it
began in the first place. Sexual attraction is stimulated by social status and polarity. Example:
Women tend to not feel sexual attraction towards men they perceive to be lower in social status.
• David Deida points out in “The Way Of The Superior Man”, that masculine and feminine energy
are the two polarities that create attraction. The more masculine the man and feminine the
woman, the stronger the attraction.
• Masculine energy is consciousness, presence, depth, solid masculine eye contact, and posture.
That is what’s attractive to the feminine.
• Feminine energy is about movement, light, being seen as beautiful, and being pursued.
• A shift has occurred with the masculine and feminine polarity where men are acting more
feminine and women are acting more masculine.
• If you want to increase attraction, men need to increase the masculine energy and women need
to increase the feminine energy. If you do this, you’ll find that it will literally turn both of you on.
• Sex and aggression are wired together in the old parts of our brain. It’s not an exaggeration to
say that if your partner is angry or aggressive, they are probably horny.

• David Deida recommends that if your partner is angry or upset that pouring sexual energy over
them will most likely diffuse the anger and aggression. Using this information is 100% your
responsibility, so proceed with caution.
• You can see conflict and fighting as a problem, or as the seeds of deeper intimacy and growth.
• The master key for self-development and growth is noticing where you snag, trip, and what your
triggers are in life. These snags are your greatest source of personal growth.
• Rules for communicating inside romantic conflict:
• We humans get emotionally triggered and we loose the ability to think rationally.
• In a relationship, one person triggered is already a danger zone but both people triggered is
a red alert emergency. When two people are triggered at the same time that is when the
relationship breaks down.
• So, the rule of thumb is only one person is allowed to be triggered at a time. If you notice your
partner being triggered you should stop and notice that the relationship is in danger. Try not to
get triggered yourself. If you both get triggered, call a time out, and take time apart for 10
minutes.
• If there is a snag or conflict don’t defend who is right or wrong. The rule is, one
communication direction at a time. Either you are communicating with your partner or they
are communicating with you.
• Hold a safe space for each other. Allow your partner to talk so they can go through a
complete emotional cycle. Only after that, you can talk it though while your partner holds
space for you. It’s important to be able to say exactly how you both feel without fear of
rejection.
• Humans try to “smuggle” emotions, blame, and aggression inside of trivial issues. Example: if
you’re mad that your partner left the cap off the toothpaste, it’s probably not even close to
what you’re really mad about. We use these things as excuses to get upset about deeper
issues. When we start to see this, it helps us get a little more control of the situation.
• Vortex issues are like getting too close to the edge of something that will suck you down like a
whirlpool. Just talking about these issues will immediately trigger your emotions. They are
important to identify because you know that one or both of you are about to be triggered.
• Create a safe space for your partner - say, “I will hold safe space for you if you want to
share”. You must match your message with your tonality, gestures, and body language to
maintain trust and let them know they are safe.
• Being vulnerable to your partner gives them the ability to come to your rescue. On the other
hand, when you’re blaming them it just pushes a wedge between you.
• Read the book, “The 5 Love Languages”. It will help you to understand the ways we each
express and receive love.
