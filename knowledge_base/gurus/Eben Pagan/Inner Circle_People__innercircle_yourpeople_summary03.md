YOUR PEOPLE: Session 3
Unlocking Meta-Programs To Boost
Your Team's Productivity
In this session from Wyatt, you'll learn how to use Meta-Programs to select the right people for the
right job and unleash your team's productivity. You'll learn how to match someone's natural interest
to the perfect job for them within your business, how to identify detail people and select them for
diﬀerent jobs than big picture people, when to hire independent people and, when to hire
compliant people.

• Wyatt divides Meta Programs into 4 main types, and it corresponds with the 4 activities we do in
life:
1. We take in information
2. We evaluation information
3. We motivate ourselves
4. We move into action

• When we take in information, the main meta program here is Sameness/Diﬀerence. Another meta
program is Primary Interest, which refers to what individuals are naturally interested in and drawn
toward.

• Not everyone is interested in the same thing. The 5 Primary Interests are: People, Place, Thing,
Activity, and Information.

• When you're hiring this makes a diﬀerence. You want “People” people for sales and phone work.
You want “Activity” people engaged in doing and action. You want “Place” people if there's a
particular place you need to focus your eﬀorts. And you want “Thing” people to be the technicians
- they're more interested in equipment than people.

• Another element of how we take in information to look at when hiring is Chunk Size. Are people
global, or specific? For certain jobs you want people who naturally focus on the big picture, for
other jobs you want people who can really go into the fine details. Some people can't see the
forest from the trees, some people can't see the trees from the forest. Depending on the job, you
got to figure out what level of detail for which you should hire.

• After we take in information, we evaluate it. At this stage, one important meta program is
Internal / External Frames.

• People with external frames need to be told when they're doing a good job. They need
continually feedback, whether it's positive or negative. People with internal frames know when
they're doing a good job, they don't need feedback, and they're not going to listen to your
feedback anyway.

• So, if you want someone who is going to be compliant, hire someone with an external frame. If
you want someone who you don't need to continually praise and blame, you need to hire
someone with an internal frame.

• This can become a real frustration, because when you're managing people with internal frames,
they don't give a damn what you think. They know when they've done a good job or not. You
need to decide if this sort of "independent" person is who you need to hire. They're harder to
work with, but for most jobs that require more discrimination, you're going to want people with
internal frames because you can't be running around giving them continual feedback on what
they're doing.

• This is critically important in hiring - do you want someone who is compliant, or do you want
someone who can think for themselves? Just remember, they won't always think the way that you
think.

• Decisions is another critical meta program. The reasons we make the decisions we do is because
they look right, sound right, feel right, or make sense. Only 10% of decisions are made because
they make sense, but the make sense people have taken control of the world with a simple
question... why? When someone asks you why, you usually get triggered into giving "reasons"...
even though 90% of the time the real answer is it either looked right, sounded right, or felt right.

• Ninety-nine percent of eﬀorts to get people to change fail, because we're trying to use REASONS
to get the other person to change. If you're going to persuade someone, logic and reason won't
work.

• You need to change the way they see it, hear it, or feel it. You need to know what mode you tend
to rely on to make decisions, and the modes each of your employees use, because if you
communicate with them in the wrong way it simply won't work.

• Understand that diﬀerent people make decisions for diﬀerent reasons, and give up that question
that's done more to destroy decisions in the world than anything else... the question "why?"
Instead ask "What does that look like? What does that sound like? What does that feel like?"
