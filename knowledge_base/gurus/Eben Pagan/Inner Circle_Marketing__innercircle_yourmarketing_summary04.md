YOUR MARKETING: Session 4
Positioning
Ever wonder how it could be that the marketing strategies of Coke, Ferrari, Southwest Airlines, and 3
Day Blinds are so diﬀerent from one another, yet each one works so powerfully? The answer is
Positioning. In this session, you'll learn what Positioning is, the 6 core Positioning strategies, and how
YOU can use them to chisel a unique and secure place for your business in even the most
competitive markets.

• There's basically only a handful of common strategies for marketing. Most I learned from Michael
Porter, Jack Trout, Al Reese, and a few other folks. One is the strategy of Positioning.

• One Positioning strategy is to be the Premium, or the high-priced high-quality leader of your
industry... the "best." The Ferrari.

• Another is to be the Value or low priced option - to be the Southwest Airlines, Hyundai, 99 cent
store, etc.

• Another Position that's very powerful is The Original, for example, Coke, Levi's, etc. The authentic
one.

• Speed is another powerful Position - for example, 3 day blinds.
• Another marketing Position is Heritage, talking about the sequence of historical events and the
story behind how the product or company came to be.

• How can you begin framing your position around one of these strategic marketing approaches?
Pick one to play with in your mind. Say you were the premium brand. How would it aﬀect your
decisions, who you hire, your marketing, how you present your company? Who would you start
targeting as your customer? How would their expectations change?

• What if you were the Value option (the lowest priced)... how would that change things? (By the
way, it's very diﬃcult to win the price game because many companies try to compete on price.
The Premium is also challenging because your customers expect the best, and if they don't get
the absolute best, they don't like it much.)

• So how would you slant your communication? How would you tell the story, how would you
frame your business, if you were The Original? The Premium? The Lowest Priced? The Fastest?

• With some Positioning strategies, be careful of going overboard. Let's say you have a push-button
website creation service, and your Positioning is speed, and you say you can get your whole
business online in 5 minutes. It just doesn't sound realistic. So, when you're using speed, you can
go too far and make it so fast that it's unbelievable. You need to find the right balance between
being fast and being believable.

• Making your marketing work is about getting the gut-level feel from the perspective of your
customer. Would your customer think 5 minutes is too fast? Would they think 1 day is too long?
Put yourself in their position and imagine how they would feel about it, whether or not they
would feel it's believable or a scam, etc.

• These approaches work because humans are wired a particular way. They are overlays to the
human psyche. When you say, "Magic 4-word phrase to eliminate back talk," I don't say "Well, she
must have gone to a seminar and figured out an ingenious way to rope me in." No. Instead I say,
"Finally! It's what I wanted for so long..." That's how humans process this type of communication.
We're wired to like speed, to think heritage is cool, to want to be the first or original, etc.

• The problem that most of us have in marketing is that someone else can have a product that is
similar but not quite as good as ours, but they are making 100 times as much as we do. What's
going on here? When I ask people who's your customer and what do you do, no one ever spits it
out in 7 words. It takes awhile for them to communicate it, and usually at the end of the multiminute explanation, no one is compelled. Isn't that weird? We have great products here, but we
can't explain them quickly and compellingly. That's the marketing challenge.

• Find the small overlap between the thing you do best and the thing the customer wants most.
• Figure out what your customer wants, then build your content around that. Give your customer
exactly what they want. Go from taking what you already have and slanting it to appeal to your
customers... to focusing on your customers' problems FIRST and creating new content that exactly
fits what they want and need.
