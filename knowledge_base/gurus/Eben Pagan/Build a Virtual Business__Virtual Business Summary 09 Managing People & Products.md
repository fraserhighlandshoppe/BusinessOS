Session 9: Managing People
& Products In The Virtual
Environment
• Management Approach #1: Recruit and hire Stars only - and make sure they have passion
and a proven track record of successfully completing projects. Stars work for impact, not for
money. Sure the money is important, but it's not their primary motivation. They want to make a
difference in the world and see the impact of their actions. To see if they have passion, ask them
why they're interested in the position and what it is about this area that interests them. To see if
they have a proven track record, ask them for past projects they've driven to completion
successfully.

• Management Approach #2: TOPGRADE by doing a serial, in-depth interview - and by using
the "Promise Of Reference Check" technique. Ask them, "When I talk to your boss, what are
they going to say are your strengths and weaknesses?" Implement this as an ongoing procedure
in your hiring process. Really dig into their work history - it'll help you identify the Stars. One of
the qualities I look for is the quality of "driver." A driver is someone who takes responsibility and
likes to deliver results. It's not someone who shows up to work to get a pay check. Stars have the
drive and aspirations to take responsibility for creating results. Also, take note if someone is too
smooth of a talker - I've learned these people are often sporadic performers. It seems to me that
people who aren't able to deliver consistent results have learned how to be charming and get
people to like them before they look at their results. Smooth talkers are usually not smooth
performers. And the reverse is often true - many of the top drivers aren't the most charismatic
people in the word. They don't place a high priority on appearances - they place a high priority
on results.

• Management Approach #3: Test prospective team members by having them do a task that
reveals how well they can actually do the work - LIVE. Give them something that shows how they
perform. For example, give them an assignment from your own business and have them write
some copy (if you're looking for a copywriter). Delay judgment on a new hire at least 30-90
days AFTER you hire them. Remember that most hires are mis-hires... so don't get too attached
too soon.

• Management Approach #4: Ask all team members to create a 5-minute "Daily Update" that
shares their results, challenges and questions. Tell them "At the end of the day, what I'd like you
to do is write me an email. It shouldn't take you any more than 5 minutes. If it does, you're doing
something wrong. In the first section put the results that you achieved today, in the second section
put the challenges and problems you had today, and in the third section put questions you have
for other team members." This forces people to assess how they performed and to make this
clear to you. There should be about 2-3 items on each section, one sentence each. It's very
simple. What you'll find is that some team members will do the daily update, but some won't. Of

• Management Approach #5: Do a daily "huddle" call or video conference to update and
touch base with your team members. By each person giving a quick update, everyone hears
what's going on in the other areas of the business. Making this daily is a great "rhythm" to get
into. Have the call last 10-20 minutes.

• Management Approach #6: Create a "Daily Dashboard" with charts of your top business
numbers and metrics - and have your team update it daily. You can use a spreadsheet, put the
dates down the left side, and the metrics across the top. You might track how many people
visited your site, how many people opted in, and how many people bought. This shouldn't take
more than 5 minutes a day. You can also put expenditures and deposits. After a month of doing
this, build a chart so you can visually see what the trend is. The visual display of quantitative
information is one of the big innovations humans have made. It'll quickly give you a feel for
where your numbers are heading - are they going up, down, or staying flat - and that'll help you
make better decisions. It also gives you a sense of control and security in your business. If you'll
chart your numbers every day for 90 days, and add a moving average, at the end of 90 days
you'll have a sense of control and understanding of your business that you never had before.
Plus you'll see how your actions make an impact on your numbers and on the health of your
business.

• Management Approach #7: Know your top priority and say it every single day at the
beginning of your huddle call with your team. To overcome our tendency to get distracted,
prioritizing is critical. You can begin calls with "Our #1 priority this quarter is..." and then the
person in charge of that result gives an update. Many people inside your company don't know
what the priorities are - they work on so many different things it's easy for the sense of what's
most important to get muddled. So repeat your top priority at the beginning of your huddle calls,
even if it seems a bit redundant. Your team and your business will thank you in the long turn.

• Management Approach #8: Use collaborative, shared documents for agendas, project
spreads, reports, split-test results and meetings. Google offers a free suite of office products that
work great for this. Several people can be looking at a document all at once, and can even
change it at the same time. This helps connect everyone through this common "hub." It's also
helpful for knowing where different documents are in your business. And when something is
really working, create a graph that shows the results and share the learning with everyone on
the team.

• Management Approach #9: Meet your team members in-person on occasion if possible - it
makes a huge difference in the long-run. It allows people to unite and connect with everyone in a
way that you just can't do virtually. Shoot for at least once a year. When you meet, you can
work on a project together and do team building exercises. When they leave, everyone will
have an increased sense of trust and communication. It'll pay off big time in the long run.
