Week #5: The Psychology of
Wealth Prevention
We’re beginning to transition from the “mental realm” of wealth creation to
the “emotional realm.” In this session, we’re going to discuss a key “bridge”
between these two aspects of ourselves – and we’re going to learn how a
few of our key psychological and emotional systems prevent us from
becoming wealthy (and what to do about it).
1.

We have “perceptual systems” that prevent us from processing all the information coming in at us
– we filter out certain information and focus on other information. These generalizations distort
our view of reality.

2.

If you understand how to use emotions positively then you will attract the people you want into
your life and create the kind of wealth you want.

3.

There are several ways we “thin slice” or make snap judgments about the World, ways we simplify
what we are seeing: deletion, distortion and generalization.

4.

“Confabulation” is when humans constantly make up stories about what happened.
Confabulation allows us to go back and rationalize bad decisions. We can make up reasons why
this happened, or we can say, “I made those choices. Why did I make those bad choices?” In other
words, we can take responsibility.

5.

“Self deception” can prevent us from creating wealth in our lives. When you do something
counter-productive –further away from having assets – you’ll confabulate that you’re not a bad
person.

6.

A “secondary payoﬀ” is a counter-intuitive or invisible benefit we get from doing a behavior that
another part of us wants to change. “What’s the payoﬀ that I’m getting from keeping myself
unsuccessful? What are the benefits of not going out and creating massive value?”

7.

A counter-intuitive practice is to give your VERY BEST ideas away. Trying to keep transactions fair
will keep you unsuccessful. Give away one of your best ideas every day. Use Twitter, a blog,
Facebook to get the message out to others.

8.

How can you set-up the conditions that will make it so you WANT to do the activities necessary to
make money (control emotions)?
