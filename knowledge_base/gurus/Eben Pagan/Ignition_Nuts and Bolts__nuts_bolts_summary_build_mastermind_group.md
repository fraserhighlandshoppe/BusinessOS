Nuts & Bolts:

How to Build an Effective
Mastermind Group
Key Points:
1. Building a Mastermind Group allows you to interact with other people that are skilled
or proficient at the things that YOU want to accomplish. Mastermind Groups are also
a big motivator as you are work on your business.
2. Napoleon Hill interviewed many multi-millionaires and discovered that people who
are highly successful tend to do something called Masterminding – they interact on a
regular basis with people who are successful in their own area and they take time to
work together and help take each other get to the next level.
3. Eben Pagan says, “Possibly the most important and profound action you can take to
create change, transformation, and success in your life is to build and participate in a
Mastermind.”
4. What is a MasterMind?
• Group with common interests and goals, that shares knowledge
• Meets on a regular basis
• Communities of purpose
5. The “Group Mind” is exponentially more powerful than the sum of the individual
minds of the people in which the group is composed. The idea behind the Mastermind
is that you create a “Group Mind” in a systematic way.
6. We literally BECOME what we surround ourselves with. We are the average of the 5
people we are around the most. It’s important to always be “stepping up” with who
you are surrounding yourself with.
7. It’s important to not focus on the material gain at the beginning of the Mastermind,
but rather focus on the higher level things you are learning from others in the group.
8. Examples of Mastermind groups:
• Example 1: group meets 4X per year, email discussion list with 10-12 people,
and conference calls as needed
• Example 2: group meets in person 3X per year, smaller/informal meetings with
2-5 people, and 1:1 phone conversations
• Example 3: meeting in person at “kick-off”, 4 members in “community of
purpose”, weekly group exercises, and Monday updates via an email discussion
list
9. How to run a Mastermind Group

10.How to pick people for your group:
• Pick people who are FAR BEYOND where you’re currently at (ideally 5-10 years
beyond where you’re at)
• Pick people who have MASSIVE POTENTIAL
• Pick people who add massive value to OTHER MEMBERS
11.How to build or “fill up” your group:
• Define your purpose to make everything valuable for the group
• Confirm “anchor members” – this is the domino that knocks everything down
after it and makes the Mastermind work. These can be 1 or 2 important members
who other people want to be around and learn from
• Talk about benefits of connecting with members
• Keep yourself OUT of the equation and use the power of each individual person
you attract to attract others
• Be picky and choose members carefully!
12.How to find people for your group:
• LinkedIn.com: nail down a specific topic, contact people who you think would be
great to discuss the topic, and then contact them about the topic of discussion
and see if they are interested in joining
• Send email to friends: keep it casual, mention you are starting a brainstorming
group, and mention what you plan on discussing
• Use your “anchor members” to attract others
13.How to conduct meetings:
• Start with 6 members
• Meet weekly for 60-90 minutes
• Start on time, end on time, and keep things on track if discussion goes off topic
• Focus on one member’s business or topic per week
• Talk about person’s “biggest challenge” and “biggest opportunity”, group gives
laser targeted feedback on next step recommendations, group introduces person
to people they know that might help them in their business, and group discusses
ideas of ways person can work through challenges and opportunities to be more
successful
• Send out meeting notes or a mindmap when meeting is over
• Meet in person if possible, even it’s only the first meeting kick-off

14.How to keep members engaged:
• Start with the right members – interview them, be picky, pre-qualify them to
make sure they are seriously engaged and committed to the group
• “Chief Inspirer” needs to be consistent and keep group going. They’re the person
that needs to reignite the team if things get off track
• Send out consistent affirmations – example: “I just got a ton of value out of that!”
• Accountability – everyone has action items that they need to be accountable for
15.How to end a Mastermind gracefully
• End on a positive tone, where everyone agrees. Example: “A few of us are
feeling that the group has reached a natural conclusion and it’s probably time
we moved on. Let’s discuss it.”
• Start your next one!
16.Ability to create Masterminds = Growth
• Books to learn more on Masterminds:
a. “Think and Grown Rich” by Napoleon Hill
b. “Networking with Affluent and Their Advisors” by Thomas Stanley
c. “How to Win Friends and Influence People” by Dale Carnegie

Action Steps:
1. Pick topic for your group
2. Pick 1 to 2 “Anchor Members” of group and a “Chief Inspirer”
3. “Fill-up” your group with the right people
4. Conduct meetings
5. Meet consistently and keep members engaged
