YOUR SYSTEMS: Session 9
Tampering, Improving, & Recovering
Energy
In this session, you'll learn the danger - and the temptation - of "tampering" with your business'
systems, why never ending improvement is critical to long term success, and how to raise your
performance by working less.

• Wyatt brought up the idea of a "second order change." When we're eﬀecting a culture or a system,
it's a diﬀerent type of change we're making. It's a higher order change. You need to ask yourself,
"Are you going to work in your business, or are you going to work on your business?" Anything in
your life that you want to change, you can either try to muscle it (work in it) or you can look at
how the system itself works and look for ways that you can operate on a higher level and change
the actual conditions of the whole system.

• Wyatt also mentioned the word Tampering, and it's a word Deming used frequently. Tampering is
when you take a stable system and you make a change to it, thinking that you "get it", but you
don't.

• Most people tamper and tweak and mess with working systems, without knowing what the long
term eﬀects are. Any time you make a tweak to a system, remember the word "tampering." Am I
tampering and messing with something that's already working? Am I being reactive and making
a change that doesn't need to be made?

• The way to figure this out is to chart the system. Simple run charts where you track the data are
my tool of choice. I like looking at daily data and then running a moving average through it to see
what actually is going on. What you'll typically find is that the system is stable in some way if you
look for it. Then watch as you change it to see what eﬀect it really has. We can't think in terms of
long time periods and slow change.

• So what do we do? We go in and make a tweak to the system... the system will start to respond in
the way we wanted it to... but the fact is with random variation there's probably a 50 percent
chance it was going to do it ANYWAY that day. So if we make a change and then think we know
what we're doing, what we may have actually done is screwed the whole system up. We need to
track variation visually over time, so you can see it.

• Tampering can also happen in customer service. When people hear more than one customer say
something or complain about something, it's tempting to extrapolate it and think that ALL
customers doing that. So if two customers complain about something, it becomes "Customers are
complaining about..." At this point I usually ask, "How many?" Usually it's just a handful, and
doesn't warrant a knee jerk reaction of changing your customer service systems.

• Deming said the two pillars of optimization were Constancy of Purpose and Never Ending
Improvement. These two can seem contradictory.

• On the one hand, Constancy of Purpose is about your values and mission. There's value in
keeping these constant... in not changing them.

• On the other hand, Never Ending Improvement is about never accepting good enough. As an
example, for a long time the American auto manufacturers had a philosophy of "build it to
specification" not "never ending improvement" like the Japanese did (adopted from Deming's
philosophy). The Japanese said it wasn't about specs, it's about improvement. And their results
speak for themselves.

• Another critical element of the system of your business lies in recognizing that life is a cycle of
expending energy and recovering energy. That's what we're doing all the time. The rituals of
taking breaks, getting great sleep, drinking water, eating high quality food... they are really all
about optimizing the recovering cycle. And for good reason - your ability to expend energy is
dependent on your ability to recover energy. The quality of how you expend energy is shaped by
the quality of how you recover your energy. The goose is recovering your energy... the golden
eggs is your heightened ability to expend energy.

• I recommend to make it part of your culture that your team recovers energy on a regular basis.
Recommend that they completely unplug from the business 1-2 days a week, take vacations,
travel, take breaks during the day, get time alone, etc. You can't make a person who is burnt out
perform beyond their ability, but if you help them recover better and get more energy, then
they'll be able to perform better. My experience is that people love to perform better. So wire this
concept of recovering energy into your business somehow.
