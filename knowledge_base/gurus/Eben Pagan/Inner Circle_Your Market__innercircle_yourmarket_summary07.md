YOUR MARKET: Session 7
"Owning" Your Category
In this session, you'll learn the power of describing the history of your category better than anyone
else, how to share this history in a way that helps prospects see YOU as the creator of the category
itself, and how to position what you do as "new and diﬀerent" from everything that's come before.

• One of the most powerful things you can do to build your credibility and really "own" your
category in the mind of your prospect is to describe the history of your category.

• For millions of years human learned who they were and where they came from by hearing stories
of historical events from people around them. We wired to tune in to stories - it's one reason we
enjoy movies so much.

• History is one kind of story. When you describe the history of your category - and it doesn't have
to be an ultra-compelling history either - your prospects now think on some level like you were
the one who created the category itself. In some weird unconscious part of their mind, they begin
seeing you as a master on the topic.

• After this, as you begin telling them more stuﬀ about how to get the results you want, your
prospects will believe you're an authority because you know more about the evolution of it than
anyone else. And if you put some graphics up that illustrate the history and evolution of the topic
even more, your prospect would probably say, "Wow! I had no idea that's the way this worked.
Amazing..." On some level they'll see you as a true expert.

• That's how humans' minds work. We're impressed by people who know the history of the thing
we're interested in. We think it's fantastic. If you don't know where your whole category came
from, you can't own it. Make it a point to know more about the history of your category than
anyone else.

• Let's take bird training for example. You could say, "Bird training has gone through 2 philosophies.
Here's the original one, and it's limitations. Here's the second one, that we've literally invented,
and here's how it's diﬀerent than what every that's been done before..." If you can describe in
detail what came before better than anyone else can, even if you're not as much of an expert as
you'd like, your prospects will say, "You're obviously a master of the old way. You clearly know that
backwards and forwards, better than anyone I've heard describe it. And if you know all that, and
you're saying there's a new and better way, man am I glad I found you."

• One of the things you can do when creating a new category is create a new philosophy, and lump
everyone who came before you into the old philosophy.

• You can say, "The old way of thinking and doing things is this..." and describe everyone else. Then
say, "the new way of thinking and doing things is this..." and describe yourself. You've reframed
yourself into a whole new category.

• One example is I might say I'm a psychological expert on humans. People may come up to me
and ask what should they do with their spouse, she's acting weird... or what should they do with
their kid, he's acting weird... or with their co-worker or whatever. If I'm a human psychological
expert, I would give advice on all of these.

• But you could say being an expert on all humans is the old way, and what we've learned now is
that children have a diﬀerent psychology than adults. And you're an expert in child psychology,
this new and emerging field. And because it's very diﬀerent than adult psychology, all those other
experts are teaching you stuﬀ that's counterproductive and actually hurting you. But you're an
expert specifically in the new category called child psychology.

• If you're talking to someone who has a 13 year old girl who's going through emotional problems,
and you say you're a psychologist and can help, versus "I created the category called Teenage Girl
Depression Psychology... and I specifically work with teenage girls who are going through
depression from the ages of 13 and 15. Here's why they're diﬀerent, here's what I've learned in my
unique experience, etc." The parent of that child is going to listen to you and say "This is
incredible! It's such a match for what I need. You're talking right to me..."

• Find something that everyone used to use, but didn't work. Almost everything works to a certain
extent, so you don't want to completely demonize it because then you seem to radical.

• If you say something like, "There's always been one approach to training birds. We call it The
Torture Method," people hearing that would think it sounds too extreme.

• But if you say, "Before now there's been essentially one approach to training birds. We call is The
Intimidation Method." This message has enough "to it" that people would be curious about what
it is, but you personally don't sound like some crazy radical or someone with an agenda.
