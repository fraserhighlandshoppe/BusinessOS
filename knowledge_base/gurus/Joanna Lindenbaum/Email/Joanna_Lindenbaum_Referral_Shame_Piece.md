## The core reframe: dispel the referral shame
When referrals don't flow, practitioners make it mean something about their worth:
- "What's wrong with me that people aren't referring?"
- "Are clients not as satisfied as they could be?"
- "Why am I the kind of coach/practitioner no one sends referrals to?"

Because referrals feel tied to the quality of our work, the (faulty) logic is: no referrals → people don't think my work is good enough. That story is the trap.

**Truth:** Being extraordinary at your work and co-creating results with clients is only ONE part of the consistent-referral equation.

## The two-part referral equation
1. **Delivery** — do excellent work / get clients results (necessary, but not sufficient).
2. **Ask mechanics** — *how* you request referrals:
   - The **words** you use.
   - The **energy** you embody when you ask.
   - The **timing** of the ask.
   - How you've **kept up** with the people you ask (relationship continuity).

So if referrals have been thin: stop the self-blame. You are not the problem. You likely just haven't learned/practiced the *ask* art yet. Once you do, referrals start to move.

## Why it matters
- Referrals are still the cheapest, easiest route to new leads who already arrive trusting you.
- The bottleneck is almost never "my work isn't good enough" — it's the missing or awkward ask.

## The "One Good Thing" analogy (business growth)
Joanna's daughter Yael wanted Honors Math despite skeptics; through weekly tutoring, extra homework, and persistent effort she got in. Lesson: growth often feels impossible/hard, but commitment + persistent work pays off. Applies directly to building a referral engine.

## Distilled actionable insights
- Separate self-worth from referral volume. Thin referrals ≠ inadequate practitioner.
- Audit the *ask*: script the words, check the energy/timing, and build a system to stay in touch with past clients & centers of influence.
- Consider a lightweight "Referral Event" cadence (quarterly) rather than ad-hoc asks.
- Treat referral generation as a learnable skill — practice, don't ruminate.
