My Drive For a Gold Medal at Worlds
by Troy White

November, 2013, I stepped back into the Dojo and re-started my journey into Kickboxing and Muay Thai. I had been out of that style
of martial art for 12 years, but I was excited to get back at it and bang some heavy bags. I had fairly recently ended a 7 year sting in
Tae Kwon Do, and held a Second Dan Black Belt. As much as I love Tae Kwon Do, it just wasn’t the same intensity of a workout that
Muay Thai and Kickboxing was.
At 45, I was an old guy in a young guy (and gal) sport. BUT, I loved it, and I was determined to get back to my old shape. Sparring was
what I missed most (not a fan of the type of sparring done in Tae Kwon Do - just not my thing). After a few months of training and
light sparring, I thought… “What the hell…I’ll do a tournament!”. Now, when I used to do Muay Thai, I was 15 years younger, was in
amazing shape, and was pretty good at sparring. BUT, I made a HUUUUUGE mistake back then… I listened to others. Everyone back
then told me I was too old to compete, that I would get hurt, that I should just give up on that idea. Stupid Troy listened! No more - I
was competing this time… 15 years older or NOT.
My [irst tournament put me up against a 6’8” 290 pound MONSTER in the [inals. The [irst punch from him sent my mouth guard
[lying. But I picked that slimy thing up…put it back in my mouth (toe jam and all from the [loor), and battled my best for the next 2
rounds. It was a damn tough [ight…but I loved it. I learned that I CAN hit hard back if need be. That I COULD survive against a
behemoth like this. And that I DID [ight my fear and do what I was scared to do. I lost the [ight, but put up a great battle, and came
out with a silver medal.
That experience has inspired me to continue to compete in 33 tournament [ights to date (winning more than I lost :). Every single one
of them (except one) was against guys half my age. And the one that was against someone my age, was a battle. I tore my left MCL in
my knee, and he tore a muscle in his bicep. But we battled hard and let old guy strength carry us through :) And now…I get to
compete on a World scale and represent Canada in the “Masters” Division (over 45). So, so, so excited about this opportunity. It won’t
be easy, as these are the best in the world, guys who’ve done martial arts for a long time, and are also at a world-class level. But it
WILL be fun!
My lesson in all this is two-fold…
Despite this having NOTHING to do with marketing, it has everything to do with success. Success is all about mastering the basics,
stretching your comfort zones, and being willing to step up to the game, day in and day out. If you want to master copywriting, you
need to learn how to write at a 5th grade level (the basics of sales copy). You need to learn the formulaic approach to grabbing their
attention, holding their attention, moving their attention to desire for what you have, and then closing the deal. Basics at the core but you need to master them. You can NOT be scared of trying new things. Yes, there will be repercussions for your actions - they
come in the shape of lack of orders, or growth in complaints, or a complete and utter loss of money. But if you aren’t willing to try
new things, you’ll never get anywhere and you’ll never be able to compete at a high level.
Use fear as a tool (I learned this as I was stepping into the ring with the 6’8” monster). That feeling of dread in the pit of your stomach
- that needs to be used as a focal point. Change what the feeling means to you, from fear, to excitement. Focus on the goal, and use
that feeling as your guiding light. Not enough fear - make your goal bigger and your light brighter. You need to be feeling that fear
every day. Just one thing a day out of your comfort zone that builds that feeling, even if just a little, is a step closer to your dreams
coming true.

7 Reasons Why Small Businesses Fail In Their First Year

Studies indicate that as many as eighty per cent of new businesses fail in their first
year. The dream of self-employment turns into a nightmare for a surprising number
of people. Understanding the reasons so many businesses fail can help you to avoid
the pitfalls and prevent it happening to you.
There are many different reasons why people choose to start a business. Some are driven by the idea of
turning a passion into a way of making a living. Others are forced into self-employment by a lack of job
opportunities. Whatever the reason, the early months are a critical time. If you can survive these and build
the foundations, your business has a strong chance of being around for years to come.
The following reasons explain the high statistics relating to new business failures.
1) Lack of planning.
Written plans with financial projections are great tools for keeping a new business on track. A plan should
describe the products and services the business will offer and list milestones for the first year. Without a
plan, you have nothing to measure performance against. Your plan doesn’t have to be hundreds of pages
long, and can just be a few pages outlining what you’re doing in the next year. A budget is a good idea too,
and check where your income is coming from, when it’s arriving, and how you’re spending it.
2) Lack of cash.
Without cash, even the most talented entrepreneur will fail. A new business will initially need cash for
stock, materials and working capital. If customers don't pay on time, there won't be funds available to
continue trading. Lack of cash is one of the main reasons businesses fail, and it's one that's easily avoided
if you manage cashflow from day one. Cash is King!
3) No marketing activity.
A business won't survive without customers. Marketing activity is essential to retain existing customers
and to reach new ones. You can launch a business with promises of orders from people you already know,
but this isn't a long term strategy for growth. Having a website, engaging on social media and encouraging
word of mouth advertising are simple marketing tactics for building a customer base.
4) Poor management.
