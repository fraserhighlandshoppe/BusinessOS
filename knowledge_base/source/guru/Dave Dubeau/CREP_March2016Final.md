The first thing investors need to realize is that their
mortgage professional should be playing on their team.

take your deals to the right lenders to best realize your
long term objectives. They’ll also be able to keep you
from painting yourself into a proverbial corner with respect to your financing.

think sharing certain details may jeopardize their deal.
Instead, the opposite should be the case. Smart investors disclose all of their details to their mortgage professional and let them do their job in their area of expertise.

Enlisting Other's Talents to Get

In order to move from
the left side of the
cash flow quadrant to

enlisting the skills of other people to assist you.

skill set doesn't meet the needs of the project.
It is possible you have done this before, but
might not even have realized it. For example,
when moving from one address to another
nearby address, you most likely have asked a
couple friends for help, or borrowed their truck.
You likely "thanked" them by buying pizza and
beer at the end of the day. In this example, you
utilized their physical attributes and transportation attributes to help with a move that would
have been difficult and time consuming to do
on your own. And you likely have reciprocated
by offering your talents to assist these friends
when needed. (By the way, this reciprocity between people does not require an exchange of
cash but rather an exchange of talents. The
result is beneficial to your cash flow).

paid contractors to complete projects outside of
your skill set and time constraints. I have been
using contractors for many different projects

CONTRACTING OUT A PROJECT

have any recommendations for people who do
good work.

What is a Property Really Worth?
I am currently working with a client who is looking at a
large commercial property to purchase and he has discovered that the Bank has a very different opinion of the
value compared to what the Seller believes it to be worth.
So how much is it truly worth? This is a question I often
receive and is sometimes (or often) difficult to answer.
The bottom line is that the value is ultimately determined
when a willing buyer and a willing seller enter into a mutually agreed contract with neither party experiencing any
undue stress or pressure to sell or buy. The challenge
lies in getting the lenders to agree with this!
Okay, you have a willing Seller and Buyer looking objectively at a property with no outside forces playing a factor! So how do they determine the value? With a single
family home the value is most often determined based
upon comparable sales, yet there are times when a willing buyer may pay more for a property than what the
market suggests it is worth if the buyer has a specific
need or purpose for the property or if they become emotionally involved with the property.
When determining the value of a commercial property,
such as an apartment building, the value is determined
based upon its ability to create a profit. Again there may
still be the occasional time when a buyer is willing to pay
more for a commercial property if they have a specific
need for the property, but for the most part the value is
based upon the financial performance of the property
and its ability to generate a positive cash flow.
So let’s review how value is determined by cash flow and
profit. To determine the cash flow for a property simply
take all of the revenues collected and deduct all of the
related expenses such as property tax, property insurance, utilities, etc… and this is called the “Net Operating
Income” (NOI). The debt service is not part of this equation; however, this debt service must be paid from the
cash flow (profit).
Once you have a value for the NOI the value of a commercial property is calculated using the “CAP rate”. In the
real estate industry the experts will look at other similar
properties which have sold and they will take the NOI of
these properties and divide this value by the purchase
price. The result is what the industry refers to as a CAP
rate. The historical sales of similar properties will create
a range of CAP rate values which investors can then apply to determine the value of a potential property.
The formula to determine value or CAP rate is: Value =
NOI/CAP. This is a very straight forward and relatively
simple process which one would think would provide a
fairly accurate value. The challenge is that the NOI can
be easily manipulated. When a seller is selling a property

they will have to include the
obvious expenses such as
those I mentioned above but
what about all of the other related expenses such as maintenance to the units, grass
cutting, snow clearing, general
cleaning, property management, legal costs to evict a
tenant, vacancies, etc.? Often these expenses are
either grossly understated or simply not included at all,
and this changes the net operating income significantly. The Seller will justify this with statements like,
“Well I cut the grass myself”, or “I do the maintenance”, and “I do the property management”, so they
don’t really cost me anything.
IF the Seller is able to reduce the operating expenses
and or overstate the revenues (potential revenues)
then the result is a higher NOI. With a higher NOI they
can make the property value look greater than it actually is. On the flip side when a lender is looking to lend
money secured by a commercial property they want to
make sure it is a sound investment. The Lenders do
not want to lend money secured by a property that will
barely cash flow because if the property experiences
some difficulty financially then the lender runs the risk
of losing some of their investment. This is why the
Lenders will do their own analysis of the property and
during their analysis you will see that they use much
more conservative numbers for expenses and vacancies.
On one hand we have a Seller trying to maximize the
value and on the other hand we have the Lender trying
to minimize the risk and somewhere in between is the
true value based upon what the Buyer determines it is
worth. When I look at a property to assess what I believe the value to be I follow the Lenders system to
determine value simply because they have the money.

Real Estate Masterminding
Whether you want to be a “hands on” or a
“hands off” real estate investor, there is one
thing you can do to create momentum and that
is to create your own mastermind group. Even if
you’re the hands off type, you can spearhead
the creation, implementation and management
of a real estate mastermind group. There are all
kinds of masterminds that you can create to help
further your own professional development as
well as that of others seeking the same support.
I’ve been part of other people’s mastermind
groups and started my own. My recommendation is for you to start your own mastermind
group, even if it’s just a real estate investor’s
support group to share what each person has
learned at a program, workshop, course, seminar.
Napoleon Hill’s book Think And Grow Rich has
the formula of the rich and successful. They created a master mind by pooling the resources,
creativity, expertise, knowledge base of a dozen
people. A dozen people have the mind power of
a genius. A dozen people can solve a Mensa
Test. Try it for yourself. To test this theory out, I
printed off a Mensa test and left a dozen copies
at a barbecue. There was the loner off in the
corner trying to solve the test by himself. Then
there was the group solving the test and they
got all the answers in less than five minutes with
ease and fun.
If you are waiting to join a mastermind group
and haven’t found one yet, then it’s time to create your own. When I started my first mastermind group, it was in my one-bedroom apartment and we met every Sunday afternoon.
Some were for a few months. Others have
lasted years. Some masterminds met every
week, every bi-week or once a month. It all depends on your commitment level and what each
person brings to the table. Send invitations to
people that you think would like to join. You can
post it on Meetup.com or Craig’s List or put a
free classified listing in BC Business Magazine
or in the Georgia Straight.

Your purpose is to attract like-minded people to create a support
network, and together,
create momentum for
all participants. If you’re not sure how to get
started, go online and look for the Robert’s Rules of
Order to conduct your meetings or you can create
your own modified set of rules. You can create a
fixed term mastermind that ends after 3, 6 or 12
months. You can create a weekly, bi-monthly or
monthly event.
As the host, you have the choice to elect yourself
as Chair or you can delegate the responsibilities.
When you delegate tasks, you will easily see who
the action takers in the group are because they will
volunteer. You can design the group’s mission
statement or open it up for discussion for topics like
who to include in the group, how many people can
attend, how many times you’ll hold the event, for
how long and where, create a meeting outline that
everyone commits to. Remember to include a Confidentiality Agreement that everyone signs. This is
part of the integrity component. Your mastermind is
created to support each other.
Real estate investing takes teamwork. A mastermind is a great way to put those skills into practice.

New Downpayment Rules Kick In
Late last year our finance minister decided to make some
changes for the mortgage industry.
The new downpayment rule states that if a person
wishes to put the minimum amount into a property
then they must pay 5% for the first $500,000.00
and 10% for any portion over the $500,000.00 to
the one million dollar mark. Any home over the
million dollar mark does not qualify for a low
downpayment.

In conclusion, I don’t think this change in policy
is any cause for panic, but it definitely is something to consider before you write that next offer. Income/rental properties are still 20% for
their downpayments so there was no change
there.
Over the coming year our government will be
watching closely for the impact of this most recent change. We could very well see some additional small changes coming later in 2016.
Stay tuned!

$600,000.00 property they would put in the 5% for
the first $500,000.00 or $25,000.00 and 10% for
the additional $100,000.00 or another $10,000.00
on top of the $25,000.00 for a total downpayment
of $35,000.00 in this case.

to slow down new home buyers as the payments
would become more expensive. In today’s struggling economy due to a low energy price that
would be too harsh to the rest of our economy.
Usually once a year we see some kind of policy
change to attempt to slow the real estate market
down.
One wrong move could have a drastic impact and
destroy the housing market here in Canada. I

These new rules took effect on February 15,
2016 and I am not seeing a huge impact to the

2) You are too busy

profit to you. For exampole, I’m from Winnipeg. The

This type of strategy is another way to get out of your

Providing complete documents upfront
(organized people don’t send documents
in piecemeal fashion)

One of the most frustrating parts of the mortgage process for clients is running into the situation where they
are tight for time on a deal and then trying to come up
with documents at the last minute. Lenders tend to ask
for the same types of documentation on most deals so
being proactive does wonders to make the process
more enjoyable for everyone.
Another proactive measure investors can take to really
set themselves apart as sophisticated investors is to
follow document-naming provisions and provide all
documents in PDF format. A consistent naming sequence makes it easier for you as an investor to find
your own documents down the road, easier for your
mortgage professional to review those documents during the mortgage process, and easier for the underwriter at the lender to review and approve your file.
Finally, by providing complete documents upfront you’ll
be able to avoid back-and-forth which is a major source
of delay, frustration, and wasted effort for everyone in
the mortgage process.
Walk a Mile in Someone Else’s Shoes
Remember that we’re all human and with multiple files
on the go, the clients that are easy and profitable to
deal with tend to get prioritized. This is basic human
psychology.
For most mortgage professionals a primary residence
purchase is simpler than an investor deal so guess
which one they’d prefer to tackle? When a mortgage file
gets to the underwriter at the lender who has to review
the documents that same concept applies.
When they have a choice between the first time buyer
that requires the review of a total of 10 pages versus
the real estate investor who requires close to 100 pages
of document review, who do you think they’ll want to
work on first?
Consider that both parties may receive incentives based
on the number and dollar volume of deals funded and
you’ll quickly see the importance of making yourself
stand out in a positive way to get your real estate investment mortgages approved and funded.
We’re all human, so what’s the solution? It’s simple. As
a borrower make yourself easy and profitable to deal
with by disclosing all of your info, providing complete
details, and being well organized. Being nice to your
friendly mortgage professional doesn’t hurt either.

Temporary Time Outs (Before & After)
Last issue I wrote about an experiment I am doing where I give one or two of my ‘bad’ or unproductive habits a time out for a month. That
means that I stop doing them for 30 days, and
then I can re-start again after that if I so wish.
For January I took a break from drinking alcohol . This was because I had definitely overindulged with the ’cervezas’ during my Christmas vacation in Mexico!
The booze break worked well. I reconfirmed
that I do not have alcoholic tendencies (a worry
because my father was an alcoholic). So in
February I allowed myself to enjoy the occasional frosty beverage. Now I find that I am
drinking less that I did before the time-out.
For February I decided to stop drinking diet
pops. I had gotten into the habit of taking a
break in the afternoon, getting in the car, and
going to the store for a pop. I’m watching my
girlish figure, so I drank diet pops. I’m not a cof-

fee drinker, so I justified this to myself as being
better than hitting Starbucks or Timmies like so
many people do.
My kids were busting my chops about how bad
Aspartame is for me, so I thought I would take a
time out from pop for a month. The hardest part
was breaking the habit of getting in the car to go
get one (not actually the pop itself). I am happy to
say that made it a month without a diet pop. Consequently, I’ve decided to avoid pop, and only
have one on a very rare occasion.
This month (March) I have deleted all the time
wasting games I had on my phone (free cell being
the worst). So far so good, and that’s been going
well...no physical withdrawals anyway (yet).
These self-imposed time outs are working well for
me. You might want to give it a try as well.
Pick a habit and just skip it for a month. It might
not be as hard as you think.
