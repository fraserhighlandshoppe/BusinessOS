several evenings searching other markets.
The first step is to choose your market.
From there, direct your focus to finding
great deals.
FINDING GREAT DEALS!
First start out by finding motivated sellers
who you can meet face to face with. This
means calling all FSBO (For Sale By Owner) properties, and arranging for a time to
look at the property. Search ComFree and
ask friends to let you know when they see
a fresh For Sale sign without a realtor attached to them.
When you go see the FSBO property and
are being toured around the home, be sure
to have both ears open. Remember the acronym WAIT (Why Am I Talking?) and always finish with another question to the
seller. During these moments you need to
have your Property Detective hat on.

1. How long have you lived here?
2. Why are you selling?
3. When do you need to sell this house by?
4. How did you determine the asking price?
5. Do you know what a Vendor-Take-Back is?
6. (in a quiet voice) What is the lowest price
you could accept?
7. What are your plans with the proceeds
from your home sale?

Be prepared! Before going to physically view
the homes have all your “ducks in a row.” This
means being or having an investor preapproved for financing, having a good home
inspector on your team, having money partner's funds already in-trust with your lawyer.
You want to be confident with your offer.
Bring a blank “Offer to Purchase” contract and
know your maximums.

A Real Head Scratcher!!
Last month in my personal newsletter that I
send out I shared a common spring problem
that many people experience and that is water
leaking into the basement. One of my readers
who is a plumber was kind enough to make
some suggestions as to what the cause was
and a possible solution. What my reader did not
know was the entire story which I then shared
with him and now I would like to share with you.
This particular property was my very first large
commercial property and when I viewed it, I was
certainly not impressed with two of the three
basement units. These units were so badly
damaged by water seeping through the foundation when prospective buyers looked at the
property they ran. As a result the seller ended
up selling the property to me for less than he
paid for it ten years earlier. The question is why
buy with such a major water issue? Upon closer
inspection I noticed that the flat roof had only
one drain which dumped all of the run-off right
at the corner where the water was entering the
building. Also, the old paved parking lot was so
uneven that it sloped towards the building and
any surface water collected in the parking lot
simply flowed towards the building.
Solution! Resurface the old parking lot with
proper grading and add a tail extension to the
down spout. Problem solved (...or so I thought).
It actually did solve the majority of the problem
and the cost of the parking lot was easily offset
in the savings of the purchase price. Unfortunately the next summer we had major water issues again.
It was a typical hot and humid mid-summer day
when I got the call from an irate tenant saying
that her unit was flooded and she could barely
keep up with a mop and pail. I went over to investigate and since it was hot and dry (no rain
for weeks) I knew it couldn’t be coming in from
the outside. I called in the plumber. He
searched high and low but he couldn’t find a
leak, so we figured it must be coming up
through the floor somehow. The search went on
for several days before someone suggested that
it was condensation on the cold concrete floors.
It was hard to believe this could be the source
given the amount of water, yet it turns out this

was the source. Now that
we had identified the
problem we had to fix it,
and we went to the hardware store to buy some
dehumidifiers. Several
days later the tenant called and they were unhappy because they had to regularly empty
the dehumidifiers and there was still water on
the floor. I had no idea what to do until someone suggested installing an air conditioner.
The air conditioner lowers the inside temperature by removing the moisture from the air and
depositing it outside. This process leaves the
inside air dry and cool, eliminating the condensation on the floor.
Now the problem is solved and as an added
bonus I was able to increase the rents for all
my basement units. Tenants now like our
basement units because we provide A/C for
their comfort which makes it a win/win for us
both☺.
So if you ever have a similar issue in a basement unit , you now have a new trick to try.
This story can also help remind you to look
beyond the obvious challenges when you are
viewing properties. In this case a wet basement which saved me tens-of-thousands of
dollars when I purchased the property.
Some of the best opportunities come when
you buy a distressed (problematic) property.
Always keep an open, creative mind.

Are You Fueled by Inspiration or
Motivation?
Have you ever had this experience?
There is a project you need to do. It is
important and must be done in the
near future. In spite of this, you can’t seem to get started
on it. You delay, you procrastinate, you waste time on
unimportant things. But for some reason, you just can’t
seem to dig into it.
If you can relate to this, you’re not alone. I’ve coached
hundreds of successful small business owners and financial advisors who experience this on a daily basis.
They find it bewildering, frustrating, even discouraging;
yet they can’t seem to overcome the problem. Even
worse, most of them beat themselves up for their poor
discipline, which further diminishes their confidence and
momentum.
Resolving this problem starts with understanding what is
blocking you. Every goal you have consists of three
components. The ‘what’ – your desired end result. The
‘why’ – your reason for doing it. And the ‘how’ – the
steps or strategies for getting there. Of these, the ‘why’
is your essential fuel. It’s what gets you into action – or
not. If you are not taking action, it means your ‘why’ is
not strong enough to overcome the resistance you have
to doing the task.
You may think that the solution to this is motivation; if
only you could get motivated! And motivation can work
but it is unsustainable. Let me prove this. Bring to mind a
situation where you feel you need to motivate yourself –
maybe to get up earlier, to go to the gym or to do your
taxes. Connect energetically with the task you’re trying
to get yourself to do. Can you feel the heaviness of it?
Motivation is based on obligation; “I need to do this” or “I
should do that.” It has pressure or fear associated with
it. Motivation can help you get things done in the short
term, but in the long term drumming up motivation depletes your energy and leaves you exhausted.
The solution is to fuel yourself with inspiration instead of
motivation. ‘Inspire’ means to breathe into. Motivation is
external; inspiration comes from within. When you are

Assessing Risk
There was a commentator on the radio last month
advising people not to borrow money to make money. What kind of advice is that? This person obviously does not know how to assess risk, does not
own a house or a business, and the probability is
very high that he is not financially free. The better
topic to address is how do people assess risk factors before they borrow to invest.

Mitigating risk is something that people should do on
a regular basis. It’s not something you do one day
and not the next. It is a part of how you operate. The
object of the game is to get a higher return than
what you are paying in interest. Part of the risk mitigation discussion is what type of vehicle is worth the
risk to accomplish this.

Collateral is a form of real security, an asset with cash
value or equity value, that provides an investor with
assurances that in the event of a default, there is
something of value left for the risk taken. However,
when security is available, this also reduces the risk
and subsequently reduces the return.

There are five indicators that we use to mitigate risk:
(1) rate of return; (2) track record; (3) reputation; (4)
security or collateral; (5) gut or intuition.

A high rate of return is expected for a high risk investment. There is a disconnect when there is a low
rate of return for a high risk investment. If you are
expecting double-digit returns, then you are not investing in mutual funds. The rate of return is also
dictated by the time factor. Is the 100% return over 3
years, 10 years or 20 years? The return on investment may sound promising until you discover the
rate of return.
A track record takes time to build but you can research the history of an individual, a company or an
investment before their current offering. For example, when I became a real estate investor, I had no
track record but I did have a successful track record
in other industries, able to share specific strategies
on how those businesses benefited from significant
cost savings to implementing systems that assisted
in doubling revenues year over year. Mind you, not
all track records are transferable per se, such as
Coca Cola’s CEO taking Apple to the edge of bankruptcy.
Reputation is in the eye of the beholder. Other people’s opinions create the reputation of a person or
company. This opinion is based on certain qualities
or characteristics demonstrated by the principals,
the employees, the quality and performance of products, held over a period of time. Consistent high
quality over a long period of time creates a high degree of confidence and trust. Inconsistent messages
do the opposite. However, even a stellar reputation
can be sullied through mis-management as befell
the once mighty Lehman Brothers. They overleveraged billions of mortgage-backed securities

The final arbiter is your gut feeling or intuition. This
goes beyond charm and psychological manipulation.
Our subconscious mind picks up tiny clues that are virtually undetectable unless you have been trained to
observe them. This information is received, interpreted
and a conclusion is made without your conscious mind
understanding exactly why you trust someone or don’t
trust someone. If you have ever watched the show “Lie
To Me” with actor Tim Roth as the world’s leading deception researcher Dr. Cal Lightman, this will give you
a clue into how detection is made at the conscious level. This is also how border guards trained to observe
people during questioning can detect when someone is
lying to them.
Unfortunately, for every indicator with assessing risk,
there is a story about how someone beat the system.
Risk mitigation is more than just a 1-2-3 step process
and should include contacting a trusted advisor before
making the final commitment of funds. We all get emotionally involved in some of our decision-making because we are human. It’s natural and normal. A trusted
advisor can review the particulars with you in a more

Are these condo buyers actually “flippers”, and are they
driving prices sky high?

has released concerning the spending habits of
people purchasing Condominiums in Canada’s
two hottest markets. Toronto and Vancouver of

You can find the complete survey on the CMHC
website to get additional information if you like.
The study found out that:
#1 – 23.5% of householders in these areas own
at least one secondary condominium in addition
to their personal home.
#2 – Nearly half of these investors purchased
their unit for the rental income.
#3 – Over 55% of them expect their value to appreciate over the coming year.
#4 - The study showed only 53% of these investors had a mortgage on their last condo purchase.
#5 – More of these people living in Toronto believed their values would increase than Vancouver. But Vancouver is closing in fast on that optimism.
#6 – The study showed that generally these
buyers are planning to be in the market for more
than 5 years. There was a trend to take longer
term mortgage products out as well.
#7 – Near 70% of these people only own one
additional investment condo. As well 90% do
not anticipate buying another investment condo
in the coming 12 months.
#8 – This report does not include people who
only own one home which may be a condo. As
well it does not include folks that own a secondary unit and rent out their primary residence.

investors are in it for the longer term. Buy and

1. You deal directly with the decision maker. When you buy a
property listed on the MLS, through

seller. You come up with your offer, you explain it to your realtor,

with the seller, find out their true

language, and get the deal done!

the seller often means there’s a lot more
flexibility in the deal. You can extend

leaves more room to negotiate price and
terms – thereby allowing you to have bigger profits on your deals.

started marketing my real estate business

little ‘active’ competition I had. I was in a

able to do so many of them.

counter-intuitive when you think that you
will have to spend a few bucks on marketing to get the word out about your real
estate business. But with the increased
profits you will be making, and the fact
you won’t be paying any real estate commissions – paying the money to attract
deals to you is a much more economical
way to do them in the long run.

Estate Marketing.

Dubeau Dives Into “Bookface”
(Okay...I know it’s Facebook)

May was the start-up of the Social Media Director program I promoted with Dan “The Man”
Lok and his business partner, Matt Astifan.
In addition to the 40+ people who enrolled from
the promotion we did, I decided to dive in and
take the program myself. Seeing that I am a
complete dimwit when it comes to actually using
Social Media myself, this seemed like the perfect opportunity to become a not-so-early
adopter to FaceBook.
As an on-line educator, I really appreciate how
the program is laid out and being taught. There
is a ‘lesson’ on Thursday evenings, homework
over the weekend, and then a Q. and A. session
on the following Tuesday. Plus there is a great
deal of additional BONUS training provided to
help us newbies get up to speed with the basics
of Social Media.
It is quite the learning curve. The advantage I
have is that I understand the ‘marketing’ concepts behind everything that we are learning.
My disadvantage is that I have never actually
been an active user of Facebook (or any other
social media). Wagner (my old marketing assistant) did set me up with accounts years ago –

but he did everything with them. So now I am
learning from scratch how to do this stuff – and
there are definitely a few ‘hiccups’ along the way.
For example: I didn’t know that updating my
‘relationship’ status would automatically be broadcast to all of my ‘friends’… including my ex-wife.
Oops. (see pics below of the cause of the update).
Anyway, I see that there is a LOT involved with
this program – and it’s definitely not going to be a
cake-walk for me (or the other students either).
But it is something that I think if you put the time
and effort into, that it will definitely pay off BIG
either for a person’s own business (which is my
main focus) and/or as a service to provide to other
business owners who don’t ‘get’ social media.
I’ll keep you informed as I blunder, stumble and
flounder my way around the social media world…
because I am a firm believer that anything worth
doing well is worth doing poorly until you figure it
out!
In the meanwhile, if you’d like to be Bookface (lol)
friends, please look me up on Facebook (Dave
Dubeau) and let’s get social!
