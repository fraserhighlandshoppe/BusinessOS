HOW DO I SET UP QUICKLY FOR THE BEST SHOT?
•

The most important things to have are a “key light” and a “hair light”. These 2
things will turn your shot pro in a hurry.

•

Key Light: The main light facing the subject directly. Soft, bright, and diffused.

•

Hair Light: A light from above and behind the subject. This gives dimension by
“outlining” the hair from behind with a more direct light. Also called a
“backlight”.

•

Reflector: Literally reflects the light from the Key Light onto the other side of
your face in order to light the shot evenly.

SECRET EDITING TRICKS

#1 - THE TWO CAMERA TRICK
•

You’ll notice in Eben’s videos that we have what you might call
a “wide” shot and a “close up” shot. It looks like I’ve set up 2
cameras and switch between them periodically.

•

It’s actually just 1 camera, shot in HD. I split the footage up
and every other cut is “zoomed in” digitally to about 140%.

•

Each cut switches between 100% and 140%. This gives the
illusion of 2 different camera angles, and creates pattern
interrupts at key points in the dialog.

WHAT IS GOOD DESIGN?
•

10 Principals For Good Design. This comes from Dieter
Rams, a famous German designer for the Braun company.

•

He was the main inspiration for the designs of Jonathan Ive,
Senior Vice President of Design at Apple.

•

I think these principals can apply across many different areas
- from graphic design, to writing copy, to creating content.

•

I’m going to talk about 4 of them that apply directly to text
and logos, which is what we’ll be talking about here.

1. GOOD DESIGN IS HONEST
• It doesn’t make a product appear more

innovative, powerful or valuable than it
really is.
• It doesn’t attempt to manipulate the

consumer with promises that can’t be
kept.

2. GOOD DESIGN IS LONG-LASTING
• It avoids being fashionable and therefore

never appears antiquated.
• Unlike fashionable design, it lasts many

years – even in today's throw-away
society.

3. GOOD DESIGN IS
THOROUGH
• Down to the last detail - Nothing must be

arbitrary or left to chance.
• Care and accuracy in the design process

show respect towards the consumer.

4. GOOD DESIGN IS AS LITTLE DESIGN AS POSSIBLE
• Less, but better – because it concentrates

on the essential aspects, and the
products are not burdened with nonessentials.
• Back to purity, back to simplicity.
