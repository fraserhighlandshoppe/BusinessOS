# How_to_prove_to_customers_real_results.pdf

```markdown
How to Prove to Customers You Can Get Them
“Real” Results

Key Points:
1.

NLP - study of the structure of human experience. How you interact with the world. If
you want to get something done in life, you set up a set of conditions that creates the
outcome.
2. “Evidence Procedure”: you have a system for being able to tell whether you are getting
closer or further from the outcome. For examples, if you want to lose weight, you chart
your weight each day over weeks. Your evidence procedure is “how much do I weigh.”
Evidence procedures are objective and can be viewed by others tangibly in the real
world.
3. Identify the evidence procedure your customer knows if their problem was solved?
What is the one thing or symbol that proves that thing is done (ex. fitting into skinny
jeans, or a partner packing his bags and leaving).
4. When creating marketing, it’s critical that you meet people in the “common space” of
concrete, measurable, tangible, and experiential. To do this, you must switch off your
abstraction mechanisms, and switch on your concrete experience mechanisms. Don’t
talk about “currency” and “signs of aging,” instead talk about “$5,000 cash” or “wrinkles
around your eyes.”
5. Tangible ideas make prospects feel they are being talked to directly while abstract ideas
do not have this effect.
6. Through marketing you want to communicate in a phrase/subject/link/headline “I’ve
got your tangible result right here.” Challenge yourself to describe this tangible thing
and measure the results (using an “evidence procedure”), so you can be successful at
communicating and finding “common space” with other human beings.
7. Also, if you talk to fewer people more specifically about their needs you will get a better
response than if you talk to a wider audience more generally.
8. "Prospective customers who don't know you, don't like you (yet) and don't trust you
(yet) are focused on one main objective... getting the RESULT they want. Your product
or service "comes between" them and the result that they want - so it's valuable to forget
about what you sell or offer, and get crystal-clear about the result or benefit your
customer is seeking. Find the benefits of the products, services, systems, and techniques
you offer. Focus on irrational, emotional, "hot button" results and benefits (including
the avoidance of pain).
9. And remember the specificity test. For example if you are saying "food" instead of
"granny smith apple" you need to refine the benefit and offer.
10. Translate features into benefits, then even more specific benefits.
```
