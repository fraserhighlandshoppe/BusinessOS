Session 4: Winning Products
& Services In The Virtual
Business Model
• Virtual Product Insight #1: Offer products and services that lend themselves well to the virtual
model - so you can take full advantage of it. Some products that work well with the virtual model,
and in my experience scale very well, are products like information products, coaching, advice,
software, games, etc. On the flip side, if you're going to be shipping a hard product and you'll
be storing a lot of inventory in a warehouse, the virtual model may not be the best fit.

• Virtual Product Insight #2: An excellent business model created by the internet is "Affiliate
Marketing" - where you sell products created by others. I personally know of many self-made
millionaires who got their start as affiliates online. It's also a great marketing channel - you can
set up an affiliate program for your own business, so affiliate marketers can go out and sell your
products on a purely commission basis.

• Virtual Product Insight #3: Another excellent category is "picks & shovels" products & services
to help OTHER Virtual Businesses succeed online. Right now, most businesses have no idea how
to success in the online world or in a virtual environment. So a great category is to sell the
products and services that help other businesses succeed online. For example, I've seen people
who have learned a lot about Search Engine Optimization go out and sell the service to other
people and have done very well. Sometimes in the process of trying to grow your own business,
you discover a technique or strategy that's even more valuable to you when you teach it to
others (for money) instead of using it yourself to grow your own business.

• Virtual Product Insight #4: Test your product out in a "stripped down" version - the most basic
version that works - to see if there is demand. For example, if you're building a piece of
software, you don't want to build the whole thing... you want to build a version that has just the
core functions that work, then see if people want it. If they do, then you can invest the extra time
to build on top of it. Most people invest a lot of time in creating what they consider to be an
excellent product, then when they go to market they realize there's something fundamentally not
"want-able" about it, no matter how "great" they make the thing.

• Virtual Product Insight #5: Keep talking to & learning from customers - and incorporate what
you learn into the 2.0 and 3.0 versions of your product. The online virtual world has given us the
ability to "iterate" - to continue to make better and better versions of our products. This isn't so for
hard goods, like books, where you must make sure everything is PERFECT before you start
distributing it. But with an eBook, you can easily edit it, then upload the newer version and
you're done. It helps if you have the mindset that Version 1.0 is just the simple product to gauge
demand.... Version 2.0 is a more built out edition... then Version 3.0 is a truly world-class

• Virtual Product Insight #6: Test prices, combinations, bonuses and other elements of your
product offer to find the one that works most profitably. This is another advantage of a virtual
business - testing these factors is much easier in a virtual business than in a traditional business
environment. Testing is critical - it takes awhile to find the combination of elements that work most
profitably. The truth is it's an ongoing process. Eventually you reach a point of diminishing
returns, and it becomes a better use of your time to create more products instead of "optimizing"
your current ones. But until you reach that point, testing can increase your profitability in a big
way.

• Virtual Product Insight #7: Don't fall back on past success - keep creating innovative products &
services - and you'll keep finding bigger opportunities. We want to continue innovating because
as we learn new skills we see bigger opportunities for our business... and we may even realize
we're playing a different game than we thought.
