Session 6: Productivity & Time
Management In The Virtual
Environment
• Time & Productivity Tip #1: Multi-tasking, distractions & interruptions are the biggest "thieves" of
our time - and they often increase "Virtually." Many people think if they could just work from
home they'd be more productivity. It's not that easy - there are a lot of distractions at home. Then
there's the issue of "multi-tasking." Tests have shown over and over that multi-tasking is a very
inefficient use of time and dramatically decreases our productivity. Watch out for it.

• Time & Productivity Tip #2: Our brains are literally re-wiring themselves, and we're losing the
ability to focus for sustained periods. In our current "Internet world" we're constantly bombarded
with bite size, short lived pieces of information, and this is "training" us to lose our ability to
focus. If we allow ourselves to constantly switch channels and we accept distractions in our lives,
we lose our ability to focus even faster. The reality is that we can only think of one thing at a
time. Practicing our ability to focus single-mindedly for a sustained period is critical to our
success.

• Time & Productivity Tip #3: "Continuous partial attention," "popcorn brain," & "disconnection
anxiety" distract us with temptation to "check again." That's what the neuro-scientists are calling
some of these modern phenomenon and distractions. "Disconnection anxiety" refers tot he
anxiety many people feel when they don't have cell phone reception and don't have access to
the Internet. This and the other recent psychological phenomenon are extensions of our brain's
wiring to scan the environment and to stay vigilant against danger and threats. But modern
technology has triggered these innate drives in a way that really does damage to our ability to
be productive.

• Time & Productivity Tip #4: Friends and family don't "get" the importance of not interrupting, so
you must "train" them into the habit. In both humans and animals, you tend to get the behavior
that you reward. If your friends and family call you on the phone or text you or email you, and
you answer the phone every time, respond instantly with a text message, or send an email right
back, how do you think they're going to become programmed? They'll learn to expect that
you're going to respond quickly, and you'll get cut up in this loop. Same thing if you're working
from home and your friends or family stop by and interrupt you. If you accept the interruption,
they'll keep doing it, because what you're communicating is "I will reward you if you interrupt
me." I personally try not to respond to anything instantly, mainly because I want other people to
not get into the habit of bugging me with things that aren't mission critical. Once in a while an
emergency happens, but most things aren't emergencies and can wait an hour or two.

• Time & Productivity Tip #5: The "royal road" to productivity is habits and rituals, & the most
important things to ritualize are products & marketing. When you're at home there are many
things that are more fun than calling up your customers and digging for their fears and
frustrations. The solution is to create habits and rituals around the key areas of your business. If
you create a ritual for something and do it at the same time every day, the first 30 days are
hard... but after that, you'll begin to feel automatically pulled into it.

• Time & Productivity Tip #6: The real challenge in the modern Virtual Business is that our
computer is both our key tool and our key distraction. It's like food for people with weight issues.
Everyone has to eat to survive, so they are exposed to the very temptation they're trying to avoid
every day, out of necessity. Likewise, we need to use our computer to build our virtual business,
but it's also a tool we use to distract ourselves and waste our time. Get clear on what you're
doing with your computer and approach is consciously - that's when you can really take control
of your productivity.

• Time & Productivity Tip #7: Start by physically preventing distractions and interruptions. For
example, I don't know my own home or office phone number. And I keep my cell phone ringer
off and I keep my phone put away in my bag. I either feel my phone ringing or hear it ringing
only once every 2 or 3 weeks. I check my phone just once every day or so to see if someone
has texted me. It's usually a waste of time to answer your phone.

• Time & Productivity Tip #8: Dedicate the first 90-120 minutes of your day to making yourself
strong, healthy and energetic - to your personal success. This could mean drinking water,
exercising, meditation, eating a healthy breakfast, etc. In other words, before you go check your
email and all that other stuff, start your day by doing what's important to YOU and give yourself
the message that you're in charge of your life. If you don't, you're essentially making other
people's agendas for you more important than your own.

• Time & Productivity Tip #9: Dedicate the first 2-4 hours of your work day to products and
marketing. This happens after your personal success ritual at the very beginning of your day. By
working proactively on your products and marketing before you check email or voicemail, you
have your priorities in the right order. This alone can really take your business to the next level.
