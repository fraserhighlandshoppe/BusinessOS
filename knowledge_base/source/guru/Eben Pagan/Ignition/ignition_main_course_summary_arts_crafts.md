How to Start a Business
Selling Crafts Online
with James Dillehay
Key Points:
Part I
1. Handmade crafts have grown into a huge market for unique gifts and collectible
treasures. The crafts industry is on the rise and many people are making income
through craft-selling businesses. These are not machine made, mass produced crafts,
but rather unique, handmade, artistic goods.
2. Why you should consider an online craft business now
• Sites like Etsy.com are huge online craft marketplaces, with 100% increases in
sales annually (over 100 million in 2009)
• In January, February and March on eBay (typically the slowest months for retail)
over 150,000 items were sold that included the word handmade

Part II
1. How to set up and run a craft business:
• Get basic business requirements in place and your legal entity created (this
presentation doesn’t go deep into that area): business name, business license,
state sales tax permit, and a business checking account.
• Where can you get ideas for crafts to sell? First DON’T just copy others’ designs.
Search YouTube for “crafts to make and sell.” Then search QVC.com and type in
“handcrafted.” Search eBay.com and look under categories, choose “crafts”
and then “handcrafted & finished pieces.” You can also see the items that are
actually selling and for how much in eBay. Visit craft shows, galleries and craft
malls.
• Produce crafts to sell or become an affiliate.
• Set up your online storefront in Etsy, eBay or your OWN site (if you want to have
a lot of control over your listings). Remember that established sites get traffic from
shoppers for you, but there is competition from other sellers.
• Promote your online storefront on your blog, other blogs, social networks, videos
on YouTube with links, Digg, StumbleUpon and others.
• Measure your results.
• Repeat steps 2 through 5 until you reach production capacity.

2. What equipment, skills and capital you will need? Pretty little actually: just$100 or so
for setting up your online store, listing fees and an internet connection. You’ll also
need to budget for materials depending on what crafts you make.
3. Where to Sell Crafts Online
• Your own domain: more freedom and credibility, and makes you look like a
legitimate business. However it doesn’t come with a traffic source.
• Popular sites: Etsy.com, eBay, Dawanda.com, Artfire.com, Bigcartel.com,
Silkfair.com, 1000markets.com, Artsefest.com, Cafepress.com, and Zazzle.com.
4. Preparation Steps for setting up your site or store:
• Take high quality digital photos of your crafts
• Write compelling descriptions (size, material, colors, weight)
• Write your inspiring personal story or artist’s biography for your “about me”
page
• Determine how much time you have to work your business and then schedule
your activities for the coming weeks
• Determine your prices, shipping options, payment options, return policy
a. Add up your material costs, your hourly labor, and 25% overhead. This is the
MINIMUM recovery amount you have to earn back, but NOT the selling
price
• Get your PayPal business account for free
• Save your descriptions and images in folders on your computer because you will
use them again and again
5. Promotion
• Free classified ads: icollectorbazaar.com, Craigslist.org, Youcraft.org, Kijiji.com,
Oodle.com, USfreeads.com, Sell.com, Blujay.com
• Getting mentioned in the Media (magazines): Homeofthesampler.com distributes
samples and promo materials from independent craft businesses to members of
the media – magazine editors and writers.
• Affiliate Programs: if you don’t want to make your own crafts, but you’d like to
sell others’ crafts you can do it on these sites:
a. eBay, Amazon, eBid, ArtPrice, Wigix, Yessy, Cafepress
b. Or create your art once and sell it thousands of times by publishing your
designs as gift cards, calendars, posters, etc. with sites like Bluemountain.com

Action Steps:
1. Get basic business requirements in place
2. Produce crafts to sell or become an affiliate and sell other people’s crafts
1. 3 Set up your online storefront
