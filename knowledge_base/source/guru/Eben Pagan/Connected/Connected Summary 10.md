SESSION
10:
Interview With Joe Polish
1. "Playing The Humor Card" can you get you very far. Being "chummy" and wanting to make people
laugh... busting on them.. and in general being childlike and playful... sets you apart from most other
people.
2. Engage in conversation with whoever you can: waiters, taxi drivers, etc. It's great practice.
3. Most people are intimidated by successful or famous people - but Joe makes fun of them. This is part
of why he's successful at connecting with them. Famous people are so used to people kissing their ass,
treating them like a god, etc., that they appreciate people who just treat them like another person. So
don't be the same as everyone else - treat them like a real human being, not an untouchable person.
4. Whenever you're talking to someone, you're either in communication with them... or you're secretly
trying to escape.
5. Most interaction is simply looking for a way to get out of the uncomfortable situation of actually
communicating with another human being.
6. The solution is to be willing to talk. See it as practice for connecting with people.
7. The Dalai Lama: "Happiness is a skill that can be learned."
8. When you're trying to be funny, your intention makes a diﬀerence to whether or not humor comes
across as funny vs. oﬀensive.
9. If you're going to be memorable, you can't please everyone. You will oﬀend some people. That's OK some people have no sense of humor.
10. If you hide who you are, you won't connect with people. If you're brash, be brash.
11. If you see two people together, ask one of them "If you were to get in a fight with her, do you think
you could take her?"
12. List people who would add value to your life and figure out how to create value for them up front.
13. The main reason people don't connect with other people is because they don't try. Get out there and
do it.
14. At the first Richard Branson dinner Joe attended, everyone was asking Richard serious questions
about the environment, politics, war, etc. Joe asked him personal and interesting questions... like what was it like to hang out with the Sex Pistols? What do you like to do with your free time? What's
the most insane thing you've ever done or haven't done? So Branson took an interest in him.
15. Joe also gave an idea to help Branson's philanthropic organization. As a result, Joe was the only one at
the dinner to get his personal email. He did a better job than anyone else at creating a relationship
with Branson that night.
16. Once you interview one well-respected person, you can name drop to get other interviews. Joe calls
this part of "manufacturing credibility." But be careful how much you claim - if you say you're friends
with someone famous, you better be friends... because people will find out.
17. Become a master of a few questions that you ask over and over again in each interview. Repetition
looks like genius.

18. If Joe is talking to someone and thinks he knows a person who can help them, Joe will sometimes get
out his cell phone, dial that person right there on the spot and connect the two of them on the
phone, LIVE. He doesn't hesitate.
19. Focus on being useful. Constantly think of how can you help people get in contact with someone or
some body of knowledge that will help them. Keep this as your mindset.
20. Contact people on their birthdays to just say hello, or send them something they're interested in.
21. "The value of any relationship is based on how frequently you laugh together."
22. If you want to connect with someone and you send them an email but they don't respond, follow up
with an actual call.
23. Your ability to connect has a lot to do with your ability to connect with yourself. Go to a 12-step
program. Volunteer to work at a burn clinic or a kids hospital. Or go to nursing home and talk to old
people. Go to hospice centers.
24. If you're depressed, go help someone.
25. Your very best thinking got you to where you are now. To get to the next level, you have to do
something DIFFERENT.
26. Make a list of the top 20 people you want to connect with in the next 90 days. Then make a list of 20
people beyond that. Send them each an actual letter or postcard. Drip on them. Send them stuﬀ that's
useful to them.
27. The Dan Sullivan R Factor Question: "If we were to meet a year from today, what needs to happen
both personally and professionally for you to feel happy about your progress?"
28. "DOS Conversions" involve 1) Dangers, 2) Opportunities, 3) Strengths
29. Build on your strengths, not on your weaknesses. You can't be good at all things - if you spend time
working on true weaknesses, you'll never make much progress. With Joe, he's good at starting things,
not good at finishing them. He could spend his whole life on improving his weakness... but he
wouldn't get very far. Instead, he hires someone to work around his weakness.
30. Spend your life on your weaknesses and you'll reach the end of your life with really strong
weaknesses. Not ideal.
31. Interviewing someone can actually create value for THEM. Especially if you get them to think about
things they normally don't. Remember this if you're going out to get interviews.
32. A genuine interest in the other person's life and what she has to say... goes a long way.
