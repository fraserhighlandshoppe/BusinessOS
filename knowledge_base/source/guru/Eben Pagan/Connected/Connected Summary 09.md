SESSION
9:
Mark Goulston Interview
1. When something someone says is compelling, it draws you in to want to hear more.
It's often counterintuitive ("I never thought of it that way").
2. Making someone feel connected to you is, in part, being able to speak and listen from the heart.
3. Most people play the intellectual card, instead of the compelling card.
4. One thing that is compelling is sincere interest in the other person. Don't try to fake sincerity to
move people to your agenda. Be open to being interested in someone else, but don't fake it.
5. Have the courage to get out of your own way. Mark didn't have this courage for years... until his
daughter was born. He believed she would be disappointed in him because he was disappointed in
himself... if he continued to give in to his social fears. He was most afraid of being put on the spot,
and saying something and being laughed at or humiliated.
6. When the vision of what you want is compelling enough to you over time, the commitment to action
naturally follows.
7. It's not that shy people don't WANT to interact, it's they don't know HOW.
8. Ask questions that 1) would elicit from them an opinion, 2) ask "what would you do", or 3) ask "how
would you feel if..." These three types of questions focus on thoughts, actions, and feelings.
9. Three things you need to succeed: 1) Mindset (a belief you can do it), 2) Skill Set, and 3) Capacity
10. If you want to make a connection with a speaker at a conference, be the first one to ask a question,
because when it gets to that point and when the audience is silent, speakers worry they've bored
their audience. The speaker will be grateful to you, and other people in the audience will respect you.
11. One great question to ask a speaker: What is something you wished you would have done diﬀerently
early in your career that would have saved you a lot of hassle later on?
12. Or contact the keynote's assistant a week ahead of time, oﬀer to ask a question the speakers comes up
with ahead of time that the speaker thinks would be productive to the conversation. And if the
speaker doesn't, let the assistant know you'll still try to come up with a question that will service the
audience and the discussion best.
13. Get people to tell stories that they're emotional about.
14. The purpose conversation is to be of service to the other person. One way to be of service is to help
people to clearly state what is most important and urgent to their success, and then do everything
you can to help them to succeed, including an introduction. The "coin of the realm" in networking
are introductions. This takes the pressure oﬀ you trying to sell them anything, instead just try to help
them get connected to people who can help them.
15. When you're listening to someone, "drill down" - ask them why is this important instead of
something else, and how you are you going to do that... this will help you think of ways to help them.
16. Some people think "I'm just really not that interested in other people, I know I should be, but I'm
not." Mark suggests to not confuse something you think is unnecessary with something you suck at.
You're so good at other things, you don't want to step into an arena that shows a lack of skill. Get over
it and practice developing your listening/helping skills over and over. Learn to care.
17. People experience Mark as real and authentic, as comfortable in his own skin (even if he admits he
can be a bit neurotic (because he talks about his fears and doubts and conflicts openly.
18. The less successful you are, the more you feel you need to cover this up, but do the opposite.
