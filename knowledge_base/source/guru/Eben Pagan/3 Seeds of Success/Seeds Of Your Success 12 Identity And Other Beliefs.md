Day 12:
Identity & Other Beliefs
Who we think we are has one of the biggest eﬀects on whether or
not we are successful.
In this training you'll learn why the concept of "I" is dangerous,
how to free ourselves from a limiting identity, why it's important
to do things that are "not you" on a regular basis, and much
more...
The concept of "I" is dangerous; because everything you say after the word "I"
deﬁnes you.
When someone says "I found out who I really am," they probably haven’t, because
it's really hard. You can ﬁgure out how you see yourself, or how others see you, and
that can be beneﬁcial. But that's just an image - it's not who you really are.
Most of us don't stop to ask where our Self Concept came from.
Who would you be if there was no one else to judge you, and you were living up to
your true potential?
Instead of seeing yourself as an individual, widen your identity to a member of your
family... and then see you and your family as part of a wider identity called a
community... and widen that to your city... and so on.
It's important to do things that are "not me" to keep your identity evolving and
loose... so growth is easy.
Look at yourself through the lens of who you're becoming... not who you are.
We create a thing's identity by:
1. Putting it into a category ("we are a mammal").
2. Looking at its function ("that person is a helper").
3. Looking at a quality ("he is mean").
4. Its relationship to other things ("that's the best", "he is a father").
5. Its interaction within a system ("that person is a catalyst").
6. Its value.
7. Deciding if it's a friend or foe.

We do most of these identity functions automatically, on ﬁrst impressions, based
on our cognitive biases, our culture, etc., and then we generalize it.
Whatever you think you are, you're probably not.
Becoming attached to a rigid identity can keep us from having the success we
want.
See all beliefs as temporary perspectives.
1. People who are seen as great heroes, at some time in their lives felt like

failures.
2. This idea can be threatening to the feel of security that stable ideas and

stable identities give us.
3. But if you want to get closer to "truth," all beliefs and identities dissolve. It's a

"quivering boundary."
We tend to believe we must see something before we can believe it. But it might be
more true that you must believe something before you can see it. This is why it's
important to create images of yourself fully actualized, because when you believe
it, then you can begin to see it.
