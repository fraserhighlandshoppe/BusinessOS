YOUR MARKET: Session 3
Your Prospect’s Irrational And
Specific Fantasies
In this session, you'll learn how to connect with your prospect on a deep, emotional level by using
the specific fantasies that are going on in your prospect's mind. When you master this skill, you'll be
bonding with your customers at a much deeper level than your competitors. That means more sales...
and eventually... market domination.

• One powerful thing you can do is get in touch with the specific irrational fantasy or nightmare
your prospect is making up in her mind. What do they secretly worry about when they're lying in
bed a night? Get in touch with this, and you'll align yourself with the emotional life of your
prospect - the life that drives her decision to buy, or not.

• Be careful here of two things:
1. Generalizing
2. Being logical. We're looking for a specific, irrational fantasy than your prospect has.

• In addition to exploring the irrational fantasies of our prospect, it's also critical to explore the
specific results they want to achieve. Some of these results won't be rational. Ask yourself, "What's
the situational result that my prospect wants? How would my prospect describe the outcome
they want in THEIR words?" Also remember the negative side - it could be a negative result that
they want to avoid.

• Explore as many situation-specific outcomes as you can come up with. And go deeper - why does
the prospect want that outcome? What happened the day the pain or problem started?

• Let's look at a specific example. If you have a nutritional product for women who are "back on the
market again" after a divorce or break up, some outcomes that have a "scoop of Irrational" in
them - and therefore are compelling - might be:
• "My man doesn't look at other women any more when he's with me"
• "People compliment me for looking so young"
• "My ex wants me back now and is kicking himself for breaking up with me"

• If these emotional hot buttons are too touchy to address with your prospects directly, you might
mention these scenarios casually, in an oﬀ handed way. "This reminds me of a client I had. Not
only did she lose the weight, but her husband stopped looking at other women when they were
out at dinner..."

• When you're relating to your prospect, it's important that you use stories about ONE PERSON. The
reason is that a story about one person is much more persuasive than a story about many people.
For instance, a story about genocide with millions of people dying isn't as persuasive as a story
about a particular person dying.

• A specific example of this is one of the most famous advertisements of all time. It was written by
John Caples. The headline was "They laughed when I sat down at the piano... but when I started
to play..." And then the ad tells the story of how all his friends thought it was a joke when he sat
down at the piano, but then when he started to play everyone was blown away.

• The headline wasn't "Learn The Piano in 4 Short Weeks." It was "They laughed when I sat down at
the piano... but when I started to play..." It was a specific situational outcome, focused on one
person.

• What all of this is really about is learning how to think diﬀerently. Thinking diﬀerently about our
customers and how to relate to them. Once you do and you get the appeal right - the headline,
the angle, the oﬀer - then your business just becomes a game about which you’re letting as many
people as possible know.
