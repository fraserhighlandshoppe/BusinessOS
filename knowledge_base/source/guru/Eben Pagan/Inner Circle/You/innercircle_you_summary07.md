YOU: Session 7
Solving The Puzzle Of Your Business
In this session you'll learn exactly the right amount of guidance to give your team, how to get them
to "buy in" to your business decisions, and how to install A-players in each of the most important
areas of your business.

• Solving a business problem is like putting a puzzle together. There are two ways of solving
puzzles.

• One is to look at the individual pieces and try to figure out where they go. The other is to look at
the puzzle cover so you get a view of the end product, and keep your focus there.

• Entrepreneurs tend to fall in the latter category. We're good at figuring out what the cover of the
box looks like, and the people on your team tend to be better at organizing the individual pieces.
But the bridge between the two is finding the corners of the puzzle and putting them together. If
you don't do this bridge, if you just tell them what the cover of the box looks like, they'll get
confused and frustrated because they need more guidance. They're not conceptual.

• On the other hand, you don't want to be standing over their shoulder saying "Put that piece there,
put this piece there..." But there are times when you need to come in and say "If you put this piece
there and this piece there it'll save 50% of your time." But that's diﬀerent. Generally speaking, you
need to get from the cover of the box... to putting pieces together... with minimum friction. But
your team is already putting pieces of other puzzles together. And if you come along and say "Hey
guys, I got this other puzzle we need to start now..." they'll see you as just bringing another one of
your ideas that probably won't work. You know that if only 1 in 5 of your ideas works, it'll double
the whole pie, but they just see it as you having another idea that probably won't work. And it's a
pain for them. These are the perceptions you have to manage.

• So these days I show up and say "Here's how I see things shaping up in the future..." Then I explain
the story and logic behind my decision, so they understand all of my thinking. I don't just show
up and say "Here's the cover box. Make it happen." I tell the whole story of where I started with
the idea, and how it grew, where we are, where we want to go, why I think it'll work, what it'll
involve, etc. If you're good enough at communicating, and they're a star, the ideas they'll come up
with to help the project along will be within what you wanted to happen... because now they've
been through the learning experience that you went through. They'll come up with options A, B,
and C. And one of them will probably be great. And if not, you can always say "Great options. I like
C, but I don't think we're there yet. Here's some more information you didn't have, go take
another crack at it..."

• Joe Polish says, "If they help you plan the fight, they won't fight the plan." Ask your team what
they think should happen. If you set it up right, they'll come up with options that you didn't even
think about it, options that are even better.

• A business is comprised of several components. One of the components is people. The
MacDonald model is dumb everything down so a high school student could do every task. The
Harvard model is to hire only the best of the best. You need to decide what models fits best with
you and your business.

• Regardless, you need to find the key protégé in each key area of your business. You need a driver someone who owns that business area - someone who is responsible for the results in that side of
the business. To find those protégées, realize that some of what you do day to day requires less
specialized skill than you think, and some of it requires more specialized skill than you think. To go
to the next level, get a process together so that someone who has a natural talent in that area can
use the system you developed and get 80% of the results if you had done it yourself. You may
need to break it up into 3 or 4 or 5 chunks, and have one person or one group of people focus on
each chunk.

• For example, you don't want someone who's writing sales copy to also do your accounting. These
are radically diﬀerent people. Divide up the tasks or areas of focus, then find people who
intrinsically enjoy whatever area they're in. So find a product research person who loves product
research for the sake of product research, and who would love to learn more from you. The same
goes for the other parts of your business, like marketing, product creation, etc. Find drivers people who take responsibility for results - and people who value education and developing their
skills.

• If you got a business that's working, and you see it's got potential to go big, and all you need to
do is execute, strongly consider following that path. Don't deviate.
