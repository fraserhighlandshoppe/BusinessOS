YOUR PEOPLE: Session 5
Trusting Your Team Can Make You Money
In this session, we dive into the concepts of Confidence and Trust. You'll learn how you can use trust
to boost the confidence of your team members, the relationship between confidence and
competence, and how to harness the power (and avoid the danger) of influencing the confidence
level of the people in your company.

• Dan Sullivan says something elegant. He says you basically need to give your people 3 things:
1. Confidence
2. Capability
3. Direction

• What is confidence? I like to describe it as lack of insecurity. My mentor Jerry used to say
Competence = Confidence. If you want confidence, become competent.

• If a human being doesn't have the thing we're calling Confidence, they won't act and they'll be
fearful. It will be a huge block to them perceiving things, making decisions, and communicating.
In my model of the way people work, we take in information, we perceive stuﬀ, we make meaning
out of it, we connect it up to other things we know, we make decisions, we communicate and we
act. If a person doesn't have confidence, that process is blocked. It gets screwed up. They become
very ineﬀective.

• I also believe confidence can operate on the group level. A group of several people can look at
something and say, "Yep, we can do that..."

• Wyatt's take on this topic is this:
Confidence is critically important, but unless it's backed up with competence, you get a lot of
enthusiastic jerks that don't have the skills to deliver and who leave things worse than how they
found them.

• So, over-confidence - having confidence but lacking the competence to back it up - is a real
problem. There's other people who have the competence but lack the confidence to go out and
take action on their skills. So there must be a balance between the two. If you err on one side or
the other - which many people do - you either don't get into action, or you don't get into skillful
action. You need both confidence and competence to have success.

• Confidence is related to trust. Trust can build confidence. And trust itself can be a virtue - because
it's something you can do to another person, that can then bring out that virtue in that other
person. By trusting someone, you can bring out the trust in them.

• Most people I know have much lower confidence than they should have in their life. And in the
areas where they have too much confidence, it's not true confidence, it's something
masquerading as confidence. It's not the relaxed, cool, "I get it" authentically confident attitude.

• One way to create confidence in another human being is to trust them with something. An
example of this is when Edison invented the first light bulb. After he invented it, he handed it to
his assistant to put it into storage until the next when they were going to show it to the public for
the first time in history. As the assistant was walking up the stairs to put it away, he dropped it and
it broke. Edison immediately began working on it for 24 hours straight to get another prototype
made in time for the show. When he had completed it for the second time, he once again handed
it to his assistant to put it away. An action likes this speaks louder than any words ever could.

• As managers and leaders, we have opportunities to install this confidence through trust. But it's
not always easy. When someone on our team makes a costly mistake, we often have an emotional
response in front of them. If you're the boss, doing this can have a permanent negative eﬀect on
that person. Likewise, trusting that same person can help them find an inner strength they didn't
know they had. One way to demonstrate this trust is by giving another person the responsibility
to do something - to "give them the ball." This can be huge for their confidence.

• Trust is an interesting word. Trust is earned - it's not automatic. It's earned by doing what you
promised to do. By giving people trust, you're basically communicating to them that they've
earned this opportunity to do this, and that you believe they have the competence to carry it out.
