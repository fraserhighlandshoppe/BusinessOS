YOUR PEOPLE: Session 4
Truly Understanding Other People
In this session, you'll learn the single biggest take-away from studying personality diﬀerences, what
the concept of Utilization is and how to use it to take your success to the next level and, why our
behavior changes with the circumstance (and what to get more control over ourselves).

• The biggest takeaway that you can get from all of this is that people are more the same than they
are diﬀerent. But, they do have diﬀerent personality traits, and if you can stop treating them like
they're all the same - if you start treating them diﬀerent and accept their diﬀerences - you'll get
amazing results with the people in your life. If you don't or can't, you'll have problems.

• For example, if you have no frame of reference or model for how a level 4, 5, or 6 will all behave
diﬀerently, you could fight and argue with them for the rest of your life. If you don't know the
diﬀerences between Sameness and Diﬀerence people, they could frustrate you to no end. If you
don't know the diﬀerence between Judger and Perceiver, you'll have big problems. If you don't
know that Perceivers take the attitude of "Hey, let's go with the flow... let's have fun... let's enjoy
the process" then you're going to personalize the fact that they're usually late and make it about
respect.

• Or, if you're a Perceiver and you don't know that the person who you think is always trying to
"control" you is simply a Judger and that's what they do, you'll take it personally.

• But if you know this stuﬀ, you can use one of the most brilliant insights that man has ever come
up with: Utilization.

• What most humans do is resist, argue with, get upset about, and feel victimized by anything that
they don't understand and don't like. Utilization is the aikido method where you take whatever is
oﬀered and you figure out how to use it, flow with it, turn it into an advantage, and leverage it to
get the outcome you're trying to achieve. If you use Utilization and you do it from an ethically
evolved perspective, you can help a lot of people all get their needs met at the same time.

• Remember, when it comes down to it, we're all trying to achieve the same things, we're just using
diﬀerent means to do it. If you understand these diﬀerent personality characteristics, then you
can be the one who can help them work well together, talk to each other in a way that works, etc.

• You can say to the perceiver, "You know what? You just gotta understand that the person over
there, that's just their personality type. They need people to be on time, they're into structure,
etc." And you can say to the judger, "You know what? That person over there just needs for things
to be fun. They need to be in the moment. They don't want to be made wrong." Then all of a
sudden these two people can start working better together, and they can really accomplish a lot.

• One way to put all of this into perspective is to view humans through 3 dimensions.
• The first dimension is horizontal - these are the diﬀerent personality characteristics, and they stay
pretty constant throughout one's life.

• The second dimension is vertical, these are the diﬀerent levels of development from Graves'
model that we hopefully evolve through in life. We pass linearly through the stages, and we know
where we're at with each stage.

• The third dimension is front to back, and this refers to the fact that our behavior changes with the
context. Context is when, where, and under what circumstance. It's the "under what
circumstance" that's a critical part of the process.

• In certain circumstance of our life we're one way, and in certain circumstances we're another way.
Sometimes we're in a good mood, sometimes we're in a bad mood. Sometimes we're angry or
depressed or anxious, other times we're having fun and partying. Our behavior changes on a
moment to moment based in the context we're in. If not, we get stuck in a certain state, and it
becomes what's called a mood.

• A mood is an emotion that we get stuck in for a long time. When a mood becomes chronic, they
become a psychological problem, like chronic depression and chronic anxiety.

• One of the best way to view Maslow's hierarchy is as a map of context. We can view it as a
developmental model, but also if we look at it as a map of context, it allows us to have more
valuable information. In dealing with Maslow's model, we need to know where we are at any
moment, and where who we're dealing with is at any moment.

• With development models, we're basically going in one direction. With personality characteristics
it's either / or. But with Maslow it's a changing process that we go back and forth within.
