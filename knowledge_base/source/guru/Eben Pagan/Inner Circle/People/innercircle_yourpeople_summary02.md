YOUR PEOPLE: Session 2
Leveraging Sameness And Difference
On Your Team
Understanding personality types is critical to getting the most from the people in your business. In
this session, you'll learn how to overcome a key personality "bias" most business owners have, how
to empower your team to stop your own personality from getting in the way of the success of the
group and, how to relate to the people on your team who have the opposite personality as yourself.

• The fact is, if you can really "get" how people are wired, you'll have something like a pair of 3-D
glasses that can help you decode human thought, emotion and behavior, and understand why
people think, feel, and act the way they do. Even more, you'll understand how and why certain
groups of people think, feel and act the way they do.

• So let's look at Sameness and Diﬀerence, which is something we've talked about before. If I ask a
room of my business advice customers who is Sameness and who is Diﬀerence, almost everyone
will say they are Diﬀerence. And that makes sense because business owners tend to be Diﬀerent
people. I'm a Diﬀerence person too. I like to change stuﬀ just because it's been the same for more
than 14 seconds. But what I've found, as a Diﬀerence person, is if you find something that works,
the way to usually make a lot of money from it is to keep doing it... in fact do a lot more of it.

• Jay Abraham said one of the biggest mistakes in business is to find something that works and
stop doing it. That's what a lot of us Diﬀerence people like to do. So one challenge is to get
ourselves to understand that when we're dealing with someone who is talking about all these
wonderful new ideas they have for our business, we have to think to ourselves "danger"... because
we probably have enough of that Diﬀerence trait already. We don't need more of that, and we
don't need someone bringing more of that out in us. We need to curb our addiction to "new and
diﬀerent."

• When you're an entrepreneur and you have a team, tell them that you will want to mess with
everything all the time, and what you need for them to do is act like a pit-bull and growl at you
when you're going to screw up what's already working.

• Another thing we need to do is start to understand what a Sameness person looks like, so we can
comprehend who they are, and learn to relate and work with these people on our team. Then we
need to teach the Sameness people to speak our language, and we need to learn how to speak
their language... so we can get along and work successfully together.

• A lot of the Sameness people I've worked with tend to be fearful of changing things and fearful of
negative consequences of doing anything new. They know how to do what they do, where they
do it, and if that place doesn't work out they need to learn how to do the same thing in a diﬀerent
place.

• So, here’s what I've learned to do if I'm dealing with a Sameness person on my team. If I tell them
let's do this new thing, the Sameness person will ask me why I want to screw up what's already

working. At that point I realize I'm doing my Diﬀerence thing, and I stop. I ask myself, "How can I
balance taking what we already do and only changing it enough so it has a high probability of
reaching a higher level of success?" This helps me temper down my own Diﬀerence thing. I try to
figure out how the thing we're already doing is the same as what we're going to be doing.

• Sameness people respond very well to this. You can say something like "I've noticed that over the
last year we used to do this thing this way. We've been evolving and changing things a little bit at
a time, now we're already doing things diﬀerently, and there's a couple of new things we could do
to make it work really well. So, since we're already basically doing it the new way, why don't we
formally say we're doing it this new way...." This is a psychology of seeing the new thing similar to
things we've already done, and this help Sameness people relate to it and get comfortable with it.

• As the leader of the business, when you show up with a brainstorm or an idea, and you just throw
an idea out there, you may come back in 2 weeks and everyone is working on the idea you had.
The problem is, you never meant for them to implement the idea... you were just throwing it out
there as a brain storm. But to your team, your brainstorms are marching orders. They're mandates,
because you're the owner, the visionary. So you have to be careful.

• Now one of the things I do whenever I'm going to bring up brainstorms, is I really set the frame up
that these are just ideas, they aren't marching orders. I tell my team if these ideas resonate with
them, let me know over the next couple of weeks, then we can have another meeting and talk
about taking action on some of them.
