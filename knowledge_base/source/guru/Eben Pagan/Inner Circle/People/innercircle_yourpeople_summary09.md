YOUR PEOPLE: Session 9
The Perfect Fit Between You And
Your Employees
In this session, you'll learn the temptation of paying people too much, why team trainings can help
you attract and retain A- players and, how to get business mentors and what they can do to help
grow your business.

• Most of us are sensitive to the idea of wanting to take good care of people. But then we face a
dilemma – do we keep paying someone more money than they should be making because
they’re a good person and we like them and they have a family to feed, or do we make sure that
everything is based on math and results… so the people who perform are rewarded for
performance, and those who don’t perform, aren’t. If you accept the idea of paying people
because they’re good people, etc., you pay the price of demotivating the true performers.

• If you want to grow your business substantially, you’re going to need to hire a few people who are
higher caliber than what you probably have right now. And when they show up and look at your
operation, you want them to say, “Now THIS is the kind of operation of which I want to be a part.”
You don’t want the people you currently have to decrease their desire to work for you.

• One way to retain top performers is by doing team trainings. They can have a huge impact on
your employees because they communicate that you want to help them grow professionally and
as a person. You can teach something simple, like “The 7 Habits of Highly Eﬀective People”, or buy
a book for everyone and talk about it on a training call.

• For customer service, a couple of great books are “Raving Fans and How To Win Customers And
Keep Them For Life”. Have them read the book, then do a 30-minute call and brain storm ways the
company can use the ideas from the book to take even better care of customers.

• For the superstars I know, money is important, but it’s just not the most important thing. They
want to learn, to have opportunity, and to solve complex challenges. If a person who values
growth is in a company that isn’t growing, they’re going to leave. If a person who values growth is
in a company that is growing and has opportunities, they’ll be happy and they’ll stick around.

• The perfect alignment is when the needs of the employer align with the needs of the employee.
Sometimes the need of the employee are going to be beyond the scope of your company, at least
when you look 5-7 years down the road. In these cases, help them get prepared for their next
move up the ladder – even though it’s at a new company – but make sure they document
everything they do and help you find a replacement well before they leave. Have this
conversation up front.

• To help yourself grow as a business owner, get some mentors. With many of them, a cold beer and
a hot sandwich is all you need to get them for an hour... to talk to someone who has already been
there. Ask them what you need to look out for at the next level of your business? You need to
answer this question before you’re actually ready to go to the next level.

• Get a few mentors – don’t make them gods – but get diﬀerent opinions and then triangulate
what the “truth” is. If you do nothing else, get a mentor and participate in a MasterMind Group.
That alone will put you far ahead of most of your peers.

• When talking to a mentor, figure out how to add value to them, and quickly implement what they
tell you and report back their results. Treat them as if their time is valuable. Don't talk to them in
generalities, asking them "how do I grow my business." Instead, within a couple of minutes
narrow down to what precisely it is you're looking for. "I'm at this stage, I have this question, and
it's not the kind of question I can get answered in a book." That'll make it more likely the mentor
will feel fulfilled talking to you, instead of drained.

• On the question of having a virtual company vs. a traditional company with an oﬃce, there are
trade-oﬀs, just like there always are in life. A virtual company allows you to attract people you
would have never otherwise attracted (they love the idea of working at home). A virtual company
allows you to keep people longer than you would have otherwise kept them (people can move to
other parts of the company and continue working for you). I can see we probably went slower
than if we would have had everyone together in a central location, but at the same time, perhaps
we wouldn’t have had the caliber of folks that we did.
