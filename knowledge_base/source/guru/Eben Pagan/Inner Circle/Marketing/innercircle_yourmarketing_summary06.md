YOUR MARKETING: Session 6
SPIN Selling & The 5 Marketing Models
In this session, you'll learn the SPIN model of selling one to one, why it's so powerful, and 5
Advertising models that are at the core of literally every business's strategy to get more leads and
customers.

• Neil Rackham wrote a book I highly recommend called “SPIN Selling”. SPIN stands for Situation,
Problem, Implication, Need/Payoﬀ.

• If you were sitting down with someone doing some one to one selling using the SPIN model, the
conversion might go something like this:

• Situtation
So tell me a little bit about your business, how long you’ve been in business, how you
started it? How much business are you doing? How much would you like to be doing? By
when? Where would you like to be a year from now?

• Problem
What would you say, right now, is your biggest problem or challenge?

• Implication
You also said you don't have JV partners. What kind of problems does that create?

• Payoﬀs
What would be some of the benefits you'd get if you got to your goal of $20k/ month?

• Then repeat back to them their situation, problem, and the implications of their problems. Say
"Let me see if I understand you... " then, repeat back what they've said, using THEIR words. Their
words is where you’ll find all the power. It's okay to use diﬀerent but similar terms, but the real
power is using their exact same words, not your words.

• Then say the important phrase, "Based on what you've told me... the next step for you is to apply
to the program...and come join us every 90 days, so I can teach you how to get the JV partners
that you want, so you can build your business to a million dollars a year, etc."

• If you're doing the 1-hour version of this, you can dig in much deeper, and do something very
important - match specific things in the program with specific things your prospect wants. Neil
Rackham calls this a Need-Type-Benefit - a benefit matched up to a specific need the prospect
expresses.

• In my estimation there are 5 models to follow when marketing and getting customers. These
models are Advertising, Direct Response Marketing, Sales, Partnering, and Relationship. These
basically are the ways most businesses get customers. I've broken down each of these 5 types of
marketing into 2 key skills. If you and your company develop these skills, you'll have more control

• In Advertising, the two key skills are:
1. Creative Design. "Creative" means a piece of Creative, like a banner. It's a noun.
2. Ad Buying. By. Ad buying is contacting, working with, and negotiating with those who have
the space you want your ads in. The big players who make great money in advertising are
killer at negotiating good rates for their ads.

• In Direct Response Marketing, the two key skills are:
1. Conversion: you must be able to turn leads into sales.
2. Upselling: you must be able to sell your customers more stuﬀ.

• In Sales, the two key skills are:
1. Lead generation
2. Live selling

• In Partnering, the two key skills are:
1. Doing the deal: you need to find the decision maker, discover what their needs are,
what the initial test will be, who will execute them, etc.
2. Partner relationship management: you need to make sure that their expectations are
being met.

• In Relationship Marketing, the two key skills are:
1. Life cycle marketing: looking at a human not as a transaction, but someone you're going to
have a business friendship with.
2. Product collaboration: I involve you in creating a product. The customer is collaborating in
creating the product itself.
