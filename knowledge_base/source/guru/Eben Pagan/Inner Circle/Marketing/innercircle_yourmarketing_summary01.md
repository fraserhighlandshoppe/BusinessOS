YOUR MARKETING: Session 1
Introduction To “Your Marketing”
In this session, you’ll learn why marketing is one of the highest leverage areas of your business, the
danger of relying on techniques too much, and how to avoid a “cognitive bias” that ruins more
businesses than almost anything else.

• In this Inner Circle we’re going to be talking about Marketing, and about how to build your
business by doing a better job of serving the needs of your customers.

• In the business world, there are a few areas that have high leverage. Marketing is one of these
areas. Once you learn the key principles, you’ll never go hungry, so to say.

• I think as a business community we’re moving away from looking for one right answer, and
instead we are realizing that the context is continually changing and we need to change course
on a regular basis.

• In this environment, relying on specific techniques too much can be dangerous. This is one reason
why I teach principles much more than techniques. There are no magic pills. Don’t get too
attached to the details.

• Instead, let the principle sink in. What I’m trying to accomplish here is the “a-ha” moment when
you say, “Ah, now I get it!” I’m teaching you the principles of success so you can be self-suﬃcient
and apply them to a variety of circumstances, rather than dependent on a single technique that
only works in one context.

• In a group environment like this one, there are diﬀerent people in diﬀerent places. That can be a
challenge, but it can also be a benefit, because you’ll learn new things when the people around
you aren’t just like you.

• To get your needs met in this program, clarify for yourself what you want to learn ahead of time –
that’ll increase the chances of you getting tremendous value from the time and energy you’re
putting into this.

• Ask yourself these questions:
1. What is your biggest challenge with marketing?
2. What’s your biggest opportunity?
3. What’s your biggest strength? What’s the area where you and your business are the strongest?

• Also, watch out for one of the biggest mistakes business owners make. In psychology it’s referred
to as the Sunk Cost Bias.

• Instead of realizing that every day is a new opportunity, we think about what we’ve already
invested in a certain project and try to hold on to it. If we invested $1 million in something, even if
we’re getting no results, we think we should stay with it because we’ve already invested so much.

• Another way to look at it is that today is a new day, and if something’s not working and it looks
like it won’t work in the future, we can walk away and focus on something else. Things that aren’t
working that you stick with… you get stuck with.

• But before you go try something else that you don’t know anything about, first really capitalize on
your strengths. There are many cases in the business annals that speak to this truth: consumer
products businesses going into the real estate business... Hershey going into the hard candy
niche… etc.

• Don’t underestimate the power of the human mind to take something that’s working and screw it
up. Find out where you’re already really strong, where you already really know your customers,
their emotions, their situations, how to relate to their situation so specifically that they feel
connected with you, etc. Start there. Start with your strengths.

• One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned from the marketing, sales, and business world is from
Napoleon Hill. The idea is Definite Major Purpose.

• From the perspective of a business leader, this is idea is key, because if you don’t know what
you’re trying to do in your business, you won’t have the motivation to do it. If you have a $1
million business and your purpose is to take it to $10 million, you’ll be focused on how to 10x your
traﬃc, or 10x your customer value, etc.

• Definite Major Focus brings discipline to your thinking, conversations, and actions. It prevents you
from flopping around in details that won’t be enough to get you to where you want to go. When
you start writing your Marketing, having a Definite Major Focus allows you to give yourself
permission to not be totally perfect in your execution.

• For example, if you’re totally committed to helping other people get a particular solution, it
doesn’t matter if your sales page has a few typos, people will feel your authenticity and you will
separate yourself from the pack.
