Session 10: Building Your
Team & Next Steps
In this session we're going to wrap up with some various ideas for growing and scaling
your business once you reach proof of concept and have a product and marketing strategy
that works. I'm going to share how to build a really great team, a few trends I see in
business, and what to do next with the material you've learned in this program.

One of the most important things to realize as you start growing your business is that no
great growth is possible without other people. You're going to need to get other people
involved, whether you hire them as employees or contractors. You're going to need to
build a team, and when you're building a team it's very easy to screw up and not do it
right.
The first people you hire should take work off of your plate. You want to "buy back
your own time," because you - as the head money maker - need to focus more and more
of your time on products and marketing. You need to delegate all the busy work off
your plate. When I started my first information business online, it was just me at my
computer in my bedroom. I soon realized I was putting in a lot of hours each week doing
customer service. So I hired a Virtual Assistant (VA). It was a great experience. I went on
Elance.com and hired a wonderful woman named Sheila. She came to work with me part
time. Instantly I had bought back about 20 hours a week of my time because now she
was handling all the customer support stuff. Giving someone else the work that you are
used to doing is counter-intuitive for many Entrepreneurs. The thing that feels more
"natural" to them is to do everything themselves. But that keeps you from going up to the
next higher level of productivity and working on the bigger issues that make the business
and you more money. So it's very important to buy back your time by hiring people to do
some of the work for you. Hire these types of people first.
When hiring people, it's important that you don't hire just anyone. That's the way to
destroy a great business or business idea. From Brad Smart, the author of Top Grading, I
learned that most people who are hired are actually "mis-hires," meaning they don't work
out. When someone is a mis-hire, they typically cost the business something like 20 times
their annual salary. It seems unbelievable, but we need to consider that when we have a
mis-hire not only does it waste a bunch of time hiring them, then they come to work and
they don't do the work they're supposed to do, they often screw things up, and when they
go they leave a bunch of problems behind. Something like 75% of people are mis-hires.
We need to do something to minimize the number of mis-hires in our own businesses.
You need to find the "stars" to work for you - the best of the best. Even if you're just
hiring someone part-time to do customer service, it's still a big deal. This is where the
problems start. If you interview 10 people who apply for the role, and you use good
interview techniques, what you're going to find is 1 or 2 of them are going to be much

better than average. Not just a little better - but heads and shoulders above the rest. That
one star candidate, if they come to work for you, isn't going to do just a little better
than the others you interviewed.. They are going to do twice as good. And because
they're a "star" and are motivated, they are not just going to learn customer service...
they are going to learn other areas as well. They are going to learn how to interact with
customers... next thing you know more sales are happening because of how they are
handling customers... and that's going to be really cool for you. You'll end up being very
thankful that you picked a star, rather than what Brad Smart calls a "C Player."
So how do you spot a star? There's a couple of ways. One way is you do a Top Grading
interview where you ask them to go through all the jobs they have ever had, one after
another in sequence, and then ask them what their expectations were when they took the
job, what their responsibilities were, who their boss was, their strengths and weaknesses,
what projects they completed, the results they got... and then very importantly ask them
this: "When I talk to your boss from that job and I ask them what your strengths and
weaknesses were, what is that boss going to tell me?" This is a killer technique from
Brad Smart. The reason we ask what their boss is going to say is because C Players
know
their boss isn't going to say anything nice about them and will hate it if you go talk to
them. All of a sudden they'll start acting VERY nervous. Whereas stars are still on good
terms with their previous bosses, and they won't be put off by the slightest with your
intention to talk to them. This is why you want to do the serial interview. Plus, when
you ask someone about their entire work history, you are going to spot patterns that even
they won't have ever noticed. For example, when you ask them for their successes and
failures, the C-Player will start making up excuses for their lack of results. But the Aplayer is always figuring out how to pull success from the jaws of defeat. They don't
make up excuses - they figure out how to get results.
Now... take what you're learned in this program... use it to first make yourself and your
business stronger... so you can them go out into the world and contribute in bigger ways
then you ever could before.
