Session 3:
Clarifying Your Communication
In session 3 you’ll learn why clarity is the key to effective communication. You’ll understand why most
communication is unsuccessful. And you’ll do an exercise to create the most specific and concrete
versions of your goals.
• Clarity is the key to effective communication. Before you can send the message, and before it can
be received, it must exist in the first place.
• Continually refine your own definitions of reality and the world, to better communicate and
achieve results. The more clarity you get, the better your ability to create messages that are more
effectively sent and received.
• When you find an idea or a concept that you want to communicate it’s important that you do
some research:
• Study the etymology, or source of the word, and the definition from where it originated.
• Read several different definitions to see where others draw the boundaries around the word.
• Look for 3 to 7 different definitions to really understand the idea and how others perceive that
word or concept.
• Look at all definitions as partially complete works in progress.
• We are an over-communicated species living in an over-communicated society, so it’s important to
get clear on our ideas and our beliefs to clearly communicate information that is meaningful.
• There is a sense of over communication so it is important to make it clear on what information is
important and what isn’t so there is no distraction with the noise that is out there.
• Humans are constantly seeking out new information and new information is perceived as
valuable information. Humans can create new information on an ongoing basis.
• The rule is that communication is unsuccessful and more of your communication is misunderstood
than is understood. Most of what we attempt to communicate is misunderstood, because we all
have different words, stories, body language, and meaning that we apply to how we
communicate.
• Clarification is key. Stop and boil it down to the most basic idea or thought, and make your
message clear.

• The next level to clarity are your values. Value systems run unconsciously. By having a value
system in place the decision making process is easy, and will keep you on the track to
accomplishing goals and clearly communicating your message.
• Clarify what you want and the outcome that you want. Challenge yourself to be more specific
and concrete.
• EXERCISE:
• Clarify your 3 key goals and make them as specific as possible. Create the specific, concrete
version of your goals. Make them measurable and clear. Example: “In 9 months I’m going to
be able to run 6 miles in under 30 minutes.”
• Then, put them into priority order with the #1 at the top. A good model of importance is 60 80% of your time would be dedicated to the most important goal, 20 - 40% on the second
most important, and 10 - 20% on the third most important.
• For extra credit, look at how you spend your time now and where the majority of your time
goes.
