Session 2:
Concepts, Strategies & Channels
Of Communication
In session 2 you’ll learn concepts, strategies, and channels of communication. You’ll learn how to use
words and thoughts to form concepts and how they work together to shape your reality. You’ll also
do an exercise to help you communicate your goals to yourself and others.
• Communication appears simple but is actually challenging. To get a message from one human to
another takes skill and expertise.
• When communication is not achieved conflict and misunderstanding are the results.
• When communication is achieved, everything internally and externally starts working better physically, emotionally, and mentally.
• Words and language mean very different things to different people. They are symbols and not
reality. So, we must use more than words to get our message across.
• Words are really just mental connectors between other words. Example: If I say “What does
‘happiness’ mean?”, you’ll start giving other words to describe it.
• Words, images, and thoughts combine into concepts. These concepts connect words, thoughts,
and ideas into a higher whole. Example: The concept you have of yourself - the self-concept - is a
connection of words, thoughts, and ideas about yourself.
• Outside of a context words do not have any meaning, just like individual letters don’t have a
meaning outside of words.
• When you put all of your concepts together, they combine into your sense of reality.
• How we see the world and how we see ourselves determines the meaning of words and ideas. If
you want to most profoundly communicate with yourself and others, you have to communicate on
higher levels.
• Strategies for Clear Communication:
• Know what you’re trying to achieve, and be willing to experiment and try new things until you
get it.

• The most important communication you have is with yourself. The second most important
communication is with the group of people that you have around you.
• Communication with yourself is happening all the time and is often unconscious, so, you
should take control of communication to communicate clearly with yourself.
• The group of people that you are surrounded with is important because the people we
surround ourselves with powerfully influence us and shape our communication and our
actions.
• The responsibility of communication belongs to the communicator. We usually make the other
person wrong for not getting our message. The more responsibility that you take for your
communication, the more you increase the chance of being understood and the more effective
your communication will be.
• Be the message that you are trying to communicate, don’t just say it. Make the words a
description of what you are already doing. When you are your message you can multiply
your communication power by 10x or 100x.
• Human communication is built on top of a foundation of animal communication.
• A great model for this is Dr. Paul MacLean’s “Triune Brain Theory”. He figured out that we
actually have 3 brains in one: The physical brain, the emotional brain, and the logical brain.
Each of these brains speaks a different language.
• Physical Brain: It speaks the language of action, movement, sensations, and physical things in the
real world. A physical communication might be, “Sit here, fill out this form, and hand it to me
when you’re finished.”
• Emotional Brain: It speaks the language of feelings, emotions, relationship, and motivations. This is
mostly between body language, expressions, and touch. An emotional communication might be
to smile, laugh, or cry.
• Logical Brain: It speaks through the language of thoughts, symbols, and concepts that represent
something else. The native language is thought and words that represent these symbols in our
mind and symbols that represent something in the real world. A logical communication might be,
“Newton’s law shows that every point of mass in the universe attracts other points of masses that
are…”.
• Use all 3 channels of communication as often as possible. You can usually use all 3 of them
interchangeably when communicating.
• Another model of communication that comes from NLP says, human being process information
and communicate in one of four different ways:
• Visual - “I see what you’re saying, let me show you something, I get the picture”.
• Auditory - “I hear you, that rings a bell, that sounds right to me”.

• Kinesthetic - “I grasp what you mean, that feels right, it fits together”.
• Digital - “That makes sense to me, the logic is good, I agree with that concept”.
• When you’re communicating should you tell the truth? All animals lie and use deception. Brad
Blanton shows that the major cause of stress is lying in the form of withholding information and not
sharing the big picture to others.
• The more you tell the truth the more you can notice your own self-deception, and the more you
can convey your truth and message to others.
• It’s better to endure the temporary pain of telling the truth than to manage the stress of lying and
keeping up the story that you are telling yourself and others.
• EXERCISE:
• Choose one of your goals and communicate it to yourself and others physically, emotionally
and conceptually.
• What is the body language, expressions, and physical postures of a person that has achieved
that goal?
• What will your emotional states be when you achieve that goal? What will it feel like?
• What are the ideas, words, thinking, and beliefs of a person that has achieved that goal?
What will be happening in the conceptual mind?
• Find someone to communicate that goal to on all channels, and explain it to them in the
physical, emotional, and conceptual levels.
