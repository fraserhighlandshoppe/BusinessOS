4. Fix Them
Now that you know which qualities are missing from each bullet, here's how to fix them.
To Add Usefulness…
Explain how each benefit makes life better. You may also detail the tangible outcome of having that advantage.
Here's how FiberFix does it with these 2 bullets:
To Add Uniqueness...
Compare your product to the standard solution, like FiberFix does here:
Or focus on ways your product/training can be used for unusual outcomes:
To Add Urgency…
Even if there's no deadline for purchasing, you can create urgency by tying your product to the events going on now in your customers' lives.
For example, stopping burnout matters now because no one wants to lose their passion for what they do. Childbirth has a built-in deadline (the due date). In this example, MINDFUL associates their training with these important events, so urgency is built into the bullet naturally.
Note: urgency is one of the hardest qualities to include in bullets. Don't worry if there's not a good way to add it.
To Add Specifics…
Comb through your bullets to find anything that feels vague or generic. Then add the features, specs, and stats that will help you make highly specific claims and prove them with clear, detailed proof.
Now Rev It Up…
One of the reasons bullets work so well is that they leverage the Zeigarnik effect, opening information gaps that can only be filled by accessing the product. This is especially true with information products such as books, courses, or newsletters.
To boost curiosity, mention an outcome your prospects desperately want, but don't give away how it's achieved. Here's an example from a promotion for a Bottom Line Inc. book
Remember, hype isn't necessary to make bullets work. The secret to revving up bullets is to build curiosity and the fear of missing out.
If your product promises a solution or information that your prospect wants, simply telling them you have the answer creates desire. The key is to target your prospects' fears, hopes, and deepest desires.
Take this example from a Bob Bly email...
Not a trace of hype, but it addresses the exact information his audience wants.
Now, go back and fix your bullets—creating desire, curiosity, and urgency with specific details. Aim for a grade of 3 or 4 on each of them, but don't stop until they score at least 2.5.
