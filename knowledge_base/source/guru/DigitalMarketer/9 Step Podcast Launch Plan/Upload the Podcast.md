Upload the Podcast
Typically, on Tuesday mornings, our audio editor sends the finished podcast to us, and we’re ready to upload!
As was said earlier, we use WeTransfer to send the finished audio file. There are other audio transfer platforms you could use, including Gmail and Dropbox.
We then download the file in WeTransfer, and open the finished podcast in iTunes.
Currently, the podcast has several blank fields (which are also called tags), such as “Artist” and “Grouping.” For organizational purposes and user experience, you need to fill out these fields, so people can quickly find you when they’re searching in iTunes. The tags are your podcast’s keywords.
To fill in the tags, simply select the latest episode, right click on it, and select “Get Info.”
A new screen will appear for you to edit these tags.
Fill out the following tags:
Song Name: the name of the episode
Artist: the name(s) of the host(s)
Album: the name of your podcast
Album Artist: the name(s) of who’s in the episode
Composer: the name(s) of who put the episode together
Grouping: the subgenres of the podcast
Here’s how we fill out the tags for Perpetual Traffic:
Song Name: EP53: Ryan Levesque Shares 4 Steps to Creating Personalized, Segmented Campaigns for Your Audience (<< episode number and title of episode)
Artist: Keith Krance, Ralph Burns (Dominate Web Media), & Molly Pittman (DigitalMarketer) talk Paid Traffic & Digital Marketing
Album: Perpetual Traffic brought to you by DigitalMarketer | Paid Traffic | Online Marketing | Digital Marketing
Album Artist: Keith Krance, Ralph Burns (Dominate Web Media), & Molly Pittman (DigitalMarketer) talk Paid Traffic & Digital Marketing
Composer: Keith Krance, Ralph Burns (Dominate Web Media), & Molly Pittman (DigitalMarketer) talk Paid Traffic & Digital Marketing
Grouping: Perpetual Traffic brought to you by DigitalMarketer | Paid Traffic | Online Marketing | Digital Marketing
And looks like this in iTunes…
The final step in iTunes is to upload album art, or cover art. Cover art must be in JPEG or PNG file formats, and in the RGB color space. The minimum size for cover art is 1400 x 1400 pixels, with a maximum size of 3000 x 3000 pixels.
To import your cover art, again rick click on the episode and select “Get Info.” Select the “Artwork” tab and insert your art from your documents. Then select “Okay.”
While some of iTunes may have changed since this was put together, the overall process remains the same.
Your podcast file is now ready to be uploaded into iTunes. Before you do that, select the episode file in iTunes and move it into a document folder on the hard drive that you can easily find when you upload.
Next, we create a unique URL with a UTM code, so we can gather information about that episode. A UTM is a simple code you can attach to a custom URL in order to track its source, medium, and campaign name. This enables Google Analytics to tell you where searchers came from, as well as what campaign directed them to you.
Now, it is time to upload your podcast to your hosting website. There are many podcasting hosting sites you can use, such as Libsyn, PodBean, Blubrry, BuzzSprout, etc. The hosting sites range in price from free to $100+ monthly subscriptions.
At DigitalMarketer, we use Libsyn for our hosting site.
To learn how to upload an episode using Libsyn, visit Libsyn’s support center here.
In Libsyn, we will upload our podcast file, and include our show notes in the Libsyn’s “Description” field. Here, we will be sure to hyperlink out to our show notes using the UTM URL mentioned early. Then we publish our episode. Exciting! :)
With a published episode, we will now have a Direct Download Link. We drop this link in the backend of WordPress, in the podcast player, so the episode can play on our show notes page. This helps with user experience, so the visitor can stay right on our site and listen to the episode, as opposed to having to be redirected to iTunes. It saves the user a step.
