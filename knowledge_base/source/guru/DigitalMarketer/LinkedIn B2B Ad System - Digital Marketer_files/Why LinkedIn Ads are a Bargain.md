Why LinkedIn Ads are a Bargain
"LinkedIn ads are too expensive."
"LinkedIn ads never work"
I hear this all the time.
Chances are your experience with LinkedIn ads has been less than favorable and it’s not your fault.
If your business is based on selling $7 ebooks, then LinkedIn ads probably aren’t for you. If your business grosses over $100,000 a year, LinkedIn ads are worth revisiting.
I started running LinkedIn ads in 2012 when I was working as the online marketing manager for a software company. I was spending about $50,000 a month on Google ads so I felt confident I could get great results from LinkedIn considering the audience I was targeting.  I ran some self-service ads, which are the small sidebar ads, offering some whitepapers as lead magnets. The cost per click at the time was about the same as I was paying for Google clicks and I was targeting the ads to the various “ideal customer” profiles we had developed.
After running the ads for a month the results looked dismal. Some of my Google campaigns converted at over 50% while similar campaigns on LinkedIn were converting at around 3-5%. My cost per lead on Google was around $10 per lead and I was paying about $50 per lead on LinkedIn. Sounds horrible doesn’t it?
At first glance, the LinkedIn campaigns looked like a failure but over time I realized they were outperforming my Google ads significantly. You see, we were selling a content management software system that sold in the $100,000 to $5,000,000 range. Our sales cycle was 12-18 months so leads generated today wouldn’t turn into closed deals for a long time. Our ads were just the first touch of a multistep sales process.
Once the deals started closing from my LinkedIn leads, I realized 20% of my leads from LinkedIn were turning into deals worth at least $100,000 while my “cheaper” Google leads were turning into new customers about 2% of the time. Spending $50 a lead to earn a $100,000+ customer was the deal of the century.
If you’re thinking $5 per click for ads on LinkedIn is too expensive then you should think about who is seeing your ads. When people are on LinkedIn they are in a business mindset and most of the time they have a healthy corporate budget to pay for your product or service. When advertising on Google, you’ll probably pay a lot more than $5 per click and the prospect may or may not be qualified to buy your product or service. Facebook ads may be a little cheaper than LinkedIn ads, but they will probably convert less depending on what you are selling.
People clicking on LinkedIn ads were like people who shop at Nordstrom. They know what they want and they have the money to buy it. People clicking on Facebook and Google ads know what they want but may not have the money to buy it. I compare them to Walmart shoppers.
Who do you want clicking on your ads for high-dollar B2B services? Nordstrom shoppers or Walmart shoppers?
I learned a lot running LinkedIn ads and I know what works and what doesn’t work. LinkedIn’s ad platform has evolved since 2012 and is getting better and better. In this EP I’ll show you what’s working now on LinkedIn so you’ll get maximum return on your advertising investment.
