How Sponsored Updates Work
Sponsored Updates work in conjunction with your LinkedIn Company page. If you do not have a company page on LinkedIn, visit this page and set one up before you proceed.
To set up your LinkedIn advertising account, go to the LinkedIn home page then select Advertising under the Business Services menu button. Once you click Get Started, LinkedIn will guide you into creating your first ad campaign.
There are two ways to create Sponsored Updates:
Create a new post on your LinkedIn company page and sponsor the post, and...
Create Direct Sponsored content which will not appear on your company page as a post. This is equivalent to a Facebook Dark Post.
Remember LinkedIn Sponsored Updates are native ads so your posts should look like content and provide useful information. The goal of your Sponsored Update is to entice the viewer to click on your post to read your content or view your infographic.
In your LinkedIn campaign dashboard, select “I want to reach LinkedIn members right in the LinkedIn feed with Sponsored Updates
Enter your campaign name using your preferred naming convention.
Next create your content. NOTE: Direct Sponsored Update content has a limit of 600 characters if you do not add an image and  576 characters if you add an image.
Here’s a preview of your ad
Once your post is created you set up your targeting using the criteria listed below. Remember you want to start broad with an audience of at least 300,000 then narrow your focus after the algorithm works its magic. Here I’m targeting women over 35 in the United States who work in graphic design or marketing since my tripwire is related to 2015 design trends. Originally I wanted to target women over 40 but LinkedIn’s targeting option only lets me target the 35-54 range.
At this time, select Audience Expansion which tells the LinkedIn algorithm to find similar people to the criteria I selected. Once the campaign is running and optimized we can expand the reach beyond LinkedIn through the audience partner network which is similar to what the Google Display Network does.
Finally we set up our budget. If your goal for the campaign is to generate clicks then select Cost Per Click (CPC). With this option you will be charged every time someone clicks on your post. If you are looking to expand your brand and want a lot of people to see your post, select Cost Per Impressions (CPM). You won’t be charged when people click on your post but you are charged when your ad is viewed. CPM is the cost per 1000 impressions and lets you get your message in front of more people.
Set your campaign start date or you can start it immediately.
NOTE: All LinkedIn ad campaigns are manually reviewed by a LinkedIn ad manager and it can take up to 72 hours for your campaign to be approved. Plan ahead so you have plenty of time to get your campaign started if you are promoting an event with a specific date. Create your campaign well in advance and select the scheduled start date option in this case.
