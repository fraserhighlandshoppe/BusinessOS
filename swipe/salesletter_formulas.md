# Long Copy & Sales Letter Formulas

Long copy follows story structure — the same Hero's Journey movies use.
The critical reframe: **the customer is the hero. Your brand is the mentor/guide.**
(Obi-Wan, not Luke; Yoda, not Skywalker.)

## The Customer's Hero's Journey (copy adaptation)

| Stage | Movie | In your copy |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary World | hero's normal life | Prospect's status quo — describe their world so they feel *seen* |
| Call to Adventure | invitation | The possibility of a result; your headline makes the call |
| Refusal of the Call | hesitation | Their objections/fears — voice them before they do |
| Meeting the Mentor | Obi-Wan/Yoda | YOU enter: empathy + authority + plan |
| Crossing the Threshold | commitment | The decision to buy / opt in — first micro-yes |
| Trials & Tests | challenges | Address obstacles, show the path works (proof) |
| The Abyss | all is lost | Agitate: what happens if nothing changes |
| Transformation | victory | Before → After transformation (their victory, not yours) |
| Return with the Elixir | reward | Deliverable: what exactly they get; the "elixir" = your product |

## Classic Long-Form Sales Letter Skeleton

1. **Headline** (see headline_formulas.md — 7 Power formulas)
2. **Sub-headline** — expands promise, names audience
3. **Open with story** — hero's ordinary world; pattern interrupt
4. **Identify the problem** — their pain in their words
5. **Aggravate** — cost of inaction; fear of staying the same
6. **Unique mechanism** — why this works when other things failed
7. **Introduce offer** — present solution as mentor's gift
8. **Benefits & bullets** — fascinations ("what you'll discover...")
9. **Proof** — testimonials, case studies, data (people believe what they SEE)
10. **Bonus stack** — cannibalize products into bonuses; value stacking
11. **Price anchoring / takeaway close** — "you may not be ready..."
12. **Risk reversal** — guarantee
13. **Urgency/scarcity** — real deadline
14. **Call to Action** — one clear ask, repeated
15. **P.S.(s)** — restates offer for skimmers; second most-read line

### Eben Pagan - Conversion Story
Story arc of a customer transformation used inside copy/emails; long copy sells better than short because it tells the whole story (motivated buyers read everything).

### Building a Storyline (FHS wiki, High Value Deals)
A good storyline is: simple (anyone can retell it in 2 minutes), relevant to the audience (entry-level vs decision-maker versions), surprising, challenges conventional thinking, specific about benefits/returns, plain language (no buzzwords). Structure: (1) prove we understand THEIR situation w/o lecturing, (2) our value proposition...
